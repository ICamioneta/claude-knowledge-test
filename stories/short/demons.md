A story of Carehope

Sunrise. Everyone gets up and immediately checks their face. Not a feather out of place. Everyone dresses into their own clothes. All cloth. Only three colours are allowed. Blue, orange, and the grayscale. Everyone gets out of their room, into the cosy wooden common space, around the fire. Everyone sits down crossed legged on their floor.

“Join together, Continuate, the Eternal Spirit of the Immortal.”

A phrase you hear from yourself, from those beside you, and muffled from those in every house in Carehope. Followed by silence. The only relief you get in this life. Ten sweet minutes of forgetting that any of this is real. Ten minutes of stillness and breathing. Ten minutes of ignorance.

“The Immortal awakens for another day”

And you get up and go do something for the day.

But it gets to you. Not just the monotony. Not just the gratuitousness of it all. Not just realising that this is all for nothing. Not just the worry of what happens when you’re cut off. Not just the sweat, the tears, the ache, the toil. It’s the screaming.

The Gods are evil, the Esten possess and supplant the ideas and morals of the Immortal. You are the Immortal. You are the continuation of their spirit. Your morals are his morals. Your thoughts are his thoughts. Except when they aren’t. Then it’s the Gods and their Esten. Possessions, planting ideas.

You ask, should we try put some green into our clothes?

You'r possessed by a demon. The Immortal doesn’t wear green. You are the spirit of the immortal, the only reason you would want to wear green is because the Gods are coaxing you away from the spirit of the Immortal. Coaxing you away from Carehope. From your family, your friends, your town, your people. Reject the idea yourself. Or, if you can’t, the priest will.

You get sat in a room with you, the priest, and everyone you know. The thought is choked out of you. They stay silent, but you see their eyes. The judgement. How could you think that, what an awful thing to think. How could you not be strong enough to reject the ideas the Gods put into you, you are the Immortal, show some strength.

If that doesn’t work, don’t expect to be seeing your friends anytime soon. Either they avoid you themselves, or the priest holds on to you, feeding you remedies, making you do meditations, making you do prayers. All until you conform. Join the Spirit of the Immortal once again.

And maybe, you’ll get to return to your own home.

“The day ends and the Immortal rests”

Back to stillness.

I’ve talked to other people before. You’re supposed to bring more strength to the Spirit of the Immortal. You bring children into this cruel cycle. But also, you talk to other people. You’re supposed to get them to join Carehope, to remove their own spirit and replace it with that of the Immortal. 

But this one person said something to me.

“I’m good”

And it gets to you. Everything. Your life, devoted to a meaningless cause. Your body, lost to the strain of labour. Your spirit, gone with any sense of self that you once had replaced with the personality you’re supposed to have. Your heart, broken as any meaningful connection you can make will be taken away from you after you… your mind. Screaming, screaming.

You hear the others in the room get out. Doors swing open. The air in the room shifts like a mountainous wind. There’s constant chatter. A calling.

“Priest! Priest!”

The door swings open again. The priest walks in. He is prepared. He is equipped with tools of the spirit. He talks to you.

“Begone from this body, minion of the gods!”

Some plants hit your face.

“Awful Esten, leave this place and return to the realm from whence you came!”

You can feel him frantically flipping through a book.

“Join Together! Continuate! Join Together! Continuate!” everyone watching is chanting at you, but it doesn’t work. You’re still screaming, hands covering your face.

The priest throws some liquid at you and it burns.

“Feel the severance of the spirit, Esten! You cannot attack the Immortal!”

The priest throws some more liquid at you, and it feels like knives.

“Feel the severance of yourself, Esten! You are expelled from this body!”

It only hurts more, but it starts reassuring you. What you feel inside matches how you feel on the outside. Screaming becomes little more difficult, and your mind goes blank as numbness floods your head.

“Begone, Esten, and have this body join the Spirit of the Immortal once more. Become a continuate!”

The priest grabs hold of your head, limp from all the effort. He forces some liquid down your throat, and it hangs for a bit, but it doesn’t hurt. It stops the hurting.

You can’t think, but you know what you’re doing tomorrow.

“Thasthel, are you of the Spirit of the Immortal once again?” the priest asks.

“yes.” I say, blindly.

I drag myself to my bed. Everyone else finds their way too. This whole thing’s seemingly reassured their beliefs. The evil gods are really out there. They really can possess us. One day, when the Spirit of the Immortal is ready, the gods really will wage war against us. We really will participate in the final battle against the Gods and their Esten. Knights wielding holy swords cutting through the evil demons and piercing the God’s foul souls.

I don’t believe that. 

I’m leaving. I’ve got nothing to lose. My whole life, this ‘community’ is a lie. What are friends if they don’t like you? What is family if they abandon you when you’re troubled?

I get out of my bed, when I’m sure everyone else is asleep. I get out of the house, out of the village. I’ve been told there’s evil in all directions, and the sun is a beacon of light, so I run away from the sun. I jump in the grass. I get my clothes all muddy and green. I keep running. I can’t stop, I can’t risk the town finding me again. I know what they’d do. I need to keep going.

The moon lights up the forest like the sun itself. I’ve stopped running, a slower pace will carry me further anyway. I see some light off in the distance. Not a Carehope town, we’re forbidden to be awake at this hour. I walk closer and I see a flag. The gold-red vee on a green-cyan background. I make it as close as I can before I collapse.

***

Sunrise. Silence. I lie awake in a cramped and tidy bed room. Three of the walls are made from well cut planks, and the stone side has a window in it. My bed’s a thin sheet of leather on a wooden frame. I get up, check my face. I sit down, cross-legged, and I say:

“Be apart, the severed, bask in the awful of the gods.”

And I sit in silence.

A heavy knock disrupts me.

“Hey! You awake?”

“Yes.” I say, “come in.”

The door creaks open as a man wearing a red-purple tunic walks in.

“We watched you pass out running at us. A bit strange, we thought, so we waited a while before bringing you in. Glad the wolves didn’t catch you before we went out. Thought you might appreciate a roof over your head, judging by the state of your clothes. That drawer there’s full of clothes, see if any of them fit you.”

“Thank you.” I spoke.

The draw was full of fabric clothes. Fibres, wools, leathers. Such a fantastic smell.

“You came from the east, you from Carehope?”

“Yes.”

“And you ran all that way in the dark, in muddy clothing?”

“Yes.”

“Wow. Well done, I’m impressed.”

I felt pride. I kept feeling pride. I puffed my chest up, look at me and my accomplishment.

“What’s your plan then.”

“I didn’t really have one.”

“How about we figure it out together?”

“That’d be great.”

“Let me go get a map!”
