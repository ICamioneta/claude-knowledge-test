title: The Monster at the Door

I live on a farm. I don't work it often, the person who owns it lets me live in my comfortable little lodge on their field so long as I help with some farm work once in a while. It's quite a lonely life, but I like it. I love watching the sunset, cuddled by the grass, crickets and critters chirping and hooting from the fields and the bushes that line the fields. I eat some stew on a tablecloth on my candlelit table. And I do the same as the sun comes up. 

I've done this for a few years, ever since I came to the farm for somewhere to live. I'm happy, I've come to tell myself, but the days blend into each other.

Until one day, when I watch the sunset lying on a spongy carpet of grass, and I feel uneasy. Just a bad bit of cheese, I think, as I close my eyes and watch the glowing sun dim into nothingness. And I notice a dead silence. No crickets, no owls. 

My eyes flash open. It's not too dark, twilight is usually plenty to see my way home. But something's wrong.

I hear grass crunching in the distance. I turn my head towards it. A bear? A wolf? No, the pace was slow and lingering, one step after the other. I don't see what's making the sound, I think it's coming from the next field over, from behind the hedge. It's probably nothing, but I make my way back to my little lodge, following the dim contours of my well trodden path back through the field. My house is in the next field, I think, that's not too far. 

Just as I make it to the crossing to the next field, I look back, the footsteps no longer coming from behind some bushes, and I see it. It was too dark to see it in detail, but I could see its outline. Tall, its chest too large, it's legs so thin, and I could see its head. It's eyes were too small, and it's teeth shone unnaturally in the twilight. 

I heard it's quiet shriek from afar. A deep gritting voice. "Can you see me?" it said, and I ran from the field's edge to my home. I open the door in a rush, and stumble inside.

In the dark, I knock the candle from the table onto the floor. I blindly feel for the drawer in which I keep my firebox. I find it and grab it. I open it up and desperately and grab the flint and iron. I strike it and light up a match, but I hear the grass crumpling outside as it approaches my house, and I flinch as the light I struck fades. 

I scramble to light it again, hitting the flint and the iron as quietly as I can, but I know it's here and it knows I'm here. I can't strike the fire again before its shape looms past my window, blocking whatever twilight was left. 

It's at my door. It casts its figure on the decorated glass. I crouch, frozen where I was with my fire box. 

"Hello..." It says, with the same low and grating voice. It hits the door, and my heart drops as the sound echoes through the room. It hits it again, and again, and I freeze.

"I know you're in there..." It moves from the door, towards a window, and I panic. I think about what's around me. I can't see. It's too dark. It's figure blocks the light. I quietly duck behind my table and it's drooping tablecloth. I go prone, and I peek at the window with my face against the ground. It's at the window, looking in, it's eyes wide and teeth invisible behind it's thin lips.

"It's so dark.." It says lethargically. I sit back up.

"I have a light..." It taunts as my house is struck with sharp shadows of the door's window from a lantern lit through the front door's glass. 

"I just want to talk..." It's voice fluctuates to a higher pitch for a moment. 

It waited outside my door, its lantern swinging jaggedly the shadows of my house back and forth. I clutched the piece of flint. It would make a weapon, if I needed it. 

"That's okay." it said in a defeated tone. All the shadows in my house moved up as it slumped down on my porch, bringing its lantern with it. "I can wait here for a while until you're ready."

I hid for a while longer. The silence was only cut by its eerie breathing from behind my door. Steady, calm breaths. It's lying in wait, It's a patient hunter, I thought. But as it waited there, sitting on my porch, it's breathing seemed... calming. 

I slowly crept up to the door. I sat myself against it. 

"I'm sorry. I just get lonely sometimes." It said, it's low voice disarming this time. Maybe I was getting used to it. 

It waited a little longer, breathing slowly, before standing up. It's wait shifted upwards, it's legs cracked as it stretched. The shadows in my house scurried around as the lantern swung in their movements.

"I'm going to leave now. It's getting too dark." It sighed. It started to walk away from my house, it's footsteps heavy against the few stones I laid out as my porch. 

I don't know what came over me. Maybe it was something in it's voice. It's calming breathing. But I stood up and opened the door. 

My door was in shadow, it's lantern light blocked by its body as it faced away from me. 

"Oh?" It said, turning back around. It's lantern light unflatteringly lit up it's chin. I froze up again as it stared at me from a few paces away. 

For a long moment, we just stared at each other. The world outside had fallen into near-total silence. There were no crickets now. No wind. Just the soft rustle of its clothes brushing against its skin.

I swallowed. Words pressed against my mouth, but I didn't know what to say. _Are you a monster?_ _What do you want from me?_ 

"C-" it hesitated. "Can I come in?" 

And I let it in. I turned my back against it and walked back into my house, leaving the door open. I couldn't speak, only gesture to it that I welcomed it. 

I picked up the fire iron. I was still holding the flint. My hands trembled as I struggled to strike the fire. I eventually hit it right, lighting a match. I leaned down to pick up the candle I knocked from the table. I could feel it behind me, breathing slowly. I slowly put the candle back on the table, lighting the wick with my match. the light was dim, but it was a much kinder and gradual light compared to their harsh lantern. 

"I'll put this out." They said, lowering the wick, "I like the candle." 

I liked the candle too. It calmed me down. I turned to look at the creature, and it was a person. Slightly taller than me, and they did have a wide chest and beady eyes, but it wasn't outside of normal personhood. 

The sounds of the fields began again. Crickets came back, owls hooted, critters moving through the grass.

"Can I get some water?" they asked.

***

We sat at the table, some seasoned stew in our bowls. We had been talking for maybe an hour by now. It was dark outside, and I was worried about her getting back to the farm house alone in this night. She was the farmer's daughter. Farming is a lonely life, she rarely talked to the farm hands, they weren't into the types of things she was. She saw me working once, alone, and asked her father about me. She came that night only to talk - to get to know me after she did her evening chores. 

She had a lot more in common with me than the other farm hands. We liked crafts, whittling, crochet, drawing. We made promises that we'd meet every other day, ideally not in the night like this. I was worried about her getting back alone, and she didn't feel very comfortable sneaking back home in the dead of night, so I laid out some pillows on the floor and gave her my blanket to sleep in and she crept away at the break of dawn.
