What a wonderful life it is, I thought, handling the tiller on my narrow boat. I do the least work on this canal. While the workers load the boat and work hard on the locks and the wizard works hard to propel the boat, I just sit here with my hand on a bit of wood steering, watching the world go by.

On this trip from Cania to Hireax, a few days journey, we were boating through some beautiful Senolado countryside. Vast fields of grains flanking your either side, rolling from the towpath to the hills in the distance. Ducks quacking in the murky brown water. The air as fresh as a grape. 

I sat by my tiller, the wizard working hard behind me keeping the boat propelled, when I noticed a boat coming in the other direction. A perfectly normal occurrence, I'd tell the wizard to slow it down and I'd steer around them. The canal is plenty wide. But not this boat, it had other plans. 

When it was about one hundred metres from the bow, one of its workers raised a strange flag. Black, with white and red stripes across. How odd, I thought, but one of the workers were more concerned. 

"Shit!" he said, "It's the Canal Privateers!" before leaping overboard into the murky canal water and walk-swimming to the towpath as fast as he could. All the other workers shared confused glances. "Where are you going?" asked one, but the overboard worker had already made it to the towpath and was trying to make his way through the bushes into the field. We boated on at our pace. 

Turning back to the other boat, I'd noticed that its driver had a tricorn on his head and an Eresonian Parakeet perched upon their shoulder, and a scruffy beard. No-one else was seen on the boat, but its cargo was concealed with fabric. 

Then, as we were passing, six poorly dressed people lifted up the tarp of the boat and shot harpoons into the side of ours. With strength they pulled out boat by theirs. The wizard, who felt his efforts of propulsion were no longer fruitful, stood up. 

"What is the meaning of this?" I yelled, to little responses but feint yelling. 

Once our two boats had touched, they jumped on our boat and drew their cutlass swords. The person who was on the tiller drew a small tube-like object, pointed it at me, and demanded.

"Surrender yer goods or face the iron of Annwvyn!" their scraggy voice rather piercing.

"I beg your pardon?" I concerned, as their crew grabbed of our workers, holding their swords to their necks.

"Gimme yer stuff, or ya crew gets it!" They said assertively, gesturing their iron tube towards our hostage workers.

"I must say this is rather objectionable, but we've no choice but to let you do as you wish." I fretted, when I noticed another boat coming towards us from ahead. The privateers rejoiced, throwing our workers into the canal. As they grabbed our cargo, our workers slowly clammered back onboard. 

"How odd" The wizard whispered to me. 

"I'll be reporting this to the guards later, if they'd believe me." I whispered back. 

The third boat had approached around a hundred metres nearby when a loud whistling could be heard from its bow. A soldier wearing a blue uniform and bicorn stood with one foot pressed forwards on the bow, extending a fine cutlass forwards towards the privateers. This was not the uniform of the guards.

"Stop, privateers, in the name of Charles Bourbon the Third!" the soldier shouted, the name of the authority I did not recognise. 

"Arr! The navy!" the privateer driver yelled, "Back on board lads! Set sail, full speed ahead!"

the privateers grabbed as much of our cargo as they could before hopping back on their boat, detaching the harpoons, re-covering their cargo with tarp, and moving forwards again. 

"You'll never catch us, yer Spaniards!" the privateer driver yelled pointing his iron tube behind him and pulling on one of its mechanisms. In an instant, a bang was heard and smoke was coming from the iron tube. It was hard to spot, but a small iron ball had shot from it flying across the canal and killing one of the ducks. The other ducks took off, and many birds from the trees flapped away.

The wizard and I stood in disbelief as the third boat pootled past us at a soft pace. At its back was another two soldiers dressed in the same uniform, one driving the narrowboat. 

"Blast!", the one not driving said with a stomp, "They're faster than us!" 

"No matter, Ferdinand. These pirates can't run forever." the driver said as he passed us. 

The wizard and I stood in silence for a while, watching the two boats slowly drift away from us. 

"Suppose we should move on." The wizard said.

"Yes, I think that would be best."
