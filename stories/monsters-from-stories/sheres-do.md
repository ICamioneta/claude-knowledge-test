At this time the entirety of Kairlle would be covered in a dense subtropical forest. Better than the actually tropical forest just north, many people would settle here, and many would move on to Adal. 

When they initially landed, the Kair were taken aback by the foliage of the subtropics, wildly different from the temperate small islands they originate from and a briliant show compared to the ocean they just crossed. Still traumatised by the history of the Hoxous, they made up stories about these briliant plants that consoled them, such as that of the keeper. The keeper is a real tree with a strange kind of flower that kinda looks like a tree itself. This plant is based from the [Hedychium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedychium) plant. Kair legend says that these plants look after life in the forest, protecting it from fire and attack by forming a magical barrier around the land.

On the theme of adapting plants, the Kair also encountered a plant with gigantic leaves, the [Colocasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colocasia). Being the first to do so, the Kair picked up these leaves and noticed their utility by drawing on them. Their letters were based on the old Wanium-based letters used in Gazi, but with extra curve to make them not tear the leaf. These shapes would form many of the exotic letters present in modern day Kithral scripts. Due to this plant's significance to Kair culture, it was mythologised that deep in the forest stood a truly gigantic plant with libraries of leaves, the scribbles on each telling the greatest of tales and scribing the most insignificant of pasts. By finding this tree, you could make your own addition to the plant writing a nice future for yourself or even condemning your enemies.

More on plants, apparently the [Passiflora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passiflora) is subtropical. Its the only plant I've looked at and thought "i would pollinate that if I were a bee". Its structure is just so unique its easy to think that someone placed the leaves on there. The Kair looked at this flower and mythologised it a significance. Not only is it the patron flower of the Kair, but its central pattern is a symbol of their people and their history. It also makes a nice tea. One of the myths surrounding the flower is a little wizard who builds each of them whenever they bloom, and enchants tea made with the flower to make it calming. The petals of the Passiflora also made it onto garments.

I can implement all three of these plants into Wastelife through Raikeor. Raikeor, the Ews community in Ereso's northern forest, has a sort of paragon-like figure in its cooperation. This creature, for now known as Arwiaran. Its some sort of creature told to have a magical defensive barrier around it that it uses to protect the forest. It is also known to have many leaf books, and probably symbolises unity or something.

Onto more direct monsters I can make from these three stories
I suppose since they're just tales given to the sheres do's new plants, Daklyn would have to make it quite abstracted from the original story, using it to inspire a monster rather than to make one.
So i suppose the first plant would inspire some sort of creature with a magical shield around it? It might have developed this as a unique defence mechanism, only to be hunted to near extinction in the modern world due to its usefulness in magic. This could be why it's almost unique to northern Ereso which is under the protection of Raikeor. This would also make it be in Eswin which I think works just fine.

The Colocsia would work as a great story element, providing the chance to give northern Eresonian villains more depth as well as introducing a new B-plot or even an A-plot in some stories. Raikeor would likely take great care for this magical plant. Immediately this conjures ideas of ghost-like floaty beings that wander the northern forest seeking intruders who want to get to the magical plant. Perhaps they screen people they contact with. Maybe they are brutal and do something fun like remove someone's hope to make them turn back. It might make more sense for them to just be guardians who can tell if someone is trying to reach the plant and antagonises them if they are. It would add an extra element of fun, mystery, plot, and Silpt-likeness to the Codia faction there if these ghostly figures had the consciousnesses or spirits of previous people who tried to access the plant, immortalising them in unwavering defence of the very thing they tried to access.

As for the Passiflora, I think I'll lean into their sedative effect. I also somewhat heard that they are carnivorous, having sticky flowers with digestive enzymes to trap and break down insects that climb onto it. Maybe the creature looks like a massive butterfly without wings, with thicker legs to support its mass. This creature emits a gentle calming fragrance that in higher concentrations knocks out creatures that inhale it. It is covered in structures that resemble the Passiflora's pettles, and can fire them from its body like a porcupine. It uses these, and deadly digestive enzymes on them, to attack its prey. 

I need to lean into the Kaer sense of worry at their new homeland. Without fully having explored this new forest, they made up stories so that people wouldn't wander off into the unknown. Many of these stories incorporated some sort of truth. for example, the land was inhabited by the native peoples, a relative of the Korongan that blended with the initial Kithral from Gazi to create the Kaer peoples. Before these two peoples met each other, on the beaches of sheres do, the Kithral people did notice some shadows moving in the distance. Whether it was a trick of the brain or the Korongan there checking out these strange new peoples, a myth became of it. The Wander shades (probably better Gazish name) was a myth about these shadows. They were guardians of the beyond, snatching anyone who were to venture too far from the beaches. Daklyn made some attempt to adapt the Wander shades into Wastelife cannon, describing a large beetle that would use subtle bioluminescence to remove or alter its shadow, that it would enlarge by flaring wing-like structures.

Stories of the Iavan lake would reach these beach-faring Sheresdoians. A couple people who did dare adventure further would trickle back stories of the lake. But, cautious as ever, the kithral Kaer would attempt to stray people from making dangerous decisions. They would tell about this lake, but also that it has a darker secret. Anyone caught in its beauty would find out its true nature, revealing a veiled abyss where the water echoed the cries of the past. This would entrap the storied adventurer, and they would loose time by the lake and be gone forever. Daklyn took from this a story of the passage of time, and made a nymph-like creature that can obscure the passage of time around perhaps some lakes or ponds present in the northern forest.
