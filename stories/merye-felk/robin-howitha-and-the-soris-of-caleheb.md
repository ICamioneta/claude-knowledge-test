**Initial Caravan Rob**
In Northlake Forest, wooden wheels of a horse-drawn wagon made little sound clattering across the maintained stone path, rays of light flashing over its rich burgundy colour. A swordsman rides a horse besides the cart, drawn by a driver. Suddenly, a tree crashes in their path. The soldier's horse trots alarmedly. He draws his sword, but in an instant it's knocked away clattering onto the path behind him. The soldier turns ahead, seeing a handsome chap with a well kept beard wearing green gambeson. 

"A-ha!" The green figure proclaims excitedly! 
"Robin Howitha." The Soldier dismays. 
"All the same! Now, you know the routine, good guardsman." Robin said alight. 

The carriage doors suddenly swing open, striking the guard's horse, which rears back with a startled whinny. A well-adorned man pokes his blue felt hat out. 

"Oh shit." The rich man exclaims before scrambling inside the cart.
"Stay put good nobleman, I hope I shall have no quarrel in robbing you!" Robin shouted.
"I shall not give away my riches so easily!" the man clumsily fell out of the carriage with a sword in each hand.
"Driver, take up arms" he said tossing one of his swords to the scared driver
"Oh! I do enjoy a good fight with equal opponents, but I'm afraid I've no time to quarrel. I brought backup." 

The Driver stumbled as they dismount, landing awkwardly on the road. The rich man looked smugly at the driver, only then taking another glance and realising that this was not his driver, but a bandit no taller than 5 feet.

"Little Jenna!" I hope you'd no troubles." Robin said.
"None at all, Robin." she said, gesturing towards the real driver, lying on the floor hands tied behind her back.
"Now, if you'd kindly stand aside sir" Robin kindly commanded.
"A duel! I demand a duel with you Robin. I'll beat you in a fair duel! Your infamous chivalry demand you honour it!" the rich man desperately proclaimed.
"Now, I'm none to turn down a good duel, but I'm afraid we're on a tight schedule sir. Another time, perhaps?" Robin said, the man in disbelief. 

Jenna rummaged through the carriage interior, her hands feeling a locked chest. With a quick nod to Robin, she hoists it up on her shoulder. Before their bewildered victims can react, they vanish into the forest.

**Camp Celebration**
Scattered across the glen, nearly five dozen outlaws talk in a hushed excitement. Some sharpen their blades against whetstones, other rest their bows. A few sit in circles, laughing over absurd scenarios and half-eaten meals. The Merye Felk make this glen their home. Though outlaws, they are an honest lot.

A voice rings through the glen drawing the attention of the Merye Felk - "Robin has returned!" The glen erupts into motion, people rising to their feet cheering as heads turn to the edge of the ridge. There, silhouetted by scattered light, stand Robin and Jenna.

"A successful rob!" Robin proclaims from the top of the glen before gleefully running down to the Merye Felk. The crowd's cheers could be heard from well beyond the glen. Robin reaches down to get the chest from Jenna. With a theatrical flourish, Robin breaks the lock and flips it open, a glint of silver coinage catches the glen's light. He raises a fistful into the air, laughing gleefully.

"Remember, my Merye Felk, we shall only do well enough! The rest belongs to the farmers of Pisentus Field, good folk who suffer while the Soris of Caleheb demands their taxes. The very same soris who dried their rivers for some experimental waterworks! What nonsense!" Robin cried, garnering boos for the Soris. 

"Melin Mux and I have seen to it that the waterworks work no more!" a Merye Felk proclaims.

"What a job you've done!" Robin says, preoccupied with handing the chest's spoils to the folk. 

Once the last pair of hands take their coins, he looks for himself in the box. Running his fingers through the heavy coins he finds a roll of parchment between his fingers. He lifts the scroll carefully, curiously unrolling it.

"A charter?" Robin murmurs to Jenna, tilting the parchment toward her. 

Jenna skims the words, her brows lifting.
"Not any charter, it's an order for a tax caravan moving across our roads, carrying Calaehebandinum's taxes to the capital."

Robin turns back to the crowd, holding the scroll aloft.
"Behold!" he calls. "A tax cart to the capital! The very carts the Soris of Caleheb hides so diligently."

"An ally, may I have?" he grins, "Who among you will see the taxes returned to the workers of Northlake?"

"I, Melin Mux, will see to it!" Melin Mux steps forwards, declaring eagerly.

A thunder of cheers erupts as hands raise in eager agreement

"Then it is decided, Ha! Take rest now my Merye Felk and do your thing at the dawn on the five. Melin and I shall see to the caravan ourselves."

**Tax Caravan**
The morning light glistened on the treetops, barely piercing the dense canopy to reach the forest floor. Robin crouched behind a thick bush, his gambeson snagging on the branches as he adjusted his stance. Across the road, Melin's hidden figure mirrored Robin's position, a hand resting on the hilt of his sword, leaves crumpling as he too adjusted. 

Robin's grip tightened on his bow as five soldiers came into view, their laughter and idle chatter cutting through the forest's symphony of crickets and birdsong. Scouts, robin thought, but without a caravan?

A cold blade pressed against Robin's neck.

"Stand up, slowly!" A voice growled behind him.

Robin raised his hands, his bow slipping from hi grasp. A soldier yanked him to hi feet and shoved him onto the road, where seven more men waited

"I present to you," Robin announced with a theatrical bow, "Robin Howitha!"

Melin's eyess widened from the bushes, his hand flicking in a silent signal. Robin gave a quick wink before turning back to the soldiers, his grin unaltered. 

In a fluid motion, Robin drew his sword, the blade flashing as he pointed it at each soldier in turn. 

"Come on then! Which of you is the Sebhar? I shall have a duel for my honour!" 

The soldiers tensed, their weapons flying to defensive positions. Before they could react, Melin burst from the bushes, stumbling as branches teared from his gambeson. Two soldiers lunged after him, but the Sebhar raised a hand.

"At ease, soldiers. Let him go," the Sebhar said, his voice calm and commanding,  "it is Robin we are after." 

Robin's grin widened. "Ah, the leader!" he danced, "Our duel will be remembered for at the very least a week!" 

The Sebhar, unamused, stepped forwards. "You shall have no duel, Robin. Surrender now or I'll have my men drag you back bleeding." 

"Well," Robin sighed sheathing his sword with an exaggerated flourish. "I suppose even the great Robin Howitha must know when to surrender. Shal we go?" 

The soldiers seized him, cutting his sword from his belt and binding his hands. As they marched him down the road, Robin glanced over his shoulder. Melin stood like a shadow behind a tree, his eyes locked on Robin's. With a nod, Melin turned and sprinted towards the glen.

**Robin's Cell**
Robin sat in the dim light of his cell, his wrists raw from the cold iron shackles binding him to a short pole. The straw beneath him was dry and reeked of sewage. The thin slit of the window offered little more than a sliver of sunlight. He shifted uncomfortably, his chains clinking as he tried to ease the stiffness in his shoulders. 

Outside, the faint sounds of commotion drifted through the walls. Shouts, footsteps, the occasional clang of metal. Nothing out of the ordinary, Robin thought, just a regular day in an Empire stronghold.

His eyes drifted to a tattered flag hanging on the far wall, Its grass-green fabric faded and frayed. The burnt-orange stripes and diamond at his centre mocked him, a symbol of the country he loved and the government he despised. What a country, he thought bitterly. What a mess.

A memory surfaced, a joke he had shared with Melin passing that flag on the road to the ambush. He smiled faintly.

The jingle of keys snapped him back to the present. The lock clicked, and the cell door creaked open.

"Alright, Soris," A guard's voice grumbled, "your prize is here."

Robin squinted as a lantern's light flooded the cell. The Soris of Caleheb stepped inside, his purple silken robes shimmering in the flickering light. In one hand, he twirled a golden dagger, its blade catching the firelight as if it were alive.

"At long last," the Soris proclaimed, "Robin Howitha."

Robin forced a grin, though his muscles protested his straightening posture. "Good greetings to you too, Soris of Caleheb. To what do I owe the pleasure?"

The Soris leaned down, his face inches from Robin's. The dagger in his hand gleamed as he pressed its tip to Robin's neck. "You know," he said softly, "I should have you killed here and now. For far too long you've been a thorn in my side with your robbing and rebellion."

Robin's breath hitched as the blade bit into his skin, but he kept his voice steady. "Ah, but my dear Soris, the only thief in the room is the one holding a golden dagger."

The Soris's eyes narrowed. For a moment, Robin thought the blade might plunge deeper. Instead, the Soris straightened, pulling the dagger away. A thin trickle of blood ran down Robin's chest, reddening his rags.

"But," The Soris said, his tone almost controversial, "My advisors have recommended I keep you alive. I'll parade you through the streets of Notami, let people see the great Robin Howitha starved and bruised. And then!" He paused, a cruel anticipation, "A public execution. An end to your rebellion." 

Robin laughed, though it came out more strained than he had hoped. "What a spectacle my execution will be! I'll have to pick my favourite ballad, or a memorable one perhaps."

The Soris chuckled. "The spectacle won't be your death, Robin. It will be an end to your Merye Felk. It will return back to legend, and you're name forgotten from its history."

As the Soris turned to leave, Robin's grin faded. The cell door slammed shut, the lantern's light fading with the footsteps down the corridor. Alone again, he leaned back against the pole, his mind racing. Jennna will have a plan, he thought, for he could not die after landing in such an obvious trap.

**The Plan**
The glen, once alive with laughter and the clatter of celebration, now stirred with a tense, uneasy energy. Word of Robin's capture seethed in their minds, carried by Melin Mux's hurried footsteps and the grim expression etched on his face. The outlaws gathered in clusters, voices overlapping in a cacophony of panic and disbelief.

"Robin? Captured? How could such a thing happen?" One outlaw muttered.

"The Soris will certainly kill him" another said, voice trembling. "What are we to do without him?"

Little Jenna and Melin Mux sat at a rough-hewn table one edge of the glen. Jenna leaned forwards, her elbows on the table, her sharp eyes fixed on Melin 

"What happened?" She asked, her voice low but urgent.

Melin sat back, his hands covered his face in dismay. "The tax cart was an ambush. Robin caused a distraction to let me get away, but I stayed and followed them. They took him to Fort Belvorus"

Jenna leaned back in her chair, her fingers brushing her cheek as she processed the news. "Fort Belvorus?" She murmurred. "There might be hope yet." 

She stood abruptly on her chair, which creaked beneath her weight. Scanning the glen, she took in the sea of frowned faces and slumped shoulders. Her voice cut through the noise like a blade.

"Alis!" she shouted. "Alis! Come here, it's Fort Belvorus!"

A well-built figure rose from a chair, setting their glass down with a thud. Alis awkwardly trotted over to Jenna and Melin's table, brow furrowed. 

"Fort Belvorus, what about it?" Alis inquired, their calm voice carrying a note of concern.

"That's where Robin is," Jenna said, her tone sharp and decisive. "We need to rescue him, and I already have something in mind. A bit of deceit, some trickery trickery, and the like. You're the Belvorus defector, you must know of a way inside."

Alis's eyes widened, a flicker of hope crossing their face. "Robin is in Belvorus? Then there truly is some hope! My dear friend Edvus is still a soldier there. He'd no doubt help us rescue Robin. The fort itself has no more men than the Merye Felk on a normal day."

Jenna's lips curled to a sly smile. "Well then, in that light, I believe we have a plan." She climbed onto her chair once more, standing not so much taller than a regular person despite her chair's advantage.

"Merye Felk!" She bellowed, her voice ringing across the glen. Every head turned towards her, the murmurs of dismay falling silent. 

"There is hope yet, we will rescue Robin Howitha from the Empire's grasp!" the crowd, though still unconvinced, stirred with excitement at Jenna's bold proclamation.

"I, Melin, Alis, and Tuchus will infiltrate the fort by..." She paused in thought. The glen fell silent, every eye fixed on her. "A cart! We'll hide inside a trade cart, and some of you will act as guardsmen and tradesmen." The glen erupted into chatter, but Jenna pressed on.

Once we're inside, Melin and Alis will rescue Robin while Tuchus and I create a distraction." A few lone cheers broke out, but most of the crowd remained tense.

"After the distraciton," she continued, her voice rising, "the disguised caravan crew will head straight to the gatehouse and open the gates. Then, we'll all fight to give Robin a safe exit!" This time, more voices joined in the cheers, though some were still doubled in doubt.

"We have to act fast," Jenna said leaving no room for argument. "Gather the cart and its fake supplies, we must leave at dawn."

**The Gate**
Fort Belvorus stood at the peak of a hill far away from the comforting roof of Northlake's wild forests. Under the cover of night, the Merye Felk had camped behind outcrops just large enough to hide a person behind. The morning sun scanned their outcrops, and a small wagon trundled its way along the winding dirt path leading up to the fort.

The cart's wrikken wheels was pulled by two tradesfolk and protected by three guardsmen, the Merye Felk in disguise. Jenna, Melis, Alin, and Tuchus were huddled around a hatch in the bottom of the cart, a thin veil of vegetables resting on a plank above. The tradesfolk struggled with the cart up the tacky road.

"Halt!" A gate guard bellowed to the cart, "We are not accepting any traders nor deliveries today."

The hearts of the Merye Felk dropped, and raced.

"What? Unbelievable!" One of the fake tradespeople began, "Do you've any idea how hard it is to walk the rough path from the road? We travel on your own call, delivering only the freshest of Terlayan vegetables - grown with the waters of Lake Daini. Do you know how far that is?"

The guard was unmoved. "Special circumstances, I'm afraid. If you've come from Terlayan Daini, then you'd best set up camp nearby. The circumstances will resolve in a day or two."

"This is ridiculous. We're on a tight schedule here, we've no supplies to waste any time!"

"We'll cover the costs of your delay." The guard said without pause.

"Wh- ah- well..." The tradesperson stammered. Jenna slumped as much as she could in the tight confines of the cart. 

"Wait!" A faint voice from within the fort was heard. 

Alis's eyes widened. "Edvus!" they whispered. 

Edvus's armour clinked as he ran to the gatehouse. "Wait." He said panting. "That's my shipment, let them in."

"Fruits from Daini? I'd never thought you lavish." The gatehouse guard quietly bickered.

"Well I expected they arrive on my shift, then I was going to surprise the guards. But now you know the surprise, I suppose there's no point..." Edvus said defeatedly. 

"I hate to see you sad, Edvus. How about we dedicate your gift to the capture of Robin Howitha!" The gate guard suggested.

"What an idea." Edvus said reinvigorated with energy.

"Open the gates!". Edvus and the other gate guards rushed to raise the portcullis. The collective sighs of relief of all the hidden Merye Felk was audible, and mistakable for the wind.
