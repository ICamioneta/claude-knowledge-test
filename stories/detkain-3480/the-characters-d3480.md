# The Workers

## Aelia
**Occupation:** Construction worker with a local government construction sector

**Background:** Aelia’s father was in the construction sector before her and set her on the track to become a builder. During her father’s job, some scaffolding collapsed, and he died. The inspection deemed this was due to weak materials being used in the scaffolding, a result of construction budget cuts made by the mayor. She and her mother held a resentment to the government since, blaming it for the death of Aelia’s father rather than the person who lowered the budget.

**Motivations/Goals:** Aelia’s main motivation is to introduce worker’s rights. She wants to change the system to be better for workers. She starts as a more reserved campaigner but is radicalised by her peers through interaction with more radical folk.

**Conflict/Challenges:** Throughout the play, Aelia will come into conflict with everyone that stands for the system. She has strong feelings against anyone who supports the status quo, and especially dislikes the leaders and the soldiers. She eventually gets over this as the soldiers reveal themselves as workers. She will have internal conflicts over her own ideas, initially not giving radicalism a thought but eventually supporting it whole-heartedly.

**Character development:** Aelia starts as an advocate of labour rights, but eventually realises the system would never support such rights. In her discussions with others she is eventually radicalised and becomes an opponent of the system. Her character will be one of blatant opponent to those who support anything less than change. She will eventually learn that many of these people are misled. While she will attempt to act more reasonably, she will never be rid of her dislike of people who don’t want change.

**Relationships:** tba

**Significance:** Aelia is the driving force of the revolution. Although she is initially only for change, she will quickly be convinced and recruited for the wider cause. She will have the opportunity to become the leading face of the working people.

**Notable scenes:** Aelia will express her hatred of the empire in the opening scene by discussing her unsafe working environment. She will talk to a more radical member of the working class before being on-board with revolution. She will express hatred towards characters who are indifferent or oppose change. She will see that these people are misled when the guard switch sides.

## Celyb

**Occupation:** Artisan Smith

**Background:** Celyb as a smith has tried to be as independent as possible, but has fallen under the contracts, quotas, and trade standards set by the Empire. The Empire has artisan smiths produce a specific number of weapons for their army, and pays them a fixed, non-negotiable, often underpriced amount. They are required by law to only sell their products to market guilds, and those prices are set by the empire as well. They are required to take materials from a specific supplier as mandated by a guild.

**Motivations/Goals:** Celyb is an opponent to the system. He hates it as it is, and joins the revolution on the people’s side. He, however, advocates for a capitalism where he will be in charge of his own private company where he will be able to set his own prices and properly search the market for the best suppliers.

**Conflict/Challenges:** During the course of the story, Celyb does not waver from his capitalist goals. This puts him in direct conflict with the majority of the workers who want a democracy. He will come into conflict with his smithhand, Hemery, in discussion of the goal of the revolution.
