# Scene 1: Tavern Discontent
The aim of this scene is to introduce the world, the workers, the theme of the story, and establish everyone’s shared goal. Many different conversations will be happening at once in the scene, in each of which a participant will be expressing a recent event that is an example of how the empire is detrimental to their interests.

Characters sit around a few separate tables. At the back of the scene is a bar (potentially with a bartender) is calis, ordering a drink.

The first table consists of Celyb, Meichi. The second table consists of Aelia, Nosric, and Carlea. The third table consists of Soren, Althe, and Mena

In this conversation, Meichi and Celyb will share their thoughts on the Empire’s economic oppression of artisans. They start with Meichi asking Celyb how their work is, at which point Celyb starts complaining how he had to put his family aside to make sure he met the Empire’s quota. Meichi will say that at least Celyb doesn’t work for a guild, where their usual products aren’t paid for individually. Celyb says that it’s the same if not worse, and that the prices for the tools he produces are set by the empire, who favour the merchants. He then goes on to say that he doesn’t even get to choose where he gets his material from, and how he knows other places sell better materials. Meichi them mentions that at least Celyb can get creative, while the rules and regulations of the guilds stop him from even imagining interesting pottery.

The second table consists of a bunch of the workers. Nosric will bring up theatre renovations that are happening, mentioning to Aelia that it must be nice working on such a prestigious public project. Aelia will mention that it’s actually a nightmare due to the constant safety concerns. She will mention that a number of workers have already received injuries. Carlea will make a joke about how her workplace injuries give people newspaper on their hands. Nosric will mention measures he takes for safety in his mostly independent workplace. Aelia will mention how she wishes she could make her own company so that she could set the standards for her own workplace’s safety. Nosric will interject by mentioning how he only really has a farce of independence as the guild he works for requires a fixed number of panels and boards for a fixed price, and how the guild isn’t paying for any of his safety measures. Carlea brings up how her workplace is so disassociated from the final product and how she feels like a cog in an automatable machine. Aelia and Nosric will mirror this notion.

The third table consists of the service workers and the textile domuz. I think that Mena is ther because she’s trying to make friends. Maybe they met with them through other workers in the service industry, such as someone who works in tailoring linking them up. The conversation starts with the service workers greeting Mena mentioning how they got connected. The servicers ask Mena what brought her down to Detkain. She says it was to work with the textiles present here, as she is interested in the region’s textile culture. Soren will bring up how it must be nice making clothing that people appreciate. She says how people appreciate her gardening work, but always give their thanks to the mayor who hires her to do it all. Althe will double this saying that he feels underappreciated for the work that goes into meals. Soren will double down saying that it feels like they are invisible. Althe will passionately exclaim his dislike to the wealth in the economy. Mena then brings up the republic and how underappreciation must be a symptom of the hereditary power that controls the empire. Soren will be curious on how the republic works, and Mena will respond. She defends the notion that under a capitalism, they would be free to start their own gardening company and receive full appreciation for their work. Mena will say to Althe that the real issue is the power distribution between the hereditary systems and the markets, and how freeing the markets would take power out of the hands of the rich.

Either each of these conversations happen at unique times so that the audience can hear them in their entirety, or they all happen at the same time

## Script

Scene 1 opens with the scenery described above. All characters are present apart from Mena who will enter the scene and start her conversation with the service workers after Aelia, Nosric, and Carlea’s conversation. All groups participate in light chatter before and after their important dialogue.

NOSRIC: (to Aelia) That theatre you’re renovating looks great, Aelia. Must be nice working on that fancy project.

AELIA: Fancy? It’s a nightmare. The scaffold’s a mess, and they don’t give us anything to protect ourselves. The gaps between the scaffold and the surfaces we work on are a metre wide when the scaffold’s steady, but it sways like we’re in a storm.

CARLEA: I’m one of the lucky ones, the worst that happens at the printer’s is getting today’s newspaper stamped on your flattened hand!

NOSRIC: I’m quite lucky, my workspace is in my house, I get to buy glasses to protect my eyes from wood shards and gloves to make sure I don’t accidentally saw my hand off.

AELIA: I would make a much better job of the scaffolding there. The contractors they get to do it only make sure its enough for us to work. If I were in charge, I’d be giving everyone harnesses and helmets.

NOSRIC: Independence is a farce, the guild demands quotas for how many planks I have to make, and they don’t pay for my safety. All that comes out of my own pocket.

CARLEA: I don’t know how much my work is worth. Its like im in a different country to the folks supplying me ink, paper, and letters. One things for sure though, I’m making my boss fortune. Have you seen the price of the paper lately?

(before the previous conversation ends, Mena walks up to the table Soren and Althe are sitting on, in time to say her line as soon as Carlea stops talking.)

MENA: Hi!

SOREN & ALTHE: Hello!

SOREN: You must be Mena, Erthil’s supplier?

MENA: yes, that’s me. Just a humble tailor.

SOREN: You’re a Domuz right? What brings you to Detkain?

MENA: Well, Lale Darie’s lovely and all, but I wanted to work with all the materials here. Ralean is a hub of culture - you’ve got all the best shipping routes from Anula and Cronar, and you border the wastes.

ALTHE: But in Lale Darie, don’t you have access to the colonies’ materials?

MENA: Yeah, we do, but you start to get used to them. To me, they’ve all lost their charm.

SOREN: Must be great having a hand in making clothing that people genuinely appreciate. People enjoy the plants on the street and the garden here, but they always thank the mayor, who just hires me to do it all!

ALTHE: Tell me about it! You think the mayor cares who cooks his food? I’m not paid for the work or the skills it takes to make dishes this good.

SOREN: Yeah, it feels like we’re invisible.

ALTHE: Its like people only care about those with money because they think they get things done, when all they do is get us to do things for them.

MENA: You know, in Lale Darie, we actually appreciate our gardeners. Those in charge are smart enough to give credit where its due.

SOREN: How does it work in Lale Darie?

MENA: Well, we don’t have an emperor. Power isn’t passed down through generations but elected by those with a direct stake in the economy. There’s none of this fixed pricing nonsense. I’d get to choose how much money my textiles should make.

ALTHE: But wouldn’t that just be shifting the power from one wealthy person to the next?

MENA: No, you’d actually have the chance to achieve something! In Lale Darie, no-one’s locked in to their roles. (addressing Soren) you’d have the opportunity to start your own gardening company (addressing Althe) and freeing the markets would take the power out of the hands of the emperor and his people.

(conversations continue in the background, now Meichi and Hemery speak)

MEICHI: So how is your smithy doing?

CELYB: it’s not great. I had to stay in work over Edindina and Edinbethna to keep up with the Empire’s quota. It’s a real shame, I was going to take my son to Calaerula’s beach but instead I spent the day with Hemery.

MEICHI: At least you don’t work for a guild and you’re actually paid individually for your tools. I have quotas on all my pottery.

CELYB: It’s not any better. The empire sets how much I can sell my tools for, and they clearly favour the merchants who I’m pretty sure have the empire on their payroll. I don’t even get to choose what price I get my materials for, the Empire’s markets have decreed that I only use the local supplier, when I know I can get a better quality elsewhere.

MEICHI: At least you can get creative. I have to make the same pots every day and I try to add what flair I can, but the guild’s regulations are so strict. I can’t even make things out-of-hours because I can’t afford my own pottery things.

(Conversation returns to normal chatter for a moment before everyone at the tables say ‘I hate the empire’ in a jumble. The tavern sits still for a moment before the scene ends)

# Scene 2: introduction of the rulers.

This scene exists to introduce the ruling classes. In it, the rulers will discuss amongst them issues that the emperor is facing. They will talk about the current situation in Rejedmana, an attempted assassination of the emperor in Rejedmana. They will move on to talking about a recent piece of popular media from a popular writer that talks about themes of republicanism, and talk about how a lot of the mayors, merchants, heads-of-guilds, and even some appointed politicians and bureaucrats have come out with openly republican ideals. During this conversation, there will be a subtext of each person trying to figure out if the other has republican ideas, but nothing will be said. There might be a joke made about how they’re too far north to be disloyal, and maybe one character who knows an awful lot about republicanism. In the middle of the scene, Althe will walk in and out overhearing the leaders talk about the current situation in Rejedmana.

The scene consists of a single lavish table with wine glasses full of presumably wine. Seira, Nilsyt, Liora, and Calis sit around the table. The two guardspeople, Elara and Cin, stand nearby but far from the table, guarding the entrance to the room. Althe will be present in the scene later to deliver a food.

The scene starts with Seira bringing up the emperor’s recent visit to Rejedmana. They will describe the visit, during which the emperor’s life was made an attempt on. Nilsyt will mention how Rejedmana’s dangerous like that, and that the emperor never should have gone there. Liora will ask why this is the case, where Nilsyt will explain Rejedmana, and in fact Lauvednolandia’s history of dislike towards authority, where he will paint it in a negative light because he believes that authority is a crucial element of society and required to keep law and order. Seira will then address Calis, thanking her for coming and asking again what her role is. She will say that she is the leader of a merchants guild in a nearby town and the head of a university there. Seira will then ask Calis what she thinks about this situation. Calis will mention how she thinks ralean is going to change, for better or for worse.

The mayor will move on to reports that Rejedmana’s recently been subject to a number of riots against authority. At this point, Althe will enter the edge of the scene carrying the food. He will overhear the conversations about Rejedmana’s disregard to authority and pause to listen before bringing the food in. The mayor will continue on about how many of the villages and towns surrounding Rejedmana and in Lauvednolandia have stopped reporting to the emperor and kicked their guards out. Liora will say how she heard that not all of the guards were kicked out and that some were able to stay, and how the guard’s equipment was taken by the regular townsfolk. Nilsyt will express his destain for a mob rule, saying that nothing ever gets done and how its doomed for failure. Seira will mention that this type of behaviour can only mean Rejedmana wants to do the same. Calis will mention that the people there clearly want change. After this, Althe will fully enter the scene and make his presence known. The mayor will express his appreciation for the delivery of food and move on with the conversation. Althe will hang in the scene for a while listening to the start of the next topic before moving on.

The mayor will start talking about republicanism by mentioning the existence of the popular book discussing it. Calis will mention how she’d heard about that book. Nilsyt will immediately respond with slight aggression, stating that the book isn’t worth considering, mentioning how the empire’s done well so far and has managed to expand its territory from the small city of Illandus to the multi-regional bastion it is today. Seira will mention how regardless, its gotten popular with the workers, and how many of the merchants, politicians, and other mayors have explicitly mentioned the rhetoric. Liora will then start to discuss a few points about republicanism, starting to talk about how it advocates for an elected leadership that promotes meritocratic government. She will quickly become silent. Calis will bring up how republicanism is a clear threat to the authority of the empire.

## Script

(Scene 2 opens with Seira, Nilsyt, Liora, and Calis sitting around a lavish table. If it can be done, the room should be decorated to look like a wealthy place. The two guardspeople, Elara and Cin, stand guard at the entrance. Althe is offstage and will make an appearance halfway through the scene to deliver some food.)

SEIRA: Have you hear about the incident during the Emperor’s recent visit to Rejedmana? He was kept waiting at the gates for thirty minutes before gaining entry, and there was hardly a crowd gathered to see him. As he left the meeting with Rejedmana’s mayor, an audacious peasant attempted to assassinate him! Fortunately, the guards intervened in time.

NILSYT: (slightly angry) Rejedmana’s just the town I’d expect that to happen in. Its dangerous, full of radicals! Not the type of place for our esteemed emperor to visit.

LIORA: Why, what’s wrong with Rejedmana?

NILSYT: It’s not just Rejedmana; Lauvednolandia’s as a whole has discontent with the Reimig’s rule. They believe that the dynasty betrayed them by relinquishing Lauvednolandia. They’re wrong; the Empire’s authority is the linchpin of stability. Look at Llofilot! It lost its entire social fabric when it strayed from the Empire’s path of law and order.

SEIRA: What’s your perspective on this matter, Calis. I’m grateful for your presence, but your role slips my mind.

CALIS: Me? I’m a merchant from Detlenth with the privilege of owning the local university.

SEIRA: Your insights are invaluable then. What are your thoughts on this situation?

(Althe enters the scene’s edge carrying food. He hears the following line, then pauses and waits quietly, overhearing the conversation)

CALIS: Ralean sits on the precipice of change, straddling the better and the worse.

SEIRA: Indeed, Rejedmana’s seen a series of riots against the guard and the garrison. One could argue that they have shown a complete disregard for authority. Many surrounding villages have severed ties with the emperor, even expelling their own guards and garrisons.

LIORA: I heard from my colleagues that some of those guard chose to remain in the town, even providing access to the armoury for the townsfolk

NILSYT: Unthinkable. What do they hope to achieve? Some form of mob rule’? History has shown that these endeavours rarely succeed. Nothing ever gets done and they always fail in the end.

SEIRA: Such actions in the towns can only indicate that Rejedmana is about to follow suit.

CALIS: The people there clearly yearn for change.

(Althe fully enters the scene, delivering the food)

SEIRA: Finally, the food!

(They all begin to eat. Althe walks away to the edge of the scene, but doesn’t fully leave, continuing to listen in on the conversation for a while.)

SEIRA: Have any of you come across that republican literature that’s been circulating. It was authored by Cana Gader, known for their involvement in the Terlayan Economic Independence movement.

CALIS: I believe I’ve heard of it. was it titled similar to ‘Ralean reimagined’ or ‘The Reimig Challenge’

SEIRA: precisely the thing! It goes by the title ‘The Reimig Challenge: A Call for a Republic of Ralean’

NILSYT: such ideas are hardly worth considering. They are championed by of self-interested, disloyal individuals. The Empire has prospered even in recent years. The Reimigs were the only ones who grew a multi-regional empire from beginning with only the city of Ilandus

SEIRA: Regardless of the flaws in these ideas, they’ve gained traction among the working classes. Even some merchants, politicians, and mayors in the south, such as the mayors of Milad and Naserich have openly embraced these explicitly republican ideals

LIORA: The concepts are not entirely foreign either. The book advocates for elected leadership and a government based on merit rather than inheritance.

(Liora considers the mood in the room and decides to remain silent)

CALIS: Republicanism undoubtedly poses a significant challenge to Emperor Ruion’s authority, no matter how far north he is.

(Silence for a moment as the characters finish their food)

SEIRA: That was a wonderful meal. Thank you, captains, for joining me, and thank you, Calis from Detlenth.

Calis: Thank you for your hospitality, mayor Seira.
