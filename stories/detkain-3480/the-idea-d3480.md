# Background
The year is 3484, and a revolution across Southern Ralean is brewing. I need to focus on the concepts of the story itself without worrying too much about how well it fits in with the rest of the narrative. If I needed to, I could always make vague references to pre-established events such as Rejedmana being the seat of the Union, and the Union actually existing.

This play will explore the themes present during the revolution in Detkain, which sits as the capital city of modern-day Houvadia. Throughout the course of the play, there will be two main sets of characters: the people and the rulers.

The people live a hard life under the empire and want change. They all prepare for the revolution, causing conflict and changes in ideology as everyone’s differing opinions clash. Then, when the revolution breaks out, these conflicts ignite tenfold and produce an interesting situation where the people are at odds with the system and each other. They will need to stay united against the Empire to achieve any of their goals.

The rulers control Detkain. These are the tradesmen that pay the workers, the captains that control the guard, the rich men who seek to co-opt the revolution in their favour. They are far from united, as each one of them struggles between their loyalty to the empire, and their loyalty to the people they love. The story will ultimately describe how the revolution became Houvadia, a questionable system with strange ideas for meritocracy.

# Character Discussion
I think I first need to discuss the characters, and what jobs they have. Here are the jobs common amongst the working classes.

The labourer. These are the people responsible for construction work, maintenance, public works and sanitation, quarrying, irrigation, and workshopping which includes blacksmithing, carpentry, textiles, pottery, clockmaking, print shops, silver and gold smiths, cooperages, Luthiers, bookbinders, leatherworking, glassblowing,  etc. These people would often do the simpler and easier tasks in these professions, leaving more skilled labour to the artisans.

The artisan. These were the heads of many of the workplaces being the skilled labourers with the knowledge and ability to make things in smiths, with expert carpentry skills, so on. They might work in workshops with their own sets of labourers, or be part of a larger guild that manages their work.

Peasants. These guys are a bit of a special case as they didn’t really work in the cities but provided for them by working outside on farms. They won’t be a very big part of the on-screen play as that is all set in the city, but they will be crucial for whoever wants to gain power in the revolution as they basically control the food. In the Empire’s system, they serve the empire under a serf-like system, so they won’t be hard to sway away from that.

Service workers. Wealthy households would have a plethora of employees managing their cooking, housekeeping, and caring roles. Service workers also do a lot of the work required for public upkeep, like tending to public gardens, cleaning public buildings, news distribution, and rubbish collectors.

Soldiers. Despite helping the rulers control the common rabble, the soldiers are very much workers themselves. Detkain, like any city, will have a small army garrisoned there on command of the Emperor. Soldiers also includes the local police and guards, who also double as sort of service workers keeping track of who and what comes in and out of the city.

Guildspeople. These are the leaders and members of the local guilds, which often in Shildo are common people with other jobs they do propping up the guilds as a part-time thing. Full-time members might include those who manage the guild, as well as crucial members for some of the guilds such as the doctor for a medicine guild, gardeners for a botanical or mage guild, and full-time adventurers of an adventuring guild or a combat guild. I need to keep a good track of how my magic will change the way this story works.

The rulers are obviously a smaller bunch. Still, their roles need to be described. This time its quite dependant on how the Emperor rules the Empire, so I won’t be able to do much useful research into that.

The Provincial manager (soris) for Detkaindinum will be stationed in Detkain. They will be one of the big cheeses, remaining loyal to the Emperor until the bitter end. They might not even be a significant character in this story, only being mentioned. This could even highlight the disconnect between the management and the people, since the story follows the people.

The mayor. They will struggle with their own loyalties, but I think they will eventually cave into the pressures of the revolution, perhaps due to conflicts in their own family. They will have an important running dialogue with another one of the rulers, coming up.

The Leader. This will be the character that ultimately changes the fate of Houvadia from whatever it wills to be into what he wills it to be. I’ve written that he’s an economics professor at a university in an unmarked town called Detfygh Samon, but this detail is less important. What he will be is a wealthy businessman who is hindered by the Emperor’s policies and control and wants him desperately gone. His desire is economic freedom, the access to new markets, reducing taxation and bureaucracy, smashing the Empire’s system to unlock a new era of economic mobility, access new resources, and progress society from the Empire’s stagnant economy.

The Captains might be a set of two captains, perhaps one of the guard and one of the garrison. The captain of the guard will work closely with the mayor and the leader to bring forth this revolution, but the captain of the garrison might not sway until after the revolution has started and his soldiers begin to disobey orders as they are ordered to fight their friends and family.
