This text is an old draft of [The Black Hearts](/wastelife/stories/black-hearts/the-black-hearts). I am working on a new draft.

By Iana Easa – a Domuz mage

# **_1._**  **_Across the Bay_**
I was wandering around the city market squalor when I overheard a band of four or five people talk about an expedition into the Vugaix. I lingered in the market for a while hearing what I could of their conversation among the voices. The rabble of the market made it difficult to hear, but from what I understood they were planning to acquire a rare alchemical ingredient called a ‘black heart’. I was getting bored of using the same combination of ingredients during my band’s travels, so I approached this group and asked a bit about their journey.

“I believe I overheard you were going on an expedition to find an ingredient for claps?” I walked into the group.

“Then you heard right. What’s your interest?” One of them explained, she was holding a notepad.

“I’m tired of using pearls and grass. I think I know what a black heart is?”

“They have strong fire and rage properties, as well as some medium to strong others. They’re mushrooms.”

“Okay, I’ll come. What are the costs?”

“The only costs are the boat ride down to the mouth of the Heabe’s river, and pay for our travel guide, a Colsens called Gareey.”

“Have I heard that name before?”

“He’s quite famous for these trips” She explained.

I rushed excitedly to the tavern where my group had been resting and told them of my pending journey. I asked for the money to do so, and the person in charge of our finances was kind enough to honour my request. I arranged with them that we would meet here in a month’s time. I couldn’t be longer than that.

I went back to the expeditioners, and we walked towards the outskirts of the city, nearer the wall. Now we were out of the rabble, I could properly make out their faces. There were five people; all of them were Domuz like me except one, who looked to be a Lemuz. The girl approached a building and knocked on the round door. The door opened at a worm’s pace, and through the crack we saw the head of a Ens poke out. When the Ens caught a glimpse of us, he quickly shut the door. He said, behind the door, that we needed to give him a few minutes to prepare.

The door swung open to reveal an exited Colsens. His face was happy and his furry arms and stubby hands were grasping backpack straps eagerly. He invited us in for some preparation talk. He asked us to kneel on cushions he’d laid out in a circle, then he asked us to introduce ourselves.

“I’ll start. I’m Gareey, I was born in this city and I spend my earlier days wandering the Wastes.”

“I’m Hodeso, I was born in Tenia. I’m in this so that I can stock mushrooms for a store back home.” Said the mage to my left.

“I’m Iana, I was born in the city All and I’m an adventurer. I want to use the mushrooms to fight the opponents we meet.” Then the Domuz to my right spoke.

“I am Siai, I was born in Easea and I’m collecting these for my master.” Then, the Lemuz spoke.

“I’m Corvan, I’m not a mage, I’m collecting these mushrooms to sell them in Anula.”

“I’m Ria, from Easea as well, and I’m in the same situation as Iana.”

“I’m Eariph, a student from the Odea Yemon. I’m researching into different claps underwater, and need a few for my research.”

I found it interesting to see someone else in my situation, I’m sure they’ll have plenty of stories to tell on our journey. After our introductions, Gareey told us of the route we would be taking. We would take a boat to the town at the mouth of the river that flows through Ens land, then we’d take a path south-east from there until we reach the region where the Black Hearts of the Vugaix grow. We left Gareey’s house, and he led us down to the city’s docks. Corvan began talking, as if he’d known us for years.

“It’s been a goldfish’s lifetime since I’ve been on a long boat ride” He said, stretching his arms.

“How so?” I asked.

“Last journey I went on I was gathering colourful sand from Cronar to sell here in Lale Darie, but I couldn’t afford such a long boat ride so I had to tag along with caravans. I barely got by, and I wouldn’t have if the caravan hadn’t gotten attacked.”

“The roads are dangerous; you need to watch yourself else you might get knocked.” Ria said.

“Even though they were armed, those bandits did not know what they were doing. They were to attack such a well-armed caravan. I helped in the fight, and the caravan gave me bread in good will. They stopped before reaching the end of Cronar, so I had to cross the desert alone.”

“So you have experience of the wastes? If you can survive southern Kyenel alone, then the journey shouldn’t be difficult for you.” Gareey said.

“Did you meet any Phax?” I asked.

“No, I was days away from Kusao. I did get a glimpse of one while passing through a town in Raelan, but I didn’t see them well enough to write about it…” Corvan responded.

I could see the docks in the distance; we were outside of the city. I see them all the time, but I still get bewildered by Lale Darie’s fantastic docks and their wonderful ships. Even from afar, I could make out their beautiful fish-fin sails and their pearly blue paints. The dock was gigantic, the beach was invisible under the wooden excellence of the piers. Corvan asked me why we need ships when we’re so adapted for the sea. I asked him why he needed horses when he had feet.

“So you’re a student of the Odea Yemon. I’m honoured to be in your presence.” Ria said, bowing slightly, and Eariph blushed.

“No please, I’m not that smart.”

“Not that smart?” I asked.

“Well, I don’t write enough academic papers. I often miss good lectures and have to use my friend’s notes.”

“Why would they keep teaching you?” Ria asked, rudely.

“Well, that’s an oyster I can’t open” she replied, looking at the floor.

“There’s a few lectures that I would be attending over the next few days.” She spoke.

“What do you do in the Underwater University?” Corvan asked, oblivious to the fact that we have quite a few universities underwater.

“Well, it’s like a hub of sharing knowledge. We have large rooms with rows of seats that all converge on one spot where a lecturer stands and talks about something. I’m meant to be publishing academic papers, because I’m young.”

The boat ride would take 2 days, we would stop for night at Salea, a Domuz port town roughly halfway between the city we started at and the destination. We had stones to throw on the boat ride, so we used this for telling stories of us and our travels. I knew I had a lot to say, and I was confident that Ria would too.

We talked for a while about the Phax. Ria said she met one, but it was on the other end of her sword. She said it was in Ralean, so she clearly gets around more than my group. Eariph mentioned how she’d love to meet a Phax, and could easily spend an evening asking questions about their society, and inspecting their body. Corvan then told that he was interested in our societies, and how Eariph dealt with living underwater.

“We don’t actually live in the water. We can’t breathe water forever, so we make pockets of air for our buildings.” Eariph explained.

“Oh, I see.” Corvan said, disappointed.

“But, whenever we’re not in a building we do swim through the water to get to places.” She amended.

We eventually tired ourselves out in conversation, although Corvan and Hodeso carried on speaking for a while. Most of us went to write our journals, perhaps making notes. Siai spent the time staring across into the horizon. The sun was getting low, the sailors said that Salea was no more than half an hour away. Soon enough, we would be able to see the town on the horizon. The land was very flat, which made sense because we were getting closer to the Heabe. The port town was fairly large, it had many large mansion-like buildings and a keep at the back. It sprawled off in a triangular fashion from the keep’s gate to the ports.

Once we docked, Gareey lead us to a lodging he knew was available, and we each found a tidy pile of leather-coated straw. I found it difficult to drift off. My head was occupied by thoughts and speculations on the journey ahead. I had never even imagined the Vugaix before, and I was about to enter it. I saw the forest, the towering white pipes leading several meters above my head. I saw the Mad and their cute communities in the mushrooms.

Gareey woke me up in the morning. I’m pretty sure he shook all our dreams. It was earlier than morning; we couldn’t have got more than three hours of sleep. He forced us out, and then we made our way to a trader who was kind enough to open earlier for us. I had brought enough food for the entire boat journey and a bit longer, but I saw Hodeso and Corvan get some fish.

Gareey kept looking at the sun, and rushing us along. He said that if we needed more sleep, we could get it on the boat. He almost pushed us onto the boat. The sailors were different. We were silent on the waves mostly because we were tired. Half of us were already asleep. I could see Siai enchanting a phial of liquid, so I went and sat next to her.

“Can I dig your rays?” I asked.

“I’m working on my enchantment skills, Master told me to practice on this liquid, so I am.” She replied.

“What enchantment?”

“I am trying to make an awareness potion. I can’t sleep unless I’m in a bed. Master also keeps needing to buy these, and he’d rather I could just make them.”

“And how about that base liquid, what’s it made of?”

“Oh, it’s just salt water with a bit of garlic juice. It’s quite a common starter, Master said.”

Mentioning ‘Master’ every reply isn’t normal. I’ve met maids of the rich before, and they’re often fun and entertaining folk outside of their occupation. Calling their employers ‘Master’ is a bit of an oddity, usually they refer to ‘My boss’ or ‘The big man’. This girl seems assimilated. I asked if I could just watch for a while. Ria and the others soon rose from their hard slumber, and Gareey suggested we share some of our past experiences.

“My group and I entered this really dark cave.” I began. Heads turned to face me; I hope they were interested.

“We lit a torch and walked far into it, then we came to a dead end where the only option was to go underwater. We believed there was a way through there.” I continued.

“We were fumbling underwater trying to find our way in the dark. Soon, one of my group said he had found the exit, but it was just where we had started. Determined, we continued to look. Soon, I felt cold stone stairs with my hands, and I followed them up and out of the water. I shouted, saying that I had found the way out.”

“You could have continued without them, kept the treasure for yourself!” Ria joked.

“We lit a torch, and saw ourselves at a complex stone door in the cave. The area was a bit cramped, as the only way out was back through the water. The door looked fantastic. It was covered top-to-bottom in interesting runes. We saw it as a puzzle to break.”

“Did you try opening it? It could have been unlocked.” Corvan said.

“Soon, we noticed a misplaced rock in the wall, and surely enough pressing on it opened the door into a giant open room. There were coffins lining the walls, as well as a fancy table sitting on the other side of the room. We cautiously entered the dim room, watching our shoulders. There was something eerie about this place.”

The others seemed engaged, so I went on.

“This chamber seemed to be the end, as there were no doors out. We noticed a small chest in the room, so one of my crew went to open it.”

“Then, the roof collapsed!” Ria joked, again.

“Inside was an old pendant, with a strange symbol none of us recognised. But, while our backs were turned as we focused on the jewellery, a hooded man crept up on us and almost knocked one of my friends!”

“It’s at that point I’d bail and take the amulet for myself!” Ria told us.

“This person was highly skilled; with just a dagger he managed to parry many of my crew’s sword swipes. We did cut him, but he cut one of us, and deeper. I realised I needed to intervene; I hadn’t been acting because I didn’t want to use ingredients. I got an ingredient for touch water, and clapped it bare-handed. It did hurt, but it clearly hurt him more as the water pushed him against the wall and even pierced his skin.”

“I didn’t think water claps could get so strong!” Eariph said.

“after the attacker was downed, we carried him out of the cave and to the nearby town. We didn’t want to leave him for dead.”

“I would’ve” Ria said, seriously.

Had Ria been there, things would have gone very differently. They might not have gone better for the group though. I wondered if that’s how she acted with her group, scandalous and abandoning. I actually questioned her about this, and she said that they would do the same to her without a second thought, and if they all did it, they would be abandoning together.

“I have a similar story, actually.” Ria began.

“Me and my group were wandering the mainland ‘Lale when we spotted a wrecked caravan. It wasn’t an accident; all the goods were gone, and there were no bodies. We made it our adventure to seek out those responsible for this innocent caravan’s falling.

“One of my group spotted a set of tracks exiting the scene, and they were identifiably the footprints of goblins, with their four-toed feet. We followed the prints, and soon enough we would follow the footprints into a cave.

“We chose to avenge the poor caravaners and charged into the cave, to rid it of the filth. Our party enchanter enchanted our weapons, and then we were ready. We dashed inwards to the cave, slicing up goblins as they fumbled to arm themselves with the swords they stole off the caravaners, but it was too late for them. I managed to get at least 2 of them!

“And with that victory and the avenging of those poor caravaners, we left, and continued on the road until we reached the town. We took the gear the goblins stole, and decided we’d give it to the merchants in the town free-of-charge.”

“Goblins don’t actually eat people.” I said.

Ria was implying that there were no bodies because the goblins ate them, but she never mentioned any blood. Not only that, but Goblins don’t actually eat people. It’s a bad stereotype for them, and they are often seen as evil. They aren’t, they don’t attack caravaners. I had a lovely run-in with a tribe of goblins. I once passed out in the absence of my group, and awoke in a goblin cave, with a full flask of water and even a few berries that are poisonous to me, but perfectly edible by the grey-skinned goblins.

Point being, I think in Ria’s story the cart of the caravan must have got stuck or otherwise couldn’t continue, so the caravaners and their guard decided to carry the goods to the town instead of fixing the cart, which would require a trip to the town either way. The goblins could have taken a few goods, but they are known to scavenge for human equipment after battles so them having swords for people doesn’t mean they knocked anyone. All tribes are different; some can be dangerous, but these few ruin life for the many.

Ria’s story produced silence in a few of us, but soon Corvan spoke to tell of the time when goblins appeared in his story. It turns out ‘goblins’ in Kyeron is a much wider term, and refers to a larger set of species than it does in Lale Darie. His story was about goblins that cling to trees. In his tale, they threw rocks at him from high up in the trees, a primitive but fatal weapon. He said that this is an understandable behaviour, because he had trespassed on their land. He said he was there to collect ingredients, to sell of course.

The day was trailing to its end, and the next port was not far. Gareey gave us a small speech, in preparation of what to come. He told us that the Wastes were far from safe, and even the unassuming steppe of the Heabe house some of the most dangerous creatures on Barogate. He told us that the Vugaix is not a passive place, and there are monsters and even plants that might attack the passing adventurer. He said to be ready, with claps and swords, for anything.

As the sun set, we entered the port town located near the mouth of the large river that flows through the Heabe. It was a very big town, and as we wandered through the streets, we felt alienated. The town was populated with lots of Ens, and not only Colsens but the other types too. If our shiny scales weren’t enough to distinguish us from the fur of an Ens, then the peachy skin of Corvan was. Gareey struggled to find a lodging for us. He soon found one at the back of the town, far from the port. Gareey told us to have a good sleep, as the journey will take three days there and three days back, with neither journey being safer. We slept soundly that night, with no tossing, no wondering what the Vugaix was like. I was in the moment; there and then.

# **_2._**  **_Through the Heabe_**
I woke up naturally, with the sun gleaming on my face. I got up and out of my sleeping bag, and went down the stairs. Eariph, Corvan, Siai and Gareey were sitting around a table, eating some food, and chatting quietly. I joined them, and the subject of conversation was the Odea Yemon, and Eariph’s living. Corvan was really interested in life underwater. As I came down the stairs, I heard him ask how they fought underwater.

“It rarely happens, but it’s almost the same as land fighting. I’ve never seen a battle myself, but I’ve heard of how it works. We use spears, just like on land, but we don’t have the strength to carry shields. It’s much more chaotic, given how we can move up and down. Casualties are massive, so it doesn’t happen often.”

“I got into a fight underwater, I’ll tell you about my first-hand experience.” I intervened.

“We got tasked to hunt a poacher by the local council who had been taking out some rare octopi and selling them for their premium price. Our leader decided it would be better if we searched in groups of two, and he paired me with a spearman. We did eventually find him, and instead of coming in quietly he decided it was a smart decision to fight us.”

“The most important thing about claps underwater is that fire and air claps turn into less effective water claps, which are already very ineffective underwater. In other words I wasn’t really of much use, but I could prepare something if the melee went bad for my teammate.”

“He had to get his spear out fast, given how we were not expecting the poacher to attack us. He could have got it out faster if it were above ground, but the water slows down wide movements like that a lot. The fighting was terrible to watch, they were trying to bluff each other into a bad attack. Eventually, the poacher lunged forward and my teammate was able to dodge, and that’s when he managed to plunge the spear into the poacher’s stomach. The worst part was the blood, which looked horrible under the water. I quickly went in to seal his wounds. He was screaming in pain.”

“So it’s not pleasant.” Said Corvan.

          In their own time, Ria and Hodeso soon came down the stairs. I knew they had been awake for a while; the floorboards weren’t good at blocking sounds of chatting. We packed our things, left the lodging, and made one last stop at the local ingredient store. I figured I might as well stock up a bit before the journey. Gareey told us that the venture through the Vugaix won’t be empty, and there are plenty of magical mushrooms and even edible ones along the way. We then left the town and wandered on a dirt road into the grassland.

“Hey, Hodeso, you haven’t talked much.” Corvan mentioned.

“No, I guess I haven’t” He responded.

“What did you say you were getting the mushrooms for?”

“The shop in my village needs them restocked.”

“Restocked? You mean Black Hearts are regularly available there? You have to tell me where it is.” I intervened.

“No, not often, but the shop keep wanted them. A powerful mage sometimes shops there and he wanted to surprise him.”

“Where was the shop?” I followed up.

“The northern islands” he replied.

“What town?” I continued.

“… Tenia.”

          Gareey intervened in the conversation, stating that there was a Nadens village just ahead, and that we would be going off-road so we didn’t upset the locals. To my knowledge, he didn’t only say this because we’re Muz and the Ens don’t like Muz, but because he’s a Colsens, the outsider of the Ens species. Before we reached the part where we would have gone off-road, we met a caravan of Nadens making their way across the road. They looked at us and didn’t say a word. Gareey seemed a bit embarrassed.

“So who is this powerful mage, Hodeso?” I asked.

“I don’t know, I’ve never seen him.”

“You mean to say you’ve had a powerful mage be in town and didn’t rush to go and talk to him? You must be mad.”

“He’s not overwhelmingly powerful, he just experiments with powerful ingredients.”

“You must have quite the job, darting across Barogate to get all the powerful ingredients for your shop in Tenia. What things has he bought previously? I’d like to know what type of things I can get at this store. I’ll probably stop by at some point.”

“I don’t know what they have in stock most of the time.”

“So you’re only collecting ingredients for them this one time? Why?”

“I’m probably the powerful mage he’s on about!” Ria intervened, “I’ve been there a few times. Got my hands on some White Mountain Root.”

I got a bit interrogatory with Hodeso then. I began seeing a few things in his story I thought were a little off, like a small-town shop needing ingredients as powerful and dangerous to acquire as the black hearts? Then he said they regularly stock ingredients this powerful for a powerful mage that lives nearby? It seemed falser as I looked into it, began questioning his reasons. Then Ria intervened backing up Hodeso’s story that I’m still not sure is true.

          We soon reached the point where we would turn off-road. The ground wasn’t much different to the road, but it was firmer, and there were more tufts of grass in the way. I think we made a massive detour to avoid the village because we never actually saw it. As we were treading across the grass, Corvan began to talk;

“These heaps of grass are reminding me of my trek through Ralean’s fields.” He began, a welcome break in the silence that otherwise filled the air.

“That was actually where I first ran into a Domuz, marches away down in Ralean, marches away from the coastline. I was sitting by a lake, when all of sudden I saw a face in the water that wasn’t my own. I was shocked out of my boots, and I passed out.”

“Passed out at the marvel of our race!” Ria said.

“Sort of. I woke up along the river, with a pretty Domuz girl sitting by my side. It was a bit creepy, come to think of it. She was wearing the same type of tight leather you’re wearing now, Eariph.”

“I should hope so.” Eariph said.

“We chatted for a while then, I had to explain that I had never seen a Domuz before. When I said I was headed to Lale Darie, she had no interest in coming all the way. She did accompany me to Nater’s End though.”

“Maybe she was an outlaw?” I spoke.

I’ve never left Lale Darie before, so I don’t know how likely you are to meet Domuz in other parts of Barogate. I have seen my fair share of Muz races, so I can only assume it’s the same outside in other areas. One thing I do know is special about Lale Darie is that there’s a lot of Colsens, and Ens are generally considered to be a creature of the Wastes. I’m sure a waste creature inhabiting a land of the Muz never happens outside of Lale Darie.

“We got attacked on the road, halfway through on our way to Nater’s End in Terlaye” Corvan continued.

“An expedition of goblins, and not the nice ones you talk about here, the awful ones with saliva dripping from their mouths, large and round stomachs with twig-like limbs and mouths too large for their bodies.”

“We call those oflings, not goblins.” Said Ria.

“They attacked us with their swords made of teeth and, naturally, we were scared. I had a sword, but the Domuz was defenceless as far as I knew. I was fighting the 3 goblins, oflings, alone. I had to do big and scary swings to keep them back, all the while watching my shoulder making sure that there were no more.”

“You didn’t have to; the girl should have learnt how to protect herself.” Ria said.

“I was confident, beating 3 would be unlikely but I felt as if having something to protect made me stronger. One of the gob… oflings stepped forwards in a clumsy manner in a way that made it easy for me to take advantage. I swung my sword against his arm and cut him severely. I might have broken a bone, the poor thing screamed with his arm bent in an unnatural way. His ofling friends then carried him away, keeping defensive. I let them leave, and hoped he was alright.”

“I don’t!” Ria shouted, and laughed awkwardly. Hodeso smiling and almost laughed too.

The tufts of grass were casting no shadows, not because they were short, we’d often kick them accidentally. Off in the distance, there were a few wild horses eating the bushes. Often in the distance we could make out a herd of animals, mostly Ox herds. This one time, we saw a herd of Ox running from what looked like a pack of wolves. Gareey did not lead us in a straight line, he often began walking in seemingly random directions all the time his horizontal eyes focussed.

          Another feature of the Heabe seemed to be that we could see for marches. To our right you could see the very tops of the enormous mountains of Barogate, towering in all their glory, the largest mountains to exist. To our right, the land subtly drops down into the far horizon. The grass was tall enough to hide our knees. Gareey froze ahead of us, his eyes darted around, scanning the surroundings. This put us all on edge, Corvan grabbed his sword and the rest of us subtly opened our satchels.

“Wolves. They’ll expect us to run, so stand your ground no matter what.” Gareey whispered. “Prepare your most frightening claps and do them on my mark.”

All of us fumbled in our satchels except Corvan and Siai. I prepared a wide but weak fire clap, and soon we had ingredients in our hands, ready to clap. I saw a wolf hiding in the grass, his eyes were yellow and staring at me with harsh, round pupils. his head was hugging the ground.

          Without Gareey sounding the mark, Ria fired an ice clap into the grass, but it must have missed the wolf she was aiming at given how in the air as high as our faces was six pouncing wolves. Corvan took a proper position, raising his sword high to use the wolf’s own weight against him.

Hodeso, Eariph and I used our claps, mine set off high into the sky, warming a room’s worth of air above us and lighting the ground like a pocket-sized sun. Eariph fired a sound clap, a sharp boom that pierced our ears and left them ringing. Hodeso’s clap was not large, instead he attacked one of the pouncing wolves with a thin spike attack, which pierced straight through its body, leaving two of them dead.

The wolf that pounced at me missed by the length of a finger; its claw left a thin mark on my arm. I could see that Gareey managed to punch two wolves attacking him back into the grass. Siai was protected, standing behind Gareey. Ria had a wolf on her, but managed to rip it off. She had visibly deep cuts on her arms.

Eariph was on the floor, with a wolf on top of her clawing viciously into her leg. I was the closest to her, and I couldn’t prepare a clap fast enough. In the heat of the moment, I swung my leg at the dog kicking it a few paces away from Eariph. The wolf then ran into the grass, retreated, 2 of its pack members laying on the floor deceased.

          Eariph’s legs were bleeding heavily. She screamed in pain. Gareey quickly knelt by her, setting his enormous backpack on the ground. Siai was enchanting a vial of liquid. I took a rag off my backpack, and used it to soak up the blood, pushing hard on Eariph’s legs. Gareey lifted a thick red substance from his bag, opened the top, and then asked me to remove the cloth for a bit. He poured it on Eariph’s legs, and many of the cuts sealed themselves.

          Eariph all the time was wailing, her back against the grass. I looked at Ria for a while, and saw Siai give her the liquid she was enchanting. Hodeso was holding tightly on her arms where the wolf had grabbed.

“Thank you, Siai.” Gareey said, because Ria wouldn’t.

“Wow, that really reminded me of that time in Terlaye when I got attacked by a bunch of crabs.” Corvan began…

“Sorry, what?” Said Eariph, under her heavy breaths.

“Maybe later, then.” He added.

Nobody spoke for a while. We managed to lay Eariph on her sleeping bag, where she lay for a while. Gareey poured a second liquid into her mouth, one with a pinker colour, he said it was to speed up the healing process. The corpses of the unlucky wolves were still nearby, their stench mustn’t have made it easy for Eariph. Gareey said to keep them there, as it would convey a message.

“So what were you going to say?” Eariph asked Corvan.

“Ages ago about the crabs in Terlaye? Well, they are pretty much as dangerous there as wolves are in other parts. When I left Nater’s End, after I farewelled the Domuz girl who had gotten onto a boat to Anula, I was wandering the roads hopefully leading to the Terlayan capital Llofiot when I came across a group of crabs crossing the road.” Corvan began.

“The thing about Terlayan crabs is that they are enormous, the size of a torso with pincers as thick as stomachs. I stood and watched them move, oblivious to any danger they presented. It was then when one wandered towards me. It was approaching rather carelessly, so I assumed it was passive and saw no reason to back away. After all, if a wolf did such they might be asking to be lead, or pet.”

“I don’t need to guess that this didn’t end well for you or the crab.” Ria said.

“Exactly. I squatted down to its level to greet it, but that’s when its claw snapped down on my forearm. It then walked away as if nothing had happened, but my forearm was cut to the bone. I screamed out in agony, bandaging it as well as I can with my other hand when I heard someone running towards me from behind. Soon, a man entered my vision and poured some healing liquid on my wound, which made it better but didn’t fix it entirely.”

“I can only imagine that you went on a vengeful journey to slay that crab afterwards.” Ria said.

“Thanks for putting my mind off the pain.” Added Eariph.

“This man, whose name was Aimer, was a local Danmuz, and rather peculiarly had a Cuemad sitting on his shoulder. He must have been no older than eighteen, and his Cuemad seemed a little younger.”

“What’s a Cuemad?” I asked, and I felt inexperienced of the world.

“A small mushroom person, usually as tall as a forearm when fully grown” Gareey answered.

In colour, it was dark red, darker than crimson, which then faded upwards to the blackest black I’ve seen in my life.

“There’s one.” Gareey said, humorously.

“There will be quite a few further on. Take this one now, and we’ll collect the rest when we get there.”

Eariph cut the Black Heart off the ground, and gave it to Siai who gratefully accepted her reward for falling. With that, Gareey continued further on, every now and then pointing out a Black Heart on the floor, where it would be cut from the ground with a knife and then given to one of us, in no particular order. Gareey told us that we should each manage to collect five each, and perhaps six if they were particularly abundant.

          The stomping got closer and closer. Gareey assured us that the Kruvan couldn’t have made it as far as where the Black Hearts grow. He insisted that their feet would get impaled, and they’d turn back immediately. He said this when a single tremor seemed to come from less than ten paces.

          We froze, and looked around. That’s when I saw a gigantic hole in the mushroom canopy, where the sun gleamed through hurting my eyes and reminding me of how well the Mushroom Trees blocked the sun. We could only assume that what had made this hole was still nearby, and for a few seconds the light coming from that hole was blocked. We rushed both worried and excited to see a Kruvan, and that’s exactly what we saw.

          Through the pinhole and on top of some squished trees we saw as much as we could. The giant tortoise in all its glory, standing ten paces above the forest’s canopy, with their tall necks as long as their legs. I could only imagine the view from up there, you must be able to see across half the Vugaix.

          That’s when a large gecko bit its neck. We all stood in surprise; I can tell even Gareey didn’t know what to make of it. If I had to describe the gecko, it had a long body with four legs and a large mouth, almost identical to regular ones. This one was a third the size of the Kruvan, the size of a room. Another latched onto the Kruvan’s tail, and another on one of its legs.

“What is that!?” I shouted.

“I have no idea!” Gareey replied, concerned.

We turned our pace and sprinted as far away from the action as we could, but the action seemed to follow us, like we were in a nightmare. As we dodged the trees, a shadow flew over the mushroom canopy. Then, no more than ten paces ahead, one of the geckos fell, collapsing the forest in its wake, creating a corridor of open space. It was then when we saw Kyenel. At the end of its landing, the colossal lizard had entered the desert, sand flying everywhere. This was the edge of the Vugaix.

          Gareey shouted, telling us to gather as many of the black hearts as we could. I took my pocketknife out, and began cutting the first Black Heart I saw. The ground was more illuminated than it should have been, the several holes created by the colossal battle introduced light onto the mycelia-covered floor. All while tremors of the attack were shaking the ground and piercing our ears.

          I cut my hand while hurrying to gather the Black Hearts. By the time I’d found my fifth, a roaring cry shattered the air. I hoped it was one of the lizards shrieking in their demise. Shortly after I had cut my sixth, another cry was heard. I then finished my sixth, stood up and looked for another. I walked forwards a few paces when, exactly where I was standing, was the keratin of the Kruvan.

          I’d nearly died. Out of the trees, everyone else emerged. Ria was in celebration, while Gareey had rushed to my aid with a few potions he was hiding in his gigantic backpack. He struggled to find any injury on me, but poured a drop on my hand where I was cut from the gathering. I looked around, and there were only a few trees separating us from Kyenel.

          I looked through those trees; the last giant lizard was in view. I hadn’t noticed, but the gecko was faced towards us, its massive eyes staring at us, around us. My heart sank, it roared, and began charging. I was sure we were all dead.

Its legs were rotating quickly, kicking up sand. There was a unanimous realisation that running was not an option, so instead we rushed through our satchels in search for anything we could do to stop it. It was too big for my readied claps to affect it in any way, and all of my complex claps would take too much time to think of.

Then, the loudest bang hit my ear loud enough to push my head off my shoulders. I looked to my right, Eariph had her hands together, her legs in a wide stance, the bright flash of a magical reaction occurring in her hands. They parted, and the flash became a silver wind flying forwards and sideways. This visible gust pierced the air, brushing my face with the feeling of a hundred knives. I was to the side of the clap, no-where near where Eariph was aiming. What she was aiming at was not where it used to be; I remembered the lizard being almost on top of us when it now was in fact struggling a few hundred paces away, back in the sands of Kyenel, lying on its back.

We ran.

“Incredible, Eariph, amazing” I shouted, I could not give enough praise.

“Let’s just get away.” She replied, truthfully.

“Where’s Siai?” Corvan added.

“Where’s Ria and Hodeso?” Gareey included.

We noticed that we hadn’t seen them since the gecko charged at us. We knew what had happened, and we know it was coming for a long time. Ria ran away, taking Siai for ransom money.

“Wait, this way!” Corvan shouted. He turned to run in a slightly different direction.

“Why?” I asked.

“I know where they went!” He said. “Want to know how?” he said, running.

“I was wandering in northern Kyeron and found myself completely lost among the trees.” He stopped for a breath.

“It was then when I noticed a path of foot tracks along the ground.” He paused again.  
“So I figured that the tracks lead somewhere, you get the point.” He said, out of breath.

We were far away enough for the gecko to no longer be a threat. We followed the tracks that Corvan pointed out, and after walking for a while we heard the boisterous talking of Ria, which quickly silenced one we heard her say ‘We’re being chased’. A noise of mycelia bending came through the forest, and Corvan and I began to run in chase of Ria. She was far ahead, but we were confident that carrying Siai would make it much harder to run.

          Gareey and Eariph must not have considered running, but I’ve been in these situations before. The feet pacing, our hands waving around grabbing onto the trees stopping us from falling. Unable to see my target but hear them instead. The sight of white mushroom trunks waving past, but still it felt like we weren’t making any progress, that was until I finally got sight of Ria and Hodeso, he was carrying Siai.

          I quickly gathered a clap mid-run, but my foot caught and I stumbled on a root, kicking up some wet dirt. It wasn’t over for me, Ria and Hodeso’s feet were attracted by the roots, kicking them more than they’d like to admit. The trees were tight. Running wasn’t a matter of going fast but rather just getting through. Corvan darted ahead, the sound of his swords clashing made it easy to tell where he was.

          I was slow enough for Eariph and Gareey to catch up. Either that, or they were going especially fast. Corvan was no-where in sight, but soon we heard a hard hit like a stone on wet wood. Step after step, we seemed to be getting nowhere. But we could still hear the footsteps in the distance, and eventually we could see some light peeking through the trees.

          We had come to an oasis, the beauty of an area where the trees refuse to grow. I breathed well for a few seconds; I had almost forgotten how musky the air of the Vugaix was, but the oases were not for admiring, not now. I saw Corvan disappear into the other side, which inspired me to put fire on my feet to catch up. I still had the clap I prepared when I stumbled, using the topmost ingredients, the Carimia Taten and Gynnag’s nail that Gareey had suggested we pick up.

          I passed Corvan, his face smelling the ground. As I passed him, he was moaning how close he was, but I didn’t hear the rest. I heard hard kicks on the mushroom trees ahead and as I squeezed through the trunks, I saw the dents that they made. I was getting close, and I saw some light not too far away. Through this oasis, I thought, I should be able to hit them.

          As I squeezed past the few trunks that hid the oasis, my eyes scanned for bodies, and sure enough I saw Ria and Hodeso, the latter of who was carrying Siai. I clapped, aiming as well as I could at Ria knowing that no matter who I hit it would slow them down. From my hands came a vicious and wild lightning bolt, that instantly stretched the paces between Ria and I and hit her lower leg. As she fell, coming my way was a shard of ice. It licked my shoulder, ripping my gowns and leaving a cold burn. Hodeso kept running. I avoided Ria in my pursuit and soon re-entered the darkness of the mushroom canopy. As I left the oasis, I saw my group enter it behind me.

          Hodeso mustn’t have gotten far, as soon after entering the mushroom canopy I heard a loud thud, a moan of pain, and came across Hodeso lying face-down on the floor with Siai nearby, who looked hurt beyond bruised. Hodeso quickly turned to face me as I approached, and released a shock clap that struck my left shoulder as I was drawing out my shortsword with my right. The shock clap hurt, the sensation like a cramp travelling down my left leg. I almost collapsed, but I was able to partly lean on a tree.

          I tread around Hodeso keeping my sword at point, making sure he didn’t make any moves until I reached Siai, who was on the floor unconscious. With all the might I had left, I hurled her over my cramped shoulder. I heard Eariph scream from the direction I had left Ria. I limped back to the oasis where I had hit Ria, all the while hearing muffled talking.

          I found the oasis, and among the beautiful mycelia and small mushroom bushes I saw Ria, standing up with her sword and buckler faced forwards, and Corvan’s longsword against her. Eariph had a sharp ice spike in her abdomen, Gareey tending to her wounds. I was carrying Siai, knowing that if I put her down to help, Hodeso might drag her back into the forest, so all I could do was watch and shuffle as two seasoned swords clashed.

          Corvan made the first attack, his longsword feinting downwards only for him to bring it up for a jab, which Ria blocked between her buckler and sword, and then backed away. Corvan extended a wide sweep to Ria’s legs, then Ria cut left, then right, and Corvan blocked Ria’s swipe while raising his sword into a high guard. Ria faced her buckler upwards as Corvan cut down from his left to right, Ria placed her sword between Corvan’s and her face, backed by her buckler, and gave a futile slice aimed at Corvan’s chest.

          Corvan acted out a cut with the twisting of his wrists in front, upwards and towards the left of Ria’s face. Her buckler came in to block it with a clash, which froze his longsword long enough for her to get a cut to his stomach, and in that moment Corvan raised his sword above Ria’s buckler and thrust it forwards across her face, leaving a severe laceration. Corvan’s gambeson soon began to turn red, but it seemed too shallow to stop him from fighting, as was Ria’s cut, leaving a strong bleeding red mark across her face.

          Corvan rushed in, twisting his sword one side to the other until finally he did one cut upwards from Ria’s left, and she had overextended her buckler. The cut dug far into the side of Ria’s chest, and although she crunched into her wound, her sword extended far enough at Hodeso to leave yet another mark on his gambeson, but it was no-where near deep enough to have touched his skin. She dropped her sword and fell.

          I had managed to reach Eariph and Gareey, and put Siai down comfortably by them. Gareey handed me a potion, its colour was tan-brown so it must have been a healing potion. Before I got up to give it to Corvan, he whispered that it was for Ria. I rushed to her downed body.

“Don’t help me.” Ria said, among her crunches of pain, almost trying to crawl away.

“No, if Corvan came and got you back up who knows what danger you might cause” I said, carefully taking off her ingredient pouch, her hands resisted mine but she was in no state to oppose me.

After disarming her, I poured the liquid on her chest cut, and although she tried to resist it, her chest would was mostly healed. I did the same to her face, all while pressing a sword at her neck in threat. I held her at sword-point for a while, until Gareey appeared by my side with a length of rope stretched between his hands.

“Anyone know how to tie a rope?” he said. I grabbed the rope, pushed Ria against the ground and tied her hands.

Silence.

“Where did you learn to swordfight?” I asked Corvan. His show was quite impressive.

“Funny you should ask, it’s actually at the end of that story I didn’t finish, the one about the footprints?”

“Do continue then.” I said. We rested in the Oasis, Eariph was still recovering from the ice spike.

“Well, I was following these footprints as I was lost in Kyeron, I figured they’d lead somewhere.”

“I came to a cave against a cliff, where the footprints stopped. An arrow whizzed past my head and ticked on the stone. I raised my hands and felt the press of cold steel against the back of my neck. When I got turned around, I saw multiple well-armed Ximuz police soldiers. They questioned me.”

“It turned out the cave was about to be raided as I approached it. Thankfu2lly for me, they believed my story; I did give them a lot of detail. The leader of the police group sent one of his soldiers to escort me to the nearest town. I offered to help the raid, but they refused saying they couldn’t be completely sure I was on their side.”

“And about where I learnt to properly swordfight, I realised that I should probably learn it while I was at the Ximus city, so I took some of the ingredients and trade goods I’d gathered from Terlaye and sold them in exchange for proper longsword lessons.”

“One other go and I won’t lose” Ria exhaled.

Eariph had decided it was time for her to be able to walk once more. She stood up and went towards Siai as if she were about to pick her up. I intervened, saying I had sustained the least damage, except from Gareey who was already carrying a backpack I could only imagine was twice the size of Siai.
