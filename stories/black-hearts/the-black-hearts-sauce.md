This document is supplementary to [The Black Hearts](/wastelife/stories/black-hearts/the-black-hearts)

The Black Hearts is essentially a fully planned character piece. It, specifically is a character piece for Ria, and historically served as an outlet for me to write about people I knew (basically ol). So now, I need to write the story with a proper sense of progression, where each character actually changes rather than being their static complex self.

I've developed a better writing style a lot since I first wrote The Black Hearts. It's a lot better, and the best of my works the short stories actually have something to say, or are character pieces. 

Black Hearts has always been an overview on narcissism, using the people I knew while I was doing my a-levels as inspiration for the characters, and sometimes just fantasy characters like with Siai.

So I'm gearing up for a complete re-write with overhauled characters that lean fully into their inspirations. The story also needs development, so I'll look into that as well.

Thraechos learns about the world is 1000 words long and is entirely dedicated to character development. I have 5 characters, and a plot to do. Ideally, I can stretch this to the full 20000 words it always needed to be.

# Characters
## Iana from All
Iana is the POV character. Historically, they have been vessels of the story and development is focussed on the people they can see. Iana in this is a stand-in for me, their development should reflect my own throughout A levels and coming to realise the narcissism of \[old friend]. Perhaps they specifically are drawn in by Ria's friendliness and confidence, but as Ria's cruelty slowly trickles into her dialogue Iana comes to recognise that she is a little mean, and during the crescendo when Ria kidnaps Siai (something that definitely has a mirror irl) it's the final straw that makes Iana reconsider her whole relationship with Ria and actually be okay with fighting her.

## Ria from Alinreg
Ria is the most important character in the story. It's her uncaring nature that brews throughout the story that introduces conflict before ultimately becoming the conflict. Being an emotional stand-in for \[old friend], she needs to actually draw people into her emotional circle, before ultimately berating them to raise herself upwards on an emotional level. She probably won't develop, her static narcissism essentially being the main villain of the plot. 

There's also something else I want to explore with the ideas of narcissism in a long story plot, and that's empathy. Not Ria's empathy, they lack it, but my empathy for someone mentally incapable of empathy. Because ultimately, narcissistic personality disorder is a social disorder out of Ria's control. It's not her fault she cannot empathise, but blame can be put on her for harming others both emotionally before the climax and physically after the climax.

Thinking about what microaggressions Ria would actually do is gonna be a pretty big part of before the climax. Trying to remember my own history, \[old fiend] was just like randomly mean sometimes, apparently to \[long friend] more so than I. This should be relatively easy to emulate just random mean additions in an otherwise normal interaction. These, to my knowledge, would serve to make Ria feel better about herself and of course she doesn't think of the person she's being mean to. 

Then later, closer to the climax, when Ria thinks she's strongly established as a leader and has made friends with everyone, she starts to push people around. Some of them might not fight back against this, especially Siai who might actually like it as a change to her normal relationship. Hodeso might like this as, for the people he's usually involved in, physical command is a large part of their thing. Gareey too might actually like that someone else is commanding his expedition, Ria would show a lot of respect to Gareey because she recognises his "usefulness". Gareey's development is centered around him learning to actually care for his expedition party as he's too ready to shove them into dangerous parts of the world. Maybe although he's initially appreciative of Ria's commanding efforts, his empathy for others eventually has him realise that although Ria is nice to _him_ she isn't nice to others. This would be what ultimately has him side with Iana during the climax.

## Hodeso from Tenia
Hodeso in the original plot is kinda just Ria's sidekick, but development demands he be his own character. So, I might just do a straight overhaul and base Hodeso on \[crazy friend] who I genuinely liked but had some things going on that were too much for me. He would be with Ria for the beginning genuinely interested in her and even joining her side during her antagonisming after the climax. Afterwards, Hodeso needs to have some serious reconsideration and eventually be against Ria, but in a way that he's still on his own team tied to whatever dodgy things he has going for him.

## Corvan Esata
Corvan should be a recurring character in Wastelife, their journeys happening anywhere and everywhere. But, during my A-levels, Corvan essentially represented my sense of adventure, a bit underdeveloped, but there in full force. Instead of Corvan's character being driven by sharing stories of his past, he should instead just like mention them from time to time. He needs to be self-driven but willing to be lead by others. Perhaps, throughout the story, he learns to be a leading figure if he needs to. He would be too ready to let other people lead the group when he is a very competent leader. It's a bit of a shallow development, but Corvan is a larger character.

Corvan Esata is probably my weakest character, actually. He's got this huge and long backstory but what does that really mean? He's supposed to be a reflection of my _sense of adventure_ what even is that? How did it develop during this period of my life?? Maybe Corvan's development has something to do with his need for adventure. Maybe all his previous adventures were quite light hearted, maybe during the black hearts he has to come to terms with the darker sides of adventuring and the real fear of death and danger on others?

## Eariph from Odea Yemon
Eariph, during my A-levels, represented my academic anxiety. My A levels were a period of real academic turmoil. I was failing at my subjects, struggling to pay attention and dead scared of exams. Eariph in the original story is just very shy about her academic achievement. She got into the University of Odea Yemon, an established and prestigious institution, but struggles with her ability to achieve academically. As for character development, Ria would push her down further, making her character be at a low point during the climax. Maybe she still does the air clap to push the Genoyg back?

Reframing Eariph's character development around Ria's narcissistic personality leans heavily into Ria's treatment of her, pushing Eariph's academic achievement down in an attempts to make herself feel better about other people just being able to do some stuff that she can't do. Eariph starts the story thinking she's a bad academic and her development is in talking to Gareey, Iana, and Siai about how she should give herself more credit.
## Siai from Easea
Siai's honestly a fantasy character that I maybe just based on a fantasy trope. But, Siai's story is one of subjugation by her "master". She was placed into this harmful relationship and only now is beginning to question it. She doesn't do a good job at hiding her origins, and ends up getting kidnapped. She's one of the characters that actually had a development in the original story. She goes from being very insular and reserved to trusting people and eventually learning to reject her master.

But now that every other character's become very fleshy, I can add more to this. I'm leaning into Ria's confidence and attractiveness as a leader during the first parts of the story, and maybe she enamours Siai a lot. Maybe Siai falls into the trap of Ria's toxic relationship for the very same reason she's in one to begin with, and maybe because of that Ria is the one that suggests she tear away from her "master" and serve her instead, promising a better relationship that she ultimately won't provide. 

This is starting to challenge the kidnapping idea of the main plot. Originally, Ria kidnaps Siai understanding who her master is with the intention of ransoming her. While this climax is okay, it ultimately doesn't serve the story's themes. It might make more sense if Siai willingly enters an abusive relationship with Ria after being convinced that her "master" is bad and that Ria would be better. With this new plot I can actually broaden the purpose of the climax into an emotional battle.

Perhaps during the climax, not only are there massive creatures fighting each other over the scene of the expedition trying to pick up as many black hearts as possible, but maybe the scenes of hurtful narcassism that Ria and Iana start to conflict in just before the climax suddenly burst and there is a massive falling out. During the scramble for black hearts, there is also a chaotic emotional battle between Iana and Ria that ultimately ends in the party splitting up, Iana, Corvan, Eariph, and Gareey on one side and Ria, Hodeso, and Siai on another.

I could even tie this into my personal experience a little more by having Hodeso be the one that realises Ria's hurtful behaviours, ultimately turning to Iana's group for help. If I really want to mirror my own history with the people these characters are based on, then Corvan might have been looking into a relationship with Ria before ultimately being hurt himself, and feeling betrayed he goes to the only people he knows will help him.

In this new and emerging plot, Siai might have actually made friends with pretty much everyone, but in the climactic decision she chooses to go with Ria. Perhaps because that relationship is more familiar. I think Ria's treatment of Siai would actually be picked up on by Hodeso who takes that as his warning that Ria is a _tad_ abusive. 

Thing is, after this new plot, once Siai is emotionally captured by Ria, Ria starts abusing Siai (and Siai accepts this as it's not that much different to her old relationship), Hodeso picks up on this and abandons Ria and tells Iana, then it's basically the same plot: a kidnapping that Iana and the group have to physically rescue Iana from. I don't think this is an emotionally gratifying ending, if Iana just successfully defeats Ria and brings Siai into emotional safety. Something powerful has to happen.

This was supposed to be a short character piece on Siai but I guess I forgot she's kinda just a plot object in the original. By turning her from plot object into a full character in The Black Heart's story about narcissism, I have completely transformed the plot into something probably a lot better and more meaningful. I'll think about the end later. Part of me wishes I had been in the narcissistic relationship longer so that I'd have more experience to draw from because I ended that relationship as soon as something truly foul entered my ears.

He gets paid for them, but he should probably have some other motivation like maybe he just likes doing these kinds of expeditions. Maybe he is a little reckless with his expedition party? The worst part about him completing his character ark by the climax is that he is then the perfect character to kill off. I can't do it! I can't kill of my character! It's so weird! It's even a little cliché!

## Leaving Esphetha
The story could start in any part of Lale because they take a boat to the heabe. But it starts in the city of Esphetha because then they can quite easily take a boat to Hapheme.

In the start of draft 2 I have Iana racing to catch the group as they left. I thought this would be a great way to add a bit of excitement to the start of the story. To make this more impactful though I need to put more emphasis on Iana needing to leave and get the ingredients. 'She really wants them for her claps' is _technically_ a motivation, but there's got to be something a little more personal, a reason for her _character_ to want to go on the expedition.

So maybe instead of just wanting to get some good ingredients, instead of her AG just being okay with her leaving, I should add some conflict. Like, she actually doesn't like her AG for one reason or another. This reason should be a conflict of character that acts as a small microcosm of the larger emotional conflicts in The Black Hearts. Perhaps her specific character change is just her using her experience in the story with hurtful relationships to decide that she needs to leave her adventuring group. Her change would be brought on by the experience of interaction with other characters, and although her relationship with the leaders of her AG wouldn't be abusive (perhaps only unappreciative or some similar thing) she still recognises that better things are possible.

So Iana hears about the expedition, asks her AG to go on it, and they decline. She argues for it and eventually decides she's going without her AG's support, which means she is strained for money. Her AG will probably just ask that she re-join, because while they are outwardly unappreciative of her contributions she also fills a very important role in the group and they know this.

So after that she scrambles to the port just to add some slight rush in leaving. This rush could play into her dynamic with her AG where she doesn't have enough time to truly explain why she _needs_ to go and ends up deciding in an instant that she's going and that she'll wait in Esphetha when she's done. This also means that when she ultimately decides to not rejoin her AG at the end she has to leave some sort of post note for them to pick up on when they look for her believing her to have waited.
