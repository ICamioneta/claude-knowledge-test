# Ereso
## 1. Serdeo landing at Synset
People lept from the boat with vigour, landing on the rocky dock surface and hastily tying the boat's ropes to iron bollards. The ropes slammed taut. The boat came to a gentle stop, jostling as it hit the dock's edge.

‘All moored!’ yelled a shiphand.
The captain raised his thumbs. He walked down the deck and greeted me.
‘well, here you are.’ He said.
‘Here I am!’ I said excitedly. I snatched Kodi’s reigns. Kyroh was besides me.
‘Where will you go from here?’
‘I’m not too sure, I’ll probably go see the spire. Do you have any suggestions?’
‘I’m not the best travelled in Ereso, but Redel’s a beautiful town of modernity. There's also the  Anlyrer Bridges in Narsyrdel and Carsedel. I'll never get used to passing under them.
‘Anywhere a little nearer?’
‘I know Ereso's ports like the beat of my heart, but any further and I’m lost. Although, I have heard things about impressive towers somewhere north of here. If I remember correctly, they’re Aerladan. Never been to Aerlada myself but I did stop in Haerul once.’
‘I’ll have fun finding my own way. Thanks for the trip.’ I stepped from the boat lifting Kodi, our weights shifting the boat.
‘No worries. Oh! I almost forgot, Redel’s university does research on Hidol, I’m sure they’d love to see Kyroh!’ We waved goodbye.

Masterfully constructed stone piers stretch for hundreds of paces down the coastline. Further on they're wooden and small. Even past the piers, boats beach on the low-tide shores. High stone walls hug the waterfront, extending just past the stone docks. We walked towards the nearest gatehouse into the city.

After dodging clattering crates, we eventually made it past a gatehouse and into the city proper. The city's bold and tall buildings weren't afraid of leaning over the streets. These buildings were of solid stone, neatly whitewashed. Intricate glass patterns lay on windows inset by the thick stone walls.

Further along this corridor of stone is a massive open square. Chatter reached out like flames from a fire; a brimming market with all kinds of local goods came into view. By the market, a granary as large as any of the houses. Next to the granary, a beautiful white-stone compound bearing two flags, the blue half-cross flag of Eresonia and another flag, no doubt that of Synset.

Walking through Synset's well-paved streets, I noticed crowds of people staring at Kyroh. They were well covered in clothing, but given how they see from their head, it was on clear show. I've no doubt gem golems are a common sight, but one wearing clothes is certainly taboo here. I scoured the city's regions looking for a place for us to stay. I settled for a place outside the city walls with a stable and a two-bed room. It was expensive, but I can't settle for a hostel with Kyroh.

## 2. Serdeo meets Meibin
Walking through Ereso's northern forest, you tend to get a sense of how strange the plants can be. Leaves explode from irregular stumps. Clumps of thick and hard grass extend beyond the treeline. Weeds with leaves as hard and sharp as glass force you to watch your step. I realised we'd entered the forest properly when the trees got taller and the road overgrown; branches making a ceiling over the road and the sun creating spots on the road through the leave's gaps.

I noticed a small frog hopping onto the road. I stopped, Kyroh stopped, Kodi stopped. It was a very watery frog, landing in a relatively large puddle of its own making. It looked at us, and then jumped onto Kyroh.

‘Don’t let it touch your skin!’ a person said frantically scurrying out of the trees.

The frog hopped onto one of the bags Kodi was wearing.

‘Don’t kill it! I need it captured!’ the person said again, sprinting as fast as they could.

The frog looked at me. I prepared a clap. I knew I was well covered, and the frog was unlikely to land on my skin if it hopped at me. The frog jumped towards me, appearing larger as it flew directly towards my face. The forest person tossed a bowl in front of my face, and the frog was caught in it. They rushed to cover the bowl, sliding a plate beneath it, and sealing the gaps with some enchantment. That's when I noticed they had done all this while carrying a large plant pot on their backs.

‘What was that about?’ I asked.

‘A Pollersellin.’ They responded.

‘Pollersellin?’

‘If it had landed on your face, all your water would be sucked out and you'd be completely dry.’

‘How does that work? Where does the water go?’

‘I don't know, probably the Hidol.’

'That's not how the Hidol works.'

'I try not to think about it, but the folks at Redel wanted to have one for study. They're so rare, I was ecstatic when I saw the watery trail one of these guys left behind.' They paused, 'You seem out of your environment, where are you from?'

‘Me? I’m from Lale, an Oenesite if you know what that means.'

‘I think I know where Lale is. I’ve spent the last decades of my life in Codia, and they don't really talk about much outside Neble.’

‘Codia? Fighting off the Ews?’

‘No, working with them.’

‘How about that.’

A little silence.

'I need to get back to Redel. Tell me where you're going and I might be able to accompany you part of the way. Out of the forest, at least.

‘I’m sightseeing, just saw the five Aerladan towers, thought I’d make my way to see the spire next.’

‘What made you take this road? If you left from Toniten, the road to Mia Mesten is much better travelled. Better, safer, and overall a faster way to Redel.’

‘I just picked it on a whim.’

We started walking.

‘And you travel alone?’

‘No, I’ve got Kyroh here!’

‘That’s a gem golem, you travel alone.’

‘They’re more than a gem golem, I’ve given it personhood.’

‘I’m not sure what you mean by that.’

'Independent thoughts, coherent speech, participation in society. Say hi, Kyroh!'

‘ai’ Kyroh said slightly waving their hand.

‘A gem golem that speaks.’ The person replied.

'With some difficulty. Bringing them to sentience was a challenge enough, teaching them language was its own struggle!'

‘Are you teaching it both Eresonain and Seaspeak?’

‘Yes. They're getting along a lot better with Eresonian, not sure what that means about the complexity of our two languages.’

‘You’re Eresonain is very good, when did you learn?’

‘I came down here once on a trip to Redel’s university, and I polished my understanding on the boat here.’

‘How about you, Kyroh? Are you any good at the language?’

‘It’s okay’ Kyroh said.

‘You confident in Kyroh’s ability to learn language?’

‘I’ve taught it plenty of Seaspeak, we can speak well enough with each other.’

‘Who is this person?’ Kyroh asked me in Seaspeak.

‘Kyroh wants to know who you are.’ I said to them.

'Me?' he turned to Kyroh. 'I'm a Velews. I usually keep myself busy with Raikeor, the Ews here. I've managed to pick up a few more jobs from the cities I go to.' 

‘And you’re doing them alone.’

‘I’ve got this plant!’ he said, gesturing to the massive potted plant on his back.

‘Has that achieved sentience as well?’

‘No, not quite yet. I still don’t really know what it is. One of Codia’s Ysgews told me it would grow to be a Codews if I nurture it.’

‘Looks difficult to carry.’

‘I had to haul it from Naternegorna across Ereso and all the way up here.’

‘Naternegorna?’

‘Nater’s End, I’m sure you’ve heard of it. Capital of Shildo? Largest city until a few centuries back?'

‘Yeah, I’ve heard of it. I got a donkey to carry my things. His name is Kodi. You can strap the plant to him if you want.’
# The Southern Revolts 1: Rejedmana
## 1.1 Lesliye after the shory
I stayed in the forest for a few days, I kept switching between my true form and Eleurin’s form so that I could get used to it. The new body soon felt like it was mine. I got really familiar with the forest, the villagers sent mobs after me. They screamed "Calapeth! Come out!" It was easy to hide. No-one can really notice me in my gas form, if they aren't paying enough attention. 

On the other side of the forest, there was a road. It was a march or two away from the village, so I doubt I’d meet anyone from there on this road. Regardless, Eleurin’s form is so different from the body I used to use, I just hope no-one from the village recognises her. I managed to sneak back into my house for some clothing, enough to keep me comfortable. On the road, I didn't know which way to go. I chose the way away from the village.

I hadn't walked for long until I saw a small group of people. Clearly adventurers, four people and a pack mule. As we approached each other, I could hear them chatting.

‘Have you ever tried Mima?’ someone spoke.

‘Yes, I have. Creamy but still mature, one of the best cheeses to come out of Ereso for sure.’ Another said.

‘Still nothing compared to your cheeses?’

‘Hireax Cheese comes to mind when I think of my experience with Mima. Mima’s good, but the exact blend of maturity and creaminess doesn’t quite match up for most purposes. Hireax is that little firmer that you really need in a cheese for it to be useable, and slightly less mature to appeal to a significantly wider audience.’

‘I’ve tried these and none of them compare to Darie cheeses. I’ll say it again, if Ralean imported Darie cheese, it would be our largest export to the region!’ A third said, a Domuz.

‘I’ve splurged on a couple cheeses from Lale Darie in the past, nothing too special.’ The first said.

‘You probably only get the long-lasting Lale cheeses. The best experience you can ever have with cheese is a freshly prepared Deorlle platter served with the richest of Dithas’ champiñones and some Iopheille-standard crackers.’

‘I’ve never tried it myself, but in Caroset they have this combination of their cheese with these things called Coffee they get from an island conglomerate in Raidal. Apparently, it was a platter first prepared by Bwydo himself!’

‘Bwydo puts together loads of bad food.’ The second interjected.

They would soon pass me. Instead of letting them go on their way I decided that I should stick to my own promises and be more sociable. This was the new me, a chatty individual, and an adventuring group was perfect. Going everywhere, never staying in one place long enough to get caught. Maybe they’d be compassionate enough for me to open up and show them who I am. I can dream.

‘Hi there!’ I said to them.

‘Hello! How are you?’ the first person said, I gathered they were an Eresonian from their cheese-talk.

‘I have to be honest with you, I’m a little lost.’

‘Seems like it from your clothing. Have you been living in a forest?’

‘That’s more accurate than I’d like to admit.’

‘Where do you need to be?’

‘I don’t really have any plans. I was hoping, in your kindness, you’d let me travel with you at least until the next city.’

‘We were on our way to Eswin, so no city in particular. If you follow this road south-west until it splits, then the west path goes into Nater’s End and the east path goes to Milad. You could stay with us and hope to find some caravan going to a city, if you’re looking for safe travel. It’s not that far until the road splits and you can go north into Naserich.’ The second person said. Their accent suggested they were from central Ralean, maybe a Rodxan.

‘Thanks for the suggestions, I think I’ll stay with you lot for a while, until I decide.’

‘Great, I’m Meibin, this is Yosher—‘ the Eresonain started,

‘I’m Serdeo!’ the Domuz said.

‘and that’s Kodi, our mule, and Kyroh.’

‘Hi Kyroh!’ I said.

‘Kyroh doesn’t speak Empiric yet, they’re still getting the hand of Seaspeak’ Serdeo said.

‘What’s their first language?’ I asked.

‘Seaspeak! If you'd believe it. They picked up Eresonain fast, so that's their best language.’

I looked at Kyroh a little closer and realised that their features were a little more rigid than I expected.

‘Is that a gem golem?’ I asked.

‘Yes, I managed to bring it to personhood! Isn’t it fascinating! The exact ingredients you need changes a lot depending on the individual instance of the gemstone, but the key is a strong enough 738, 110 T-tensor to match the Spessartine’s 229.55, 255.38 T-tensor during casting to bring into full affect a 576, 272 T-tensor. All you need then is— ‘

‘It’s a bunch of science, basically.’ Yosher interrupted.

‘If you want to simplify ten years of my research, then yes. Are you much of a mage— sorry, I realised we haven’t got your name.’

‘Its Lesliye. I haven’t done any clapping or casting, I’m lost at the basics, what you just said was a bunch of numbers.’ This was a lie, I knew a few enchantments.

‘I’ve been a mage and a wizard for longer than Serdeo’s been an academic and I don’t know what they’re talking about.’ Meibin said

‘Practice is an art in itself, but understanding the core of what makes claps, enchantments, and gemcasting work boosts your hidomancy to the next level.’

‘I’m going to have a hard time fitting in here’ I said

‘Don’t worry, I’m a fighter through-and-through, and they’ve kept me for long enough.’ Yosher said.

 I noticed that the pack mule, Kodi, had a large potted plant strapped to their back.

‘What’s up with the plant, if you don’t mind me asking?’

‘I got it from an Ysgews in Codia, they told me that it would help me in my time of need. I’ve been thinking it’s a Codews, so it’ll be roughly another year until I find out.’ Meibin responded.

‘Ysgews? Codews?’

‘You’ve never heard of the Ews? You have one here in southern Ralean, the very Eswin we’re going to see. They’re a species of forest-related creatures.’

‘Sorry, I don’t get out often.’

‘I’ve been running around Shildo helping them out with some major issues, these four have been tagging along for the ride.’ He said while gesturing towards Serdeo, Yosher, Kodi, and Kyroh.’

‘Do you think I could join? I don’t know what I can offer yet, but I’m pretty good at staying quiet, if you know what I mean.’

‘We’ve been doing some dangerous things, so you’re free to tag along but know Eswin’s going to have me do something very important.’

‘That’s okay. If I’m to be honest I was lost in more ways than one.’

The clouds were starting to thin, the sun getting brighter. My clothing wasn’t covering enough, if the sun shone on me it would have given me away.

‘Sorry, I can’t help but notice the sun’s getting quite bright. I have really sensitive skin and I can’t let it go into sunlight. Do you have anything I can cover myself in?’ I asked.

‘Uhm, yes, sure. Kyroh’s wearing some covering clothes, they were supposed to avoid the strange looks. It doesn’t really work. I don’t have a spare change of those clothes, but I have more combat-ready garments for Kyroh to wear if you want to change into their clothes.’ Serdeo responded.

‘I might have to do that. Gem golems don’t dirty their clothes, do they?’

‘No, not in a way you'd notice.’ Serdeo said, and turned to Kyroh.

‘Kyroh! Ch'fanme besoin de vos amicloter!’ Serdeo said. ([[Foreign Language Stand-ins Juha#Lesliye after the Shory|see here for translation]])

‘Roaison?’ Kyroh responded.

‘Al a el peau sensible. Ils ne travaillaient pas à vous muncher de bilo fachon.’

‘d'alleumette.’ Kyroh started taking off their clothing. I was strangely surprised to see a perfectly normal-looking Spessartine golem underneath, slightly transparent and with an orange tint.

‘I’ll go change in the forest.’ I said, taking the clothes and walking into said forest.

## 1.2 Lesliye meets Eswin
We were deep in the forest. Meibin had told me more about what the Ews were, and the Ysgews especially. Serdeo said he helped Meibin do ‘his thing’ in Raikeor.

‘I think this is the place. Are you ready to see an Ysgews, Lesliye?’ Meibin said.

‘I don’t know what type of preparation I would need.’

The forest here was dense, and we were far from any Muz-built road. We walked a vague clearing in the bushes that Meibin seemed at home in. It started to feel like the trees were looking at me. I could see a clearing up ahead, golden sunlight lighting up the bark of the trees. All of a sudden, the trees *were* looking at me. Two humanoid tree-like shapes turned out to be Codews holding spears.

‘Oucis! Cviri id is?’ one of the Codews spoke. ([[Foreign Language Stand-ins Juha#Lesliye in Eswin|translation]])

‘Jarsami bos! Mum id Meibin. Mum sini Codia. Is, mum i hecllona.’ Meibin said, and showed them something on his necklace.

‘Cyiri id usu hg’alaam?’

‘Dosurscellu mum i lullaq mum. Jaamur id hibaat hg’alaam. Mum icyorcis jaamur.’

‘Baalki jaamur li as uluttu wixcul, un li as uluttu wixcul.’ The codews turned to us.

‘You are welcome in this village, but you stay close to Meibin.’ He said to us in Empiric.

‘Come on then!’ Meibin urged us all in.

The village was strange, simple wooden houses populated the area. Not far ahead was a massive tree stump, with huge wooden horns as tall as a building, and a face.

‘This is Dodellu, our Elder.’ One of the leading Codews said.

Every side of Dodellu stood these towering ews people who were at least four metres tall. Each one held a different weapon; the weapons were made of metal but glowing a vibrant orange.

‘Un cyashas id Meibin. Mumat t'avlas c'it un, osgwn dannase Herews Codia bosiri un ryara acllis.’ The Ysgews spoke with a deep and commanding voice.

‘hu, tuw id mum.’ Meibin responded.

‘anx id aclliscyish. Iqit'uttu. Hapucapur. Obsi ditalurces Ralean ryara id dital qhg’it jem. Eswin ryara id zaral.’

‘hani as un ryamcul, tot mum haraxarasi noso as?’

‘cus beces heses as tot, osgwn bachg’as canasi mum.’

‘Rejedmana. Ryanasi hane'ni sint'u cubusu.’

‘Jaq'an. Qgh’es.’

Meibin turned to us.

‘So, where to next?’ Yosher asked.

‘Rejedmana. I’ll fill you in on the details as we travel.’ Meibin began leaving.

## 1.3 Lesliye discovers the plot
We had taken a foot path north of the Ews settlement until we hit a road, and then turned west to reach the city of Naserich. The city’s first gatehouse appears like a wild cat jumping out of the trees. The rough stone cuts of the walls are a little unwelcoming but blend the in with the surrounding forests like an overgrown ruin. Walking through the gatehouse is like entering a portal into another realm: a massive woodland clearing with the edge of the forest like a wall. The first gatehouse is the entrance to the clearing; the city's inner walls stand from a well-cut stone. Entering the city proper, you're surprised by the sparse nature of the buildings and the greenery within.

‘Well, here we are, a city. Best leave you to it, Lesliye.’ Meibin said.

‘Leave me to it?’

‘Naserich is a lovely city. Well protected, attuned to nature, I’m sure you’ll find some work easily.’

‘I don't I want to stay here.’

‘Dodellu has me doing uncertain work, I don’t know how dangerous its going to be and I don’t want to bring a civilian into this.’

‘Can’t I come with? I can make myself useful.’

‘Are you good with a sword?’

‘No, but I can learn-’

‘learn here. If I know Naserich, then if you follow this road, take the left just before the square, the building on your left should be the public hall. Or, you can head straight to the fighter’s guild which is on the other side of the city. Ask around, you’ll get there.’

‘But-’

‘We have some things to do here, please don’t follow us.’ Yosher added, ‘Goodbye.’

‘Goodbye!’ Serdeo said.

‘Adé’ Kyroh said.

‘Goodbye, and good luck!’ Meibin ended.

Then, they waked away. The crowds of the city were mild but people still avoided walking by Kyroh. I stood there a while, pondering my next move. When they were finally out of sight, I continued down the road. I noticed the buildings. They were all made out of some sort of wooden bricks, perhaps treaded in a special way to harden them. They were held together by a dirt-clay mortar. Above the ground, floors overhung the streets a little, with their wooden structures keeping the same colour as the wooden bricks.

As I neared the city’s central square the smell became kindly present. A floral aroma that freshened the city street squalor. The normal aroma of the city wasn’t gone, only masked by the freshness of the flowers. The city’s square is more of a garden, and a busy one at that. Intricately laid out patterns of vibrant flowers ordered neatly in the flower beds. A sign saying "The people's hard work - don't tread!". 

I got out of the garden and went down a street I'd already passed. It was a bit narrow and populated. Signs hung from above doors. One of these signs simply said ‘work in the city’ with a painting of a hand pointing towards the building. I figured it was probably the better place to start rebuilding my life. I walk in.

Immediately behind the door is a large counter, keeping the majority of the building for the organisers there and only a small part of the floor space for me. On the far edge is a workstation and a large contraption loaded with uncountably many small metal letters and words, and a large bottle of ink.

An organiser is behind the counter talking to a mailperson with a large satchel slung over their shoulder. The mailperson puts a brick of letters on the counter, and I overhear conversation.

‘Is that all of them?’ the organiser said.

‘Every city south of Cania, all except Kovedlajo and Milad’

‘I hope this all goes well.’ He takes a slow glance at me.

‘I’ll be with you in a second.’ The organiser says to me.

He looks back at the mailperson, and hands them a bundle of letters and a few posters. The mailperson takes them, and before running off makes a gesture at the organiser. They raise their fist, palm forwards. The organiser glances at me again, then returns the gesture to the mailperson. The mailperson ran off, and the organiser turned to me.

‘Hello! Can I help you?’ he asks.

‘Hi, I’ve just come into town and am a little short on pheg. Not sure how long I’ll be staying, so I thought I’d get some work somewhere, get me through this city and maybe to the next.’ I lean into the counter.

‘Right, let me see…’ he crouches down and opens a cupboard. I quickly peek at the letters still on the counter. They were slightly spread out so I could see where they were from. Just like the mailperson had said, there was one for each city south of Cania, and the one on the top read Rejedmana.

‘Here we are!’ he poked back up and I quickly snapped myself away from the letters.

‘Eiveri always asks for a pair of extra hands, makes you wander what happened to the last few! Other than that, seems like the merchants could always use some labourers to move things. Mason’s guild wants that too, but I’d only recommend that if you’re stronger than you look. Aaron’s asked for a clerk, if you’re any good with numbers. I’d say we could use one here, with all the new work I’m doing.’ He went silent after saying that.

‘Anything interest you?’

‘If you have work here, I’d like to help.’ I said.

‘Unfortunately, the Soris, who *technically* owns this *Kidhmat* ([[The Empire of Ralean#The Bureaucracy|public service]]), can’t stretch his massive budget for that. If you can do clerical work, you should probably go see Aaron.  He’s quite far from here, have a look at this map.’ He got a map from under the counter and flattened it out.

‘You’re here, and Aaron’s here. Memorise these streets. They are your streets now.’ He looked me dead in the eyes.

‘Sounds good, I think I have enough to spend the night somewhere, I might decide on a job tomorrow.’ I said.

‘Great. Here’s the nearest hostel, get back to me if Aaron scares you off. He does that sometimes.’

I left the building and walked off. Not towards the hostel. I was curious. I decided to sit in the gardens for the rest of the evening, until the sun started to fall. It was dark, but I could see just fine. Something was up with those letters, and I wanted to find out. With one of them from Rejedmana, I’m sure the adventurers would like to know too.

The streets were eerily silent. I was still wearing Kyroh’s clothes, and while they may not have concealed them well, they were perfect for me. I had kept my tools concealed in my dark original clothes. These clothes also concealed my shape. I pulled up the scarf to hide my face. I slid across the gardens and found my way into the street that the job building sat. I found an alleyway that went down the side of the building. The alleyway revealed a door into the building. I silently tried it, but it was locked. I took out my tools, and the lock was as easy as any other.

The door led to the floor behind the front desk. The smell of paper and fresh ink stuck across the room. I snuck across the stone floor past the wooden staircase to the front desk. The desktop was clean, but I noticed the well-labelled draws. The few windows let in so little light that I could barely distinguish the letters.

I unpacked a small silver cylinder from my bag. I learnt to enchant things in the forest. I mostly did rocks with a dim light so that I could see a little better at night. I tried to remember the basics, but that got me no-where, so I brought myself back into the forest with a rock in my hand. Closing my eyes, I imagined slowly turning the rock, feeling a water-like resistance, before it clicked. A dim light brightened my eyelids, and the cylinder was glowing.

The shelves were labelled ‘jobs’, ‘outgoing’, ‘ingoing’. I opened the ingoing draw. The mail was partitioned by place of origin, sorted by provinces then by country. I scrolled through the provinces. Ilandusdinum, Vezandusdinum, Vadeidinum. I opened Vadeidinum and scrolled through the letters one-by-one, but I couldn’t find the letter from Detvadei I had seen on the desk. I spent a while scrolling through the mail in the drawer, then the mail in the next, and in the one after that, but those letters weren’t there.

I checked the entire floor for letters, thoroughly and quietly flipping through every piece of paper in every drawer. I even checked the floor for any ashes, in case they had been burnt. There was nothing on this floor, so I headed for the next.

The stairs were separated from the rest of the room by wooden boards. I could see the first floor had a landing with a few more rooms behind doors. I crept up the stairs. Every other step would creak, so I started testing every step before making my ascent. Once I was on the landing at the top of the stairs, I still had to be careful. The floorboards were hidden under a thin carpet.

I checked the room at the head of the stairs. It was a bathroom with no letters. The next room was a small bedroom, bed on the opposite side with someone sleeping in it. Next to that was an end table with a candle and a pile of six opened letters. The door started to open on its own. I quickly slowed it down before it slammed into the wall. I delicately stepped across the floor. Halfway across the room, one board made a loud creak. The bed rustled. The room returned to silence, and I was able to snatch the letters from the table. I left the room just as carefully, avoiding the loud floorboard, and read the letters outside the room’s door.

I took out the letters.

‘Good news from Rejedmana. All the city’s part of the union – and the guards are helping as well. Come the fourth week, the armoury will be open to the public and all roads to Cania will be closed.’ The letter continued with specific details about the whole region’s population, union membership by trade, and the arms stored in each town, followed by a short writing on how the region was sure to succeed in an upcoming revolution.

I unwrapped the next letter.

‘Things are well in Detkut, raids happen weekly but our insiders lead the police to the wrong doors where they can. I’m glad we’re starting in three weeks, not sure how the army would react to finding out half the city’s in the union!’ it continued with the same details.

The other four letters, Detkain, Detvadei, R.H. Ercio, R.H. Leishi, all gave similar reports. The cities and their towns ready to burst out of the empire. I copied the letters, put them back. Before I left the room again, I noticed another slip of paper. It only had a place, a time, and a word. I copied those down and left the building.

The time on the paper was early in the morning. The place, I scouted, was some door accessible through a backstreet not far from the centre. The word was ‘Glisbus’, which doesn’t mean anything. When the sun rose, I watched the clock carefully. The city started to move, as people left their houses and had places to be. Within minutes, the city was as busy as ever. People meeting in the garden-like plaza, market stalls being attended to, the rush of getting to work.

I waited for the time on the card to pass, then a little longer before walking down the alleyway and to the door. Faint voices could be heard behind it, but I couldn’t make out any words. I subtly changed my face. I knocked on the door, and the chatter paused.

‘Oh! Let me get that!’ a voice from beyond the door. A slight murmur was heard inside afterwards.

‘The Empire is beautiful this morning!’ the voice said to me through the door. I checked the copied card’s word.

‘Uhm, Glisbus?’ I replied.

‘Yes, yes, welcome.’ He said as the door opened.

Inside was a sizeable room with a few tables pushed against each other. Around that table stood several occupied chairs.

‘Welcome, ally!’ several people from the table said. I took off my hood.

‘Yes, hello!’

‘Are you new? I don’t think I’ve seen you here before?’ the person who opened the door said.

"Why don't you recede them so that we can cross?" 

"Ah, well, Kyroh's recasting ingredients are _very specific_ and I've only managed to find a strong enough 738, 110 T-tensor in a _Seltythcarn Nathyr_ which is a very rare ingredient only found on the border between Dithas and Duela."

"When those soldiers come back and they haven't found the ingredients they will offer to escort us to Naserich. Their priority is making sure we wont have any weapons when walking through the bulk of the army and regrettably a spessartine golem is a weapon to them. They probably have a few ready to cast."

"Kyroh's not a weapon" Serdeo protested.

The river was high and calm, dirt banks barely held together by the roots of trees and grass that grew on them. It was a muddy brown making it impossible to tell how deep it was, but we had just walked through rainfall and it was much higher when we walked this path the other way.

"Have you got a plan?" I asked

"I can't wait for you in any town, I can't keep Dodellu waiting, this is important information."

Serdeo sat in thought. Kyroh approached him, and they discussed something in seaspeak. They talked for a while, and before they could reconvene with Lesliye and I the soldiers came sprinting back to us on horseback. 

"Hey!" A soldier began, "Our mages did not have the exact ingredients you requested, but they deemed the following enough to re-cast your golem once you were past the army, so that you can make the rest of your way to Naserich and get your special ingredients." he said to Serdeo.

The soldier dropped the ingredients into Serdeo's hands. Serdeo analysed them.

"These are not sufficient to recast my instance of the golem." Serdeo claimed. The soldiers clopped back a bit and discussed briefly amongst themselves.

"We will do a special offer for you travellers. You must recede your golem to pass through the army, and two of us will escort you with haste to Naserich. You will find two of our protections three times the equivalent of your spessartine golem, and then you can get your niche recasting ingredients in Naserich."

Serdeo stood still for a while. He shared a glance with Kyroh, then with me. I shrugged. He looked in my eyes for a long time.

"I accept these terms." Serdeo bellowed.

He walked up to Kyroh and placed his hands on him. After focussing for a while, Kyroh began to shrink under his clothes. First the sleeves flopped, and the trousers dropped. Kyroh was soon a pile of clothes on the road. Serdeo dug through the pile and picked up a fine-cut spessartine gemstone, careful square angles highlighting the naturally formed beauty. The gemstone made mesmerising patterns of light on the ground as Serdeo raised it from the pile of clothes. He gathered a hard container from Kodi and meticulously placed the gemstone inside of it. 

"Thank you." I said to Serdeo.

"Come on then." The soldeirs said.

The shouting of officers was heard long before we saw any of the bridges their commanded soldiers had built around the original hard stone one. Thousands of soldiers worked in a coordinated unison, cutting trees and assembling wood stacks long enough to fully cross the river. The original stone bridge was outstanding among the crude stick paths. Soldiers marched across the assortment of pontoons in their hundreds, the army ahead and behind seemingly spread out across the fields and the forestry. The scouts made passive gestures towards the working soldiery. We went across the main stone bridge and it felt like a privilege to walk across something so sound when one of their makeshift bundles of sticks fell into the river as soldiers crossed it. Engineers looked at the soldiers treading the river's high waters disappointed, shaking their heads as the swimmers frantically tried to make contact with the river's edges. 

Once we were past this rabble, true to word, two of the scouts carried on with us. We walked twice as fast as we usually would and rested not nearly long enough in our way stops in towns. The two scouts with us wisely took off their diamond ensigns, but they still collected a few sneers from the townsfolk. They didn't even dare go near Nasarch, deeming us to be "close enough". 

When Serdeo was sure they had gone, he scurried to retrieve the solid box he had placed Kyroh's spessartine gemstone into. He rummaged to retrieve two sealed paper packages, one of which he placed back into Kodi's pack. He was fast but careful, extracting the gemstone from its casing and sitting down to perform the cast. Gemstone in one hand and ingredient pack in the other. He brought his hands together in a tightly managed clap and Kyroh's spessartine form emerged from the gemstone.

Kyroh looked at Serdeo.
