# Lesliye Thenxal
He tore my sleeve off in the hot searing sun. My newly exposed forearm burned, a raw scorching pain on the body I had made for myself. 

“What are you hiding, Les-ril? Come on, show me!” His words sliced into me.

I'd been secluded my entire life taking up as little space as possible. I knew my place in the village, and that was in my house. I tried not to get in the way, not to gather any suspicion. But I see the flaw in that now. I didn’t eat, and I wasn't seen eating. No-one had ever seen me get food for myself.

I never contributed anything to the village either. I never gave help, said a word, or even appeared. I just took up the home, thinking that hiding would keep me safe. I had plenty of people knock on the door to check if I was still there, or if they forgot what that building there was for. Maybe if I had opened up, not been as afraid to go outside, I’d have been remembered more.

“Show me Lesliye! Show me what you’re hiding!” he tossed my torn sleeve to the ground and shoved me hard into the wall of my house.

I think another mistake I made was not making any friends. I never talked to people, I couldn’t build up that support network, someone who could anchor me in the community. 

But I had someone. Once.

They kept knocking on my door, day-after-day. I ignored it at first. It annoyed me. But then they started asking questions through the door. Questions about me, and how I was. I didn't respond to those either, but eventually I surprised myself by answering. After a week, we, we started talking about the world, its wonders, its horrors. 

The attacker pressed me harder into the wall, his eyes furious and narrow in suspicion. He yelled another taunt, but I wasn't paying attention. All I could do was remember her, the person at the door.

Eventually, I warmed up to them. One day, when she knocked, I opened the door. Underneath the sombre grey sky stood my unsurprised door knocker. She wore a colourful and playful dress, and her smile was like the sun. Eleurin was her name. We went on walks together and talked for hours

On one day, we sat on a fallen log by a creek. I felt so comfortable there and with her. I confessed to her that I didn't know how I'd come into this world. My earliest memory was sitting alone here by this log 19 years ago. It was a memory that weighed on me. I was sad. A darkness that wasn't just a feeling, but a darkness that was me.

Back in the harsh sun, my exposed forearm mottled purple like a rash. My attacker's face twisted with hate as he saw it

“A Calapeth! I should’ve known!” he said, pulling an imbued knife from his pocket.

“Leave me alone.” I replied, tremor in my voice turning to steel. 

For a second his eyes widened in fear, and his grip loosened. In that moment I let go of the disguise I had worn for so long. I vanished, becoming a thick, dark mist that slid across the earth. I couldn't see, but I could feel his frustrations. His footsteps heavy as he stomped through my gaseous form, hitting nothing but the floor.

When his footsteps grew faint I reformed. Not into the Lemuz figure I had used for the past decade of my life but into what I truly was. A Calapeth, a ghost-like being. A dark absence that light struggled to define.

He was there waiting. He lunged, his knife grazing the outer edge of my shadowed form. But he was confused by my body and the knife wouldn't harm me. I took the chance to run faster than he could hope to follow. In moments I was back in the forest. I found my way to the log by the creek. I remembered that moment.

*And I cried.*
*Cried,*
*Until it became night.*

I couldn't stay alone. I needed a friend. I needed an Eleurin.

I wiped my face and looked around. I needed to change my face for my new life. I shifted my form, moulding my appearance into how I remember Eleurin to look: bright, open, and a little strange. I wouldn't know where she was, but I'd carry her memory as a guide. A reminder of the kindness she'd shown. If someone recognised me, maybe they could point me to where she was. 

This time, I couldn't make the same mistakes. I'd be talkative. I'd find ways to contribute. I wouldn't hide, I'd become part of something.

# Serdeo Aufenyth
I put my gloves on. I inspected the ingredients on my table one last time. The right side of the table was a spessartine gemstone, my fifty-fifth. Cut to a specific perfection. Polished until it was worth a hundred blocks of Haer. The left side of the table contained a mix of ingredients. Ipexa’s Mushroom, a thin and tall Vugaix variety. Red-Gold kelp from the Danmyr Shallows. A stone from a Vyach Safron, a large and dangerous shark-like creature. And a specially blended mix of plant-based ingredients the formula of which I had only arrived through time consuming trial-and-error.

I picked up the gemstone. I picked up the ingredients. I turned around, there was plenty of room in this field. So, I brought my hands to a clap.

Immediately, bright orange light shone from my hands.  Feeling the gemstone grow between my palms was the sign I had to drop it. It fell to the floor, faces from its marvellously cut area shooting outwards. The faces built themselves up, suddenly forming the torso of the golem, then the limbs, and the head.

 It stood in front of me, slightly taller than myself. The outside observer might say that it looked like any other Spessartine golem, but I could tell the difference. After casting hundreds, you pick up on the differences between the structures of certain gemstones. You realise that each golem has a sort of personality, you can tell in the way it conducts itself.

 The golem turned around and looked at me. Most gem golems I cast, they understand what I want them to do. It’s unfortunate, really. They have their own sort of personality but they’re beholden to you through some means of casting. Not this one though.

 It looked at its hands. It realised each one had five fingers. I raised up my own, showing it the same number of fingers. I waved my hand at it. I greeted it.

 “Hello, magnificent!”

 “E-ull- ouh.” The golem responded.

I wasn’t prepared for this. I’d been working up to it for the last ten years of my life, and not once did I truly grasp what I was trying to achieve. A new life in this world, created by my own hands! I mean, people cast gem golems all the time. They’re useful for work, protection, but this one? This one’s sentient. This one just self-actualised. This one spoke Seaspeak.

I’m forgetting about childbirth. People make new people all the time. But this isn’t that, this isn’t a screaming child. Although, They’re probably clueless, in search of answers. I suppose that makes me the father? Does that mean I get to nurture them? Watch them come to understand the world? Oh, what an exciting prospect, and good research project too. Not to downplay my own achievement here, but sure the golem has realised itself. Now the real test is to see if they can become a person. Can they talk meaningfully? Do they have their own opinions? Do they have a name?

More importantly, how am I going to teach it Seaspeak?

I offered them my hand, and they, confused, grabbed it with theirs. The crystalline hard structure wasn’t the most comfortable for me to hold on to, but nevertheless I walked with them towards my personal accommodations. It looked around with its eyeless head, taking in the strange campus surroundings. I passed a few colleagues on the way there. Those which I don’t talk to often looked at me with confusion. Those I did talk to looked at me sternly.

I sat the golem down in a chair. It took a while for it to fully understand the concept of a chair, but it got there after watching me sit down first. Unsure of where to go, I started talking to it. I made a few gestures, saying my name and gesturing towards myself. It repeated what I was saying after me, but always in some accent. They said my name like ‘Ehr Eiho’, a chair was said like ‘air’, the table was ‘eifle’. Still, it seemingly understood the concepts.

Then, I gestured towards myself and said “me, Serdeo. You?” and repeated that gesture until they got what I was trying to catch onto. They repeated after me a couple of times, then got hung up on the word ‘you’, which they began to pronounce perfectly. they paused for a moment.

“k-“ they began, “Kee oh”.

“Keo?”

“Kyroh.”

I was overjoyed. I could barely think about what this meant for gemstones everywhere. What was this? A gemstone’s limited consciousness unleashed showing the living person contained within? Merely the concatenation of my own intense work to bring a gemstone to life, using the most specific of ingredients to create a person where there was none before?

Then, there was a knock on the door. Sheepishly, I got up and answered. Kyroh stood up with me, but I asked them to sit back down. The knock happened again. I walked over to the door and opened it, and looking at me was the leader of the institution.

“I’ve heard you’ve chosen to go against our best recommendations…” she said, peering indoors and at Kyroh, “and I see it too.”

“I just don’t get it. We’re supposed to be a research institute! Why are we so backwards in our thinking? Don’t you see what I’ve discovered here! This could completely change the way we think. A new revolution in Hidol research!”

“It’s unnatural. You can’t give a gem golem personhood, and you shouldn’t. A gemstone is an object, a pretty thing you look at. It’s a tool for aesthetics, and golems are a tool for labour. Nothing more.”

“That’s all just talk. Look at what I’ve actually done here! Tool or not, they can speak, and they have a name! It’s Kyroh! It has a name, Yurethin!”

“I don’t care what name you’ve given it, Aufeneth. We’ve warned you not to pursue this research and you did so anyway. We can’t tolerate this petty rebellion against the Institute. We’re revoking your membership and blacklisting you. I hope you can find comfort when your gemstone recedes.”

“I…”

“Leave it, our minds were made up years ago, and it seems yours was too. Goodbye, Serdeo Aufeneth. I hope we never meet again.”

She closed the door.

Silence.

I slumbered back to the chair opposite Kyroh. Depressed, I looked at them. Their emotionless crystalline face wasn’t even capable of staring back. They gently raised their arm, and comfortingly stroked my leg.

I had no reason to stay in Easa. Over the next month, I sold everything I didn’t need, took what I could with me and a pack mule I decided to call Kodi. Then I left. I said goodbye to Easa on a boat to mainland Darie, wondering where I would find myself. I worried a bit about Kyroh’s life, I had enough ingredients to bring them back if they did recede, but I was dead scared that they wouldn’t retain any memories.

Kyroh would eventually recede during an encounter with a Aredvic, but I brought them back without issue, and their memories remained, easing tension on my soul, and as always raising more questions.

# Arkha Vthnieolksh
The first thing you have to understand about me is that I was born in northern Neble. The culture there, it’s different. It’s warlike. Its angry. At an early age, you’re forced to kill something. Usually it’s something small, like a rabbit. I’ve always felt wrong doing it, but the cheers of your friends and family drown out any doubts you might have.

Out there on the front lines, fighting our neighbours in a ritual battle of aggression we do for no apparent reason, sympathising with the enemy makes you feel like an outcast. It makes you feel wrong. We aren’t even given a proper reason to fight, it’s just ‘in our culture’ and ‘we’ve been doing it for centuries’. When you don’t feel right doing it, you don’t come to the conclusion that there’s something wrong with the system. You can't. Instead, you think that there’s something wrong with you.

I was sitting in a tent surrounded by the people I had to call my friends. I had been around bloodied steel the entire day, I needed fresh air. Leaving the tent, I saw a strange glowing light from over a hill. It wasn't a large light, just a small and round speck at the peak of the hill. Fascinated, I walked toward it.

The light flew behind the hill. I pursued it further. I soon reached the peak of the hill, and the light was in clear view. It wasn't any light, it was a small statuette. A Demon of Suffering, emitting an angry dark red light. 

I knew what it was and what they do. Every person is taught stories of them as children and made aware of their deeds and activities. But I couldn't turn away. The demon's presence felt no different than the air of my camp.

"There's something wrong with you" the statuette said in a deep and soothing voice. I tried to ignore it.

"You don't find it fun." it accused. 

I started to walk away

“I can make it fun.” 

I knew I shouldn’t have listened to it. A horrible decision, but you can’t drift upstream.

“Come closer, I will make it fun.

I walked closer to the statue. I could feel this deep volume emanating from it., like the air itself was pulsating back and forth. I extended my hand out to grab the statue.

The red light became solid. Massive cloth-like tendrils shot out from the statue, curving toward me. The statue was unravelled like it was a ball of cloth until all that remained was the dark clothen tendrils. They flew towards me piercing my skin. Hitting my head. Flying through my chest, my arms, my legs. I could feel a rumbling from within me. A deep sensation, both relaxing and painful. 

And then it stopped. I stood on the side of the hill, no statue, no demon, I barely felt different.

Next time I went on the battlefield, it was obvious something had changed. Before, I would have been reluctant to kill, it would make me uncomfortable inside. But now, it was a joy. Watching my sword glide through the feathers and skin of those I fought against. Seeing the screams of glory and pain from both sides, it all made me excited. I felt joy in killing. Joy splitting skulls in two. A joy watching those on the ground get trampled by the army’s masses.

The rest of the war was over so quickly for me. I had so much mindless fun. I expected to be praised for my ruthlessness. I was finally killing the rabbit without hesitation! I thought all of the camp would praise me, and that we would get along better.

None of this happened. Instead, the estranged people I called my friends looked at me in horror. As if there were a point where ruthlessness was too unkind. I wasn’t forgiving in spars. I was unfriendly and rough. I didn’t stop when I was asked to. I was abandoned by those I was supposed to call friends.

I had to reflect upon myself. These murderous urges. They weren’t me. Those good feelings that I got when someone else was hurt, what would the younger me think of that? I forgot about my values, I forgot that everyone in the equation was a person. I realised that these evil thoughts weren’t me, but a demon.

So, I fought the urges to cause harm. I abandoned Neble. The further away I got from causing harm the more tempted I was to do it. I have dark thoughts all the time. I imagine doing bad things to people, even though I don’t want to. But I try my best, but sometimes I fail to fight enough.

I’ve had to run from so many places because of an accident I caused. A temptation I couldn’t stop. I would say I’m used to it now, but I’ve got to be careful. I’ve got to stay a few steps away from harming anyone, or else I might slip and stab someone in the back.

# Meibin Bitric
I hid in the bushes watching two diacut machete their way through the forest, cleaving branches and twigs as they carved a path. I followed them, keeping track of their every transgression against this soil that was not theirs. My companion, Irkhet, was stalking along on their opposite side. We were waiting for the perfect time to strike.

Then, in an instant, one of the diacut tripped over a branch. Irkhet fired their crossbow. The bolt whizzed out of the bushes and into the other diacut’s armour, penetrating it and hitting the diacut in the leg. The diacut let out a short scream before reaching for their poleaxe. That’s when I lept out of my bush, leaves brushing past my face as I grabbed ingredients out of a quick pouch on my satchel. The diacut was facing where the bolt had been shot from. I clapped with the ingredients. I placed my hand against the diacut’s back armour, and a flow of viscous white liquid appeared from my hands and wrapped around the diacut’s armour. The acid ate away at the armour’s metal, revealing a torn garment underneath and some burnt skin. The diacut turned around as quickly as they could, swinging their poleaxe. I ducked underneath it. Irkhet leaped from the bushes with their spear, pinning the tripped diacut to the ground with a spear to his neck. The other diacut swung at me once more with their poleaxe. I drew my dagger and deflected the axe’s swing, before finessing it into the diacut’s face.

“Wait! Don’t kill me! I mean no harm!” the tripped and pinned diacut said to Irkhet in Cutsh.

“In all my time here I’ve never seen you take prisoners,” I said to Irkhet in Bharkent. The diacut was pleading.

“I don’t, but this time its different. The Elder wants for one of these, that’s what we’re doing.”

“You could have told me that, I might have killed both of them otherwise.”

I addressed the diacut, “We both know that’s not true.” I responded in Cutsh. The diacut let out a sigh. Irkhet tied the diacut’s hands. “Get up, now! You’re going to meet our elder.”

***

We approached Kakuvar, the village elder. A Ysgews, Codia’s greatest beings. Huge trees rooted deep into the ground connecting them with the rest of Codia. Their massive crescent-shaped horn-like branches each as thick as the trunk itself branching into the air. Either side of Kakuvar, the large warriors of Codia.

“Irkhet.” Kakuvar addressed, “A task completed.”

The warriors took the diacut prisoner from Irkhet and dragged them into a nearby grove-house.

“Meibin.” Kakuvar’s voice again, “I sense unease within you.”

“Yes, Kakuvar. You know I plan to leave tomorrow.”

“You have much to do outside of Codia, I am aware. Your presence has been more than felt in the roots, they thank your devotion and protection.”

“It’s been an honour and a duty.”

“For all you have given Codia, Codia returns. Accept these gifts.”

One of Kakuvar’s warriors approached me with a wooden pennant, the shape of which signified this village. I took the trinket. The warrior returned to Kakuvar’s side and picked up a potted sapling. The pot was about half a metre in diameter, and the sapling was small inside of it. The warrior approached me, and handed me the plant.

“What’s this?” I asked.

“In your future, in times of hardship, Codia will come to your aid. Carry this child Ews with you. Nurture it, and it will become an ally to your side, as you have been to Irkhet.”

I had no idea how I was going to carry this around.

“Where are you going first?” Irkhet asked.

“Barogate. Shildo. I have a lot I need to do there. I hope I can return to Codia someday.”

“Eswin lives in Ralean, and Raikeor in Ereso. Wear the pennant, and they will know of your service to Codia.” Kakuvar said, “Go now, have your final rest in the village.”

“Yes, Kakuvar.”

# Ethenri Nethyl-Vethonthye
The Nethyl-Vethonthye family is one of the three most wealthy Maeves families in Terlaye. Its roots go back centuries, to 3200 when most of Terlaye was under the Pri-Coner (Private Conqueror) companies of the Second Eresonian Empire. The north stayed true to the feudal monarchies that Terlaye defined the country since the fall of Nao Techia. The Kingdom of Ateimot was in rule during this time, worrying for its continued existence alongside the three other Terlayan kingdoms. The Kingdom of Ateimot ruled over its home city, Ateimot, but also had the city of Silenna under its rule.

My family’s legend tells of a rogue hero who, in defence of the King through guile and plot, earned their place as a vassal and ruler of one of Silenna’s districts. That hero was Harlea Vethonthye, who eventually married another powerful figure in Silenna solidifying her family, the Nethyl-Vethonthye family.

Throughout the next 50 years, when the Pri-Coner companies still controlled southern Terlaye, Harlea and her husband took pages out of the Pri-Coner books. They bought the means of production, purchased Silenna’s guilds with their land-control. Their children continued this trend, eventually making my family the most influential in the city, and arguably the most influential in the kingdom, except of course the King's dynasty.

The Eresonain Civil War affected Terlaye. At this time, Ereso was one of two crucial economic partners. Although the economic severance hurt some Terlayan kingdoms, the Kingdom of Ateimot made some de-jure territorial gains when southern Terlaye was released from the Eresonian Empire. Other kingdoms were newly established by tracing lineages, giving Naokeiot and Reumanas their own monarchs. During the civil war, my family did attempt some company-building in the distraught Ereso, but once the Councilists took over all their investment was ruined.

Other families started to take notice of the Nethyl-Vethonthye success and started to copy it. My family’s power was drowned out among every other family that came to rule their cities. There was a good few power struggles over Silenna itself, and the surrounding towns and farms. My family wouldn’t always come out on top either, territory was lost. But, the family’s legacy was great, and its prospects remained excellent.

Then tensions rose. The Reimig family got greedy. The Reimigs, of course, being the Emperor of Ralean’s family. After finishing off the Kingdom of Ithun, a southern Ralean kingdom that worked on the Terlayan feudal model, their interests turned onto Ralean’s bordering regions. The west was a lost cause: you can’t conquer a desert. The north equally so, the riverbank before the mushroom forest was liveable and there are a couple of Ralean towns there, but past that it’s the danger and mystery of the Vugaix. There’s also the Heabe, which the Reimigs tried to conquer before, but their colony became independent and Difmuz-controlled. The Aredvics displayed their ultimate control over Heabe. So, there was only one region for the Empire of Ralean to consider. Terlaye.

The War for Central Shildo happened only 80 years ago. Since, my family has retained their value of wealth and power over Silenna. The Empire’s system accommodated the power-structures already in place, so my family got to stay. But, with the forced economic weakening of Terlaye, my family and all others like it were weakened too.

Now, we get to me.

I am Ethenri, the fourth child of my parents, Ichecka and Levar, the heads of the Nethyl-Vethonthye dynasty. Levar, my father, married Ichecka out of love, and also as a bond between our great families of Terlaye. Being the fourth child, I have no hopes to be the leader of the family, save my two sisters and brother suddenly become very ill.

I was raised in a loving family. They even remained accepting of the less fortunate friends I would make out of the working class of Silenna. We would adventure the surrounding lands, and that’s where I would find my calling.

I always felt a level of shame playing with people who were in the lower socio-economic class than I. Early on, I talked to my parents about it, and they suggested I hide my richer identity. In fact, they taught me to do so. I dressed like a commoner, talked like a commoner, and learned improper posture like a commoner as well. They decided on the family name I now use: Laelec.

My family wasn’t always supportive. There’s a little bit of shame that comes to having your high-class son only interact with commoners. It’s a jump to go from your children engaging in high-class society, participating in rich dinners, to a child running around in the forest with a wooden sword.

I sometimes wonder if it’s a method of disowning me. Giving me a different family name to work with, letting me go on my dangerous adventures. But in the end, it doesn’t matter. I still get to do what I want to do, and they remain supportive whenever I need them to be.

When I finally did find my adventuring group, I had my family allocate small amounts of money to helping me out on my adventure, which ends up being allocated to the party fund. Its what makes us a sponsored adventuring group, although the others don’t know who the sponsor is.

# Kyroh
I came to life one day. I’ve been told how, but I don’t really understand it. A couple plants and other ingredients just clapped together in someone’s hand is enough to make a new life. Still, here I am.

I’ve been nurtured and taught a little. Learning to speak was a bit of a challenge. I’ve had to learn three languages as well. Seaspeak was by far the hardest, not even because it was the first. Eresonian is the easiest, then I learnt Empiric which was a bit of a challenge, but I got there in the end.

My whole life I’ve just been following my caster around. Being a gem golem, people get a little weirded out when I act independently, or like a person. Even to see me wearing clothes is a little odd. I do have an outfit that hides my entire person, but the way I walk is different and noticeable, so I can’t avoid others being eerie about me.

I’m mostly useful in combat. I can create javelins at will, and I even have a pouch for them on my back. Creating them does take a lot of effort, I’ve discussed it with my caster Serdeo and apparently, it’s a lot like casting an enchantment. I can’t do it lots of times in a row, which is why I have a small store of them on me at any time.

That’s pretty much it. I’m Kyroh, a Spessartine golem that’s also a person.
