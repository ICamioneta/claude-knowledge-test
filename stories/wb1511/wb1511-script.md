Here's a video script and description for my three-act [wb1511](/wastelife/stories/wb1511/index) video.

# Act 1
Ideally, I would start the continuity by filming myself on a beach, probably burry beach because its just a nice part of my country. Perhaps I open with a thoughtful and artistic scene (perhaps even a little pretentious) of a video of myself on the beach and a voiceover along the line of:

" Have you ever built something just for yourself? something no one else could ever fully understand? Why do some of us pour ourselves into imaginary worlds, knowing that no one else may ever explore them? Is it escapism or a search for connection?

Then I would cut to a direct video of myself sitting on the beach with a book in my hand (maybe containing the script for easier non-digital access. It's thematic - I do all my writing on my laptop but I aint pulling that out on a sandy beach!)

" One day I was looking through the files of my worldbuilding project, Wastelife. I converted the project into markdown files for obsidian a while ago, and I knew there were still a few unconverted files hiding that obsidian wouldn't show. I was looking for my short stories when I saw a strangely named file. This was wb1511.txt, a story I don't remember writing and whose title means nothing. Let me read it out for you.

	"Hey, wait up! I want to talk to you."
	"ugh, what is it."

" A surface level reading of that story would see two characters, one oblivious and one knowledgeable, talking to each other about the basic lore of the world, one character's naivete acting as an excuse to explain basic worldbuilding information to the reader. But what if I told you that there was more to this?

# Act 2
I want to perform act 2 in Kidwelly Castle. Throughout the video, I'll likely flash maps on screen to show people what I'm talking about, and I wish I had more in depth images but that's just not something I can provide. The thematic significance is that the castle is supposed to reflect on the lore I'm explaining in act 2, and the medieval style the world has.

" The topic of the conversation is a lot deeper than it seems at first, but to fully understand its depths we need some information about where it takes place, the characters involved, and the current political and philosophical situation of the world. 

" I need to say that none of this analysis is baked into the text. I wrote this a long time ago, and I'm now analysing it with information I have that I gained outside of the text.

" No matter how you analyse it, this is a conversation about species of people. In Wastelife, a species of person is a species of subspecies that participate in person society. This is the ability to talk and communicate, living in towns, and other familiar things. The 'of person' bit is a descriptor on the capabilities of certain species. The species part is a recognition of their biological differences.

" Species of person are biologically distinct, they cannot reproduce with one another. Species in Wastelife are either naturally developed or specifically created by a god. Subspecies are biologically distinct but maintain a connection to the root of the species. Many species only have physical members of their subspecies, for example there is no Muz person. In these cases, it's more of a category of compatible and similar subspecies.

" All of this is common knowledge in Wastelife. Everyone knows it. While it is technically possible that the oblivious Kaemuz character in the story managed to avoid this information for their whole life, I think it's unlikely. Their naivete may instead be an intentional act to make a political and philosophical point to the Domuz character.

" In the text, the Domuz makes assumptions about where people are from based on their species. They assume the Kaemuz is from Ereso, and that the 'hairy people' and 'feathery people' are Ximuz and Dos from Kyeron or Anula. While their initial assumption about the Kaemuz is correct, the Kaemuz makes a point that it is not at all true for the others.

" So where did this idea come from?

" In Wastelife, certain species of person developed naturally in certain regions, or were originally placed in a certain region by a god. The Muz, for example, were initially placed somewhere in Shildo but later moved to other parts of the world and then, through a process of accelerated evolution, became the subspecies we are familiar with today. The Domuz naturally adapted to their massive island archipelago by developing hydrodynamic scales and webbed appendages, and the Kaemuz adapted to their challenging and varied subtropical climate by bulking up.

" But this is all a matter of cultural anthropology and the origin of species. In the modern age, its main relevance is in a majority of a certain subspecies in a certain region. People know this, so why does the Domuz make these assumptions?

" Cultures that stem from Domuz-majority societies, primarily found in Lale Darie, place a large emphasis on aquatic activities. These are ritual swimming, aquatic marriages, and even subaquatic settlements. Unlike basically every other culture, these cultures massively favour those it originates from, the Domuz. 

" Historically, non-aquatic participants of Domuz cultures have been supported by the society and culture, the individuals of which would supply those non-aquatic species with aid such as enchanted breathing equipment and specialised wetsuits. But to set up these supports, the culture necessitated the usage of species-based distinctions.

" This, in the modern colonial state of the Republic of the Domuz, has caused the idea of biological culturalism to emerge. It is a naturally emerging idea with no real malicious roots, but it is a dangerous idea. It is the belief that someone's species, their immutable biological elements, matters a lot in society. While this aspect is true and even important to Domuz cultures, this often includes the idea of a 'native region', which posits that where a species originally developed or where they were placed matters in a social context. This makes it an ultimately harmful idea where, with the right material incentives, could cause a lot of discrimination.

Pause to let that settle, and to make the shift in topic a bit easier on the ears.

" This conversation takes place on the island of Ceseison, one of the lesser populated islands in the Llynllanes island archipelago, which is part of the larger Raidal island chain which sits here in relation to the wider world, west of Neble and south-west of Ereso.

" I haven't actually drawn all of the islands in this overview, only the ones I know are there. There's definitely more here.

" Hanan is one of the larger towns on Ceseison, and is a bit of a cultural mixing hub. The scene takes place on a beach a short walk south of Hanan.

" Acadym is another island in Llynllanes, the larget and most populated one. Reneinil is a town on Acadym. Since the islands are relatively small these two towns, Hanan and Reneinil, are quite close together.

" So this conversation being between a Domuz and a Kaemuz is no irregular thing. It's a common cultural melting pot for Domuz politically connected to the Domuz Republic, and the Kaemuz societally connected to the region where they are most demographically popular.

" This isn't the end of the non-coincidences. The geographical region Ereso is primarily controlled by the political body Eresonia, which follows egalitarian Councilist organisational principles. These egalitarian ideals directly conflict with the Domuz Republic's colonial nationalism. These two nations are separated from their conflict by the massive rainforest of Kyeron, but Llynllanes and Baron Gazi places them much closer together.

" So in this analysis, I propose that the Kaemuz is actually trying to make a distinct political and philosophical point to the Domuz. They may recognise the ideas of biological culturalism, and while their obliviousness at first is likely a playful act, later on it is used to convey a philosophical point to the Domuz. The point being that they shouldn't be making these assumptions based on biology. An attempts to dissolve the Domuz' ideology by means of contradiction. 

" The Kaemuz notices that the Domuz made an assumption about their place of birth based on their species, and to combat this brings up examples from their past where the person's species' native region is not where they were born. While the Domuz insists that their families eventually came from these regions, this is not really relevant.

" The text ends with the Domuz saying they are from Reneinil. There is some ambiguity in what this actually means, whether the Domuz moved to the town from Lale Darie or whether they were born in the town or on the island. I personally would like to assume that the Domuz was actually born in Reneinil, punching more inconsistencies into their world outlook.

" So there you have it. The text is actually about a Kaemuz acting oblivious to make a political point to the Domuz.

# Act 3
I don't know where to do Act 3, but I want it to be in a sort of studio-looking bedroom to reflect the personal nature of act 3. There would have to be a long pause between act 2 and act 3 to solidify the difference between the acts. It would start.

" If you've stayed with me this long, you might start to wonder where the original question of the video went. Well, that was a cheeky hook for this, the analysis of my analysis. You see, reading and recontextualising that text, wb1511, highlighted an issue I, and no doubt other people have, with worldbuilding.

" There is a high chance that the original intent of the author was not at all this deep political and philosophical intrigue. It's more likely that the younger and less attractive version of myself just wrote a story on a whim. Through my modern lens, through exploring and learning about my own setting, I have managed to reframe this old and simple work into something so much larger than it is. 

" It is pretty unlikely that anyone else ever will be able to produce this analysis of the text because it relies on an understanding of the Wastelife worldbuilding. This understanding, as of now, is something I alone possess. While it is possibel for another individual to interact with the worldbuilding of Wastelife and its stories enough to get the same levels of understanding necessary to make the same analysis, the chances that my creative world will get that popular is miniscule.

" This is all in ignorance of how, no matter who gets involved in Wastelife, I will always be the solitary creator. The solitude of a creator is a permanent aspect of deep creativity. Even if someone was by my side, reading everything I wrote, engaging in every activity I could conceivably host, only I can ever understand the world in its entirety.

" There is a near zero chance that my world gains a Lord of the Rings level of fame. I, and many other worldbuilders, have accepted this. I don't world build for fame, recognition, or even individual merit. I world build because I enjoy the process, and I find it deeply fulfilling. There is something uniquely satisfying about my ability to provide a natural analysis of a pre-exiting text based on the context of the world alone. My ability to create such an analysis without adding any information to the world is a testament to the growth of the world, and my growth as an individual.

" Worldbuilding is an insular hobby. I have tried to rope my friends into Wastelife. I really have, but these have not been a success. This ties into the personal aspects of world building. Part of why people world build is to get lost in a fictional world where they have ultimate control. One where they can freely express themselves, and explore endless possibilities. The difference between that and what Wastelife, and probably some other worlds, is that they have become larger than this.

" Wastelife as a worldbuilding project isn't actually my primary worldbuilding project. I actually only world build a world called Xeiat. It is a realistic world, with a development of history mirroring our own but with unique geography and cultures. Wastelife is, actually, a popular multimedia franchise within this realistic world of Xeiat. It's a sort of multi-layered approach to worldbuilding. 

" My understanding of Xeiat and Wastelife is truly larger than my understanding of our real world. This is essentially the point of worldbuilding, to create a safe world where everything is in your control and you can know anything. But what make Xeiat and Wastelife different to this is how it's become as large, as complicated and vast, as the world I physically inhabit.

" Through establishing the worlds in material reality, through applying realistic developmental processes to the world, the potential of information stored within the premises of the worlds matches that of our real world. In developing Wastelife, it alone has enough foundation, enough complex and scientifically understood phenomenon, and enough interpersonal connection contained within its idea that it has become as full of potential as the real world.

" But I could never discover enough about these worlds. The sum of my knowledge about Xeiat, Wastelife, and everything I know about the real world is less than the sum of knowledge many individuals have on the real world alone. My ability to discover these worlds is humanly limited. It is inconceivable that I will discover enough of Xeiat and Wastelife to make the quantity of knowledge about Xeiat in the real human population match the quantity of knowledge that the real human population has on the real world.

" But to the people in Wastelife, that is their real world. Someone in Wastelife, perhaps someone obsessed with political analysis or a philosopher, knows enough about their own world to make the same analysis if they were given the text.

" And to the people of Xeiat, someone would be enough into Wastelife to have learnt all about its lore. Provided the text for the first time, they might make the same analysis as me.

" I wonder is my multi-layered approach to worldbuilding a reflection of my deep internalised loneliness? Is it not enough for me to create an entire fantasy world, whose potential for information is so fast? A world I love to explore so much with the characters within it, is it not enough?

" Is it then enough to have created an entire larger world. A realistic world where people are more or less the same. A world where my fantasies are popular media franchises, and people enjoy them as thoroughly as I do?

" Is it a reflection on my deep internalised loneliness that I have created a whole world of 7.247 billion people where around 50 million of them have purchased the Wastelife equivalent of the Silmarillion and at the very least 500 thousand of those people have an understanding of Wastelife lore on par or greater than my current own, so that I may not only fantasise the world of Wastelife but fantasise the connections I would have with those people?

" Maybe it is that. Maybe it is enough. Maybe.

" Maybe I should find a Xeiat therapist to give me Xeiat therapy

" Or maybe the act of worldbuilding, to imagine that therapist, is therapy within itself.

pause again

" Have you ever created a world, or anything, so deeply personal that you have to wonder if anyone could ever understand it? Do you wonder if they could ever understand you?

" Thanks for your time.
