# The White Sprite
I was wandering with my group, our heavy shoes clunking along the cobbled road when Guiese, one of our more perceptive members, spotted a dot of white rock among a pine wall. It became apparent that the white stone was the wall of a small and quiet waystation and inn with a few seats outside. Its construct was that of bright white stone, with pine beams crossing all over the structure supporting a second floor and a flat roof. This architecture was Eresonian, old and decrepit white blocks piling up to be its walls. I identified a carving on its wall, permanently cursing the Eresonian blocks with the tavern’s name, the White Sprite.

My group nudged me forwards; I was the talkative one. Although Guiese’s eye may be far, mine are wide. I can notice things around me easily, the clashing of mugs as two Danmuz end a good round of False Pretence. Some arbitrary chatting about politics. The loud sipping of White Beer out of a thick glass cup. The boisterous callings of some drunkard asking for more. In the crowd of the tavern, it’s the people that stay silent that I look for, like the old man sitting at the counter keeping to himself. He must have a story.

“Good evening old chap! Mind if I sit for a chat?”

The old man lethargically gestured towards the seat next to him. I sat energetically on the seat, and pulled it closer towards him.

“You seem like you could do with a few more hours of life! Come on, what’s on your roof?”

“It’s,.. ” the man paused for a second, “It’s my son.”

“Hm?”

“I used to see him every day right here in this inn. He works on the roads, he used to maintain this road. A week ago he stopped coming. I searched the entire length of the path and all around it but I couldn’t find him. He would have told me if he was leaving.”

Missing people is a regular job for my band. Often, they’ve gotten stuck down a hole or in a cave when no-one was around. We get lost pets quite often too, one of the many disinteresting activities we do. Our free labour is often spent on some mindless tasks like getting ingredients for a mage. Sometimes, this can lead to more interesting encounters. Perhaps with wildlife, or some bandits trying to get the better of us. But usually the most interesting part is talking to the client.

“Missing, you say? I doubt he could have ran away.”

“It’s the fort!” he frustrated, hitting the bar’s counter, drawing a few strange looks.

“A fort? The Empire controlled type or the bandit infested variety?”

“There’s a fort half a march away just off the road to Serven. People say it’s infested with ghosts, old soldiers that died there. They say it was some failed hidology experiment to turn people immortal. I don’t see the truth in it, but what else is there to believe?” He looked down, eyes almost tearing up.

“An abandoned fort? What makes you think he’s there?”

“A girl. She went missing a while ago from the town, they hung papers up and I remember seeing her in the distance at the fort’s blockade. She wasn’t herself. Her movements didn’t seem human, and she never saw me. Something’s happening in there. Ghosts may not be real but that fort is under a veil.”

“Abandoned forts are never as they seem.”

A fort’s a bird’s nest of adventure! You’d think that most of what we do is delve headfirst into danger, scavenging ancient caverns and questing to faraway lands. While we do get those adventures from time to time, most of what we’ve been doing are lazy jobs like searching fields for a lost dog or some basic courier’s work to get a good meal. It’s not as consistently exciting as most people think, but every now and then we get an interesting destination such as this fort.

Abandoned forts don’t exist, whenever the last inhabitants disappear, be it the Empire leaving or an infestation cleared, they’re claimed by the fastest fish. Often, they like to hide the fact that it’s claimed, which is usually the case with bandits. I’ve heard some different stories that tell of a hidden order of mages operating using what was thought to be an abandoned fort, and even ones of golem infestations.

“I’m part of an adventuring group, and I’ll build your bridge. We’ll go and check out the fort, see what we can find.”

“Really?” the man seemed cheerier, but still had a tone of sadness in him, “You’d do that? I’d go there myself but my legs ache every step I take.”

“Yes, I’ll go. which way was this fort again?”

“Half a march on the road to Serven, you’ll see the blockade. Thank you, and don’t get yourself killed for this.” He took a large sip of his beer.

“How about that girl?”

“Oh yes, she was tall for a Lemuz, and well-fed with thick eyebrows.”

# My Group
I dodged the crowd wand exited the tavern, my group was standing a fair length away on the other side of the road, only conversing among themselves. They seemed to read the expression on my face, and were eager to discover what interesting quest I had scoped out.

“What you got for us, cheery?” Guiese asked.

“Oh, nothing special, just a lost son and a fort!” Their eyes widened.

“I hope it’s not like the last one…” Taiene said, horrific images plaguing her mind.

“The murder cult? It can’t be that bad.” Guiese mentioned.

“I’m still having nightmares about that prison cell.” She said.

“I’m still having nightmares about your vomit!” I replied, not the brightest brick in my brain.

“You must be glad to have someone strong enough to carry you after you fainted.” Lili remarked.  Once again, not my proudest moment.

What my party lacks in public conversation they more than make up for in skill and strength. Guiese alone could stop a charging bull, mostly by charging back at it. He can start a charge with as much force as everyone else can give for the entire length of the battle. That’s not to say that everyone else is lacking, we need our ranged fighters Lili and Taiene. Taiene tends to use claps, the ranged variety more often than not. She’s a minimalist, her mage’s pouch more organised than a king’s bedroom, and barely large enough to carry two apples. Lili’s an archer, her arms are undoubtedly the strongest of the group. Her bow punches with more force than a javelin, I’ve seen her hit hands through shields. Every one of us have tried to draw her bow, and each of us struggled to fire a single shot. Istwy is our other close-ranged fighter, he’s a mage like Taiene. He’s got a sizeable pouch stuffed with ingredients of all kinds. He tends to use touch claps for damage, but he’s quite a generalist too. One other important thing about Istwy, he’s a Sosophax.

“So where’s this fort?” asked Lili, the group following on their way.

“The man said half march along the road to Serven, there’s a blockade.”

“Half a march! That’s so far…” Taiene whined, stumbling forwards for a bit.

“The walk will be good for you!” Lili said, slapping Taiene on the back.

“You’ve been on longer walks before.” mentioned Guiese.

“But we barely spent any time at that tavern!”

“We don’t have the time to spend. You don’t go into taverns anyway.” I said.

“If it’s anything like the last time, he was dead a week ago.” Commented Guiese.

Taiene winced.

“You can’t say that! How many murder cults could be in Terlaye?” asked Taiene, who winced once more at a thought.

“I bet five Pheg on bandits.” Lili intervened.

“Five on cannibals!” Guiese added, Taiene looked disgusted.

“Five on illegal hidists!” I proposed.

Our shoes clunked on the well-made stone pathway. The ground on the horizon was covered in a thick sludge of honey. The road was a corridor, the trees invading our flanks were merciful enough to leave a hedge of flowers between the road and themselves. We walked through flies, warm in our gambesons.

Taiene plucked a white flower from a bed of surprisingly damp plants. As she pulled the top from the stalk, a white spurt of liquid sprayed from where the plant tore. She brought the plant up to her mouth and sucked on the end, where the plant was still oozing white liquids. Her face appeared repelled, and then kept the flower in her hand, picking more up along the way.

“You see that?” Lili started, gesturing towards Taiene’s actions, “I think it’s Norbel Anser, quite similar to Norbel Sevet, but the taste is different and it’s torfol properties are useful.”

“Someone’s keeping up on their local ingredient knowledge!” Taiene shouted backwards.

Istwy gave a look. I can’t read a Phax, but Taiene looked back and seemed to understand it.

“I’m going to smell them!” Taiene shouted.

I’ve never understood Istwy. He’s been in this group for several years, ever since we went to Upper Ralean. He doesn’t speak much. He told us that no Phax spoke often. Those were the only words he said that week. We don’t treat him differently because of this, we don’t push him into talking or situations where he has to. We just let him tag along, talk when he wants. Taiene seems to have this odd connection with Istwy. Maybe it’s because they’re both mages, but she can always tell what he’s thinking.

# Encounter on the Road
Guiese pointed out a caravan coming our way further along the road. His eyes might be far, but the reason no-one else noticed it was because our eyes were pinned to the road beneath like a fly stuck in amber. The cart’s noise soon joined the scorching road, the clatter of the metal it must have been carrying soon invaded our thoughts. It was not a gentle sound, like the rushing of a stream. It was cruel, it was violent.

We passed the caravan. There was a good number of people, some outfitted well with rich linen green garbs, clearly from Ralean. The others were guards, local by the looks of things. You can tell because of their breastplates; they have this circular pattern by the collarbone. That’s the mark of a smith in Serven called Rova. The locals call Rova legendary, but I’d say there are far better smiths down south.

Once the caravan had passed, we heard a metallic approach of feet rushing towards us from behind. We turned around quickly, and it was one of the caravan guards. He spoke to Guiese initially, people always do. He has the same weaponry as me, but his armoured shoulder pads and his fine moustache give him a leader’s look. He wears a gambeson like the rest of us, but his legs have iron plates sewn on.

“Hey! You!” the guard said. Guiese looked especially worried, as if he’d accidentally stolen something from the caravan.

“Yes?” I interjected, stepping between him and Guiese.

“I wanted to drop a leaf and say that we lost one of our men somewhere along the road.”

“Oh?”

“We don’t know where but we recently counted that we only had nine people, but we left with ten. We couldn’t stop to look for him, we’re late on the delivery as it is, so if you see him along the road could you apologise to him on our behalf?”

“He’ll have to run to catch you up!” I joked.

“Thanks, in advance.” The guard ran off back to the caravan, which was quite some way away already.

A man gone missing along the road. How do you not notice that? If you’re running late then you wouldn’t let him wander off into the woods. At that moment I saw only a few possibilities of what could have happened to him, possibilities that had to be discreet and silent.

“A lost caravaner, a lost boy, a lost girl.” Lili brought up, “I’m beginning to see a pattern”.

“Cannibals seems more likely now” Guiese mentioned.

“I hope not.” Taiene told.

“I should have made a bet on slavers!” Lili added.

“No, it can’t be that. They wouldn’t have taken on a fully armed guard.” I said.

“What is it then? Another murder-cult?” Lili laughed.

“No, the murder cult was that one time!” Taiene instantly objected, Lili smiled.

I saw something move among the bushes. It wasn’t fast, it didn’t move much. I could tell it was almost limping through the trees. I couldn’t see what it was exactly, only how it moved the bushes, how they waved differently than those affected only by the soft breeze. Something was there.

I stopped in my tracks, without saying anything everyone knew I had a raised guard. Taiene  and Istwy opened their satchels, Lili knocked an arrow, Guiese drew his sword. I didn’t have to say anything, but by looking at the bushes everyone else seemed to realise there was something there. It was hard to make out, and we couldn’t jump to any conclusions, so I shouted aloud.

“Who’s treading the grass?! Show yourself, you’re walking the banks here. We have arrows knocked, and if you don’t come out, we’re going to test the waters.”

Silence came from the bushes. Still, the clear patterns in how the leaves moved continued

“Right, you’ve had your warning. Shout now or you’re getting shot.” I gave Lili a hand signal, telling her that she should shoot her bow.

Lili drew her arrow back. It slid against the body of the bow, the stretch of the string stressing backward. Then, when the body of the bow seemed it should snap, she let go. The arrow slid through the air, barely making a sound. It hit something in the trees with a flap, followed by what was clearly the sound of a body kissing the dirt.

We were slightly shaken by this. We’d imagined that in this situation whatever was there would surrender, or at the very least charge at us through the bushes, or maybe rustle away. I approached the bushes, Guiese and Istwy on my flanks. I drew my shortsword, gripping its firm leather handle to peel back the branches, and to our surprise we saw a dead body. A rotten body, with the thick stench of death, a few flies buzzing around and skin turning to dust, with Lili’s fresh arrow embedded in its skull.

I spotted a sleeping man not far behind where the oddly dead body was. He was far in the greenery, and I wouldn’t have noticed him if not for a strange sound played by his breathing and a well-placed leaf. With the help of Guiese, I carefully cut the bushes around him and lifted his armoured body out of the crinkling branches and on to the road, where it was clear that this man was wearing the same guard outfit as the caravan that had rattled past a long time ago. In the heat, Taiene prepared a small potion and shoved it down the man’s mouth. She said it was meant to “restart him”, and with a few coughs and the smell of bad breath, the man sat up from the ground instantly.

“What?” the guard exclaimed slowly with a worry.

“Hey, we found you knocked out among the bushes.” I explained.

“Really? Well, I suppose I should get my bearings. I was doing what?” He asked, clearly confused.

“Listen, you were guarding a caravan. It’s probably more than halfway to the city already! Go and chase it, tell them what I did.”

“Right, yes, I’ll do that then.” He said, clearly taken aback by his aggressive slumber.

The guard jogged away with that familiar clunking. Lili looked strangely at him as he sweated off into the distance. We continued treading, making our own noises forwards across this pine-lined path.

“I wonder why” Lili broke the dumbfounded silence.

“Makes you think. Maybe it was a secret wizard who feigned his death, but his enchantment only did old bodies” I replied.

“That seems convoluted. I know I shot straight, so maybe it was a bandit carrying a dead body to act as his body double should they run into danger.” Lili added

“And that isn’t complicated! I suppose it rules out cannibals. I saw the flesh on that poor soul.”

“Or, maybe, that had nothing to do with the lost people.” Taiene said.

# Rest by the Bridge
“How far do you think the fort is?” Taiene asked tiredly.

“It can’t be far. It’s dusk now; half a march won’t be much further.”

“Hey look! A bridge!” Taiene said, pointing off in the distance “Can we rest there for a while?”

I looked to the west, the tall trees like a city wall blocking the sun’s arrows. “We can, but not for too long.”

Taiene walked with energy and motivation. Knowing Taiene, it was understandable that she wanted to rest at a river. She’s a local Terlayan. She and Istwy often sit by rivers picking up what I’d assume are ingredients for their claps. Fishes and weeds.

The bridge came closer as we walked in the steep shade of the trees. The air was warm; there was a calm breeze that patrolled the road’s clearing in the trees. The bridge wasn’t far ahead. It was a small and quaint stone bridge, hopping over the small stream that otherwise cut the road. The river itself was more of a deep stream, only a couple few paces wide. It ducked, twisted, and turned to avoid the sentinel trees.

Taiene looked from atop the bridge at the river. She checked east and west, then decided to come down from the bridge and walk downstream. Then, she sat at assumedly the best part of the stream nearby. Istwy and Lili walked over to sit next to her, while Guiese and I sat a few paces back, closer to the trees. Then, true to my expectations, Taiene pointed into the river and Istwy reached in and impaled a fish.

“That’s a Nater’s Fiend, right?” Asked Lili.

“Close, but no. It’s Caesi’s Spitting Salmon. See this line in the scales?” Taiene explained, “You wouldn’t find Nater’s Fiend here anyway, we’re marches away from Nater’s River.”

Taiene reached into her satchel and rustled up a sheet of cloth-like paper. She placed it on her lap, spreading it out neatly. Istwy presented the fish to Taiene, still impaled on his tail’s spike. She slowly reached to her side and carefully slid the pierced fish from Istwy’s tail. She placed it meticulously on the paper on her lap, and wrapped it up, folding over every hole. She put the fish in the top of her satchel.

Istwy leaned forwards into the river, almost falling in as if he were looking for a piece of jewellery. Then, he reached into the river once more with his tail. This time he brought up some sea leaves, weeds even. Taiene looked at this catch, and she seemed surprised. We didn’t wait much longer after that. Taiene decided to unwrap the fish and bring a knife to it just before I said we should get moving again.

# The Blockade
The stone bridge was smooth and tall enough to lift me up just enough to see some stone defences off in the distance. I could tell that they were of Empire origin even from this distance. The flat sides were recognisable. As we walked closer to it, a tall stone wall enclosed the road. The road felt more like a corridor in a building than a road in the middle of the wilderness. Once we had reached it, the gatehouse was much smaller than I’d thought from a distance. It only had two stories

Two relatively small twin buildings stood either side of the road. The products of masterful stonecutting were slowly succumbing to the elements; I could see that the corners were broken and the whitewash diminished. The buildings had an extra floor, which overlooked the road with small holes from which an archer could fire from. In the centre of these two buildings shot a wooden corridor connecting the two, going over the road. Underneath that was two open gates, made from finely cut logs with iron bracing.

“Is this it?” Guiese sounded, unimpressed.

“No, it’s not an Empire fort, if that’s what you mean.” I told him.

“So where is it?” Taiene said, disappointed.

“There should be a door to this gatehouse somewhere, then a path leading up to the fort.” I said, but there was no entrance in sight.

“I’d say…”  I walked over to where one of the open gates touched the wall.

“Hap’s luck!” I said, reaching my hands out and touching the warm iron bracings of the gate, “There it is.”

As the gate on the east tower swung open, it revealed another door behind it, attached to the wall. I went to try for the door’s handle. I shook the door, but it was locked. I took a step back to look at the door. It was heavily braced with iron, and on the top and bottom of it were holes for keys. Surprisingly, we don’t actually come across locked doors often. None of us carry a lockpick. Taiene, who was standing behind me, opened her mages satchel.

“It’s very dry today.” She said with a smile on her face.

“You can solve this?” I said, not entirely sure what her plan was. I looked around, and Lili was smiling too. Istwy too seemed like he knew what she meant. Guiese looked at me and shrugged.

Taiene chose a plant from her satchel, as well as the seaweed that Istwy had snatched from the riverbed. She placed them both in the palm of her left hand, and extended her right in front of her, facing the door. With her hands at right angles and her left facing up, she swept her left hand through the air and clapped with vigour, with the ingredients in the noise. I could see from over her shoulder that her hands were glowing orange. She slid her left-hand forwards, away from her other hand, and touched the door. In an instant, flames covered the door, and it was set alight.

“That broke the bone” I said.

“I’ll crash it with my shield once it’s burned enough.” Guiese suggested.

We sat down a bit away from the burning door. Smoke erupted from the doorway and eventually came out from the windows above as if they were chimneys.  It might seem reckless to start a fire, but Empire structures are surprisingly flame resistant. Even with how dry it is, fire won’t do much to an Empire fort. We waited for a while on Guiese, who peered at the door, judging its integrity. Even with a fire close by, I couldn’t say it was that much hotter. Taiene laid the fresh fish just besides the fire.

It wasn’t long until parts of burning wood dropped from the door. Guiese stood up with his sword in his hand. He walked up to the door and began poking it. He pushed on the wood with his sword, and the burning wood fell inwards. He pushed on the bracings, and what was left of the door slowly opened inwards, revealing the inside of the fortification. I jumped to my feet and walked inside, carefully treading over the charred wood.

The room was perfectly rectangular, and made the entirety of this sides ground floor. I could see on either side that there were places for Empire soldiers to store equipment. Some of these places, like weapon racks and barrels, still held a few pieces of equipment. I walked over to a weapon rack, my footsteps echoing across the room. I took a closer look at the equipment, to see if there was anything of value. It was all mostly rusted, so I moved on up the stairs, which were opposite to the door.

 The upper floor was similar, but most of its equipment was bows and arrows. Lili spotted an arrow in a bucket, and picked it up. She looked at it carefully, poked the tip with her finger, bent it slightly, then put the arrow back. There was still some smoke in this room, so we moved on quickly through the wooden connection to the other tower. Leaving the western tower was easy. The door was directly in front of us and was broken open. It lead to a short stairway that took us back to the ground, on a road that led uphill.

# The Fortification
Guiese spotted a flag peaking over the hill in the distance. I was sure it could be nothing but a withered old Empire flag. Still, we treaded carefully waking up the hill, partially in the trees. It was dark, but we couldn’t light a flame. The moon was good in the sky. We decided to split up and observe the fort from different angles. Taiene passed a few awareness potions to each of us. Guiese and Lili went around to the left and Taiene and Istwy to the right, while I found a good place from the bushes to watch the gate.

The fort was not well-maintained. The Empire likes to take caution in making sure everything they own is clean and polished. This flag was pale. I could only just tell that it was the flag of the Empire, with the diamond and the two stripes. The flagpole was stained with bird droppings. The stones were overgrown with moss, and a few vines creeped up the walls like siege ladders. I could tell just from the outside that this was not a small tower-fort, but a full fortification complete with wooden palisades encasing a bailey.

The Empire likes to keep the external layout of these castles uniform. The main feature is the keep, which was hidden by the walls from my angle but attached to them on the opposite side. The keeps are a series of interwoven rectangles all two storeys tall that surrounded a central building, a octagonal stone tower four stories tall. The Empire likes to build there forts high. It’s a necessity in a land like Terlaye where the trees are tall. The stone keep would be surrounded by a whitewashed wooden palisade with a platform and a gatehouse acting as the courtyard’s only entryway. The gate was wide open, they are left like that when the Empire abandons them. When they are closed again, you know someone’s occupied the fort.

I watched the gate, sleeplessly drinking the awareness potions Taiene had made. She’s not that great at wizardry, and her base liquid tastes like watered milk and it probably is. Nothing happened for a while until a tall woman walked from the gatehouse. It’ s known that it’s a bit unusual for a young woman to walk out of an abandoned fort, but that wasn’t the most unusual thing about her. The way she walked seemed like she was barely holding onto consciousness; half-asleep. She slumbered out of the gate but not too far, her arms hanging from her body and her back barely keeping form.

The way she walked wasn’t the only odd thing. From what I could see, her skin was uncoloured. I would even had said that it was peeling from her body in places. She wore a long town dress, but it was unkept. It was stained all over. I could tell that under her garbs she was gaunt, her face and limbs were bony. From her face structure, I could tell she was a Lemuz. She stood outside the gate for a while, idly, and then walked back inside. I observed the gate a while longer, until Lili and Guiese showed up behind me. We decided to wait for Taiene and Istwy before sharing anything, and soon she came.

“I’ve got something”, Taiene excitedly exclaimed, “A light flashed in one of the rooms and I saw a couple shadows walk by. Only that light though, it was bright at first but faded quickly. It was an unnatural white light, probably a clap or enchantment, not fire.”

“We might have something too”, Lili replied, “A figure wandered on top of the tower and looked down at the trees for a bit. They didn’t have a longbow; I wonder if they were a lookout? Their movements were unnaturally slow, too lethargic to be a person trying to keep quiet. Their head was resting on their shoulder too.

“I’ve got something, and I was up-close too. A woman left the castle, stood outside for a bit, then stumbled back in. Her body was like a walking carcass, and I’m afraid to say that her movements matched with the person on the roof.”

We all knew what the threat was. Necromancers are extremely rare, the last one documented had appeared centuries ago. That necromancer was in Vania, and was defeated by the Hero of Vania. The undead still roam the lands he occupied. There’s that famous tale of a Necromancer from a millennium ago. Tharen, who created a kingdom of the dead. People who lived in cities under his control had to face being oppressed by their own ancestor’s rotting corpses. His rule lasted 20 years before he was defeated by a great hero.

“We should probably do something while they’re not expecting it.” Guiese suggested.

“We know nothing about this, we don’t know what we’re up against!” Lili replied.

“Necromancers.” Istwy whispered. A silent shock to all of us that he would speak. “Your universities forbid the knowledge, ours do not.” He paused. We encouraged him to share more.

“A bad enchantment, they are stuck with the knowledge. Their lives are bonded to the whisper in their head.”

“So they enchant corpses?” Lili enquired.

“Like a bone-bind?” Taiene proposed.

“They make conduits, and the conduits raise the dead.” Istwy replied.

“Well, now we know what we’re doing. Let’s go save a life!” insisted Guiese.

“I’m still not sure about this” Lili objected.

“I think it’s better to attack now. The necromancer might be making a conduit out of the person we’re looking for as we speak.” I added.

“We found a trapdoor covered in dirt around the back, we can go through that.” Taiene said.

We crawled through the bushes to where Taiene had found the trapdoor. I feel like I could still know more about what we were fighting Istwy could have said more, but I don’t want to push him. Eventually, Taiene stopped and dug through the dirt with her hands, revealing the wooden trapdoor we were searching for. She opened it. The room below was dark, you could barely make out a floor. There was no ladder attached to the trapdoor either. I got out a piece of rope from my kitbag and tied it around a nearby tree. I then carefully lowered it into the dark hole. A stench of death wafted over me as I leaned over the dungeon’s cavity.

# The Dungeon
I tugged lightly on the rope. I lit a torch and dropped it into the darkness. It illuminated a floor, landing in a small puddle. I grabbed it firmly and crawled down into the darkness. My heavy boots splatted in the puddle. I picked up the dropped torch.  I couldn’t see far with it. Guiese then slid down the rope, then Lili, Istwy, and finally Taiene, who almost gagged at the stench. Each of us had our own torch.

There were jail cells lining a wide sewer-like corridor. We stood at one end of the corridor, and at the other end was an intersection with several other corridors, and some stairs that I knew lead up into the keep of the fort. I knew that those other corridors were identical to this one. There was a constant drone coming from deeper in the dungeon. Taiene was curling. As we walked forwards, Lili stayed close to Taiene as if she were about to take an arrow for her. Guiese and Istwy seemed unphased. Both of their heads wandered the room in fascination. I was weirded by the wetness of the floor.

“This place, ” Taiene almost threw up as foul air entered her mouth, her words rudely interrupted by her gagging. She covered her nose with her hand.

Then, I saw something move in a cell, in the corner of my eye. Then it coughed.

“Someone’s in that cell!” Guiese said urgently. He dropped his shield.

I rushed to the cell door. It was locked. Istwy froze the lock with a clap, then broke it with his hands. He and Taiene rushed to the withering woman. Taiene put her bag down on the floor and reached in for a health potion.

“Pydred, how is she still alive?” Lili said after looking at the girl’s almost corpse.

Istwy unearthed a few wounds and cuts on the woman’s body. He held her rags aside as Taiene poured the translucent red liquid on her wounds. Her cuts rapidly closed. Her bruises cleared up. # Corridors
The first room up the stairs from the dungeon was still dark. It had a window, but it was a tiny air slit to the outside. In one corner of the room was a stone platform, too high to be a bench and too low to be a table. There was an age-old blood stain on the table. On the opposite side of the room there was a dusty cabinet, and a few shelves. There was a disgusting basket which held mouldy clothes that even a moth wouldn’t touch.

Moving forward would be difficult. Although the exterior was standardised, the floor plan inside the buildings are random. That’s not to say that it’s impractical, every corridor and room has a useful use, and the positioning of them always seems perfect. Although the layout is random, it can get quite predictable to someone who knows what they’re doing and where they are going. I lead onwards from this room, sword in one hand and my buckler and torch in the other.

There was a corridor right outside the door going left and right. The corridor made turns, so I could not see far in the corridor. It was quite narrow, there was barely enough room for two people shoulder-to-shoulder. I knew what I was doing. I removed my gloves and felt the wind coming from the left. I took a moment to carefully consider where I had been outside the fort, and considered where I had moved. Then, I confidently turned to the left, the group followed.

I noticed movement from in front of us in the corridor, just around a left and then a right turn in the corridor. No doubt it was undead. I signalled this to my team with my hands.
