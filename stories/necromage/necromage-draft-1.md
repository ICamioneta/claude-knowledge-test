By Maris Honswy – a Ereson Adventurer

# The Tavern
 I was wandering with my band of friends when one of them, Guiese, spotted a nice, lonely tavern alongside the road.  As adventurers, we made it our primary interest to scout gathering places for someone in need. The tavern was one of those ancient buildings by the Old Architects of Ereso, famous for their white stone and triangular wooden beams. I entered the crowded tavern alone, as I usually do. My group waited outside. Past the numerous crowds I saw exactly what my group tend to look for – an old man sitting by the counter looking depressed. I approached him, knowing that there was a select number of things he might tell us.

“Good evening, mind if I sit and have a chat?”

“Of course, take a seat.” The old man said unhappily.

“You seem like you could do with a few more hours of life! What’s on your roof?”

“It’s nothing. Well, it is something. It’s my son. He’s gone missing.”

A missing son. It’s nothing out of the ordinary for my band and me. We approach people everywhere, while traveling, while in a gathering, but especially in local taverns like this. We tend to collect a few quests, and generally we don’t ask for money. Mercenaries ask for money, and if we were mercenaries, we’d be a lot more professional, and wouldn’t approach a potential client like this. What mercenaries fail to realise is that there’s more to life than battle and gold. We get paid in a special currency, fame. It’s equivalent to gold, you can buy rooms, favours, items, and some things gold can’t buy like genuine appreciation. Another thing about being paid in fame is that only common people are able to give it, and so we only deal with a certain kind of client, one with less financial fortune. Sometimes, their jobs for us can be boring, like they need a certain thing gathered or something picked up. Sometimes, the quest can be more interesting, like in this case with a lost family member.

“Missing you say? How old is this son of yours? I doubt he could have run away.”

“It’s the Fort! It’s got to be the Fort! I swear I heard someone in the town say they saw a girl by there, and recognised her from the village. I haven’t been able to find that girl in the week I spent trying! Something evil is keeping people in that fort, and they’ve got my son!” he frustrated, hitting the bar’s counter.

A fort, Bird’s nest! A lot of the time, like a lost pet, we wander around the local area calling its name and following any cries that could have been made by the lost pet, but this man reckons his son is in a fort! The reason we adventure is for the excitement of crawling through caves and abandoned buildings, visiting far shores and delving headfirst into danger. Most of the time we don’t get that. When we do, it’s rare, and exciting.

“A Fort you say? What type, the heavily guarded imperial kind, or a bandit-infested variety?”

“It’s abandoned. No-one’s occupied it. The Empire abandoned it years ago, and since then no-one’s been there. They say there’s spectral soldiers that torment any that come near. Some failed Empirical magic experiment, where the soldier’s souls were left to damn the building. What ghosts need with my son is beyond me.”

“How long could a perfectly suitable Fort be unoccupied for, surely someone has claimed it?”

“I don’t know, adventurer. If you want to go there, be careful. Ghosts might not be real but there has to be something protecting that fort.”

There we have it. An abandoned fort. They are not common, as I said. Whenever a fort is left uninhabited it’s quickly claimed by the fastest fish, be it the local militia or a band of bandits. Forts never go uninhabited; their occupants just hide in it. Most of the time it’s just bandits who don’t want to get noticed. Sometimes it can be different, such as a golem anomaly or a band of independent mages. They are often dangerous, but that’s what we live for.

“Which way might this abandoned fort be?”

“It’s a march along the road to Serven, you’ll see a road that leads further north. Follow that. Don’t die for it, promise me.”

“We’ll have your son, and that girl, before the next moon. Farewell, wise bloke, and don’t wander off!”

# Introduction to the Team

I found my group outside the tavern, they all seemed eager to hear what quest I had found for them. I usually do all the talking in our group. I scout out the most exciting mission for us. They act as if it’s a skill, talking to people, finding their issue. It’s not, you just need to ask the right questions. I don’t understand how my band is unable to do that, it could be a lack of confidence, they always say I have a lot of that. The confidence they lack in conversation, they give during battle. Especially Guiese, he charges with as much force as any of the others could give during an entire battle. That’s not to say the others are lacking, though. We need our ranged fighters, both Taiene and Lili fulfil that role. Taiene tends to use claps, the ranged variety. She once tried to tell me what ingredients she used, but I can’t remember. Not as if my hands will ever be able to clap. Lili’s a strong woman, she wields the bow. She can hold an arrow back longer than any soldier, and her bow isn’t light either. Istwy is our other close fighter, except instead of taking arms he prefers a more magical approach, performing touch claps, usually behind Guiese’s shield. One other important thing about Istwy, he’s a Sosophax.

“How far did you say this turn-off was?” inquired Guiese.

“Only a march.”

“A march is really long!” whined Taiene, in a child’s tone.

“The walk will be good for you!” Lili bellowed, slapping Taiene’s back, and Taiene jumped.

Guiese looked away in disappointment.

“Remember the last abandoned fort?”

“Yeah,” mentioned Guiese, “a murder-cult, who would’ve guessed”.

“I’m still having nightmares about that jail cell” Taiene remarked, shivering.

“I’m still having nightmares about your vomit!”

“You must have been glad to have had someone strong enough to carry you after you fainted.” Lili said to me.

Taiene was picking up a few herbs along the way, and Istwy was trailing behind.

“What do you plan on doing with those?” Istwy asked Taiene.

“Smelling them!” she said with a pluck.

“You see Maris, that’s Norbel Anser, a weak ingredient that mixes with bees” Lili told me.

“Someone’s been keeping up on their local herb knowledge!” Taiene said, bending her back.

I didn’t know Lili was looking into claps. She was not the type to be able to perform them. Her arms and hands were made for the bow. I could never see such strength produce the careful balance needed for claps. It made me wonder what she was learning the herbs for. She could never hope to clap, not without a major change to her lifestyle. Maybe it’s just because she liked Taiene, and wanted to know some about her passion. We kept on walking, meeting few others on the road. We did overtake a caravan to Serven, it looked like they were trading foods.

“How long is a march, anyway?” wined Taiene.

“About this long” I said, pointing at what was clearly the road the old man mentioned. There was a sign by the intersection, so we went and read it.

“Fort Rungroad, sounds like the place.” Guiese said, walking immediately down the route.

“What? It’s not right here? How much further?” Taiene continued

“I don’t know, but I can’t imagine a fort being far from the road.”

“I hear a river. Can we find the river first?” asked Taiene.

“Sure, okay, if it brings your strength up.”

Lili smiled.

Taiene and Istwy both liked sitting by the river, often Taiene would point at some point in the river and Istwy would reach into the river and grab a fish, or a crab, or some plant. They seem to have some kind of connection, where they can understand each other without saying a word. Istwy rarely talks, he once said that all phax rarely talk, and that it’s just their trait. Those were the only words he said that week. We don’t treat him talking like a special event, it could make him feel different. We treat him like we would treat any Muz.

“The river’s just behind these trees, not far off the road” I noted, it’s rushing was barely masked.

“Woo! Come on, let’s rest!” Taiene said, dashing into the trees.

The rest of us treaded through following her. Taiene sat with her legs dangling off the harsh drop into the river. Istwy and Lili went to sit by her, while Guiese and I sat further back on a comfortable seat of grass. Taiene pointed into the river, and Istwy reached in with his tail and grabbed a poor fish.

“That’s a Nater’s Fiend, right?” asked Lili.

“Close, but it’s a common River Betty. You see this line in the scales?” Taiene explained, “You wouldn’t find Nater’s Fiend here anyway, we’re days away from Nater’s river.”

Istwy’s tail snatched some weed from the bottom of the river.

“Nice spot! I didn’t think they grew around here” Taiene said, while cutting up the River Betty.

“Mages, right?” Guiese said sarcastically.

“Can’t get enough of them!” I replied.

We didn’t spend too long at the river. We only stopped to calm Taiene’s feet. She’s been on walks longer than this, so maybe she just wanted to take some local ingredients from the wildlife. Istwy caught a few fish, while Lili and Taiene were picking some rare herbs. We soon started walking, Taiene cutting the few fish apart and tossing the useless parts for the crows. To the normal person, this might seem psychotic, but she insists they have good magical properties. She washes regularly.

# Recon
Guiese spotted a flag peaking up over the forest, and as we walked on, it came apparent it was an old Empire flag still waving on the castle. It was not well-maintained. The Empire take great caution in making sure everything they own is well cleaned and polished. The flag was pale, and was stained by white and brown bird droppings. The stones were overgrown with moss, a few vines crawled up and closer to the sun. We could see that this was not a small tower-fort, but one large enough to house a courtyard, albeit with minimal services within.

The castle was painfully standard as far as Empire castles went. The main feature of all empire forts was the keep, usually a series of connected rectangles all two stories tall surrounding a central building. This building would be a five-storey square tower, all of the buildings would share the same rectangular style. The Empire liked to build their towers high, an unnecessary feature for flat land here in Terlaye but a desired addition in the hilly forests of northern Ralean. The rectangle buildings surrounding the tower would come to a sudden stop, where they would be replaced with tall wooden palisades, creating a small courtyard about the same size in area than the keep. I could not see into the courtyard, but usually this is where training would occur, and where a few war services were hosted such as armour fitting and fletching. All of the buildings should be whitewashed, but nature has taken over and the whitewash is now greywash.

Charging into the castle would be a death sentence. The inhabitants would not be expecting it, but there could be over twenty of them and there’s only 5 of us. As our standard operations go, we would all carefully observe the castle for any movement. This would involve us splitting into three groups and hiding. Usually, Guiese and Lili would go alone, then Taiene and Istwy would find an angle and I’d be left watching the gate day and night. We would always gather near me at night to discuss anything, and to make sure none of us had been captured.

I watched the gate, sleeplessly drinking awareness potions Taiene gave us to keep our eyes open. There was no movement during the day. The windows were still, and the gate remained closed. I waited there until nightfall and I finally saw something. A girl came out of the gate, and gathered some of the dirt on the ground. I observed this girl, she wore a long town dress skirt and was rather gaunt. Her skin was not peach, and I might even say that her skin was peeling off at points. This was probably the girl the old man had mentioned was seen. I watched the windows as well, and sometimes I could see the darkness within shift slowly. If something were moving inside there, it would not be very fast. I waited until we all got together to talk about my findings.

“Sothain”. I heard a woman’s voice behind me. Not to worry, for Sothain was our safe word.

“Idralis” I replied, to avoid instant death at the hands of my own teammates. Lili and Guiese were standing behind me, weapons drawn in the dark. I heard a sigh of relief.

“We should wait for Taiene and Istwy” Guiese said.

And soon enough the same event would happen, but with Taiene being behind the voice instead of Lili.

“I’ve got something”, Taiene began, “A light started in one of the rooms and I saw a couple shadows walk by. Only that one light though, and it faded quickly. It was unnatural, a bright flash, probably a clap but not fire.

“We found something too”, Lili began, “A figure wandered on top of the tower and stayed there for a bit. They didn’t have a bow so they were a lookout. Their movements were unnaturally slow, too lethargic to be a person trying to keep quiet. Their head was always resting on their shoulder too.”

“I’ve got something, and it was up-close too. A girl left the castle, collected some of the dirt, and went back in shortly after. Her body was like a walking carcass. I’m afraid to say that her movements match the person on the roof’s.”

We all knew what it was, for it was quite obvious. We’d never actually encountered one before, only heard stories. It was a Necromancer. There is a famous tale of a Necromancer from centuries ago. A Necromancer named Tharn tried to create his own kingdom with necromancy. The stories are horrific, his entire guard and army consisted of reanimated corpses, the people who lived in cities under his control had to face being oppressed by their own ancestors. He was taken out by a hero, who fought the Armies of the Undead with the help of the kingdom, and struck down Tharn after reuniting fragments of an ancient weapon. They say the cities Tharn ruled have never slept well since. Some of them were even abandoned. I’ve only heard stories.

“Necromancer” Istwy whispered; it was a silent shock to us that he had spoken twice within a day. “Your colleges of magic forbid knowledge. Ours do not. Necromancers grow their magical field and pour it into dead bodies, sacrificing part of themselves for a thrall.”

“separating a magical field? How does that work? I thought they were bound to the thing producing them?” Taiene said, worried.

“Necromancers do it with a clap. It’s a rare clap you cannot make with natural ingredients. Some say necromancers are born with knowledge beyond words, and spend their lives being the thrall of a whisper inside their head.”

“Gwalloph, isn’t it? He puts voices in people’s heads.” Guiese remarked.

“Maybe, but gods are not evil. Necromancers are.” Istwy replied. I too had a question.

“How do they raise great armies alone?”

“Conduits. Special undead made from the living. They carry large amounts of the Necromancer’s magic and distribute it to the dead as they see fit. They suspect Conduits are the first thing Necromancers create.” Istwy replied, a more than suitable answer.

“How come your colleges don’t forbid knowledge. Surely that would make more people use it?” Taiene asked more.

“We are not of the gods.” Istwy replied, ominously. He stopped talking after that, and gestured towards the castle.

“I think it’s better to attack now, rather than wait for later.” Taiene said. It was almost dawn.

# The Dungeon
And like that we wandered headfirst into unknown dangers, but Istwy seemed to know what we were up against and he was with us along the way. We all sneaked to the back of the castle. Taiene said she spotted a secret entrance to a lower level. It was a small round hatch that lied inconspicuously in the dirt. We went through it and found ourselves in a dungeon, underneath the castle. There were jail cells lining the corridor, every other cell being equipped with a skeleton. There were drones heard from deeper within the dungeon, but we didn’t have to concern ourselves with that yet. Taiene’s body was curling up, shivering. She was visibly disturbed and almost vomited. There was a thick, sick air that smelt of decaying corpses. Lili walked closely to Taiene, as if she were about to take an arrow for her. Guiese and Istwy seemed unphased, but both their heads wandered the room in fascination. I was weirded out by the wet floor.

“This place,” Taiene almost threw up as her open mouth let in winds of foul air. She was about to say something, but her words were rudely interrupted by her gagging. Lili moved in to comfort her.

I was leading the way, and as the inside of a cell entered my vision, I saw something I couldn’t imagine.

“Someone’s in that cell!” Guiese quietly shouted, and Istwy rushed to the door, reaching into his satchel to collect a few ingredients.

“By Pydred! How is she still alive?” Taiene whispered in shock. She gagged again after her words came out.

Istwy did an ice touch clap, which froze the metal enough for it to be opened. He and Taiene then rushed to the withering girl and tended to her. Taiene reached into her satchel and got a small vial of empty liquid, which she spent some time enchanting before she forced it down the prisoner’s mouth. The imprisoned girl coughed quietly, and soon opened her eyes. A few scratches and bruises on her body cleared up. Taiene, Guiese and Istwy were all kneeling down, looking at her and trying to clean some of her unhealed cuts. Lili and I stood back, watching out for any undead lurker that might interrupt us. Soon enough, we had got the girl talking.

“what…” she coughed weakly, “water.”

“You’re safe now, you’re in a dungeon. We found you in this cage, Keep quiet.” Taiene said. Guiese got his flask and offered it to the girl.

“Thanks,” the girl said, before coughing some more. Guiese had taken off a coat and wrapped it around the girl, who was previously in damp commoner’s clothes.

“Am I safe?” She asked, under her breath.

“Yes, you’re safe with us.” Taiene said, rubbing her shoulder comfortingly.

Guiese and Taiene both carried the girl to her feet.

“Do you know where you are?” Guiese asked.

“I was in the field, collecting ingredients for my brother” she replied drowsily.

“Then I was being dragged across the dirt.” She continued.

“Now I’m here. What’s going on?”

“You’re in a prison cell, under an abandoned fort.” Guiese said straight. Taiene was helping her stand as she regained her strength.

“You remember those Necromancer stories?” Taiene whispered close to her face. “There might be a Necromancer here. Don’t worry though. We’ll keep you safe and get you out.

Lili turned around, and gestured that something potentially hostile was in bound.

It was a member of the undead. It was slow and slowly lurking towards us. I suggested that she fire an arrow at it, see what happened. So, Lili took her bow and nocked an arrow. She drew the bow back, and fired it directly into the undead’s shoulder. The undead’s arm then flopped limp, but its head turned and looked at us. It then began to stumble slowly in our direction. Just when it looked like it was about to scream, Lili sent another arrow through its throat, and it dropped on the ground. It made a noisy splash as it hit the ground. We all sped up that which we were doing. We walked hastily yet silently along the dungeon, making sure we check every cell for more people.

“What’s your name, may I ask?” Taiene said to the girl.

“It’s…” She paused, probably taken back by the sickness in the air. “It’s Miria.”

We walked around the entire dungeon; every cell was empty. We soon again passed the place where we had felled the undead creature. We saw that a few undead were also there, including one that appeared like a fresh body, but had glowing green eyes.

“Conduit” Istwy commented. Miria turned to look at him, and was surprised that a Phax was in our presence.

“A Phax, right?” She said, and Taiene gave her a deep glare. “Right” Miria noted, and she fell into silence.

“We have to decide what we’re going to do.” Guiese agreed to my statement.

“We could sneak in and look for this boy, or we could fight every undead we come across” Guiese proposed.

“If we don’t take out as many as we can getting in, then escaping will be difficult.” Lili said, and Taiene agreed. We all seemed to agree.

“Can I have something?” Miria asked. “I think I have the strength to help.”

Taiene handed her a short dagger. “Stay close to me,” she said.

We all readied ourselves. Guiese brought his shield up, ready to charge. Taiene reached into her satchel, preparing some ranged claps. Lili nocked an arrow, ready to draw. Istwy gathered an ingredient and dropped a bit of poison into his hands. I drew my own sword, and we waited for Guiese’s charge. Soon enough, he ran in. He crashed into the conduit, knocking it to the ground. He then drew his sword back, ready to cut through its evil glowing eyes. Lili shot an arrow straight into the stomach of another undead, which I then rushed at with my longsword. Taiene threw a fire projectile, taking out a single undead with ease. The undead I was up against swung his mighty arm at me, I quickly parried it and sent my sword directly into the corpse’s heart. It then fell to the ground. Guiese dispatched the conduit with a swift slice to the head, and Istwy took the last one out with a touch clap of poison.

“That was over quickly. I thought they were meant to be hard to kill” Said Guiese, with a disappointed frown.

“It only took a single clap for that one to fall” Taiene said, but she was happy the fighting had ended.

“Even with the arrow, I still feel like it should have taken more hits to down.”

It was a swift battle, but we were thankful these things were not hard to kill. Miria cried shortly after our easy battle, but Taiene comforted her on whatever was the bother. We creeped slowly up the stairs now that we knew there were no alive prisoners in the cells. The room beyond the door had no enemies. You could easily recognise it as somewhere the Empire once held. There were small Empire trinkets on cupboards, basic empire plates made with a recognisable Empire marking. Grand but common cupboards made to a standard. It would be a comfortable Empire room, had it not been for the stench. An Empire fort would never get this messy, with cobwebs at every corner and dust coating the surfaces like a thick fog. We decided to continue through a corridor, looking for a way to the room where Taiene had seen the flash. We reckoned that the Necromancer had caused the flash by creating a Conduit. We could only hope it wasn’t the boy we were after.

# Hall Intrusion
In an Empire fort so boringly standard, the floor plan is chaotic. Even though the outside is the same, the corridors and rooms inside are arranged like a maze, to trap attackers. That’s not to say that it’s an impractical layout, there are still neat corridors attached to rooms, somewhat predictable if you know what you’re doing. It’s only the exact layout that changes. We peeked through every door, and it appeared that we were in the soldier bunks. Very few of the rooms were inhabited by walking corpses, and the number of them was consistently below 4. They were easy to take care of. We continued to walk towards the room Taiene mentioned.

We soon came at a set of stairs, iconic to the Empire fort. When normal castles would have thin circular stairs, an Empire fort would keep them straight and narrow along the wall of a room. We climbed the thin wooden stairs, and soon reached the second floor of the area. It appears that we would have to go onto the second floor to reach the rest of the keep. It was here where we were met with our second conduit, surrounded by more undead than we’d fought before.

            Even though we were outnumbered, we all managed to take them down without much problem. Taiene was getting tired, and said that if there were this many undead, she would have to sit some battles out, in order to preserve ingredients. Miria took part in the battle, taking out a single undead. She remorselessly forced the corpse to the ground, then repeatedly plunged the dagger into its chest until it stopped moving. It was an efficient, if messy, tactic.

“I think we are about in the right spot.”

“We are about where I thought the flash came from” Taiene said whilst peering out a window.

“It’s got to be through here then.”

Taiene straightened her back and readied a few ingredients. Guiese put his arms up as well, and Istwy gathered a few drops of poison. We all knew that the necromancer should be behind this door.

I opened the door, and just as I’d expected it was an Empire hall, with a throne at one of the walls. Although it’s described as a hall, the room is still only one storey tall, with pillars rising out of the floor keeping the wooden ceiling up. The door we came through was to the right of the seat that sat against the wall, and we saw to our left there was a boy being forced to kneel before the Necromancer by a few conduits. Miria ran away once she saw the conduits, and Taiene followed her. Lili followed Taiene, and the rest of us were left to confront the Necromancer alone. There was no turning back, he had seen us once we opened the door. He was a tall man wearing red robe garments. He held an enchanted staff, with a round gem placed on the top.

“As you’ve been so kind to show up here unannounced, do come in.” the Necromancer said in a confident voice. “Tell me now, why have you come to this abandoned fort? Was it curiosity? Were my Conduits not subtle enough in one of their abductions?”

“An old man pointed us here. He wants his son back. The son you were about to turn into a conduit.”

“Oh this boy?” he said, and the conduits stood him up. “Go on then, get him.”

The conduits threw the boy towards us, landing him on the ground. Istwy approached the boy, and took a potion out.

“A Phax, eh? They say you scorpion people are immune to the curse Necromancy brings. I’d like to see your immunity as you get torn limb-from-limb!” and then the conduits charged.

Guiese and I both took defensive stances, and then leagues of the undead appeared from the walls. We were clearly outnumbered. We retreated to the door with the boy, and Guiese held them off as best he could with his shield. He defended high as I tried to cut their legs and stop any attempts they had at hitting Guiese’s legs. We were left defending the door as hordes of the undead threatened us. I was cutting as many as I could, and Istwy was searching in his satchel for some ingredients.

Lili then walked into the room, and instantly nocked an arrow at the sight of us struggling. She said that Taiene should be here soon, with Miria. The two soon did arrive, being chased by the undead. I quickly stopped hitting the legs opposing Guiese and defended the door behind us instead. Lili took her sword out and began defending with Guiese. I looked at Miria, and she was still in tears, Taiene still comforting her. Istwy turned at Taiene, and asked her a question.

“You carry Ash Skeep.” Istwy stated to Taiene.

“Yes, let me find it.” She replied.

An undead scraped my arm and another hit one of Guiese’s legs.

“I hope you know what you’re doing…” Taiene said to Istwy, as he prepared a few ingredients.

Taiene found the ingredient, and Istwy took it in his hand. He walked to Guiese.

“On my count, open a gap ok?” Istwy said.

“Got it.” Replied Guiese. Lili stepped back and readied her arrows.

“Three.” I got another scratch on my arm, but Lili came over and began dangerously shooting arrows over my shoulder.

“Two.” I heard an undead scrape again at Guiese’s legs. You could hear the Necromancer inside laughing.

“One!”

Guiese stepped back and shouted in pain as his foot sprained. The undead piled in, squeezing each other against the doorframe. Istwy stepped up and readied himself for a clap. He brought his hands together, and from his hands a great flame spewed. It went forth from his hands, engulfing all the undead, finding its way between the small holes in the corpses and scorched out the other end. The open door that was there got consumed by the fire. I hadn’t noticed that Taiene was also preparing a clap. She clapped, touching the ground just in front of where I was defending and a magical barrier appeared, blocking the undead entry from the downstairs. We waited for the fire to clear, and we saw many dead bodies on the floor. We entered the hall; the Necromancer was still sitting on the throne.

“Where did you find that clap, Phax?” he asked Istwy, but Istwy gave no response.

Guiese stayed in the room to tend to the boy, everyone else was with the necromancer.

“We can’t let you abduct people and resurrect corpses any longer. Yield now and the gods might find favour in you.”

Miria was struggling to look forward. There were two Conduits standing by the Necromancer.

“What makes you think you’ve won this? That barrier won’t hold forever.”

Taiene jailed a conduit with a clap, surrounding it with a magical barrier.

“So be it. Prepare yourselves, heroes!”

Lili shot an arrow straight into the Necromancer’s head. He dropped dead on the floor, blood slowly pouring out of his head.

“This was not going to end any other way,” said Lili.

The room sat in silence for a moment.

“You think that’s enough?” the free conduit said with a deep voice. Its body was slowly turning into the Necromancer. Soon, the conduit was the Necromancer.

Lili drew her arrow at the Necromancer again,

“No!” Shouted Miria. “Don’t!”

Lili complied, and undrew her bow.

“I remember that girl. Wasn’t this conduit her brother?” he gestured towards the jailed conduit, who was hitting the walls of its magical prison.

“You are not invincible” Istwy said, as he prepared a clap.

“Not even claps can defeat me! Nothing can! Hurt me and I’ll become the next conduit!”

Istwy prepared a clap regardless of the Necromancer’s warnings. We were all standing in front of the Necromancer, unsure of what to do. If we killed him, he would reincarnate himself. Regardless, Istwy seemed sure he could defeat him, with the right clap. He readied a few basic ingredients, then sprinted towards the Necromancer. The whole time, the Necromancer kept laughing, insisting nothing could kill him. That’s when Istwy quickly turned his trajectory at the Necromancer’s previous body, and his attention at the staff. He clapped, touched the staff, and the Necromancer’s eyes filled with fear.

The gem on top of the staff shattered, and the necromancer swiftly hit Istwy with a wind clap, throwing Istwy into the ceiling, and back down on the other side of the room. Istwy said how he could now not reincarnate. Lili quickly drew an arrow, and the Necromancer created a wall barrier to block it. Shortly after, Guiese and the boy ran through the door, and Guiese said that the barrier would not hold up much longer. Escaping the Keep wasn’t difficult. Although all of the undead were still alive, they appeared to be acting off some basic instinct. They were often found surrounding a Conduit, or in small groups. Most of them seemed busy finding a Conduit to follow, as many did not attack us on our way out. We left through the front gate, and we killed the undead girl at the gate before we left, to give that mystery some closure. Miria followed us back to the white tavern. It was the middle of the day when we got there, and sure enough I saw the old man sulking at the bar as he had done the day before.

“We have found your son! Here he is, physically unharmed but traumatized.”

“Thank you, adventurer! Thank you thank you thank you! Please, I don’t have much, accept this small sum of gold I would have used to buy beer!” The man said in joy.

“Please, we don’t accept money for our service.”

The bartender called me over. “I see you’ve cheered up that old man. I was afraid I’d have to kick him out. He was making the whole place gloomy. Here, bring your friends in and accept some free food and drink. You can even stay the night if you so wish.”

That’s the reward we work for. I brought my friends in, and we all went upstairs to find some good rooms. Miria was still with us.

“What are you going to do, Miria?” Asked Taiene.

“I don’t know. I feel so wrong. I don’t want to go back to the town.” Miria replied.

“If you found fighting the undead adventurous, then join us! We could use the extra hand! We’ll pull in some favours and get you any gear you wish.”

“I can teach you! I know a bit about enchanting, but I mostly do liquids! I’ll teach you the basics!” she said enthusiastically.

“Yeah, that’s nice.” Miria replied.
