The Aredvic are a faction of chariot-riding nomads with a minuscule settled populace. Their chariots are made from a variety of materials depending on availability, but the majority of them are made out of fungal wood. They are pulled by horses native to the steppe, and a few people ride the horses directly forgoing the traditional chariot, but breeds of horse capable of being ridden are rare.

The Aredvic title is recognised by its own unique sovereignty. There is no real "Aredvic" faction, instead it is a general descriptor of the several otherwise wholly independent Aredvic factions across the Heabe. The idea of an "Aredvic" to these factions is based on cultural ideas on rule over the steppe. Each of the factions maintain their own territory, often forcing traders and settlers to pay tribute or tax to their organisation. As part of enforcing their territory, they will bar other Aredvic factions from doing the same in their territory. Depending on factional relations, they may bar passage outright.

Although each of the Aredvic factions maintain their own foreign affairs (foreign being with non-Aredvic factions such as [The Empire of Ralean](wastelife/factions/barogate-lifeland/the-empire-of-ralean.md)), the cultural identity of the Aredvic ties each of the factions closer to each other than to non-Aredvics. This is characterised with the Aredvic identity as being a sort of "brotherhood". Familial identity is strong within the Aredvic factions, common in nomadic organisations.

# General Hierarchical Overview

Each Aredvic faction has a unique internal system and struggle, but generally speaking they all act with a similar hierarchical structure. This structure relies on the terminology of family.

## Family Tribe

For every Aredvic faction, the Family Tribe is the atomic structure. This is a group of roughly 200 people each with their own internal structures. These family tribes can be blood linked, perhaps all cousins of a father, but more often than not it is a found family, a family in everything but blood. When a family outgrows its interconnective limits, at roughly 300 members, it will split. Some book keepers like to document these splits for the purpose of familial history. While the emphasis is on allegiance to the leading tribe, some Aredvic factions make use of the binary family tree to create unique organisational structures.

Family tribes are the Aredvic faction. Any given family is a family of a faction, say a Codelt family or a Hezan family. Allegiance to the faction and the lead tribe is often emphasised above all else, with the interest that when a family dishonours this allegiance it can lead to factional splits. It is even often preferred that a family leave their faction for another if dispute is unresolvable, rather than have that dispute turn into sub factionalism. 

## Leading Tribe

The idea of a Leading Tribe permeates throughout the Aredvic factions. The specifics of the leading tribe's role depends on the hierarchical structures of individual factions. It is even possible that the "leading tribe" is actually a democratic republic made up of individual leaders from the other faction's tribes.

The role of the leading tribe, no matter the political organisation, is to rally the tribes from the other factions. This can be to perform targeted action such as an attack or a resistance to an external force, or to decide matters of policy that the familial tribes must adhere to.

## Settlements

Permanent settlements - outside of [Camp Hess](wastelife/factions/barogate-wastes/camp-hess.md), [Cosbon Complex](wastelife/factions/barogate-wastes/cosbon-complex.md), and [Hegrel](wastelife/factions/barogate-wastes/hegrel.md) - are small village communities of often Ews folk. They are entirely separate from the Aredvic. The Aredvic do not act as a kind of government, but will often ask settlements pay a tax to honour the Aredvic sovereignty. The exact nature of this tax highly depends on the faction, but in general this is a strictly oppressive organisation.

## Independent Nomads

There are many non-Aredvic nomads living in the Heabe. These are the general inhabitants who follow the seasonal flow of water down the steppe's slope. Much like the settlements, their position in the Aredvic organisation is often that of an explicitly oppressed and exploited class. Most Aredvics will collect tax or tariff on the independent nomads living in their land. The independent nomads are not in any way tied to the Aredvics, but taxes are often low and only serve as a way for Aredvics to enforce their sovereignty.

## Bridges

The Bridges of the Heabe have a crucial role in passage through the Heabe. All of the Heabe's rivers are large and impassable especially by chariot, and there are no local resources to built a makeshift bridge. For that reason, the few bridges that do exist are incredibly important to whatever faction can control them. because the faction boundaries are almost literally defined by the rivers, the bridges are always on the faction border. 

The main benefit of controlling a bridge is the economic. A faction might not always meet a trader or traveller out in the open, but almost everyone will have to cross a river and they will have to use a bridge to do so.

Joint control, as in fares on both ends, is seen as a disrespectful compromise. The bridge needs to be controlled by one faction, and control is always enforced by violence. Often, the controlling faction will have a permanent outpost established to protect the bridge.
