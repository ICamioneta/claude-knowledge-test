The Cosbon Complex, or simply "Cosbon", is a city-state in [The Heabe](/wastelife/regions/barogate-wastes/the-heabe). Although sovereignly recognised as a fully independent state, it's legal and righteous agreements with the [Natin Aredvic](wastelife/factions/barogate-wastes/aredvic.md) make it seem more like a city on Natin controlled land. Regardless, Cosbon enjoys no official ties to the Natin as the Natin profit from allowing safe passage through their lands for traders reaching Cosbon.

The city-state also manages a few farming and mining towns that supply the city with its food and resources. Because of this, Cosbon is self-reliant in the matters of food and resource.

The main draw of the City of the Cosbon Complex is the large silver ruin located in its centre. This structure, the '_cosbon complex_' of the state's namesake, is the large extrinsic element of a larger structure that extends for unexplored kilometers in any direction and about 200 metres deep. The above ground elements are fully explored and their structures have been adopted by the city. What were once dirt and overgrown ruins have become shops and houses. However, the deeper parts of Cosbon are dangerous and unexplored. 

Many of the entrances to the deep subterranean structures have been sealed off to avoid any automatons or preserved gem golems creeping up and harming Cosbon's citizens, but this subterranean structure is a huge tourist draw for Cosbon. Adventurers from pretty much everywhere delve into the complex in search of prehistoric treasures, and the markets above ground are full of them.

No-one really knows how far the subterranean sections of the complex stretch, but researchers and adventurers suggest that there is a deep layer of connectivity between every silver ruin.

The origin of the town itself are somewhat unknown. It is suggested that the surface complex was merely scoped out and inhabited by random people during ancient times.

# Politics

The name "Cosbon" is shorthand for all facets of the idea: the land, the people, the city, its towns, and the political structures that bind them all together. The political structures of Cosbon are more of a suggestion than an authoritative presence.

Cosbon maintains a state whose sole intent is to provide services around the city. This includes providing food, medicine, and maintaining infrastructure. The Cosbon State also includes a legislative and judiciary branch. Participation and voting on legislature and matters of the judiciary is performed solely by "stakeholders" who are citizens that pay money into the state's budget.

Although this payment could be thought of as a tax, there is no obligation to pay and the more money that is played into the budget the more an individual or organisation has control over the legislature and judiciary. 

The Legislative and Judicial Court is a combined council held by stakeholders and stakeholder representatives who vote on matters of the law and the meaning of law. In the founding documents, it is said that every stake held is a position in council, but due to the population of the city and the amount of stakes held in general this has been abstracted to a few representative "slots" that can be filled by representatives. These representatives can be the leaders of political parties or corporate officers, but what matters is that their vote on council is only as powerful as the amount of stakes they claim to represent.

## Political Parties

Political parties formed naturally in the system of representatives. They put forward a set of political goals and campaign to have stake "pledged" to them as a representative. This pledging is only done by those who want a representative. Any stakeholder is entitled to a position on the courts, but an average citizen who may only hold a stake or two would have their individual vote massively overshadowed by the representatives who hold on to thousands of pledged stakes.

Certain corporations, such as trader guilds or the organised farmers market, hold stakes on their own without being tied to any person in particular. These organisations, if they have enough stakes to be meaningful, will send their own representative to any councils held.

## Executive

Cosbon essentially has no executive branch. Instead, enforcement of the law is dependent on a mutual trust. Guards are hired by corporations to protect their private property, using legislature as law. This system currently works because there is no clear dominant in terms of executive force, and any single executive force not adhering to the law would likely be targeted by those who do adhere to the law.

Political parties also often maintain their own executive force maintained with party donation and membership fees which many citizens decide are more important than pledged stakes themselves.

Whenever there is factional conflict that threatens the peace of Cosbon, there is a tradition that all other executive forces form a military coalition to oppose the faction breaking the law, reminding them that any action that they wish to do needs to be codified in law - in the agreement between all factions. This system is based on the principle that an aggression against the law is an aggression on all stakeholders. If a dissenting faction had enough military power to overpower the coalition, then they would also have enough money to directly change legislation so the scenario is by default unlikely.

Cosbon's public services do include a "public guard", but this guard explicitly exists for the protection of the common citizen against common misdemeanour. They are primarily concerned with acting as a mediator between citizens when they are in conflict. The public guard also investigate murder, stealing, and other things that are generally agreed upon to be a crime. Unlike private executive forces, the public guard aren't expected to uphold the law. This is because the law was written as an agreement between corporate entities and not as rules for citizens. There are no codified rules for citizens, so instead the guard operate on a common understanding and what is culturally not taboo.

## Natin Stake Holding

The Natin Aredvics exercise their power over Cosbon using this mediative system. They have always been one of the "corporate" entities involved in the city, and when the government was formed they founded the "Natin Institute" which acted as a settled body of Natin Aredvics who were politically involved. They still exist and their internal organisation hasn't really changed. They are financed by the Natin Aredvics, and their members of council vote for explicitly pro-natin terms.

In the modern political climate, certain citizens have become "pro-Natin". It's the belief that the Cosbon Complex should be entirely controlled by the Natin Aredvics under a protectorate system similar to that of Cosbon. This comes from a genuine concern over corporate executive control, and the idea that the city could self-govern its own executive force and realistically achieve this by elevating the Natin to a position of sovereign power. The "Aredvic Party" is a political organisation representing this interest, as the Natin Institute does not allow citizens to pledge stakes to them. The actual voting of the Natin Institute is a lot milder than that of the Aredvic Party, as the Aredvic Party's demands are more radical than the Natins are comfortable with.

The Aredvic Party's support of the Natin Aredvics is reliant on the idea that an Aredvic faction could not directly control a town, as the Aredvic organisation is inherently nomadic and hierarchically structured - not suitable for the governing of a town. Historically speaking, this is entirely true. The only other settlement in Aredvic land is Camp Hess, which is officially a protectorate enjoying full protection by the Hezan Aredvics with the caveat of needing to pay tribute. Cosbon already in some way pays tribute to the Natin, by means of its traders paying for passage through Natin lands.

## History

The origins of this system are entirely corporate. In the beginning, several large organisations had their own police force and essentially ran their own government on whatever land they could control. This led to dispute and bloodshed, so the collaborative legislative and judicial branch was devised to sort the company's disputes with civility. It wasn't until fairly recently (3263) that civilian stake holding was allowed and the "pledge" system was formed as not to clutter the councils. This change was a concession by the companies who were faced with a mild revolution where this change in system was the primary demand.
