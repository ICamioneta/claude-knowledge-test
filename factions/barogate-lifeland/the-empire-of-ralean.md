The Empire of Ralean is a centralised absolute monarchy with elements of bureaucracy, regional governance, and remnants of feudal structures.

## The Emperor of Ralean, Shalûn

The Emperor of Ralean, a title traditionally called (the) Shalûn. The Shalûn wields absolute authority over the Empire. Their role is both in military and political, acting as the head-of-state and grand Rostavar of the Ralean Army. However, due to the recent southern revolts and a general understanding that Ruion is untrained in the matters of military (as he was overtrained in governance of dissident provinces i.e. Terlaye) the grand Rostavar of the army is instead Dienent Carfatha, a senior officer and child of one of the leading figures in the conquest of Terlaye. The Carfatha family has long historical ties to the Reimigs. 

Succession of the Shalûn is hereditary; it follows the eldest Reimig. However, it is always the truth in Empires of this sort that political manoeuvring is an important factor. Many eyes watch the Shalûn, looking for a weakness which they can leverage to dethrone the Reimigs. It has happened before, but the Reimigs were able to reseat their power. The dethrone of the Reimigs is the most severe threat, but weakness could be met with smaller compounding changes made by the empirical court to empower regents or governors. 

The title of Shalûn was granted to the then-leader of the Reimig Dynasty in 3102, and the legitimacy of the Shalûn is symbolically attached to their dynasty, believing the Reimig leader to be the strongest and most competent of all the Ralean dynasties at the time. Total autocratic control over Ralean would fall into the Empire's hands by 3261.

## The Empiric Council

The Emperor relies on a central advisory body that handles day-to-day governance. This council is a mix of hereditary nobles with ties to old dynasties, officials appointed by the Emperor, and competent bureaucrats. The council consists of a few key roles.

### Grand Vizier

A powerful authoritative figure that oversees the Empire's bureaucracy. The Grand Vizier is historically a position appointed by Shalûn, but taken from a pre-existing powerful dynasty. This position's power is almost counterweight to the absolute power of Shalûn.

### Rostavari

The Empire of Ralean's military is vast, and contains several distinct armies. These armies are commanded by Rostavari, who have full control over their respective army. Traditionally, the role of Rostavar is chosen using the Military's own procedure. With the Emperor's absolute authority, they are allowed to and generally will appoint their own Rostavari. 

Rostavari are coordinated by the Grand Rostavar of the Empire, which is usually the second role of the Emperor, but Ruion has delegated this position of his to former Rostavar Dienent Carfatha.

At the Empire's military height, during the conquest of Terlaye, there were a grand total of 5 armies. One of these armies were disbanded at the end of the conquest, with another being dedicated to the defence of Terlaye.

Since the southern revolts, two armies with a lot of basis in the south had its members defect, and many of the Empire's highest ranking and most competent military officers defected to the Republic of Ralean. Ruion has created a new third army to help conquer the south, but the forces of the other Ralean-based army was split in this creation, and both armies are small for an army. This leaves 3 Rostavari positions on the Empiric council.

### Sarvadar

The head of treasury in charge of managing the Empire's economy. They are responsible for managing the state-run economic activity, as well as overseeing private corporations and the private markets with regulations.

### Lead Soris

The Lead Soris oversees provincial management, ensuring that governors and local leaders remain loyal to the Emperor. The role has recently came into challenge, as the Emperor's trust in the former lead minister was lost during the revolts.

### Safarnavân

The Safarnavan oversees the Empire's foreign affairs, primarily managing peaceful relations and secondarily espionage outside of the Empire. Historically, they also managed internal affairs, but this task was recently given to a new position in lieu of the southern revolts.

### Nezârnavân

The Nezârnavân oversees the Empire's internal relations, including espionage and sabotage. This is a recent title created by Ruion to help manage the southern revolts.

## Provincial System

After the conquest of Terlaye and the subsequent loss of the southern provinces, the Empire is a fractured realm with vast provincial disparities. Before the southern revolt, the Empire consisted of four major regions: Eysenre (northern Ralean, 6 provinces), Senolado (central Ralean, 10 provinces), Desheg (southern Ralean, 6 provinces), and Terlaye (8 provinces). Since, all Desheg provinces and many Senolado provinces were lost. Terlayan provinces are governed differently than Ralean-based provinces, but all report to the Emperor.

A **Soris** is a provincial governor who wields significant power over their territory. They are either appointed by the Emperor or chosen from the noble dynasties of Ralean. After the southern revolts, Ruion has ensured the loyalty of the Soris with the help of the lead internal diplomat and the lead Soris. Soris who are not appointed directly by the emperor are almost always lead nobles from the local noble dynasty. 

The bureaucracy acts as a check on Soris power. Bureaucrats stationed in certain provinces are tasked with monitoring Soris loyalty and performance.

Each province has a degree of autonomy but is required to pay taxes, raise levies for empiric defence, and enforce empiric law. Teryale's integration into this system remains rocky, as the people of Terlaye are not fully loyal to the Empire, and Ruion has had to make intelligent work of who he appoints as Soris of the Terlayan provinces, mixing loyal Ralean dynasties with local Terlayan feudal nobility

A Dux is a martial governor of a "dissident province". Duxes appear when a province is deemed dissident by the Emperor or other key figure, and the province is essentially placed into a martial law. Duxes answer directly to the Emperor (or otherwise the Grand Marshal) and have the broad authority to suppress dissent on their own command. All of the revolting provinces have been assigned a Dux, but since Empiric control of these provinces is lacking, these Duxes generally have a relaxed job of waiting for some of their province to become governable by them.

### Terlayan Provinces

Terlayan provinces are currently all managed slightly differently, but in general the old monarchs are still in power, but their families are expected to structure themselves as dynasties rather than simple monarch families. Any semblance of democracy in the old system is gone, such as any elective monarchies or any councils of lords. The monarch's dynasties have a "Soris" selected from among them by the Shalun.

## The Bureaucracy

While the Soris and dynastic nobility hold much of the power, the Empire of Ralean is reliant on a professional class of bureaucrats, engineers, and scholars who run its administration. These professionals are promoted within the system based on competency. 

Certain high-ranking positions within the bureaucracy require officials to pass complex examinations that test their administrative abilities. This is the Empire's attempt to avoid bureaucratic corruption and to ensure competency within their governance.

**Kidhmatac** are departments of the bureaucracy concerned with performing certain civic tasks, such as finance, the military, and civil works. These agencies report to the Emperor and the Council. These agencies have their own hierarchies with empiric servants stationed across provinces to monitor compliance and collect taxes.

## Military Stucture

With the near complete loss of its naval fleet, the Empire's military is focussed on land-based power.

### Standing Armies

The Empire of Ralean maintains a number of standing armies, each of which are essentially fully-sized armies commanded by Rostavari. These armies consist of trained individuals who are often tied to their province. During times of war, Soris are expected to raise additional levies to bolster the size of these armies.

#### Empiric Savari

Empiric Savari make up the core of the standing armies. They are the full-time professional soldiers.

#### Provincial Levies

The provincial levies are raised by Soris during times of war and crisis. The level of training Levies receive differs wildly depending on the province, but provinces with long military traditions such as Eysenre provinces have levies almost on par with the Empiric Savari.

#### Dynastic Retinues

The Dynastic noble families of Ralean maintain their own personal army of soldiers. These units are often integrated into the standing armies in times of war, but doing so is the choice of the noble in charge of the army. Usually, the dynastic retinues serve under their own company section of the standing army, under the direct command of the noble who is allowed to take the Marshal's command as a suggestion.

#### Hierarchy

**Rostavari** are the leader of standing armies. They have a role in The Empiric Council, and have full control over the Empiric Savari and Provincial Levies in their army. They are appointed by the Emperor, but the Emperor has to balance appointing a competent Rostavar with nobles with a dynastic claim to command and political loyalty. Rostavari lead their armies on campaign, but are also often used to maintain internal security within their regions. They are also important local power brokers, coming into conflict with Soris, the bureaucracy, and local dynasties when attempting to influence politics.

**Sepervari** are commanders of divisions within the army, typically around 5000 soldiers per division. These are often professional soldiers or nobles groomed for command by their dynasties. Sepervari are appointed by Rostavari, with the Emperor occasionally ensuring political loyalty with his absolute authority. 

**Sebhar** are unit commanders of about 100 men. These are professional soldiers who have demonstrated battlefield leadership and tactical expertise. There are very few noblemen Sebhar, but young nobles are often Sebhar to solidify their political role and gain military experience to eventually become Sepervari. While Sebhar have little political power, they are the backbone of the army and are responsible for maintaining discipline and ensuring that the orders of the Sepervari and Rostavari are followed. When levies are raised, additional Sebhar from the Empiric Savari are chosen as levied soldiers cannot become Sebhar.

#### Rostavar conflict with Duxes

Rostavari often find themselves in conflict with Duxes. Suppressing internal revolts is a key role of the marshal, and enforcing empiric law and maintaining the Emperor's control is of their utmost importance. However, Duxes, the military governors of dissident provinces, come into conflict with Rostavari as they are generally competitors for the Emperors favour. Given how they both have the same task, it is often a competition to see who performs their task most effectively. When a Dux is appointed, a Marshal's power in the province is actually reduced as Rostavari are no longer granted certain provincial executive rights.

#### Factionalism

High-ranking military officials often form factions hinging on three different factors of political power. There are the noble dynasties of Ralean who wager economic power and local control, There are professional soldiers who are tied to the bureaucracy, and there are appointees either by the Sepervari, the Rostavari, or the Emperor directly, who adhere to loyalty to the Emperor.

This factionalism has somewhat benefited the Emperor in the past, in a sort of divide-and-conquer way where each of the factions (i.e. professionals, nobles, and appointees) are easier to control than one unified force come opposition to the Emperor. But, in the recent term, it is essentially factionalism that has lead two entire armies to dissolve and become soldieries of the south, and many of the Emperor's professional bureaucrats and nobles defected to the Republic.

### Shalûnbar

The Shalûn's personal guard, the Shalûnbar are elite troops and political enforcers. They are fiercely loyal to the Shalûn and are often involved in rooting out dissident within higher ranking Empire systems, like within Dynasties or the Soris.

In times of war, the Shalûnbar would serve directly under the grand marshal, and would often be commanded to join the standing armies for particular operations. Although they listen only to the Shalûn, the Shalûn may ask them to listen to a Rostavar of an army.

## Economic Control

The loss of Ralean's coastal provinces has severely impacted trade, with the only remaining ports being in Terlayan provinces: Nater's End and Reumanas, the latter of which needs to pass through an Eresonian-controlled river to exit the lake it is on. These ports are overloaded, and Eresonia denies passage for many Reumanas-bound ships as not to overload their own ports within the lake. The southern revolts have almost entirely cut off trade with Ralean's anulan trade partners, such as the Empire of Plaen, the Western Neble Imperium, The Roughwalk Imperium, Edris Nation, and UTHEC. Now, the Empire's main trade partner is Kyeron, its only lifeland-based neighbor. 

### Companies

To operate within the Empire, companies must be chartered within the Kidhmatac. Further, to operate within any specific province, a company must obtain the operating license from the Soris of that province. 

To ensure legal protection, companies may either:

- Hire a registered militia company group
- Register with the local guardsmen of Soris control
- Register with the Professional Guard
- Fund their own guards (and go through the related bureaucracy with both the Kidhmatac to obtain limited armament licenses).
- Add themselves to the list of properties protected by another company's limited armament license.

A company that does neither of these will not have any assigned guards to help protect their shop. The policing system is limited. Private Theft is the purview of the local guardsmen, and once an item is gone they will likely not investigate or prosecute if it will be a challenge for them. 

### State-controlled economy

The Empire maintains some control over key industries such as mining, weaponry, and agriculture through different registered systems. The Empire has a number of Kidhmatac companies given certain unique rights by the bureaucracy headed by the Sarvardar. The Soris maintains their own host of companies tied to their provincial system, but these are more similar to regular companies with certain provincial rights. 

These rights are usually tax exemptions, bureaucracy workarounds, or exclusive resource rights. There is no precedent as to whether Kidhmatac companies or the Soris have the final say for exclusive resource rights, tax exemptions from the other's system, or bureaucratic exceptions from the other's systems. Usually, either respects the systems of the other, and when this status quo is challenged conflict follows. 

In general, it pays to have the favour of the Sarvadar, and therefore the Shalun. However, these matters are often too frivolous for the Sarvadar, but instead is handled by an organisation specifically created to negotiate in such issues. It is legitimised by the Sarvadar. Soft power and favour goes a long way.

### Taxation

Much like anything in the Empire, there are a few different systems. 

The primary source of money for the Empire and its bureaucracy is a land tax. The Cadastral Kidhmatac maintains a large set of records on land plots and their value. The Cadastral Kidhmatac's surveys happen maybe once a century - they are highly political affairs. The main goal of the Cadastral Kidhmatac is on collecting the cadastarl tax. The last cadastral survey was upon the completion of the conquest of Terlaye, but with the southern revolts and Casnih's map there is talk of standardising the cadastral survey into a decade affair. 

There also exists the Excise Kidhmatac, an organisation focused entirely on chartering the sale and movement of goods. They work closely with the professional guard to prevent piracy and illegal tax-averting goods transfer. The tax is often levied on the trader, although this is a recent push by the last few Sarvadars. They determined traders had far too much wealth. This is also contributing heavily to the trend of traders "settling" by investing in industry, however the southern revolts have greatly reduced confidence and restarted trade interest. 

Although the provinces are assigned a section of the tax budget, they also often collect their own taxes. The exact form of tax, and on who it is collected, depends on the province. Usually, this is some form of tax farming where the Soris sends out tax collectors to random areas to collect money. These systems are perfectly legal and highly exploitative. 

Sometimes, nobles will also tax the people on their land in the way Soris do. The dynastic nobles already have some exemptions. Some exemptions are vague, and whether the dynasties must pay tax to the Soris is unclear.

## Noble Dynasties

The Empire's core positions of power are still held by old noble dynasties from before the Reimigs were granted Shalûn and before they finished conquering the entirety of Ralean. These dynasties maintain their estates as private property and wield considerable influence in the bureaucracy, as Soris, and in the military.

Though many of Ralean's dynasties have lost their traditional privileges of control over their state to the Empire's centralisation efforts, they still act as regional power brokers in service of the Emperor. In return for maintaining local peace and providing military aid in the form of command and private armies, they enjoy generous degrees of political autonomy over their estates.

In contrast, Terlayan nobles have been strained. Many of Terlayan's nobles have been forced into abdication, and those that have sworn to serve Shalûn have still been forced to connect with Ralean's dynasties through marriages and the exchange of property. Despite this, they remain present and often contradictory to the Shalûn and their goals.

## Final Word

The Empire of Ralean is an integrally complex structure where the various facets of the system are constantly in opposition, and its important to note that what's described here is still a broad overview. It relies on the important understandings of the Emperor's keys to power, being the members of the Empiric Council, but each of these members are more or less free to form their own structures underneath that are not devised here. # How the Ralean Empire destabilised Terlaye’s Economy
Terlaye’s economy is broken. You can tell when certain cities need supplies from others. In other regions, every city is self-sufficient. Supplied for food by surrounding farms, supplied for growth by nearby quarries, supplied for work with armouries, smiths and nearby iron mines. It’s true that certain resources are usually needed of other areas, like certain minerals, maybe no-one has found an iron deposit close enough to the city.

when the Ralean Empire conquered the lands of Terlaye nearly a century ago, they did it through blood and sweat. A family claim was given to the Empire of Ralean, as previously the two kingdoms had intermarried, and since the Terlaye government had been replaced with another blood line. This gave the Ralean Empire a true claim, to restore the control of the original Terlaye blood to the throne of Terlaye.

After the battles were long and hard-fought, the lands of Terlaye came under the control of the Ralean Empire. The Empire knew that Terlaye might soon revolt, so they set up systems to ensure that they would remain in power for a long time. They came up with a system that would ensure their control over Terlaye.

They would make it so that every Terlaye city relied on another to properly function. This meant that food would have to be traded between cities, tools would come from the other side of the country. This may just seem organised but in practice, conquer one and the rest fall. This meant that even if one city were to rebel and become independent, they would not be able to sustain themselves for long. Even if the entirety of Terlaye would fall, they would not be able to restabilise their economy before Ralean was able to take one city.

They truly secured their control by being the ones to provide efficient caravans for Terlaye. They built better roads, they provided soldiers to guard caravans. Overall, they’ve been a haven for greedy traders who make profit off this reliant system. Even if another area invades them, they can still supply Terlaye cities using Ralean homeland goods.
