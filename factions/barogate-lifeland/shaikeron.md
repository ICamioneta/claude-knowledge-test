# Government

The government of Shaikeron is often called a Cavilldom. This term originates from a political analyst by the name of "Guxlauff Henryk" who detailed the ways in which clans and the church organised themselves in Kyeron in 2900, and named this form of government on the Cavillshulae. Government in Kyeron has never managed to be a well centralised thing, it relies instead on the conservative enforcement of an ever-evolving status quo between the powers that be. 

A Cavillshula (Cavill River) is a river that begins in Gordolmonre and flows westwards towards the Cadole ocean. There are three Cavillshulae in Kyeron. They hold regional importance due to their wealth and infrastructure, but are not tied to the religion in any way.

The Cavilldom has been the system of government in Kyeron arguably as early as 2300, but it is uncritical to consider this the same government as the modern government, or even at all related to the government presented in Henryk's original document.

## Religion

Unavoidable in discussion on the government of Kyeron is the topic of Religion. The modern Ximuz religion dates back to holy books and tales popular around the 1500s. It's emergence after the 5005th Cyfun Annual are attributed to the already highly spiritual people of Kyeron searching for spirituality amidst the confusion of the events of the distribution of the True Nature of the Gods. 

The religious book is a collection of accounts and legends told by various sources of the prophet Mana, who spoke of a soon-to-be-born mortal who would alone have the power to rival the Gods (without ascention) and will build a realm in Eternity for the people of Kyeron to live a life of luxury. This soon-to-be-born mortal is called "the messiah". It is common to believe that the messiah will also lord over time, and is therefore aware of all past actions of the people of Kyeron, and has the ability to bring the consciousnesses of the people forwards in time and into their paradise given their piety.

Since its original conception, the religion has become highly organised. The roots of its organisation are around the 1900s, when Aroru was declared a holy city. Religious figures consolidated power around this city until, by 2100, the seat at Aroru became a powerful centre of Kyeron culture and the religion as a whole. A more formal control by the church over the various clans and nations of Kyeron would occur by 2300, owing to the argument that this century saw the birth of the Cavilldom.

## Church Rule

The Cavilldom is primarily identified by church rule and mediation between independent ruling clans.

Land and its ownership is not centrally managed. It is pseudo-legal and often very argumentative. Clans must claim ownership of land and enforce it with their own militaries. These claims are often in dispute. Disputes are often left to sit peacefully unless disputed land becomes valuable for some reason, often through taxable value. 

The church uses this same system, however due to its military might and cultural significance, its own internal documents are respected over clan claims. Historically, the church has not needed to fight to gain land. Instead, it is often handed to them freely by the clans for one of several reasons. 

The first is piety; constructing a chapel is a necessity for any town, and the land of that chapel is then automatically owned by the church. When this has happened, the chapel often claims for itself generous amounts of land to form its estate. 

The second is land claim; building a chapel on disputed land is a way to gain legitimacy on your claim. Once the church gains control of the land, the chapel will declare in its own records ownership of the disputed lands to the founding clan. Clan wars have sometimes been around the foundation of new chapels. While it is generally frowned upon to attack a church under construction, many clans view the disputed land as valuable enough to warrant the reputation hit. This has also caused construction races, where two clans attempt to build a chapel quicker than the other, however shoddily built chapels won't be accepted as legitimate by the church.

The third is favour and legitimacy. Much like piety, clans have historically just granted the church estates to gain their favour or to legitimise their current clan's rule. Land given to the church is almost impossible to get back, so over hundreds of years the church has simply come to own a significant share of all Kyeron's land.

The holy city of Aroru is a metropolis entirely governed by the church. The largest continuous area of land owned by the Church is the great beach, which is a large sandy beach that stretches homogeneously across a specific section of Kyeron coast like a desert. The beach is considered to have religious significance, and is most likely formed by winds that carry sand particles from the other side of the ocean to this specific spot. It may also have had some Eternal intervention in its creation. 

The very reason why the churches are so powerful is because their ownership over land is strong, while clans claims are weak in comparison. So long as strong records are maintained, even land abandoned by the church is still owned by it (although it is customary to be lenient if that land is to be developed by non-church forces). 

## Status Quo Legitimacy

A core part of the Cavilldom is that there is no centralised body that holds legitimacy. Trust in the church is enforced entirely by peer consensus. Consequently, the church holds a very exclusive relationship with documenting. Only documents by the church are truly recognised. While internal clan documents may be recognised by the given clan, these are often seen as illegitimate without church support or church copy. For example:

- Church-dated events such as treaties, coronations, and land charters.
- Archives of land claims, lineage, and pacts. 
- Marriage, adoption, the legitimisation of heirs.

This was largely the system outlined by Henryk's original document. Since its release and popularisation, it has been retrospectively mythologised and is often seen as a foundational document to the system of government. 

## The Clans

The clans themselves are somewhat indistinguishable from dynastic families of warlords. Throughout history, a number of clans have gained and lost territory. They have split and merged. 

Each of these clans has a different internal organisational structure. Some are purely dynastic, whereas many maintain extended "families" that includes lax adoption of people who promise to be useful to the clan. Within this, the clans may have a single autocratic leader who has strict rules in lineage, or they may host a more democratic system of organisation. 

There are common axes to clan governance. Certain of these axes can change over lifetimes, depending on leadership. Some axes have several of their values exposed at once for a given clan. For example, legitimacy source often changes depending on leadership and legitimacy source often depends on a few different sources rather than only one.

Here are the axes of clan governance:

- Legitimacy Source. Each clan believes its right to rule comes from a certain source. It also generally defines how the clan interacts with the church.
	- Sacral source is belief in legitimacy through church recognition. 
	- Lineal believes in ancestry and mythic descent. 
	- Martial legitimacy is the belief through might. 
	- Contractual legitimacy is often idealised on oath and agreement. It's weak.
	- Pragmatic legitimacy often views legitimacy on a more material front. 
- Succession mechanism. This is how leadership changes hands between clan members.
	- Primogeniture. The eldest child of the previous leader. 
	- Selection. The internal ruling mechanism has its own rules for selecting a successor e.g. autocratic leaders may select whomever they want.
	- Oligarchy. Notable clan members forming the oligarchy vote amongst themselves for a leader. Democratic process varies. 
	- Meritocracy. The clan structure resembles a bureaucracy, where a leader is often unstably chosen based on some merit-based criteria.
	- Contest. A leader's leadership is constantly under contest and may be challenged at any time.
- Internal Power Distribution. Who in the clan actually makes the decisions? Defines a clan's internal mobility and conflict resolution norms.
	- Autocratic. The leader rules all absolutely, the clan exists to serve the wills of the leader. The leader and their appointees often solve internal clan disputes.
	- Council-dominated. The leader is heavily constrained by the will of a council lead by elders or warlords. Internal disputes are handled by the council members with larger disputes prompting assembly. 
	- Bureaucratic. Offices and bureaucratic roles matter more than other clan structures. Often includes judicial systems that handle internal disputes.
	- Diffuse. A clan's power is highly decentralised and its members rule with relative independence. Generally dominated by factional struggles or church / leader mediation.
- Social Mobility. Who is allowed to become a member of the clan.
	- Closed. A strict hereditary dynasty where disinheritance means exile from the clan.
	- Adpotive. A more generous hereditary dynasty that often adopts for personal or meritocratic reasons. Disinheritance doesn't mean anything unless there is succession of a certain type.
	- Service. Captains, mayors, and bishops are welcomed to join the clan's organisation.
	- Mass. Anyone promisingly not a peasant, worker, lowlife, or layperson may enter the clan.

## Church Organisation

This document will use words that mirror the catholic church. I will eventually come up with my own words for things. To be very explicit, the exact roles and rituals of the Kyeron church are different, so this organisation is more to help officially give structure to Kyeron's church. 

Central to the church's organisation are the priestly orders. These orders are strictly hierarchical. From the top:

- Shai: The semi-elected, semi-hereditary leader of the church. The spiritual leader of all Kyeron and the lord mayor of Aroru.
- Cardinals: Provincial dictators of Aroru's provinces and members of the sacred college of cardinals. They must assist the Shai with their duties, especially when it comes to governing the metropolis of Aroru.
- Archbishops: Govern large metropolitan areas and provinces called archdioceses. The archdioceses are usually irrespective of clan borders and purely administrative for the church.
- Bishops. Govern regions known as dioceses. They are numerous and often very involved with governance and spiritual organisation. A load-bearing layer for the clergy.
- Priests. Serve one parish, priests carry the most local relevance and serve as cornerstones of all communities. They are often more detached from church organisation and serve clan-owned land as much as they do their own church estates. 
- Deacons. Ministers and managers of church estates with no priest-level significance, such as those estates far from towns and villages or independent estates among a town already served by a priest. They are ordained to perform many rituals of the church.
- Members. Non-ordained members of the church are those sworn to serve the church. They are often not considered to be ruled by any clan. They are people like nuns and monks. 

The church is often idealistically portrayed as calm and united. In reality, its members vouch for power in one of the only systems with social mobility in Kyeron.

## Modern Disorganisation

As of 3300, the church has lost the favour of many clans, who became cynical of church rule and the organisation of the Cavilldom as a whole. In the modern day (3480), this movement and its sentiments are widespread across Kyeron and threaten a nationwide civil war between the loyalists and separatists. 

This is not viewed with significant concern by loyalists, who have many similar historical instances of rebellions against the church to point to. Separatists will emphasise the changed modern character of this specific rebellion. Regardless, both loyalists and separatists maintain the conservative status quo of church rule, even if one side does so reluctantly. 

Historically, separatist movements have always targeted the church's monopoly over documentation. They've attempted to establish new Kyeron-wide bureaucratic organisations, however these were always abandoned when a clan found the church's documents more agreeable or favourable.
