# Government

The Domuz Republic is a unified republic made up of three main culturally distinct regions - Gazi, Lale, and Darie - along with a collection of colonial holdings. The government is based in [All](/wastelife/settlements/gazi-lale-darie/all), but devolved governments exist for West Lale and Gazi in [Eseia](/wastelife/settlements/gazi-lale-darie/eseia) and [Easea](/wastelife/settlements/gazi-lale-darie/easea) respectively. It's government is sometimes referred to as a Maritime Republic to reflect its lack of a dedicated land army and unique military organisation.

Alongside colonial governments, which have wildly varying governments, the devolved states handle matters related to culture, education, local economic development, and regional infrastructure, while the central government handles the rest. 

The republic, established after the Domuz Revolution in 3189, replaced the monarchy with a highly federated democracy. However, this system was reformed in 3324 to centralise power for governmental and developmental efficiency. Some federated states instead became devolved territories.

## The Central Government

The Central Government manages the vast majority of the Government's functions, with devolved states managing matters of culture, education, local economic development, and regional infrastructure.

### Presidency

The president holds executive power as head of state and head of government. The president is elected alongside the national assembly based on wealth voting, where votes of citizens are based on tax contribution. This gives economically successful citizens more influence in government by design, leading to a government with the interests of industrialists and businesses at heart.

The responsibilities of the president include:

- **Proposing Legislation:** the president is allowed to introduce laws or reforms to the National Assembly for debate and voting. While the president does not get a direct vote they have significant power in the law-making processes.
- **Limited Veto Power:** the president can veto laws passed by the national assembly, but this veto is overridden by two-thirds majority vote in the house of representatives or by over half majority in the senate.
- **Admiral Marshal:** The president oversees the military and national security, gaining the high rank of Admiral Marshal. With the republic's naval-focussed military structure, Admiralty is the highest rank and ground forces are organised around the naval forces.
- **Appointing Ministers:** The president appoints the council of ministers, who help the president run the country, focussing on certain aspects of governance. The ministers report to the president, but are accountable to the National Assembly for their decisions and policies.

### Ministers

The **Council of Ministers** is the president's executive team, managing specific aspects of government and policy. Ministers are appointed by the president and can be dismissed at the president's discretion. However, the president must gain some approval from National Assembly for significant appointments such as Minister of Defence and the Minister of Finance.

The Council of Ministers is a number of positions appointed by the president. This body oversees a number of national governance matters. 

- **Minister of Defence** responsible for national security, colonial defence, and military presence in frontier territories.
- **Minister of Finance** who oversees taxation, the national budget, and economic policy.
- **Minister of Foreign Affairs** manages diplomatic relations and foreign policy with colonies and their native populations, the neighbour states of Baron-Gazi and Oedreci, and relations with Shai Keron and the Lailon Aredvics
- **Minister of Colonial Affairs** handles the governance, coordination, and development of the colonies ensuring they align with the republic's policies.
- **Minister of Culture** ensures the promotion of the Republic's cultural identity while balancing the preservation of Lale Darie's diverse cultural practices.

and others. The president chairs the council.

### National Assembly

The National Assembly is a bicameral legislature with two chambers: the House of Representatives and the Senate. The house of representatives is elected by the population, while the senate provides equal representation to different cultural regions. The assembly passes laws, while the senate focusses on regional concerns.

#### House of Representatives

Representatives in this chamber are elected every six years in line with the Presidency through a proportional representation system, where seats are allocated based on the population of elective regions. This house focusses on passing national legislature and overseeing government operations. As the larger of the two chambers, the house is primarily responsible for:

- Approving budgets and taxes
- Proposing and voting on domestic and economic legislation
- Providing oversight to the executive branch of government through hearings and inquiries

Laws typically originate in the house of representatives, where they are debated and voted on. If a bill passes the house, it moves on to the Senate for further considerations.

#### Senate

The senate is designed to represent the cultural regions of the republic. Each cultural group, usually divided by islands, selects a number of senators:

- Iophelite gets 4 senators
- Lale Deornite, Darieddite, and Gazaite get 3 senators each
- Feixienite, Ephuesanite, and Oenesite get 2 senators each
- Lephiolite, Dalmawite, Seosite, Spaerite, and Gharite get 1 senator each

representation is not directly proportional to population, rather it ensures that each culture's voice is heard. This ensures that smaller groups are protected and not overrun by majority groups while majority groups still have the most senators. There are 24 seats in total

The senate focusses on regional and cultural concerns within the central government, acting as a safeguard for the republic's unity. It also reviews and advises on laws passed by the House of Representatives, ensuring that these laws do not infringe on the autonomy or rights of the republic's distinct cultural groups.

- The senate may amend or delay legislation to ensure minority cultures and devolved regions are not disproportionately affected
- The senate can also propose laws that deal with regional governance, infrastructure, and cultural preservation for regions within the Central Government.

The Senate's seats are often grouped into collaborating groups that make sure minority voices are heard without the larger cultures being able to ignore individual demands.

- **North Laleite** group is the promise between Oenesite, Dalmawite, Seosite, Gahrite, and Spaerite groups to support each others concerns. It shares 6 seats
- **Subaquatic** group is the promise between Lale Deornite, Lephiolite, Dalmawite, Oenesite, and Gazaite communities to support each others concerns, especially in relation to underwater communities. it shares 10 seats
- **South Darie** group is the promise between Feixienite, Ephuesanite, and Lale Deornite (who is not from the geographical region but supports the group) to support each others concerns. It shares 8 seats

### Judiciary

The judicial branch is headed by the Supreme Court, which ensures that laws passed by the Assembly adhere to the constitution. The courts can arbitrate disputes between devolved governments and the central government, as well as disputes involving colonial territories. The supreme court are also permitted to change the constitution, which happens semi-frequently.

### Legislative Process

1. **Initiation:** Most bills are proposed in the house of representatives, but they can also be introduced by the president, ministers, and the senate. 
2. **Debate in the House:** Representatives debate, amend, and vote on the bill. Each voting round, representatives are given three options: approve, delay, deny. Voting rounds continue until the end of the maximum consideration period, or the bill is approved or denied in majority.
3. **Senate Review:** The Senate then scrutinises the bill further with the idea that they assess the cultural impact of the bill. The senate can approve the bill and send it across the house for signing. They can amend the bill and send it back to the house for debating. They can also reject the bill, where it is also sent back to the house. 
4. If the bill passes between the senate and the house three times, the house sends 24 members to the senate to form a conciliation committee. If no conciliation is reached and the senate still rejects the bill, the house can override the senate's rejection with a two-thirds majority vote. 
5. At any point in the process, the president can veto the bill, temporarily putting the bill on hold for amendment by them and the council of ministers. The president then sends it back to the house with their recommendations and changes. The veto can be overridden with two-third majority house vote, or a simple majority senate vote.

## Regional Government

Two regions have devolved governments responsible for handling local matters with their own methods separate from the Central Government. These matters include:

- Culture and language preservation
- Education and schooling
- Local taxation and resource allocation for infrastructure
- Policing and public order within limits defined by national law

Although regional governments are usually founded on the basis of cultural differences requiring a unique institute for each of these things, the central government does a good job at providing these things for the diverse cultures it directly controls. 

The four devolved governments are Deor (otherwise known as West Lale), Gazi, Lephiol, and Dalmaw. Devolved regional governments have their own local assemblies headed by a governor. They have specific local powers which are constitutionally protected.

Regional election's terms depend on the region. Deor and Gazi have 4-year periods, while Lephiol and Dalmaw have 5-year periods.

## Colonial Governance

The colonies are divided into several distinct categories with varying degrees of autonomy from the central governance.

### Imperial Colonies

These colonies are tightly controlled by the Domuz Republic and its central government. They have locally elected councils, but their governors are appointed by the president of national assembly. The imperial colonies remain economically and militarily integrated with the republic. The two imperial colonies are Mepheisatha and Yphofthle.

### Self-Governing Colonies

These territories have a high degree of independence especially in local governance, law enforcement, and economic policy. Companies operating within these colonies largely handle governance through private means. However, the republic and the presidency maintain a vested interest in these areas, sometimes operating state industries in the areas. The two self-governing colonies are Cyrox and Asthenel.

### Frontier Colonies

Due to their location in Llyhai, a hostile but resource-rich area, these colonies are militarily governed. Frontier colonies are highly autonomous in terms of defence and resource extraction, but answer directly to the Republic's central military command. The frontier colonies are Isles of Mourn, Bhethfei, Fauson, Fanthel, and Phemfbharei.

### Autonomous Territories

These colonies are entirely self-governing, often due to geographic isolation. Through nominally under the Domuz Republic's sovereignty, they manage their own affairs, including military and economic policies. The autonomous territories are Baron-Gazi, Oedreci, and a rumoured but not yet confirmed third colony established on the continent Mothdao.

## Political Parties

Although there is no strict definition in the system, political parties have formed to represent the interests of certain groups, Political parties' own organisations differ, but they all have a leader. Generally, political parties will propose their leader for presidency, and parties with significant presence in the House of Representatives historically have their leader appointed as Minister of Finance. 

### Progress Party

The Progress Party is a party representing industrial interests. They are pro-business, support economic liberalism, and want a technocratic governance. They are one of the more powerful political parties, consistently electing their leader in the position of president. 

### Cultural Conservationists

The Cultural Conservationists is a political party seeking regional autonomy and decentralisation. They are popular in devolved regions and regions of a minority culture. Members of this party easily attain senate positions.

### National Unity Party

The National Unity Party is a party who want centralisation of government and national unity, with respect to minority cultures. They support a strong centralised government without devolution and with minimal colonial autonomy. While they don't advocate for assimilation of minority cultures, they want a single national identity to be recognised with its sub-cultures respected in their own right. They prioritise the importance of national infrastructure projects, and believe that a unified political and economic system is key to the republic's success. 

They are another powerful party, often getting many representatives in the house and are often in opposition to the progress party and the cultural conservatives. Their head is often Minister of Finance.

### Colonial Freedom Party

The Colonial Freedom Party is a populist party that advocates for "freedom for the colonies", which attracts the uneducated who believe the colonies should be more independent and devolved for cultural regions *and* industrialists who want more control over colonial resource extraction away from government control.

### Sailor's party

The Sailor's Party is a small party with a large grassroots base representing the workers and peasantry, with a focus on naval folk who are the backbone of internal trade. 

# Military

The Domuz Republic is a maritime nation with its entire military built around the navy. Given how it hasn't had any land-based threats since the Nvxin-Chalsie treaty of 2831, this seems justified as almost any land operations occur with direct assistance of the navy in transporting and landing ground troops. All military branches operate under the command of naval officers, ensuring seamless coordination between sea and land forces.

**The Navy** is the central military institution of the Domuz Republic, controlling all aspects of defence, offense, and colonial protection. Ground forces such as marines and coastal garrisons fall under naval command and are treated as extensions of naval operations.

## Structure

### Admiral Marshal

The President holds the mostly ceremonial rank of Admiral Marshal, symbolising the direct relationship between the navy and the highest office of government. While the president does not engage in day-to-day operations, they do get the final say on major military decisions, and the admiralty report directly to the president.

### Admiral

Admiralty is the highest governing body in the military, overseeing military activities on both sea and land. Admirals control fleets and ground forces in their operational regions with each admiral responsible for both naval and army operations where they are assigned. 

Admirals command a fleet and associated ground forces in their assigned area. Admirals make strategic decisions on how to deploy their forces for naval and land-based operations.

There are four admirals in the navy:

- **Admiral of the Home Fleet** who is stationed in Gazi Lale Darie and responsible for immediate protection of the homelands.
- **Admiral of the Colonial Fleet** who oversees operations across colonial regions, primarily stationed in Llyhai.
- **Admiral of the Trade Fleet** who is generally concerned with protecting trade routes and managing fully autonomous colonies. Because of the widespread nature of operations, the Admiral of the Trade Fleet gives lots of autonomy to their commodores and captains.
- **Admiral of the Reserve Fleet** who manages logistical support, shipbuilding, and maintains a solid reserve fleet in case of emergencies if support is required by another fleet.

### Commodore

Commodores command squadrons of ships within larger fleets.

### Captain

Captain of an individual ship.

### Naval Officer.

A ranked officer on a ship with specific ship-based duties.

### Marine General

under Admirals, coordinating the army of the fleet during ground operations.

### Marine Captain

Often second-hand men of ship captains, commanding ground battalions forces tied to the ship.

### Marine Officers

In control of companies, the smaller divisions of the ground forces tied to larger ship battalions.

### Port Commander

Port Commanders command ports tied to a specific ship. Officially, that port is the home base of the individual ship. Realistically, these ports are quite scattered, and while ships can return to their home port they often dock in larger ports with the rest of their fleet or squadron.

## Colonies in Dithas

The Domuz Federation operates four colonial administrations in dithas, the region being the primary focus of Domuz imperialism since Nvxin-Chalsie. Most of these colonial states operate on a habitual and extractional basis, although Dithas is well known for its mineral barrenness, some parts of the region do sport useful resources. Many parts of Dithas are already inhabited by the Dimad, who aren’t always appreciative of the Domuz’s habitations.

### Mepheisatha

Mepheisatha, the city and the administration, is the largest by dry land area and operates deep into the Dithas’s forest. It is a long-standing and well habited part of Dithas, with roads established and the large city of Mepheisatha.

### Yphofthle

Yphofthle is often called “Lesser Lale Darie” because geographically it appears to be a copy of the east-leaning island shape of Lale Darie, and even has shapes mimicking a small Dithas. The region’s main city is Baron All, which is located in the expected position on the similar geography. The region is somewhat well established, but all other settlements in the administration are small.

### Cyrox

The Cyrox region is not as established as the previous two, but it does operate a fair few of resource-extracting settlements inland. The population of Cyrox is distributed relatively evenly between the settlements, with no single settlement standing out in terms of population.

### Asthenel

Asthenel is a more recent addition to the Domuz Federation’s regime. The City of Asthenel sits on Dithas’s border with Duela. The city acts as a station for those traveling into the Duela. It is also a trade and cultural hub, with over half of its citizens being [Fall](fall.md) peoples. The region is actually controlled by the Fall there, as they are a majority in the population.

## Colonies in Llyhai

The Domuz Federation operates a continued struggle against the dangers of Llyhai, drawn by its resource-rich surface. Populations in Llyhai are extremely low, and every person has to balance their work life and their defensive life. Llyhai is full of threats. Many of those threats are capable of laying waste to entire towns. Some can lay waste to entire cities. If you’re settlement isn’t prepared and ready to defend against town-ending threats, then end it will. If its not ready to pack up and run against city-ending threats, then end it will. Llyhai is also full of riches, easy to find but challenging to extract. Every person must be a good defender, and a good worker.

### Isles of Mourn

The Isles of Mourn is a colonial settlement and one of the two first Llyhai settlements. It is named so because of all the death and destruction brought there, in mourning of all the brave fallen Domuz who lost to Llyhai’s harsh environment, flora and fauna. The Isles of Mourn has no permanent settlement, but there is a large community of settlers on the larger island that rotate around the island hitting a few good spots. There are also a few communities on the mainland.

### Bhethfei

Bhethfei is a collection of communities and a single settlement that’s attempting permanence and has succeeded for about 120 years. The City, called City Bhethfei, is yet to face a city-destroying threat. Some of this is due to a number of sacrifices made to deter or draw away those threats from the path of the city.

### Fanthel

Fanthel is a number of communities present on the northmost islands of Llyhai. There used to be a permanent city, but it has since been laid to ruin. The people who continue on that island live in fear from the city-ending threat that caused City Fanthel to be lost.

### Fauson

Fauson is a small and recently created community of Llyhai settlers

### Phemfbharei

Phemfbharei is a community with a large underwarer presence, but the subaquatic surface experiences the same dangers as the overground Llyhai, so no permanent settlement is possible still.
