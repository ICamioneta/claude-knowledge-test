# Modern Perceptions of the Gods
It has been over 1500 years since the 5005th Cyfun Annual and the people of Barogate have had ample time to react. Most people and governments have come to see the Gods as the forces of nature they are largely described as in the pamphlets resulting from the 5005th Cyfun Annual. These beliefs have been re-inforced by Medr's association with the former bandit-king turned God Leider. They were re-enforced within recent memory when Medr associated with Tharn, associating the God of Skill with the deaths of over a million Shildoans and the re-animation of multiple millions of corpses. 

The nature of the gods is now clear and publicly known: they are aspects of the natural world. They do not define it, and they did not create it. Rather, the natural world creates them. Their morality is also clear: the gods are only concerned with the "expansion of their domain" irrespective of mortal moral values. Another of the oldest gods who has historically been incredibly collaborative with mortals, the God of Knowledge Adnabyd, is open about his history of immoral actions against mortals in the interest of expanding his libraries of knowledge. 

In the modern era, now that we are aware of the true nature of the gods, steps have been taken to broker some sort of agreement about the affairs of gods and the morality of mortals. During the aforementioned necromancer uprising, Medr had made promises to the Shildoans that she would not ascend Tharn to godhood. Medr, in the interest of her own domain alone, worked with the hero in the development of his unique skills. While working with Tharn to improve his necromancy, she was also working with the hero who wished to destroy him. 

It is through this relationship that mortals have some control over the actions of the gods. If we refuse collaboration with gods that act immorally, then we can stop the gods doing things we deem immoral. The gods know that if they are seen as immoral, they will loose moral collaborators. For the gods, then, their actions walk a fine balance between their own interests and the common moralities of people. 

# Modern Theisms
With the dissolution of the churches as organisations of god worship, the spirituality of people has been lacking somewhat. Many people still proclaim themselves to a god's domain, although there are barely any gods without poor moral considerations. The most common example of this are the temples of healing and the temples of hospitality. While the temples of hospitality have generally been disbanded in favour of commercial institutions of hospitality that are in some way subsidised by Ysbiti and his Esten, Adferiad's Temple of Healing has seen almost no organisational change. The most significant change to the temple of healing is that patrons no longer prey to Adferiad, rather they thank the workers of the temple.

With this general increase of education, many have proposed the existence of the "Hidden God". This belief comes from those educated on the existence of realms - pockets of reality within Eternity. Every realm is connected to some god, all apart from the Mortal Realm which is said to have predated even the gods. The mortal realm is then an outlier in the idea of realms, making many believe that it was created by a god that predates all of the gods, the hidden god. 

Some proponents of the Hidden God believe that this god is known to the known gods, but is kept a secret from mortals for whatever reason. Others propose that the hidden god is hiding from everyone for their own purposes. People in opposition to this belief cite how most Gods have Esten who are immortal workers, and this hidden god would have no Esten. There is also the matter that the known gods predominantly interact with their own realm, the Eternal City (Cyfun's realm), and the mortal realm, infrequently venturing into each other's realms. 

Another modern theistic belief is in the God of Gods, the "Supergod". This would be a god that exists above the gods we know of. A genuine creator of the mortal realm and of all the other gods. This belief is challenged by the lack of evidence, but it is also unfalsifiable. The God of Knowledge, when proposed with this theory, stated that they see no reason why we should believe that there is a god above the gods when the situation of that god existing is identical to the situation of that god not existing. 

There are two other modern theisms and they are fairly similar. The first is the belief that the collective mortal consciousness is the god of the mortal realm. This relies on unknown matters of history, such as if the Muz peoples were in fact the first peoples. It is known that the Muz were created by the original six gods, they have provided ample evidence for this and the archaeological evidence is in support of their claims. 

There is however clear archaeological indications of intelligent civilisation predating the gods discovery of the mortal realm: the silver ruins dotted around all highlands of the mortal world. What happened to this civilisation is a mystery, but it is clear that it was inhabited by people with lives as complex as our own. The belief is that these precursor peoples were the original collective mortal consciousness, the split form of the God who created the mortal realm. 

The final modern theism is Ews mysticism. The belief is that the Ews are the god of the mortal realm. We are aware of the creative capacity that an individual Ysgews has, and how when forming connections through the Wire they form a semi-collective consciousness through forum. The idea of Ews mysticism is that the Ews were the original creators of the mortal realm, but this god-consciousness has been split between the continents and then severed further by the actions of other mortals and their deforestations. 

Regardless of Ews mysticism, large forests such as Codia are often described as having their own will that the Ysgews merely follow. In Ews mysticism, the forest would then be the god of the mortal realm, and the Ysgews their Esten. While the Ysgews are most certainly mortal, proponents of Ews mysticism claim that if the whole world were a forest of connected Ysgews they would become immortal. 

Aside from traditional theisms, there exists the cult of the cavilldoms, which believe a messiah who is soon to be born to mortal parents will inherently have powers that surpass those of the gods. This future messiah will lord over the gods and time itself, and will therefore judge the actions of all people retrospectively. The belief is that this messiah runs existence in a sort of super-realm of their own, and that other super-realms are a heaven and a hell which he puts the consciousnesses of deceased mortals. 

# Conclusions and Thoughts
It is the general belief of the known gods that there are no entities above them. As beings with a greater capacity for consciousness, who have lived thousands of mortal lifetimes and who are generally the most proficient in their domain, I am inclined to believe them. It is Adnabyd's belief that we should focus on understanding the natural world rather than get lost in theology once more, especially as the entire purpose of the gods exposing their true nature was so that mortals could act naturally as themselves without the influence of a faux god. 

Each of the theistic ideas discussed in this text are certainly interesting thoughts, but they are either contradicted by current knowledge or their truth has no effect on the natural world. It would make sense for society to continue down its atheistic path, where the gods are known not as divine entities but forces of nature of their own domain.
