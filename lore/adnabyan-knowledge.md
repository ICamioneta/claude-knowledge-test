An overview of the topic of Adnabyan Knowledge and the need for distinction. 

Throughout our developmental history, our cultures have taken the words of the Gods as the implicit truth. Actors of the gods need only prove their divine capability through a creation that mortals are not capable of to be legitimised. Then, what they claim is true is trusted. We have trusted that the gods were the utmost masters of their domain, and that the words they speak are true.

With the advent and popularisation of the scientific principle, truth demands observable and repeatable facts. While the god's words have pointed us towards observable and repeatable facts, they are not infallible. Adnabyd, the God of Knowledge, claims that he and his Esten follow principles similar to the scientific method to verify the truth of information collected in his library. As the mortal scientific community, we cannot trust this information without first verifying the truth for ourselves. 

Adnabyd's library of truths carries some weight. On matters of the physical sciences, Adnabyd's truths have always been verified. Almost every scholar trusts Adnabyd's truths on the physical scientists. However, in the name of scientific rigour, we cannot truly accept these into the body of mortal works without additional verification. In recognition of their source, we call Adnabyd's truths that have not been sufficiently verified by mortals _Adnabyan Knowledge_. 

The concept extends beyond Adnabyd. Adnabyan knowledge refers to information obtained by directly convening with trusted gods. Adnabyan-Esten knowledge is a subset of information gained from the Esten of the gods and not the god themselves. Most Adnabyan knowledge is truly Adnabyan-Esten due to how more frequent the Esten interact with people in place of the gods.

**Categorisations of Adnabyan Knowledge**

All Adnabyan knowledge falls into four categories. 

Confirmed Adnabyan knowledge are claims from an Adnabyan source that the scientific community has independently verified. The knowledge ceases to be Adnabyab: the results of the reviews are adopted into scientific literature. 

Unconfirmed Adnabyan knowledge is knowledge from an Adnabyan source we are aware we can confirm but have not yet scrutinised. We should not accept unconfirmed Adnabyan knowledge as scientific consensus. We should use it to guide our actions. 

Contingent Adnabyan Knowledge is knowledge that could in principle be tested if we had developed some speculative scientific technology that would allow for the claim to be observed. If we ever were to develop these devices of observation, the contingent Adnabyan knowledge becomes unconfirmed Adnabyan knowledge. 

Anecdotal Adnabyan knowledge are claims from Adnabyan sources that are inaccessible to verification, usually due to the passage of time or because they are philosophical in nature. They may be grounded in personal reasoning rather than empirical evidence. 

**Acceptance of Adnabyan Knowledge**

Acceptance of Adnabyan knowledge has been a highly contentious topic within the scientific community. It is usually subject to the relationship the community has with the Adnabyan source. Adnabyd himself has consistently been a reliable repository for scientifically provable truths. We generally accept Adnabyd's Adnabyan knowledge.

Adnabyan knowledge from Sirphni, the God of Lethargy, and her Esten have consistently proven to be incoherent or demonstrably false. It is widely accepted that Sirphni's utterances are unconsidered or deliberately misleading. For another example, Adnabyan knowledge gained from Gwalloph, the God of Madness, has been unpredictable. Contingent Adnabyan claims made by Gwalloph and his Esten have turned out to be both true and false. The true and false statements from Gwalloph are indistinguishable, so we discredit Gwalloph on the grounds of inconsistency. 

Most other gods are somewhere inbetween the self-disciplined rigour of Adnabyd and the lies and ignorance of Sirphni. Every piece of Adnabyan knowledge has to be carefully considered by every individual. Give harsh conditions to the source before you choose to believe in it. Adnabyan knowledge is always based in trust for the god.

**Accounts of History and Eternity**

Anecdotal Adnabyan knowledge is often on history and the events of Eternity. We cannot go back in time and verify accounts of history ourselves, the best we may do is reference the Adnabyan accounts with mortal accounts and weigh the trust we have of both to discover our best narrative. Adnabyd's accounts on history are perhaps the most fallible of all Adnabyd's accounts. They most often contradict highly trusted mortal accounts. 

Adnabyd is honest about the wary truthfulness of his accounts on history. They are most often observations of his Esten, each an individual with their own perspective and biases. Together, Adnabyd hopes that a joint and useful understanding of history may be reached. 

Accounts of Eternity and its history are similar, except they rarely have mortal accounts. The mortal accounts that are available are deliberate witnesses or simply mortals in the god's realms at the time. 

The physical sciences behind Eternity is hard to categorise. Many believe that it is inconcievable that mortals will ever be able to observe the eternity beyond the mortal realm. Others like to reserve the possibility. The accounts of the Gods on Eternity and the Hidol are largely considered anecdotal Adnabyan knowledge, but some reserve the possibility that they may be provable in the future and are thus contingent Adnabyan knowledge.
