For those who have read about the forbidden resurrections, bone binding seems a horrific equivalent. 

The potency of necromagery is in its scalability, how a necromage in his basement may go from having a few undead thralls to terrorising a city with a legion of conduits. The horror of necromagery is its upheaval, the walking lifeless corpses of your friends and family. 

Bone binding is none of this. It is neither scalable nor does it necessarily involve the dead. It is not even a spell: it is an enchantment. Those who conflate it with necromagery betray an ignorance of both.

Bone binding has its origins in power-hungry wizards attempting to recreate the power of the necromage. Lacking any spellcasting skills, they utilised their wealth of knowledge of enchantments. The first successful bone-bind was done by some students in the early medieval era. It is true that they initially practised the enchantments on the skeleton corpses of people. Through new techniques, this is not at all necessary. 

Bone binding creates energetic links between the bones in a skeleton. Through carefully tuning the enchantment, these links can act as muscles and ligaments. Through fine control, these bound bones can be made to stand up, or to act. It is not an easy practice nor is it practical. Bone binding is significantly more difficult than gem casting, and gem golems suit every purpose a wizard might have for bound bones. A seasoned wizard will find it easier to learn how to gem cast the golem they want over learning bone binding. 

It is however deeply interesting, and a creative expression of wizardry. This is why we still practice it to this day. 

Bone binding does not require a corpse, although it traditionally uses one. Bone binders may procure their own corpses ethically. Skeletons without flesh are the usual target of practice, however present flesh creates a more structural body. 

Most modern bone binding wizards will prefer to create their own "skeleton" or "corpse", often out of wood and cloths. These constructed skeletons are far more sightly, and have a potential to be more durable when made out of the right materials. Wizards may even build their own ligaments. The constructed skeleton needs to mimic the body of a person for the bone bind to work normally, and this itself is an art of craft. 

In more bespoke bone-binding circles such as my own based in the City of Detvadei, we raise the funds to commission a local and cooperative smith to forge us steel bones for our most prized bone-binding skeleton. For wizards who are serious about the craft and who are particularly wealthy, a similar project may be proposed to their own smith.
