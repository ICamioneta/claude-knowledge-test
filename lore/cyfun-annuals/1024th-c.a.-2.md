Topic of Discussion: Overly enthusiastic synthesis of mortal means of trade.
Proposed by: Tanea

Opening statement.

**Tanea**
It has come to my attention that some Eternals in our escapades to the Mortal Realm have become accustom to synthesising large quantities of certain minerals rarely found naturally on the mortal world, and proceeding to distribute these materials to mortals in exchange for goods and services without respect for their value to mortals in their societies. Beyond this, instead of de-synthesising the materials upon leaving the mortal realm, some Eternals will leave the materials often in large quantities in the mortal world. 

To be specific, I spent much of my mortal life working hard to be able to afford certain metals and minerals that were rare: gold, silver, tungsten, vocriene, obsidian, oshias, rhenium, as well as several rare "gemstones". As Eternals, we have the ability to synthesise these materials directly from the Hidol with great focus, however as a mortal the only way I could acquire these materials was by exchanging the fruits of my labour for them, trading with other mortals who had aquired the materials through their own methods, eventually tracing back to a mine where these minerals are extracted from the ground with much labour. 

These "rare metals" are rare in the mortal world not necessarily because of their actual presence, but due to how few mines there are extracting the minerals that are close enough to the surface for the mortals to get them. 

In the mortal realm, mortals will tie a certain value to anything and everything. They will then be willing to exchange an item of some value with another to an item of a similar value. Value is to some extent subjective to each mortal. If a mortal does not have much of one resource they will assign it a higher relative value compared to another mortal who has more of that resource. If a mortal then wants that resource, they will seek to give that other mortal something they have in higher quantities and is therefore less valuable to them, in exchange for the object that has a higher relative value to them. 

Often, this process is abstracted into these rare metals. Mortals have agreed that due to scarcity, these rare metals are valuable to all. So, then, a farmer who might have no actual use for silver may exchange a chicken for a piece of silver so that they may then exchange that silver to a tool smith for a new hammer. This is a useful transaction when the tool smith themselves has no use for a chicken. While the tool smith may also have no use for silver, they may then exchange it to someone else. There is no necessary end-point in the transaction of this rare metal, however silversmiths may directly turn their acquired silver into jewellery, exchanging it further. 

What has been happening is that some Eternals have manifested in the Mortal Realm alongside copious amounts of a rare metal, with the understanding that they can use it to trade with mortals. Often, these Eternals will not understand the value of the metal, which changes wildly throughout the decades, and trade a large amount of a rare metal for a relatively mundane object. 

However, this is destructive to the mortal economy. Think of it this way: an Eternal may trade a kilogram of silver for some novel wooden trinket. The mortal would have given that wooden trinket away for a small piece of silver, but hearing that the Eternal is willing to trade a far greater weight of silver for it, they would not object as they could then exchange that kilogram of silver for their equivalent of a thousand small wooden trinkets. 

Think of it this way: if an Eternal traded a hundred silver pieces for a chicken, not understanding the value of a silver piece or a chicken, the mortal could then exchange every one of that hundred pieces for a chicken each. The mortal would then have 99 more chickens because of their exchange with the Eternal. But where did the 99 more chickens come from? 

The answer is that they have come from no-where, but there is now more silver in that circle of trade, devaluing silver and disrupting the mortal society. 

This is in clear violation of a foundational principle of Eternal-mortal interaction: that we may not drastically intervene. Although giving away copious rare metals may seem trivial, it in fact has a far greater impact on the mortal ecosystem than expected.

**Cyfun**
What do you propose as an action we can undertake to remedy your problem?

**Tanea**
At least we should educate all Eternals about this, and enforce it socially as we do our other principles of Eternal-mortal interaction.

**Glinphe**
This is hypocracy! You violate the principles constantly! Are you not aware of your own forged artifacts? Mortals seek them out of tradition, it has fundamentally altered their societies!

**Bwydo**
The mortal greed for shiny things predates Tanea, you know this.

**Glinphe**
My point still stands! While Tanea's artefacts were materially sourced from the mortal world, their enchantments weren't! Those were made after ascension, and there is little difference between creating copious silver and creating powerful enchantments. From a Mortal's perspective, both required significant labour, and a good enchantment on a sword can be worth just as much as a kilogram of silver.

**Tanea**
Are you suggesting I "locally source" my artefact's enchantments? This is impossible, it would take a thousand of the best wizards to perform the enchantments I do, and they do not exist. I also could not hope to normally source the payments for these wizards.

**Glinphe**
Then you should not be able to do them! I call for a stop to Tanea's artefact creation! They have enough!

**Cyfun**
Settle it, this is another discussion. I'll append it to the agenda.

**Glinphe**
It isn't another discussion! If I can't coerce a mortal with heaps of gold, then Tanea shouldn't be allowed to coerce all mortals with the acquisition of his useful artefacts.

**Cyfun**
Other opinions?

**Adnabyd**
It seems to me that mortal society has evolved in such a complex way that we must now be more careful of our interactions with it, perhaps we are to reconsider our interactions with the mortal world entirely. 

**Diofalux**
I have studied the impact that small interactions, both Eternal and Mortal in origin, can have on the outcomes of the future. The interaction between two mortals and a mortal and an Eternal have similar severities.

**Rhif**
I spend most of my time interacting with the mortal trade system and its intricacies. Dumping a kilogram of, say, gold, and for a mortal to find that. It is enough to change the course of their life entirely. From that alone, they could build a manor, buy an army. 

**Ymdig**
But your average person won't know enough on how to do all that. 

**Raifel** 
Gold is not the ultimate power. Status is. Symbolic however it may be, for a king to have the loyalty of an army is enough for them to take an enriched peasant's gold for themselves.

**Glinphe**
Okay, but if Tanea is allowed to distribute his artefacts, Medr is allowed to make mortals more powerful, and Caesi is allowed to destroy fleets of ships with storms of their making, then I should be allowed to explore the limits of mortal greed by synthesising as much gold as I need!

**Tanea**
The original discussion was careless trading, which Glinphe does on purpose but many more Eternals do out of ignorance. I say we impose hard rules on how much trading commodity we are allowed to bring with us into the Mortal Realm.

**Glinphe**
I can accept that, so long as we account for the cost of anything we move to the Mortal Realm, not just rare metals and other common commodities. 

**Dyddiad**
A limit imposed across a calendar year, perhaps. 

**Tanea**
One of my artefacts is worth enough gold to disrupt a small economy, are we to say that I can only release one artefact a year, and that Glinphe may destroy a small market once a year?

**Rhif**
Perhaps you shouldn't be allowed to release artefacts as often as you do, Tanea.

**Tanea**
How about Caesi's storms? How do we value those?

**Adnabyd**
I do not think we should attempt to value every intervention and visit we send to the mortal realm. Perhaps for now it is enough to impose hard limits on traded commodities. 

**Cyfun**
We cannot value every act, so we must choose. We regulate what is measurable, or accept that we're applying rules unevenly and regulate all interaction.

**Glinphe**
Why should we policy synthesised material if Tanea's enchantments, Medr's training, or Caesi's tempests aren't? To the mortals, all of these have value.

**Caesi**
Hold on. Storms are natural phenomena, the only disruption on value they have is when ships sail into them get destroyed. It's hardly my fault if a captain decides to sail into a notoriously dangerous sea.

**Tanea**
And by that logic, it's not Glinphe's fault if a mortal stumbles onto a pile of gold they "left" in the mortal realm!

**Caesi**
My storms are not placed in the mortal's path. They are my recreation; an aspect of my domain.

**Glinphe**
Me creating copious gold is part of my domain! Greed! I test the mortal's capacity for greed!

**Tanea**
And you encourage it. 

**Glinphe**
So do you. When a mortal hears of a numbered list of "artefacts" their immediate urge is to have all of them. I was overjoyed when I discovered this. In another future, I'd think of us as friends, but here you're hypocritically targeting me. 

**Cyfun**
Settle down.

**Glinphe**
Don't you take sides, oh Eternal of the Eternal City. You're lucky your domain has nothing to do with the Mortal Realm. You never have to argue for the very ability to continue your domain's practices.

(None speak for a while.)

**Tanea**
My original proposal was not really about you, and I see how it may target your domain's activities. My original issue was with Eternals trading carelessly, but perhaps it is enough to enforce the limit of treasures synthesised for the purposes of trading, while allowing us to continue the creation that forms part of our domains even if it heavily disrupts mortal society, through trade, through will, through greed. 

**Glinphe**
Like Sirphni. She's not furthering her domain leaving her trading commodity in the mortal realm, she just does it out of laziness.

**Sirphni**
Don't call me into this. I'm just here for the food.

**Rhif**
I propose we create a limit on material produced explicitly for trading, based on a proportion of its value to some material of consistent value, such as gold. I can place Esten across the mortal world whose purpose is to assess the values of commodities, so that we may make accurate judgements on what is considered reasonable trading commodity.

**Tanea**
That seems reasonable to me.

**Cyfun**
I'll formalise the proposal. Rhif and their esten are to manage what is considered reasonable trading commodity in broad areas of the mortal world. This limit shall be for a year's worth of trading commodity for each Eternal, which can conditionally be carried over if an Eternal makes a case for a large financial acquisition. Material and non-material goods introduced into the Mortal Realm for the purposes of furthering our Eternal domains are exempted from this limit, within reason.

**Tanea**
And what is "within reason"? We do not want anyone to destroy markets to "further their domain". 

**Adnabyd**
The limiting of the actions of Eternals who wish destruction of mortals, their world, and their societies has long been an issue before you're ascention, Tanea. There have been many early occurrences of massive Eternal interventions, and we in our Eternal society know that we should not leave such large impacts on the Mortal Realm when we have our own realms to toy with.

(Dihenydd nearly interrupts)

Even if "toying with mortals is not the same as playing with our Esten". We have agreements on these fronts.

I shall call it into the vote.
