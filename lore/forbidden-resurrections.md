Necromagery is a potent evil. Guards, adventurers, and Caedyll everywhere identify and stop these threats as soon as they appear, in fear of a second Dictatorship of the Dead. 

The practice is a magery. That is, the utilisation of two or more ingredients to cause their torfol to react. It is not a common magery thanks to its notoriety. The exact process is unknown, but what is known is that the summoned cadaver's organs are one part of the magery. The other ingredient is unknown, and should stay unknowable. 

Through the joint utilisation of the torfol of the caster, an actionable link is made between the undead and the living. This link is like that of a gem golem and its caster, but it is a polluted stream. Through a different method, and a more corrupted torfol of the necromage, the summoned undead may be a conduit. Undead conduits can summon their own corpses, bound to the unnatural torfol of the conduit which itself is bound to the necromage. 

It is known that, during the Dictatorship of the Dead, the ruling necromage Tharn created a second-order conduit: a super-conduit capable of raising its own conduits as a conduit raises the undead. This allowed Tharn to create his empire and threaten to raise every corpse buried in Shildo. 

Necromagery may be a natural skill, however it is known that the God of Graves, Bedd, pioneered the method in an ancient time. Before the Era of Truth, a cult of Bedd is known to have existed and frequently contacted the God. Demanding a way to talk to their dead, Bedd experimented in a mortal body the ways in which the mortal consciousness may tie itself to the bodies of the dead. Through Bedd's success, necromagery was created. 

The cult was initially horrified, this is not what they had asked for, but eventually they were greedy with the power. The cult now remains in ancient memory, but it's cruel canon persists in dark and unknown circles. After several necromage tragedies post-truth, Bedd has apologised for his creation of the art, but they are helpless to stop it.

I have seen the practice of necromagery turn scholars dark.
