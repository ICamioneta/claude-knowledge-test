# Introduction
I am Harvey, a scholar at the University of Barogate Lore in Northern Ralean. It’s been my recent interest to accompany my fellow scholars on trips around Northern Ralean, where I would primarily scour nearby towns and settlements alike and take note on its inhabitants. Northern Ralean is an ideal spot for diversity, it has contact with 3 of the Waste’s regions as well as the rest of Ralean, which connects it to Anula.

# Notes on Ralean’s Diversity
The entirety of Ralean is then a prime spot for diversity, both north and south having access to many regions. The north, however, is arguably better for this given how it holds borders with the Waste regions of the Heabe, Vugaix, and Southern Kyenel. Although bordering Kyenel might seem to lack an advantage, the area of which we border is crucial for land trade between Ralean and Cronar. There is also a natural spread of the diversity of the south, allowing Northern Ralean a good population of Southern Ralean’s diversity as well.

On my short expeditions, I have made count of all the species and subspecies of people I have seen, some of which I made the time to interview, to see their purpose of being here, if they were the migrants of their homeland or if their fathers moved to the area before them. Rather excitingly, I have seen all species but the Fall, and I haven’t seen any Ximuz which is odd because they are a Muz.

This is not surprising in the least. If anything’s surprising it’s how many of the other species of person are here. Even some from Anula have come over to take lands, such as some Nebdos and Nidos. It’s surprising that Domuz manage to make it this far into Ralean, where we don’t hold a border with the sea.

The land of Northern Ralean is not general, it’s not nice and flat. Here, it can get quite mountainous, which surprises me when I see some of these species that are in no way adapted for mountains. I’ve seen many of the Ens subspecies call here their home, not only Colsens, but Nadens, and Popens too. The existence of Domuz here is also difficult to explain, although we do have many nice lakes, they are not the salt water the Domuz are used to swimming through.

# Stereotypes and Descriptions of Muz
As a Muz, I find it easy to get along with other Muz. In general, everyone who attends the college is a Muz, except from the two Colsens I’ve seen wandering about. As this is a scholarly article intended for all walks of life, I will describe what is familiar to me.

## Lemuz
We are the people of Ralean, we have prominent cheekbones with well-shaped jaws, are often good at walking. We are often described as being slightly stronger than the average other muz. We are fabled for our unity aspect, apparently our readiness to band together and help each other.

## Kaemuz
Quite confusingly, the Kaemuz are not from Kyeron, and are told specifically to not have any heritage from the region. Kaemuz are the inhabitants of Ereso, and have slightly sharper features. The Kaemuz are well known for their Architecture affinity, and it’s difficult to have not heard of the Spire. Kaemuz are more magical than most Muz, but still less magical than the next Muz on the list.

## Danmuz
The Danmuz from Terlaye have softer features. They are mostly known for their peacefulness. This is not to say they are pacifists, they merely prefer a peaceful life, and they are more than ready to fight for it. The Danmuz are also known for being the most magical Muz subspecies.

## Domuz
The Domuz are from Lale Darie, a set of islands and a bit of mainland way up north. The Domuz are especially different from the other Muz. It’s hard not to notice that their skin is closer to that of a fish’s scales, albeit softer. Rather interestingly, these scales can be colourful. Some have a reflective property that makes a rainbow-like shape, and som are naturally tinted blue, or green, or purple. There are even more defining features, like their hands and feet are webbed, their hands less so to retain their grabbing function. The people are beautiful, and its cities are wonderful.

It is as such the Domuz are well-equipped to deal with the ocean, or other bodies of water. We are all familiar with their cities underwater, but from tales I’ve heard their above water game is even better. Their buildings on land are especially exotic, nothing like the square-like patterns seen in the Lemuz Empire. Their boats are also something to marvel at. It might seem odd that a species well equipped for the sea needs boats, but in my interview the Domuz said they need boats in the same way we need horses.

# What are the Dos
In Southern Ralean, I’m told there are many Dos, just as many as there are Domuz here. In Northern Ralean, we still get our fair share of Dos. These bird-like people inhabit all of their continent, Anula. Unlike our continent, Barogate, Anula has no wastes. It’s all inhabitable and inhabited by these Dos. The Dos are bird-like people, possibly just like what you would imagine a bird to look like in humanoid form. The primary difference is that their wings are no longer for flight, and also have a hand at the end that looks similar to their feet.

## Nebdos
The Nebdos look like falcons, their heads in the same manner, their feathers being brown and claws being longer. They are towering, the few I met were taller than me by a half. They are known to be the militant ones of the Dos, with great armies and impressive physical strength. Their homeland is full of forests, some of which they cannot touch because cohabiting them are the Treemuz, not a muz by any means but holds the rough appearance of one, but being made out of trees and logs.

## Nidos
The Nidos look more like crows than anything else. They are shorter than the Nebdos, but still towering compared to me. I am of average height. The Nidos are known to be naturally intelligent. I only ever met one, and he described his homeland as rocky in the west and farmable in the east.

# Being in a town of Korongan
On my travels I met many Korongan, travellers from across the wastes from the unexpected fertile land who decided to leave and settle here. Rather unexpectedly, there was a town almost exclusively inhabited by Korongan. The Korongan are about three fourths the height of a Muz. They are incredibly physically capable; their bodies are almost unnaturally muscly. In my interview with a group, they told stories of their incredible strength. They say that it’s often told that they are small and weak, when in reality they are small and strong. They told tales of their homeland too, they mentioned that they need to be strong to survive the dangers of the Wastes, and to fight the Phax when war starts.

# My experience with the Ens
Ens are the goat people of the Heabe. Northern Ralean has a border with the Heabe, but Ens here is still a surprise to me. There is a river that flows through the Heabe where most of their settled civilisation lives. This river starts in the middle of the Heabe, and then flows upwards, so almost all of the Ens, especially the settled ones, inhabit along or at least near this river. I have not seen many Ens, most of them were Nadens, I’ve seen at most three Colsens and only one Popens

## Nadens
Nadens are the most common form of Ens, they are nomadic and wander the entirety of the Heabe including the southern parts so I shouldn’t really be surprised that some decided to merge with towns in our mountains. They are goat people, I’d imagine they are fairly easy to imagine, it’s just a goat standing on two legs. Their hands are sort of interesting, and they are known to have trouble grabbing things. These descriptions are applicable to all Ens, and any other Ens are a deviation from this.

## Popens
There are a few Ens subspecies that have no affinity with settling, and are purely nomadic. The Popens, for example, live the nomadic life in the upper parts of the Heabe, closer to the Barogate mountains. Their hands are much closer to hooves, and I saw the one I met sprint on all fours. Its legs were significantly beefier than the Nadens and its fur was thick and brown instead of white. Their torsos are also larger and more bulky.

## Colsens
Colsens have a high population in Lale Darie, almost all of them live in the Lale Darie, co-inhabiting it with the Domuz the same way the Akiiogan live in Thagryene with the Nudos. It was a big surprise to find a group of 3 living in a town, given how their main place of habitation is way up north. The Colsens have much smaller horns, to the point where they appear as small hard bumps on the head. Colsens are known to be smarter than the rest of the Ens, almost comparable to the average Muz.

# Mushroom People
Mad are the mushroom people of the Vugaix, and I’m aware they also live commonly in Dithas. The people are wildly varied, so there’s not much you can say about them collectively. The most common thing is that their skin is like the outer skin of mushrooms, and almost every species has a mushroom cap on top of their heads, they often cannot remove these.

## Buemad
Well-known as the most widespread of the Mad, there are no colonies or settlements made by Buemad in Northern Ralean. Where I’ve found them, they have found a place to fit in among Muz villages. They share the height of Corongan, but have the likeness almost identical to an average Muz, that is they are not muscly. Their caps are sometimes very different, some being large and flat while others are smaller.

## Pucmad
Pucmad are well known to live in Northern Ralean as they are very similar to the Oflinig. They act the same, look the same, except they are more mushroomy. It’s not unknown that Pucmad live in these parts, but they are quite hostile and therefore were not available for interview.

## Cuemad
The greatest surprise to me was finding a Cuemad. I’m almost confident it’s the only one in Northern Ralean. Thing is, they don’t talk often. In their communities they communicate almost exclusively using body language and expressions. They are also no taller than a forearm is long. From what I know, they are almost always in the form of a Muz, but usually skinny and thin. Their mushroom hats are usually semi-spheric, but there is known to be difference. The one I met was coloured yellow, but I am aware that Cuemad come in every colour available. It was very small, and as the stereotype goes, very cute.

# An odd frequency of Phax
Phax are well-known for their lack of social skill, almost never talking even among each other. They don’t have much in the way of body language, their scorpion features make it so that even if they did communicate through expression, they would be impossible for anyone else to read. Instead, there’s a sort of understanding between all Phax in what the others are thinking. Surprisingly, here in Northern Ralean, there’s quite a few Sosophax.

## Sosophax
Sosophax are well known for being the only Phax that ever leave their homeland. Also being the only Phax that ever make cities above the soil. Northern Ralean, and Ralean in general, is known to be the place with the second highest population of Phax, second only to Kusao itself. Describing a Phax is hard, and best done when comparing it to a regular scorpion. I took the time to form this description, and even worked with a zoologist from Kyeron (Not a Ximuz unfortunately) to make the proper comparisons.

There are 4 parts of a scorpion, the head, torso, abdomen and tail. On a Sosophax, the head is tilted a square angle to its torso, and then there’s a sort of short neck. They only have 5 limbs, that is two arms, two legs and their tail. The arms come from the top torso similar to Muz, and their hands terminate in claw-like structures that also share some hand features like opposable thumbs, which seem to be the claw. Their legs are peculiar, they stand with these odd three-segmented legs, where the first segment from the top goes forwards, then the next backwards, then after that it’s forwards again, then there’s the foot. The intersections of these leg parts are roughly at square angles. Their tails are humongous, but mostly long and thin. They can arch them all from behind their backs, over their shoulders and still be able to grab them with their hands. Their tails alone are the size of a Dos, but they themselves stand about as tall as Muz.

With that description out of the way, it is well known that the Sosophax are the most sociable of the Phax, and are the only ones that construct cities in the land and manage diplomacy with other nations. There’re even stories that say they have refugee parts of their cities for species other than Phax. If you see a Phax outside of Kusao it’s almost definitely a Sosophax.

In Northern Ralean, I’m surprised about how many Sosophax I have seen. As we already know, they are not ones to leave their homeland and go talk to everyone they can. Strangely, I saw at least ten on my travels, scattered all over. I remember seeing one performing forestry, and some wandering local areas.

# Lindi
The Lindi are a sort of short, bipedal person who are often green. Their body is split into segments, and they have some seemingly vestigial legs on their chest. They have been described as caterpillar-like.

# Heresay about Other species and Subspecies
I have heard stories, or maybe overheard a few conversations, about species I have not met myself. Given how I have no first-hand evidence, take the following information and toss it to the road, as it may not be entirely trustworthy.

## Ximuz
The Ximuz are the monkey-like people of Kyeron. They have hair-covered skin and prehensile tails. Despite their seeming adaption for the forest, they still create cities on land and chop down the forest to create them.

## Nudos
The Nudos are told as the normal of the Dos, the farmers, and hunters with a more sedentary lifestyle, none of the intelligence-seeking of the Nidos or the warmongering of the Nebdos. In a conversation I overheard, part of the discussion was that people fail to recognise the strength of farmers and the keen eyes of hunters.

## Akiiogan
I don’t know much of the Akiiogan except that they live in Thagryene alongside the Nudos. They probably farm alongside them, I can imagine it would be easier to plant if you’re closer to the ground.

## Tolusphax
I was told of someone’s encounter with a Tolusphax, and how it attacked them and they barely escaped with their lives. It is said that these phax are not friendly, and although they can be bargained with, this is only on rare occasions.

## Gofophax
Rumor has it that there are Phax living in mystical caves filled with gems that illuminate the land like a twilight sky. I have seen these gems, they make good ingredients for claps, but I personally won’t believe massive caverns full of them, and large enough for some Phax species to construct a city under the ground.

## Fall
Members of this species share features with several different types of reptile, and are very similar to the Phax in the sense that they can be reasoned with despite their inherently aggressive behaviour. They live perhaps the furthest away from any civilisation, in the mostly unnoticed swamps of a region north of Kyenel called the Duela.

# Interviews

As part of my research in writing about the species of Barogate, I made time to interview as many as I could.

## Interviews with my fellow colleagues
The easiest for me to interview are my fellow colleagues at the University. I have interviewed five of them. The first, Hendy, is a Lemuz who helped me form my analysis of the gods. The next, Iya, is a Eresonian Kaemuz who is interested to similar subjects and thusly attends the same lectures that I do. After that I questioned Leyz, a Lemuz who is from Southern Ralean, a friend of one of my friends. The fourth is Haiah, a Domuz who grew up in Terlaye but has been to Lale Darie on multiple occasions. The last is Mejiu, a Colsens I went through great effort to track down.

### Hendy

**What’s your thoughts on the Lemuz stereotype on unity?**
Well, the thing about Lemuz unity is that it has a massive history. People say it because of the Ralean-based Empire that has existed for a thousand years. As I’m sure you know, it’s very small now. I’m not one for politics, but as an inhabitant of Ralean and a citizen of the Empire itself, the empire recently split in two. If civil war is enough to convince people that Lemuz unity is in jeopardy, I don’t know what is.

**What’s the most normal thing among Lemuz?**
While I’d like to say banding together, the whole ordeal with the south challenges this.

**Any particular thoughts on other species of person?**
It seems like we all worship our own gods. Some gods the people worship I’d never heard of, but sometimes they say they’ve never heard of our Ralean gods. This has led me to question not only the legitimacy of their gods, but also the legitimacy of those of our gods that are often quiet. That’s a conspiracy theory for another time, though. I heard the Sosophax worship no gods.

### Iya

**What are your thoughts on the Eresonian Architecture?**
Everything around here seems a bit boring and dull in comparison to the buildings back in Ereso. I’m not criticizing, it’s got its own type of style, a sort of lower-class wood and whitewash. The only structures that ever caught my eye are the castles around here. They are a bit plain, but it seems as if the Ralean Empire can really make plain work.

**What’s the most normal thing among Kaemuz?**
Architecture, obviously. We aren’t all architects, but we can easily acknowledge what makes Eresonian architecture so great and unique. Another thing that connects us is the Spire, you know, it’s taller than the Barogate Mountains, and can be seen for a hundred marches as a great beacon of Eresonian greatness.

**Any particular thought on other species of person?**
No. None at all.

### Leyz

**What are your thoughts on the Lemuz stereotype of Unity?**
Not true. The Empire up here hides it, but the Southern parts of the country are in shambles. Families are torn apart in their beliefs of the Ralean Empire and the new. I came up here to study, of course, but also to escape the awful conditions we’re forced to live in. I’d fight for it, but I’m a scholar not a warrior.

**Are you unique for leaving Southern Ralean?**
No. People are leaving all over, mostly the diverse species that are there that don’t want to get mixed up in Ralean politics. I’ve heard of entire villages packing bags and leaving for refuge all around, even one that said they were going all the way to Vezoen.

**Any particular thoughts on other species of person?**
I’m mostly here to study the species of the Wastes, there are so many interesting creatures. I’ve almost seen all the ones we know about, except those cave dwelling Gofophax. I’ll actually be heading out on an expedition just to go see if they exist soon.

### Haiah

**Have you ever lived underwater?**
No, not really. I’m quite adventurous, and sometimes join adventuring groups only temporarily to get a bit experience of the outside world, collect ingredients maybe. You see, I’m doing work on the pure magical properties of ingredients, things like how they get them, or other in a similar manner. During one of these adventures, it was safe to say I got lost, and was actually being chased by some bandits. I ran for a nearby lake, and it was the first time I’d been underwater for a while. I jumped in and hid for hours.

**What’s Lale Darie like?**
It’s great. The islands are all so different, full of interesting people and more importantly great geological features as well. The Eresonians might go on and on about the fantastic architecture there and don’t get me wrong it is something special, but the houses and buildings in Lale Darie are where it’s really at.

**Any thoughts on the Colsens or any other species of person?**
The Colsens are great, like the perfect mix between brains and brawn. I could say they are on average duller than Muz, but there are some great minds in the Lale and these minds reside within Colsens heads. You told me you haven’t seen any Fall? I have, let me tell you a bit. There’re quite a few different ones, there’s some really built-up ones that could break a Muz arm with nothing but their mouths. There are the sneaky ones that seem to slither around, making no noise. Then there are the more normal ones, they are much easier to get along with.

### Mejiu

**What are your thoughts on the common opinion that Colsens are less intelligent?**
Well I see where they are coming from, Ens aren’t known much for their smarts, especially the most common Nadens. But Colsens are just as smart as Muz. I guess on average they could be seen as a little slower, but deep down we all have equal cognitive abilities. They often say the trade-off for our smarts is our lack of horns, where instead we have hard patches on our heads. This is fair.

**What’s the Heabe like?**
The Heabe? Not the homeland of the Colsens. Maybe our original habitat, but the Colsens are from mainland Lale Darie. We’re the outsiders of the Ens, but the Domuz are more accepting.

**What’s it like being a Creature of the Wastes?**
Right, well, here’s something for your future interviews. The Muz and the Dos have this idea of classification, the ‘Living’ species of Person and the ‘Waste’ species of person. They, and by extension you up until now, classify Muz, Dos, and Gan as ‘Living’ species because you live on the most fertile lands available. Then, you lump all other species into the ‘Waste’ category. Although I can see this as roughly fair, it’s not a good way to classify creatures. In reality, we shouldn’t be classified. Us ‘Creatures of the Wastes’ are no different to the Muz or the Dos or the Gan, we just originate from more hostile environments. Arguably, Neble is more hostile than the Heabe, with the Tree people I’ve heard of.

**Do you feel alienated in the University?**
No, not really. Most people are fair, and are aware that I am equal to them. The only thing that makes me feel like I stand out are questions I keep being asked about the Wastes. They’re fine on the most part, but hopefully when you publish this people can stop asking and start reading.

## Interview with an Adventuring group
On my travels to meet as many species and species as possible, I and my group of scholars came across a group of adventurers on the road. It’s a common verity that every adventuring group has one exotic species with them, such as a Phax or Ens or Dos, but this adventuring group of six had four exotic members. These were a Korongan, a Sosophax, a Nebdos and a Popens named Silgorli, Keria, Samone and Bigo respectively.

### Silgorli

**What are your thoughts on the general stereotypes of the Gan?**
You think we survive the Wastes by sticking close to the floor? No, we fight, and build, and farm like our lives depend on it, because they almost definitely do. All Korongan are as tough as me, ready to take down a kruvan if it came close to a village. The strongest of us can defeat a Flutsxa alone, better than any non-magic user of here could do. Those Akiiogan seem meek in comparison. It’s hard to believe we’re the same species.

**What is a Kruvan and what is a Flutsxa?**
See, you probably won’t ever have to think about these things. A Kruvan, simply put, is a gigantic tortoise the size of a small town. They come treading into our land from Kyenel stomping our crops and walking over our houses like they were nothing. Flutsxa are horrific predators, with six legs, sandy bodies and many holes where their nose should be. They shout the most horrific cries, and come for our cattle, and trash through our villages, that is if we let them.

**What do you think of the Phax?**
I used to think they were just like the Flutsxa, predators that exist only to hunt and eat from our livestock. Turns out, not all of them are as horrific as the Allophax. The Sosophax are the ones Cronar kingdoms usually war against, and they are usually over petty disputes. The Sosophax are very sociable, not in the sense that they talk often but in the way they like being around people. My idea of what a Phax was changed when I met Keria.

### Keria

**What do you think of the Phax stereotype of being predatorial creatures?**
Not true, we have cities, some with refugee parts.

**What’s Kusao like?**
Warm sands and orange rocks.

**How do you tell between male and female Phax?**
Men are not much wider and slower, and never carry children.

**Why did you leave Kusao?**
Wanderlust.

**Any thoughts on other species of person?**
Fall are interesting.

### Samone

**What do you think of the stereotype that Nidos are intelligent?**
We do have some good inventions to our name, siege equipment, the bow and crossbow, and many good ship improvements, but I wouldn’t say that’s because we’re more intelligent. I think it’s fairer to say we are more innovative, which I believe is separate to intelligence.

**What’s Vezoen like?**
I only spent my childhood there, my family moved to Southern Ralean for work early on in my life. From what I know, which mostly comes from my parents telling me stories, Vezoen has an east and a west, in the west you have a land where rocks are littering the ground, and poking out of it. It’s where Anula’s mountains die down in the north, so the land feels like the mountains are submerged. The east is different, more habitable with farmlands and forests everywhere, not too dissimilar to southern Ralean.

**Any opinions on the other species of person?**
No, none in particular. I’d like to see a Domuz though, I’ve heard they are especially innovative among the Muz. In their designs, that is.

### Bigo

**What do you think of the Popens stereotype of animalistic nomads?**
Almost true. We do not live in towns, we live on the grass. We are more like animal than other, but still smart.

**Why did you leave the Heabe?**
Nomads threw me out. Went south and joined these. Stuck with the group, we have many adventures. I have been to Kusao.

## Interview with a Danmuz with a Cuemad
In a particular town, I saw an incredibly interesting sight in the corner of my eyes. A Danmuz with a Cuemad sitting on his shoulder. My group was only passing through the area, but I had to stay behind and ask these people. Upon greeting them, I learnt the Danmuz’s name was Aimer and the Cuemad’s was Misha. Aimer said that Misha doesn’t really speak to anyone but him. The interview took place with both of them present, Misha sitting on Aimer’s lap.

### Aimer & Misha

**So what’s your story? How did you meet?**
Well, I used to collect some ingredients in the southern parts of the Vugaix, not far from Northern Ralean, when I saw Misha crying on a toadstool. She was a child back then, almost small enough to get trodden on. So, I approached her and offered her my flask of water. That’s when she just clung to me, hugging my arm. I reckoned it was best to get her to a safer place, the nearby town, so I went there and laid her down on a bed. Weirdly enough, instead of telling me where her home was, she just wanted to come with me. She’s better for it.

**What are you doing here?**
Oh? Just passing through actually. It’s a complete coincidence that we (Aimer and I) met in Northern Ralean, which just so happens to be where Misha and I first met. We were on our way to Thagryene, just exploring really. I was hoping to join some adventuring group on the road, but I’m yet to meet one that wants me. I only know a bit about magic from the times I was picking mushrooms, but I’m no mage.

**How old is Misha now?**
Well, we don’t really know for certain. Cuemad don’t track their age like we do. If I assume she was roughly 12 when I found her, she’d be about 24 now. It makes sense, they seem to mature at the same rate as us. If you’re wondering, I’m 29.
