# Introduction
I'm Corvan Esata, currently a professor of the University of Cultural Anthropology in Allesoden. In addition to being a respected institute for uncovering the history of the development of societies, the university also acts as a hub for adventurers. There is a small portion of truly respected individuals stay behind to read anecdotes and study, but the vast majority of the university's registered students are frequently everywhere on the six continents but the university. The university celebrates its adventuring culture and hosts numerous classes based entirely in self defence and other topics focussed entirely on adventuring rather than studying anthropology through an academic lens. Here at the University, members are dungeon delvers, ruin grazers, road travellers, and they need someone to tell them how to do it without getting killed.

# Assessing the situation
To understand what archetypes you might need in your group, you must first consider what you’re up against, and what you’re doing. Different situations require different methods to handle them after all. I’m aware of a few occupations that generally come under the umbrella term ‘adventurer’, here they are listed along with that which they will be encountering.

## Scavenger
Sometimes just called ‘scer’, the scavenger is still an adventurer who wanders around scouring battlefields for valuable equipment. This is illegal in some parts, so if you’re using this as a guide to get into adventuring and you wish to become a scavenger, I advise you check with your local authority on their scavenging policies. Scavenger here does not include those who follow armies to pick up the aftermath of battles.

A scavenger usually wanders the roads aimlessly, meaning that the main thing they would be up against is the road’s dangers. A backpack full of goods can be enticing to a bandit. Scavengers are often also quite poor; scavenging is seen as the gateway to other types of adventuring.

The needs of the scavenger are good, voluminous carry weights, light arms. It is always advised that scavengers have a pack mule and large baskets so that they can carry the largest amount of equipment. Scavengers should not fight often, but it is sometimes important to defend yourself.

Scavengers should be good as one-handed weapon fighters, and perhaps include a buckler or shield. This is because as previously mentioned, scavengers are early adventuring groups who might not have chosen their preferred archetype. Most archetypes use one-handed swords as a backup and knowing them as a scavenger will make future archetypes easier to learn.

## Bounty Hunter
Bounty Hunters hunt criminals. Bounty hunter generally refers to an individual or group of individuals that hunt for one or a few people for police reward, or as a favour. Most people with bounties will run, especially when severely outnumbered, so it’s often crucial that bounty hunters keep their arms and armour athletic so that they may chase effectively. Bounties sometimes do fight back, and the preferred arms depend on if you’re in a combat with a bounty group of roughly the same size, or a single person.

Bounty hunters also often need to carry the person for imprisonment, which means that they need a good method of ‘holding up’ their bounty while traveling. A hold-up is when you threaten their escape with an instant attack, one that cannot be dodged. For this, crossbows are often used with sword pressing a less effective method. Other methods of detaining include shackles or magical bounds.

Sometimes, those who set death bounties ask for proof of the deaths, which can go from iconic items to heads, and heads are often quite gruesome to carry. Firstly, it’s preferred among bounty hunters to carry an effective implement to cut off heads with. As unsavoury as it is, the cut parts of the heads are often charred to stop bleeding, and then put into a basket where the team quickly go to collect the bounty, so whoever called it can throw the head away.

Good archetypes for fighting one-man bounties are polearms such as Spears and glaives. In a two verses one situation, it is always preferable that the two carry polearms, so you can imagine that a whole squad doing so would probably be very unfortunate should the bounty chose to fight. Should the bounty chose to run, however, it’s useful to have a crossbowman to incapacitate them at a moment’s notice. If you are unable to hit them as they run, it’s often useful to have some athletic swordsmen with a shortsword to chase the bounty without the inconvenience of a polearm or crossbow.

It is in the same way that mages can be useful. They can perform hold-ups with a touch clap at bay, and be very athletic while still having defence options. Wizards might even be able to imbue potions that boost certain athletic traits that aid in the chase.

If the bounty is a group of similar size, then it’s preferred to have a more balanced team. The polearms might want to ditch for swords, and you might want one or more individuals with longswords or even great swords to fend off multiple at once.

## Camp Raider
Camp raiders are similar to bounty hunters in the sense that they might be taking the camp as part of a government-issued job. There are usually three ways to approach camp raids, those are from far away, straight in, or stealthily. The easiest is from afar, where bowmen are preferred, and maybe a few mages. Head-on raids are far more dangerous, but sometimes necessary when bows are not options. Bandit camps are often built to withstand bow volleys.

In the case where the camp is corridor-like, polearms and shields are by far the best option, the polearm could easily be replaced with swords too. Blocking off the entrance and walking through as an orderly group gives bandits no chance to escape and not much chance to fight back. Be aware of traps if this is your tactic, because getting behind a shield wall will be the main priority of the enemy, as well as throwing rocks from above.

In the more exiting case when the camp is more open, be it a hall or out in the field, a good balance is preferred. Longswords, magic users, and bows of all types are preferred to counter the opponent at an individual level. In terms of armour for both of these raids, heavy armour is your best bet as agility is not a requirement.

Stealth cases are more than the most difficult. These cases limit weapon choice, forcing you to use a dagger and have silent, lightweight armour. The best part of this type of operation is efficiency, Pulling off a stealth mission without anything going wrong is incredibly satisfying. A useful tactic when performing stealth is to sneak in to place everyone in good positions. Magic is almost a requirement to aid in stealth, either muffling, literal invisibility or perceived invisibility are the most useful effects.

## Monster Hunter
Monster Hunters can be those who hunt down one difficult beast, or even those that hunt groups of more unnatural creatures like oflinigs and rogue golems. In the case that they’re only against one creature, ranged weaponry and claps, as well as polearms, are always the best bet. In the case that it’s several like oflinigs, then bows and magic are still the best bets but other than that a greatsword or longsword might be preferred. Carrying the monster might be difficult, unless only proof is required, where tools to cut off defining features become a necessity.

## Expeditioners
Expeditioners are one of the more recognisable adventuring types, and often consist of a group of individuals all seeking a certain thing from a certain place. They search for rare artefacts hidden in history, or just ingredients that grow in a certain place. Expeditioners should usually be prepared for anything, bandit raids, monster attacks, inconveniences, protective golems. They also have to consider being able to carry whatever they’re expedition is for, which often requires some well-made bags and satchels.

For armour, heavy is often preferred by close combat types, and the type of melee weapon doesn’t really matter as all are useful during expeditions. It is, however, crucial to have at least one longsword or great sword for the times when you’re outnumbered. Magic users are more than important, the ability to use powerful claps can get past even the largest charging Genoyg. Enchantments can be more than useful to overcome any obstacle, to heal any wounds and for any other purpose.

Expeditioners are the most varied type of adventuring group as they will end up facing a series of challenges, especially while wandering the Wastes, which expeditions usually go into. Without a polearm, your group will find hostile creatures difficult. Without a mage, you will find sturdy opponents difficult. Without a swordsman, you will find duelling significantly harder. Without a ranger, you might find yourself outmatched by something that can use distance to its advantage. Expeditioners should always strive for diversity.

Most of what applies to expeditioners also applies to ancient dungeon-delving as being able to handle most situations is most important.

# Weapon Analysis
I’ve seen possibly every single type of weapon on the two continents and even a few beyond. Each class has a specific use, even then they have subclasses for specialised usage. Here’s the few I know of, in order of combined frequency and importance, keep in mind that certain weapon variants are used more frequently in places I’ve been to less, like Greathammers in Neble.

## Polearm
The Polearm is the most important class of weapon ever to exist. It encompasses Spears, bardiches, Greathammers, Halberds, and more obscure things. To generalise Polearms, almost all of them are a metal head on a long wooden stick, and they usually have the ability to jab. Polearms are the best in so many situations, although the Spear might be limiting if you’re up against multiple people, weapons with sharp edges like the Halberd can threaten multiple opponents, especially if they’re weaker than your average Muz. The best counter to Polearms of all types is a good shield, but even this sometimes is not enough to counter the excellence that polearms carry.

### Halberd
The head of a Halberd has a spike similar or identical to Spears, while also having an axe head out of one side and a vicious spike on the opposite. Swinging the axe head causes tremendous damage against shields and cuts easily into inadequately armoured opponents. Even heavily armoured opponents will freeze before getting hit by the axe head, the tremendous amounts of force it can generate are exceptional. Using the spike side is a sure way to get past chain, as well as pierce everything but the strongest of armours.

### Spear
The Spear has been the go-to polearm for armies since the earliest times in history. It’s simple, easy to use in groups. I probably don’t have to explain a Spear to anyone, but in general it’s a long stick with a spike on the end. The material, shape, or any other quality of the Spear can vary, so long as the forward-facing point is strong. Spears excel in combat with two-handed weapons like the longsword, as well as most situations where you outnumber your opponent. It’s important to note that most  Polearms can be used as a Spear, so its advantages are added on top of any advantages given by other types, unless specified otherwise.

### Glaive
Glaives are mostly just elongated spear heads to the point where they have a long cutting edge. These are normally to be used identically to the spear, with cutting being an extra option added. This allows for cuts during the thrust, on the return if a near miss is obtained, and allows it to be swung during especially specific scenarios. Other similar weapons like swordspears are used the same as glaives, and therefore are under the same classification.

### Bardiche
The Bardiche might look like a long axe, but it is separate as the axe head extends past where the stick ends. This end is usually sharpened, giving a spear function. As the heads are usually larger than the axe heads on Halberds, they can deal more damage. They tear through light armours.

### Greathammers
Greathammers are similar to bardiches, but maximise the blunt force of the axe head by turning it into a specialised hammer. This type crushes armour, and is effective against anything inadequately padded.

## Swords
Onto the second biggest weapon class – the sword! Swords are known worldwide to be the adventurer’s weapon, while polearms are always seen as something for army men. Unfortunately, lots of beginners don’t understand the applicability of polearms and only ever use swords. The right way to do it is to use polearms with a good shortsword at hand. Swords are mostly what they usually are, sharp blades fitted with handles, cross-guards and pommels, and their use is the most versatile.

### Daggers
While not explicitly swords, this is the only real category these iconic weapons fit into. Daggers are by far the most popular weapon choice given their common use as secondary weapons and as the commoner’s choice for self-defence, or the thug’s choice depending on your local laws. Daggers come in all shapes and sizes, but are generally used for thrusting or stabbing accurately.

### Handling Sword
Handling swords, or arming swords, are the quite long bunch where they can sometimes be two-handed but it might be uncomfortable as gripping the pommel is usually a requirement. Handling swords have a good deal of length and control, without compromising or going in too far. Handling swords make for good back-up weapons and pair excellently with shields. They are one of the go-to backup weapons, along with a buckler. They are useful in most combat scenarios, but closing in on someone with a polearm might be difficult without a sizeable shield.

### Shortsword
Shortswords are pretty descriptive, they’re shorter and mostly ever only accommodate one hand. They are usually used as backup weapons since they don’t take up much space. The short length is almost always accounted for in the form of a companion weapon, like a dagger or a buckler. Shortsword specialists are hard to come by because longer options exist and are usually better as a main option.

### Hand and a Half Sword
Hand and a Half swords are the scale up for handling swords. They can often be gripped comfortably with both hands, are not too long to be used one-handed, and are overall good specialist options for those who want good reach as well as some offhand weapon such as a shield. Hand and a Half swords, at a stretch, can be used identically to longswords given the user carefully overextend from their usual positions to get deeper cuts, compromising for the blade’s length.

### Longswords
Once again very self-descriptive swords, and the length above hand and a half swords. They are too unwieldy to use with one hand. With both hands on the handle, they are only as heavy as a shortsword. Longswords are the height of interesting combat, it’s one of the most skill-based weapons. It’s also incredibly versatile, half-swording allows for spear-like use, and its length gives it a decent capability of fending off multiple opponents. Its major weakness is projectiles, as it uses the offhand that would be useful for shields.

### Sabres
Sabres encompass most curved swords that are meant for slashing. Sometimes they curve instantly, sometimes their curve is nearer the top. They aren’t too common so it’s fair to group them as one. Sabres aren’t much different from whatever other sword of their length exists; they can still jab. There are different methods of using them, which focus more heavily on slashes. They are useful for when the opponent isn’t heavily armoured.

### Thinswords
Thinswords are built for stabbing, and don’t have much use as a slashing weapon. They are incredibly thin, but often as long as a longsword if not longer. Their weight is equivalent to a handling sword. They are somewhat good at poking at the openings of the armour, but you can do that with a normal sword. It also takes incredible skill and precision to handle a Thinsword effectively. They have almost no parry value, being thin and flexible. Their hard counter is pretty much everything longer than them.

## Blunts
Blunts are a greatly viable weapon class especially when your enemy is heavily armoured. Most blunts cause internal damage, broken bones and the sort. These take much longer to heal using potions, so they’re generally thought of a brute’s weapon. An important thing to note about blunts is that their combat is completely different to swords, without any handguards or elongated blades strikes usually have to be precise and calculated, there’s not much in the way of duelling with most of these, there are exceptions of course.

### Staff
Staves are the most appreciated of the blunts. Long and hard, they can crack a skill with a full unprotected swing. Staves can also be used as spears, but then you might as well have a spear. It’s important to note that spears can be used as staves at a stretch. Staves don’t have many counters because of their long reach and hard swings, but if you can find a longer weapon you’d be better in using it as always. Staves also look casual, meaning they don’t usually bat eyes with people. They are one of the first go-to adventuring gears.

### Baton
Batons are essentially refined sticks, but they are not to be underestimated. When made properly out of a strong wood, they can be light while still providing the impact force of specialised blunt weapons. They are easy to carry, easy to use, but usually have no handguard meaning the hands are vulnerable. Useful against everything but more heavily armoured opponents, they break internal structures and can even leave cuts depending on the sub-type.

### Warhammer
Warhammers are fashioned sticks with a particular type of head. On one side, the head extrudes to create a specialised bashing weapon. On the opposite side, there’s a pick. The hammer end is extremely effective for breaking internal structures and heavily denting even padded armours. The pick end is effective at piercing that which the hammer end has dented. It goes straight through most armours and is especially brutal on the person it strikes. One thing to note about using this end is that it often requires some force to pull back out, meaning after severely damaging your opponent they may just cut you dead if your strike didn’t incapacitate them.

### Flangehead
Flangeheads are maces with flanges. These flanges usually manifest themselves as protrusions along the stick, the amount of them vary. These flanges have a wide variety of spikiness. The spikier ones are effective at cracking at armour, however they can blunt relatively fast to the less spikey ones, where the flanges operate to direct the force.

### Rockhead
The rockhead falls in the mace category, but specialises in strikes against relatively unpadded armour and to break bones. The rockhead usually doesn’t actually have a rock on the end, rather a particularly fashioned metal part. These can be quite decorative, although with use decorations might be dullened. The most basic of these have a splodge of heavy metal on the end.

### Porcupine
The porcupine is yet another mace where the head is usually a spherical blob of metal with multiple spike protrusions from the centre. These are capable of penetrating armour as well as causing massive damage to bodies. One thing to note about them is that they, much like the pick side of a Warhammer, can get somewhat jammed in bodies. If they can be pulled out of a body, it’s a certain victory, but the difficulty of this might lead to your own demise.

### Spiked Baton
As its name suggests, the spiked baton is just a baton with spikes coming out of it. Often, these spikes are on two or four sides of the baton. These are essentially improved batons, exchanging some of the baton’s raw blunt force with penetrating and damaging spikes. Although spikes can cause very visible wounds, it’s important to note that less internal damage is caused, so the damage that is caused is often more healable.

## Axes and Picks
Axes and picks serve roughly the same purpose. They have a good weight to them and penetrate armour decently, as well as smashing through shields with proper form. Axes are almost always cheaper than swords, and have a stronger head. They beat spiked blunts because the likelihood of one getting stuck is decreased. Picks still have this likelihood, but are expert armour piercers. Most axes have a pick on the opposite side of their head.

### Hatchet
Hatchets made for battles usually have smaller heads, which up their penetration capacity. They often don’t have any other functional side, and therefore are less versatile. The biggest advantage of the Hatchet is how light it is. It makes for a good secondary if the chances of you encountering an armoured opponent are low but still present. They also have better penetration heads than wider Battleaxes

### Battleaxe
Battleaxes are essentially bardiche heads on shorter sticks. Because of this, their use is much less like a spear, and more similar to a regular axe with a pick on the other side, with jab capability from the spiked top. They are by far the most popular and versatile of the axes, and are the basis of a good axe for this class of weapon.

### Felling Axe
Felling axes in combat are usually repurposed civilian felling axe heads on a straighter stick. These are incredibly cheap and are almost a makeshift weapon. Their broader head makes them less viable for armour piercing goals and even heavier and less secondary-viable, but they make for excellent cheap weapons that have a good shield-destroying capacity. Something to note is that Double-Sided Axes are identical to Felling Axes but with two sides instead of one. This ups their versatility only a notch.

### Pick
Picks are usually exclusively on the other side of another head and are there to add more versatility to a weapon, but sometimes there are weapons that are just picks. These are incredibly cheap and only ever used on a budget, anyone who can afford it will get an axe or hammer with a pick on the other side. Just so it’s defined, picks don’t have to be exactly like pickaxe heads, sometimes they come in more intriguing shapes, so long as the end is sharp and it’s swung like a pick.

## Bows
Bows are the most common of the ranged weapons. Allegedly invented by the Nidos, these are bent wooden sticks with a line running from one end to the other. To actually use the weapon, you need ammunition for it. Most bows use arrows, small but long sticks with a few feathers on the back and a spear-like head. You put the arrow on the string then pull the string back, and when you let go the arrow will fly fast and far and impale something.

### Standard Bow
Standard bows are usual pieces of wood with a string attached and perhaps a place to rest the arrow. They usually have curves that make the bow more powerful, but straight variants do exist which are easier to make. Standard bows are used by adventurers and army men. The strongest bows are held by the army, where people who use them have been trained since they were a child for that singular purpose. The strongest of bows will never be pulled back by your average adventurer.

### Curved Bow
Curved bows are small bows usually used on horseback or for the carrying convenience. These bows can be pulled really far back, and have very long arrows. These bows don’t pack much of a punch but are still deadly in their own way.

### Short Bow
Short bows are bows which are short. They are small and usually really easy to draw back. The arrows they use are quite short too. They lack a lot of power, but are convenient to carry and are perfectly capable of killing. Short bows are the easiest bow type to get into if you’re looking to become a ranged bow fighter.

### Wheeled Bow
This bow is rare, expensive, and complex. It uses a system of pulleys that only a specialist can understand, but in the end, you can get the same power as a strong longbow with the difficulty of a weak one. They are extremely hard to maintain, as if a string slips from a pulley there zis no way to get it back on without special tools.

## Crossbows
Crossbows are a similar alternative to bows. In general, they consist of a body of wood with a bow attached to the end, where a bolt is loaded and fired from. In comparison to bows, crossbows generally have a shorter range than bows but fire more consistently because the user is not in control of how much power is packed into a shot. Most crossbows use bolts, which are similar to arrows but usually thicker and a lot shorter.

### Simple Crossbow
Simple crossbows are usually just constructs of wood that manage to make the functionality of crossbows. These are the most common overall because they are the type that armies use. This is due to their easy construction and simple maintenance. On the higher end of the quality spectrum, the bow may have a slight curve in it. These versions can be used by more developed armies, but usually by adventurers.

### Heavy Crossbow
The heavy crossbow is a large variant of the crossbow that usually has a complicated method of drawing it, for example a cranequin or a lever mechanism. These bows are large and can hold a great amount of force within them. They are mostly used by armies because they are large and bulky, making them impractical for adventure.

### Hand Crossbow
The hand crossbow is the only crossbow or bow that you can operate with only one hand. They are light and usually of a good construction. Although you can hold them in one hand and shoot them like that, you do need another hand available to reload the weapon. Because they are usually of a good make, they are not that less powerful than a simple crossbow.

### Repeating Crossbow
Repeating crossbows are a clever invention that automatically loads a bolt when you pull it back. They are almost always weaker than other crossbows, but the quick reloading factor means that you can fire several bolts a lot faster. They often use a more specialised bolt that suit the loading mechanism.

### Wheeled Crossbow
Wheeled crossbows are extremely rare and I have only ever seen 8. They are genius inventions similar to the wheeled bow, but I’ve been told that they were adapted from that invention. Wheeled crossbows are extremely powerful and gain from the same benefits that the wheeled bow does. Instead of making bows easier to pull back, the wheeled crossbow is just as hard to load but carries significantly more power.

### Shibel’s Crossbow
This is the rarest crossbow I’ve only ever seen twice, and I only saw it the second time because I was looking for it. The bow part of the crossbow is actually facing backwards and uses wheels and pulleys to redirect the string. The crossbow’s effectiveness can be likened to that of the wheeled crossbow, but Shibel’s crossbow fires a lot faster and therefore is more accurate.

## Slings and Throwing Weapons
To top of the ranged weapons, following are a variety of slings and weapons designed for throwing. While you can throw daggers, they aren’t covered in this section because they weren’t originally designed for this class. Slings use the power of circular motion to throw objects surprisingly far distances at speeds faster than most bows. Throwing weapons are a diverse class of weapons. Because the weapon is being thrown, you generally cannot carry as many of a throwing weapon as you could arrows or bolts.

### Atlatl & Javelins
Javelins are specialised throwing spears that can be effective at short ranges if the opponent is unarmoured. They are equally effective against creatures which is the primary reason adventurers use them. Atlatls allow for the javelin to be thrown further and faster, making them more useful against light armour.

### Sling
The favourite weapon of our childhoods, a simple sling can send stones flying at alarming speeds, enough to kill most small animals and damage medium sized ones as well. On the higher end of quality are the slings often utilised by the army. Properly made slings have massive range and can kill quite easily, as they are usually thrown upwards and hit heads. Even though they are most lethal when hitting the head, they can just as easily break bones in the chest or on the limbs, making them just as useful for adventurers. A variant, the staff sling, is almost never used by adventurers but offers further range and power for armies due to leverage.

### Dart Sling
The dart sling is a sling specialised for using darts or bolts. The extra penetrative value that darts give slings makes them more effective against armour. While they are in use by the army, dart slings usually see use in adventurers given how they are more damaging than rock slings.

### Shuriken
Shuriken describes all metal projectile weapons where pretty much every face is sharp. They are useful for throwing short distances. They usually do not have much penetrative value but can be thrown quickly. Shuriken are versatile weapons. While they can be thrown directly and stuck into opponents, they can also be used stealthily, glancing across the opponent giving them a wound they cannot trace. They can even be imbedded in the ground and used as caltrops.

### Chakram
I’ve only ever seen Chakram used by travellers from Mothdao. It is a perfectly circular weapon with sharpened outsides. I’ve come across five travellers from Mothdao and three of them, the ones from the desert region of the continent, all carried chakram. They placed one on their cloth hats to protect it from some blows, and carried seven or eight on the left arm. In combat, they put it on their finger and spun it around several times. When it hits the enemy or a creature, it cuts through all.

# Stories of Weapon Effectiveness

## [Ruvljzy yh Ebithewa](/wastelife/characters/ruvljzy-yh-ebithewa) (ɾʉʋɮɤ ɶ eʙɪðʍə)
Ruvlijzy Yh Ebithwa, or just Ru as ve didn’t like everyone mispronouncing their name, was a traveller I met in southern Ereso and actually stuck with for a year. Ve came from the continent Mothdao from a nation ve called ‘ivshahn’ (ɪvʃɐɲ). Ivshahn’s lands are mostly an arid desert, but most of the population lives aside a single river. Ru was in the Ivshahn military as an adept soldier, but ve “had an issue” with their commander and was thrown out of the military. Ve, someway or another, found themselves on Barogate.

I first met Ru around Port Kardel. We were both passing through: I had just come in from Iadaseo in Neble with a small band of adventurers, and ve were wandering around the port looking confused about themselves. I approached them for a talk, as I usually do, and ve told me ve had just come in from a port up in Lale Darie and didn’t really know where to go.

Ru’s attire stood out to me instantly. It was one of the reasons I wanted to talk to them. Ve wore a thick cloth dress with large, padded sleeves with nothing on their legs, and a grand turban. Ve were well armed, noticeably having an exotic shaped sabre, and perhaps more importantly eight thin round metal rings on their arms, and another on their turban. I didn’t bring them up during the conversation on the port, but I managed to get him to join my small adventuring group.

We were wandering from Sedo to Vichiku when we were stopped and threatened by an armed band. When the band first stopped us, there was no way of telling how many there was but I expected from experience that they were about 20 people strong. As a honest band of 7 adventurers, one of us not versed in combat, our group tried to avoid any conflict without making any compromises. One thing lead to another and we were outnumbered in combat.

When it was clear that fighting had started, the first thing Ruvljzy did was take one of the rings of their left arm and threw it expertly at one of the bandits in the group that confronted us. That projectile flew through the air and sliced straight through one of the bandit’s legs, cutting it right through to the bone.

Ruvljzy was an expert with their sabre. In that combat ve went up against a spear and a sword alone. With another projectile and some impeccable footwork, he outmanoeuvred the spear and parried the sword in a few actions. He won that encounter, totalling four people in that entire combat. Two of the people in our group used spells, which sent the rest of the bandits running.

It was after the combat when Ru and we talked. Ve told us that the projectiles ve used were called Chakrams, and that they were of common use all over the deserts of Mothdao and even elsewhere. Ve said that the first thing that struck them coming into the continent was the lack of any Chakram as they are so common in their home nation.

But I suppose we do have something similar to the chakram: the wizard wing. Wizard rings are rings that wizards enchant so that they can throw them for an offensive effect. But even though they share the same shape and are both used as projectile weapons, wizard rings are not sharp and are never large enough to fit around the arm. Their shape is merely for magical and constructive efficiency, while chakrams have that extra offensive capability.

## [Kyelee Ebhand](/wastelife/characters/kyelee-ebhand)
Kyelee Ebhand was a Reyens I met in Darie who was incredibly handy. He was a crossbow connoisseur; one of the few craftspeople of the wheeled crossbow on Barogate. Kylee owned a Shibel’s Crossbow. I was searching for another after initially seeing one in southern Llyhai used against me. It was in heavy disrepair and I couldn’t get it to fire. I had been searching for someone with the knowledge to repair it while walking across the continent. Someone had eventually pointed me towards a crossbow master in Darie who might own something similar.

Kyelee didn’t explicitly know how to repair one, but he told me that he got the crossbow from Shibel, the best crossbow smith from the cold continent from the north. I never found Shibel during my travels up there. Either way, Kyelee noticed what parts of my crossbow were missing and replaced them. He repaired it so that it looked like his, and it worked like a charm. It shot farther and faster than any other crossbow I had ever seen.

I used the crossbow in many combats since. It shot clean through armour, and even straight through certain gem golems. It was unstoppable. I even learnt some enchantment to make the bolt fly faster. It broke once more, and instead of trekking all the way up to Darie to meet Kyelee again, I sold it to a Akiiogan crossbow smith for a high price. She claimed she could fix it, and keep its wonder in circulation. I never checked back on her.

## [Shames Veshici](/wastelife/characters/shames-veshici)
Before I went adventuring, I was in the army. Truth be told, I went back to the army for a short while but decided I preferred adventuring. In the army, I had a good friend called Shames Veshici. He was in the same group as me, and we both carried the same armaments. We were spear combatants with slings as an easy projectile weapon. I can’t tell of my own sling’s effectiveness, but once I was standing in a separate division to Veshici and watched the rocks fly across the battlefield and onto the opponent’s army. Although it was an indirect attack, the enemy was unprepared and I saw at least an eighth of them drop, whether they were dead or if they just had a concussion.

I convinced Veshici to come with me once the fighting was over, and he did. On the road, it was just us two. We were following the coast from Ralean to the border with Kyenel. We were attacked by a starved wolf along the way. Shames was still carrying his army sling, while I had moved on to my staple weapon, the longsword. I tried to keep the wolf back with the length of my blade, but in the end, it was Veshici who was throwing rocks at army speeds straight into the dog’s body. Eventually, the dog was subdued hurt extremely by the sling’s rocks. I dropped some healing potion on its wounds and carried onwards.

At a port town near the border, Veshici decided he couldn’t leave Ralean. I continued alone on a boat to Cronar to gather the colourful sand that sells for plenty in Lale Darie.

## Ria from Alinreg
Ria was a troubled individual who I journeyed through the Heabe and Vugaix with. We were part of a larger expedition through the wastes to get the rare spell ingredient called a Black Heart, which is a type of mushroom. The journey overall was exiting and interesting, but Ria and her companion ended up being quite an antagonist. One thing led to another and she ended up on the other side of my longsword, and I against her sword and buckler.

We were in the middle of an oasis in the Vugaix. She had sent an ice spike from a spell straight through one of my colleagues. She survived, but only because of another ally, our guide, who was healing her. This was an expedition of mages, ria and I were the only full melee fighters.  She was wearing scale armour that covered the front and back of her body, as well as a skirt. I had no hope of cutting through that, so I had to aim for the arms, underarms, face and legs.

I wouldn’t have remembered everything that happened in the combat now if it wasn’t for one of the members writing a memoir of the entire journey. Her name was Iana Easa. I recommend you read the entire memoir, but I’ll write down my combat with Ria here from my perspective.

I made the first attack, starting in a middle guard and cutting lightly downwards, to get a reaction from ria. There was no reaction, so I followed through with a light thrust, which Ria displaced to her right safely, then backed away. I made a fairly telegraphed cut to Ria’s legs, which she withdrew. I had to raise my sword quickly to block two cuts in order left then right. I had my sword in a high guard, and Ria faced her buckler upwards in anticipation. I cut diagonally starting left, which she blocked and shallowly cut my gambeson.

With my sword bottom right, I made a safe cut upwards and towards the left of Ria’s face, which she blocked well with her buckler alone and then cut my stomach shallowly. In the same movement, I raised my sword above Ria’s buckler and made a thrust that glanced Ria’s face. At that point I believed I understood Ria’s tactics, or her understanding of my tactics.

I rushed in with a horizontal cut top left to top right, which I moved downwards and cut upwards across the side of Ria’s body. Her defence in the first part was solid, but she hadn’t been expecting the added aggression. Although she crunched into the wound, she managed to leave another mark on my gambeson. Combat ended there, as I had won.

In that combat, I felt as if we were equally skilled. I felt as if I’d won on the advantage I had with reach on my longsword, but remembering the combat properly, I made good and dangerous decisions.
