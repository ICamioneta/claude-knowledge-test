The account of Wizard culture in Barogate and Anula by Orsolya Venn.

Let it be said that I am primarily a traveller, but keeping in the company of high wizards necessitates some learning of their craft. For conversation, if not its own practicality. 

Most who use hidosis do so without flamboyance and self-distinction. They mend wells, warm hearths, heal with potions. In these lands, rich in hidol, such a practice is woven into daily life as thoroughly as bread. Proper wizard culture is another matter. It is a particular clique of the fascinated hidists who have dedicated themselves wholly to the art. 

The origins of these cultures on Barogate andd Anula trace to the ancient Nao institute in Naokeiot. The names of the first wizards of their cultures are lost to time, and each modern descendant has their long-lasting input on the culture. 

There are two ways wizards enter a culture. Either they are self-taught high wizards who find a culture for community and mutual improvement, or they are an apprentice of a current wizard. Wizards of a culture usually find themselves in wealth, from the great demand of their services, the fortune of their parents, or the patronage of a society. 

# Tower Wizards

The tower wizard is a creature of solitude. He raises a spire far from any towns, often with a modest compound of ground buildings at its foot. Custom holds that a true tower must have no fewer than five floors. Seven to ten is held the proper measure: a height that shows accomplishment without vanity. To exceed it is thought excessive. 

There is however a sect known as the skyscraper wizards, who aspire to build as high as stone and sorcery allow. They are a competitive lot, and their towers are built with many defences against the sabotage of their rivals. It is useful to pretend you have not visited a rival's tower. 

The tower wizard's isolation is not only of distance but of society. Supplies are left at a chosen spot for townsfolk to gather, and payment is left in kind. Any dealing that requires speech is entrusted to a domestic servant. The servant's pay is exceptional, and the wizard's favour is a great benefit. The hours are so generous that I know several who took the position for the leisure alone.

Should you win an audience with a tower wizard, understand that it is a rare communion, and not lightly given even among their peers. Most speak only with their servants, and then perhaps five times in a day, no more.

# Hat Wizards

Hat wizards are rather social, collecting company where tower wizards would flee it. They gather in friend-groups of like power, commonly two to eight in number, though larger convocations are known. Wizards of any culture often wear hats, but the hat wizard wears one that is large, pointed, and gloriously coloured, usually with robes to match. 

The hat is a matter of self expression, with some groups wearing their own special uniform. Drab robes of grey or brown are held in contempt, save for the very old, who are forgiven, being drab themselves. Despite this aspect of cultural norm, I have found that old wizards can be the most egregious. 

The hat wizard is restless. They may own a house, but it will be far humbler than their purse deserves, for they are seldom in it. They roam. They walk the roads, dine at inns, and forage in the field. Given the means to fly, they will nearly always fly.

A word of warning: should a hat wizard ask whether you would care to go for a walk, decline politely. A hat wizard's "walk" is a trek of a full week across the whole of the country. To abandon it midway is to be shunned, and I say this from experience.

In keeping with their sociable nature, the hat wizards form institutional societies. These style themselves as authoritative and administrative tribunals but are, in practice, occasions for party and sport. The most celebrated in Shildo is the Hat Society of Redel, which draws its members from as far afield as Reumanas.

# Vault Wizards

The vault wizard is obsessed with artefacts and his vault. It is considered dishonest, cowardly, and an affront to decency to conceal a vault's whereabouts. Honour lies not in hiding precious items, but in how well they is guarded.

Their protections are masterful constructions: magical locks, proximity enchantments, and (for the unpretentious) plain mechanical traps. A dozen layers of defence is common. With equal passion to the defence of his own, the vault wizard labours to break the vaults of his peers. Custom permits the taking of one item from a fellow wizard's vault. Two, if one is feeling cheeky. Taking three or more items is unbecoming. 

The vaults themselves are seldom idle. A wizard will bring out his treasures and lease them to local folk who can afford the price, or sell or auction them outright. Theft of a leased item is uncommon, for the wizard is often well entrenched in his community and will hunt the thief without mercy. But should a thief take an item directly from the vault, that is a fair cop. The wizard will let it go, though he may try to steal it back. It is much to the benefit of any town that a vault wizard should settle nearby, for they bring a great deal of wealth with them.

Though devoted to their vaults, these wizards are restless adventurers, spending more than half their days in pursuit of new artefacts. They often go alone, hire mercenaries, or gather a rag-tag band of friends. They may also travel with other wizards, though in the company of fellow vault wizards, the fair division of spoils is said to be a considerable difficulty.

# Scholar Wizards

I confess that the scholarly wizards are the least exciting of the four, but a complete account must include them, for they still carry their own quirks.

All wizards push the limits of hidosis, but the scholar does so under the orderly pretence of science. He is always attached to some academic institution, usually one concerned with hidology. As a wizard of great power, he often holds a high post. Though he may equally be a fringe and somewhat wild affiliate whom the university cannot bring itself to hire outright, being rather too much for ordinary academics.

The scholar is forever aggrieved that his powerful peers hoard their discoveries or delay publishing their breakthroughs. He is known to pursue other wizards for their secrets, in the name of the public good. If one of these approaches you, and you know anything beyond the mediocre wizardries and mageries, humouring their conversation is sure to keep you for the day.
