This is a collection of stories on Mortal Ascensions. For those unaware, it is said that if a mortal dedicates themselves to a domain in the same way that a God dedicates themselves to their domain, then that mortal will be capable of ascension to Eternity. Another God must help them with the final steps. Usually, this is Medr, the God of Skill, as this process is considered a skill. However, in rare cases such as one covered in this collection, other gods have ascended mortals. 

# Cryver, the first Mortal Ascension
Cryver's house was on fire, and within it lied his son underneath a burning wood truss. Cryver tried to lift the truss, but with no success. His son remained trapped. He watched his son die knowing that only if he were strong enough, he would have been able to save his son. There, he dedicated his life to strength training every day. He prayed to Medr before every day hoping that his training would be successful. At the end of every day, he would try to lift a wooden truss like the one that had killed his son, hoping that he would finally be strong enough. 

Decades had passed, but he still could not lift the truss. He cried out to Medr, cursing that she did not make men capable of the strength needed to lift the truss. Then Medr appeared to him, and Medr made a proposition to Cryver. She said that he would let Cryver decide how strong people can be, so that they would be strong enough to lift the truss, and that she would make Cryver an Eternal, the Eternal of Strength. Medr said to Cryver that he would help others to go through what he went through, and worse. Cryver was to become a God in return for his eternal service to mortals. 

Cryver took the offer, and he said to Medr to let people win enough strength to carry houses. Then, Cryver ascended to Eternity, and he became the God of Strength, not only in body but also in mind. 

# Tanea and the 75 Great Relics
Millennia ago when the Gan first found iron and when the technology had spread to the corners of Barogate, there was a Domuz smith living in the Lale called Tanea. They were a skilled smith, making high quality farm tools or weapons on commission. One day when smithing, passers noticed an odd behaviour from Tanea. He was saying meaningless phrases, and forging like a mad man. He had been cursed by the God of Madness, Gwalloph. He still paid his suppliers, so the people let him forge, mad with the prospects of completing some impossible project.  

He had been working for decades when passers could hear him get exasperated. He was almost angry, shouting insanities aloud like "They must be complete" and "I need more time". Not long after, he collapsed without explanation. All of the items he had forged in his madness disappeared alongside his life. 

On that day, a new God entered Eternity. Medr had been watching Tanea in his final moments, and noticed that he could be ascended. So she did so, and Adnabyd taught Tanea how to be a God. When Tanea created his realm of Eternity, he saw the seventy-five projects he had worked on during his mortal life. He decided that with his newfound power he would finish his mortal work, and gave the seventy-five artefacts seventy-five powerful permanent enchantments. He scattered his seventy-five great relics across the Mortal Realm as a memory of his former self, and now adventurers and all alike scour the world for their resting places. 

# Adferiad and the Temple of Healing
Adferiad’s childhood was ridden with war. She was born to a soldier and lived her earlier life as a camp follower, seeing the death and destruction that war brought with her innocent eyes. But, as she grew up, instead of becoming a child of this war she became a peaceful figure who helped all that she could. After reaching an age where she could leave her carers in a war-camp, she decided to get into politics with one goal: end the senseless struggle of war that dominated her childhood.

The year was -2160. Adferiad grew up experiencing the horrible War of the River. Instead of becoming a propagator of it like many others did, she went to a city in Vezoen and swore that she’d end the war if it was her dying breath that did it. She spent a decade fully understanding the war and its history, even though much of history had been lost to it. She became a loved person for being a person who showed love to all hurt things. She eventually managed to lead a group of people in helping fallen armies and nursing them back to health.

She looked at the war she had been born into. It was a war of hate: both sides seeing some kind of cultural separation between each other and both believing that they deserved dominion over the river crossing. She decided that she and her followers would heal people indiscriminately, forcing soldiers of both sides to lie besides each other in her healing beds. By talking to the other, these soldiers began to realise that their war was for nothing. They were all good people. When they were healed, they would go back to their families and share these newfound sentiments. 

Once the fighting had stopped, Adferiad decided to create an order with the people who helped her nurse armies. She had the building be constructed in Cosaden, a town situated on the river with an indestructible crossing. The town was completely destroyed. Souls scattered around the ashes of buildings. She had her followers rebuild the town and bring new people into it. The town became a flourishing place, all thanks to her order, the Temple of Healing. She spent the rest of her life growing the order and keeping as many people in Vezoen and Thagryene.

Eventually, she became not only the most well-known healer in the world but also the best at her skill. She could bring people back from the dead with her knowledge in healing. Medr saw this skill in Adferiad, and came to her with the knowledge that she could be made to ascend. Adferiad took up this offer, knowing that her domain would be that of healing, and that she would be able to save more mortal lives as a God than she ever could as a Mortal.

# Ofidea and the Snakes
Ofidea was not like other people. She spent a lot of her time with snakes. Ofidea was a Difmuz Sandcamper, living in the great deserts of Kyenel. From a young age, she was accidentally left behind by her Difmuz family, and wandered off in a random direction to find them. Unfortunately, she had gotten herself lost by falling down into a sandy ditch, so that when her parents came back they could not find her as hard as they looked.

Down in this sandy ditch all alone, Ofidea had nothing to do but wait, perhaps for enough sand to blow in so that she could walk out. Within the same day, a snake found Ofidea, this young girl stuck in this hole. It crept up on her, ready to attack a free meal it found in the empty desert. That is when Ofidea, in all of her courage and wisdom, grabbed the snake from behind her by the neck. She looked at this snake, treating its struggling body with great care, and decided to name it Davin.

Ofidea’s first action was to use Davin as a piece of rope, using him to climb out of the hole she was in. Soon, after wandering Kyenel aimlessly with Davin in her hands, she got hungry. It was at this point Davin realised that Ofidea meant no harm to him, as he knew that if she wanted a quick meal, she could have easily eaten him. Ofidea, assuming Davin was her friend, let him on the sands for him to find food for her. Instead of running away, Davin stuck around Ofidea without biting her, thinking that she’d be an indispensable companion, and indispensable did she prove to be.

Ofidea and Davin wandered Kyenel aimlessly for a day, until Davin saw a vulture fly off in a direction. Usually, he would be too slow to get to the corpse in time with his own wriggling body, but with Ofidea’s legs he pointed in that direction using his tail, and she took the hint. Ofidea ran after the vulture, and off in the horizon saw what Davin knew was there. The giant carcass of one of Kyenel’s many giant beasts. Using its carcass, they fed for several days.

Around the carcass, there were several snakes, feasting on large chunks of meat they could find. Davin approached these snakes, and told them about Ofidea’s aid to him, and how helpful she was. All the other snakes couldn’t quite understand whatever Davin was saying, given how they were illiterate snakes, but they soon caught wind and started following Ofidea as well. Ofidea wandered north, advised by Davin, carrying him on her neck and all the other snakes hanging off parts of her body.

Eventually, after going north following vultures to food and picking up more snakes on the way, Ofidea had gained an extremely successful following to the point where each snake could not hang from her body. Instead, only Davin sat on her shoulders as the rest of the snakes had to slither along. Although it was slow, Ofidea soon came to a wooden cart hidden under some sand. Using the snakes she had gained as a following, she and all the snakes dug the cart up, and there was enough room on the cart for all of her snake following.

Eventually, she would reach the edge of Kyenel, where the desert becomes a harsh slope down into the wet bog of Duela. She had reached the northern parts of Kyenel infested with cacti, and there was not far for her to walk until she had reached Davin’s destination. 

She wandered the Duela, as although Davin knew what he was looking for he did not know where it was. The cart, full of snakes, got stuck at every turn. Even with the snakes digging it up, they soon got fed up and ditched the cart to follow Ofidea by slithering. Eventually, after wandering hungry for what seemed too long, something happened to Ofidea that Davin was hoping for, else their adventure was for nothing.

Ofidea had gotten ambushed by a hunting pack of Fall. The Fall are the reptile humanoids that live in the Duela, and luckily for Ofidea and Davin, it was a Matafall. Usually, the Matafall would have attacked and killed Ofidea, who may I remind you is a young and otherwise defenceless girl. As courageous and bold as she was, she would not survive a hunt from a group of Matafall. But the Matafall looked upon this child and saw her surrounded in snakes. The Matafall decided to bring her peacefully to their camp, which was built in a clearing with every house having an open top.

Ofidea was brought to their leader, who upon seeing her asked when dinner was, even though she was still covered and followed by Snakes. The bold girl and the elder Matafall did not speak the same language, but the leader of the village knew everyone in town and had her brought to one of his kind who spoke the sandcamper’s Shaiou. From then, communication was easy. The Shaiou-speaking Matafall knew about as many words as the child covered in snakes. So, they talked about Ofidea’s journey.

Ofidea told them that Davin had been directing her and all of the snakes, and how she’d been helping lead them on an adventure. The Matafall looked down upon Davin, and talked to him in Fallet. Davin could not speak the language, but repeated back to the Matafall what sounds he could pronounce. The Matafall at that moment had an idea, the same idea Davin had when he pointed Ofidea north. The Matafall were to teach Ofidea and Davin and all the other snakes their Fallet language, so that they could talk.

Ofidea and her snakes for the next few years lived with the Matafall. She was told that she’d have to hunt for herself, as they could not feed the many snakes, she brought in with only their hunting. So, as the snakes learnt how to talk, Ofidea was learning how to hunt at the same time. When she caught good game, she shared her food from among the snakes. They lived like this until all of the snakes could speak, and Ofidea had grown into a courageous and strong young woman.

Ofidea grew up with her snakes, and enjoyed talking to them since they could both now speak Fallet. Davin and Ofidea got to know each other even more, and Davin and Ofidea grew to respect each other greatly. They already had a great deal of unspoken respect for each other, and the ability to properly communicate with each other only grew their friendship. She also talked with many of the snakes who followed her on her journey, each of which said they respect Ofidea because of her courage and leadership. They followed her and Davin because they truly believed they were going somewhere, which they only did because of Ofidea’s confidence. They say that even when starving, they looked at Ofidea and knew that she was starving too, but she walked on, and so did they.

Ofidea wandered back across the desert, now with snakes she could talk to. She was armed with tools, and easy ways to quickly transport all of her snakes. Soon, they came back upon the small hole that Ofidea had fallen into as a small child. She thought nothing of it. Her family and her falling being a distant memory she could barely remember. Davin remembered the moment well, reminiscing of his past life as a starving snake, thinking he’ll make a quick meal until he got grabbed. He truly believed it was the end of his life, but was amazed at how firmly she grasped with no fear in her eyes. Davin found it scary, but also inspiring. That’s why he chose to follow her from then and onwards.

Ofidea and her snakes stumbled across an ancient silver ruin. She chose to make this ruin her home, and settled down in its upper layers. It is like this she lived for the rest of her life, tending to her snakes, keeping them fed and keeping them well. Ofidea went on many more journeys, always with her snakes. She met her old family, who she barely recognised but they recognised her well. Davin recognised them as well, so there was no question they were her family. Ofidea cared for all snakes, for they were her life interest.

Medr, who at this time was learning some old Ens skill that had only just been created, came upon an old Ofidea at the end of her life, and her snakes, who at this point were not the same snakes because of the snake’s short lifespan. Medr looked upon Ofidea, and saw the same godly potential she had in few mortals before. Medr knew that Ofidea could become an Eternal, but still she checked that Ofidea was good. She sat down with her, not revealing herself until she was sure that Ofidea was a good candidate to become an Eternal. Eventually, she heard of all of Ofidea’s stories, about the lives and deaths of each of the snakes that followed her, of Davin’s story, and how he will remain a martyr to her snakes. Then, inspired by her tale, Medr revealed herself, and told Ofidea that she could become an Eternal.

Ofidea stated she already felt Eternal to the snakes, them living no longer than two decades. She had seen the life and death of so many of her followers and their families. She said that becoming an Eternal wouldn’t change how she felt, but she accepted the offer anyway. She was excited, knowing that she could have snakes as her domain. So, when Ofidea died, she ascended to Eternity and became the God of Snakes.

# Mwender, the God-Emperor of Annwvyn
Mwender was born in 723 as the child of a prominent king in Annwvyn. They were taught of their continent, and soon believed that all of Annwvyn should be theirs. So they studied hard in the matters of the court and of war, and when their parents died they rose to a weak throne with one goal. They were to become the Emperor of all Annwvyn. 

Mwender spent sixty long years working to make himself the emperor of Annwvyn. Many kingdoms he was able to negotiate collaboration and subservience with, often with some careful condition that Mwender would have to balance. Other lands, Mwender was able to conquer directly with his champions and knights. After fifty years, they spent a large sum of money paying a true master of the arts to create the most grand throne for his seat over Annwvyn. After sixty long years, they only had one more land to ask to join his Empire, one final ultimatum to become the Emperor of all Annwvyn. 

They arranged this meeting. The first grand council of Annwvyn. All of the kings under his banner attended, and they would come out of it having united all of Annwvyn. They just had the throne installed so that they could leave the meeting and immediately sit on it. In the meeting, they managed to unite all of Annwvyn, making all its kings swear allegiance to their banner, paying him taxes and providing him a small retinue of experienced soldiers to join Mwender's personal guard. 

They left the meeting room and marched to his throne room. They treaded gloriously across the purple carpet they had made that was a rich tapestry of their history of conquests. They knew that his work was not done. They had reached the ripe age of 77, and they thought to themself that they would spend the remainder of their life securing the future of their children. They approached his seat, turned around, and began to lay their regal buttocks on a soft silken cushion. It is then - just before their weight pressed down on the glorious silver throne - that they suffered a heart attack and died. Too many fatty foods, perhaps?

Mwender did not know this, but many of the gods were present during Mwender's final meeting, watching as onlookers and impersonating guards. Many had left once the meeting was over, but Medr and a few others followed them to the throne room. All of the gods could see it: Mwender had the capacity to ascend to godhood. As Mwender began to sit on the throne that Medr had helped create, the gods were alarmed to see the heart attack begin within Mwender. In a moment of spontaneity, Medr had Mwender ascend to godhood.

Mwender ruled the Throne of Annwvyn from godhood while they were discovering their domain. But they grew bored of this mortal affair, and soon abdicated the throne. The Empire of all Annwvyn would only last 26 years, and after Mwender's abdications the throne would fall into ruin and legend, ushering an age of war between the kingdoms that Mwender had spent his mortal life brokering peace between. Mwender is said to have larger ambitions he abandoned the throne for, ambitions that no mortal could understand.

# Leider and Medr
Leider was born into a life of banditry. Originally hailing from Annwvyn in poor conditions that would warrant the success of banditry, they formed a roving bandit group. The group would grow in size until it had touched and robbed every corner of Annwvyn. When there was nothing left to rob, he had his group of roving bandits to another continent - to Barogate - to continue his lifestyle built on the backs of stealing and pillage.

After he had pillaged all of Annwvyn, Medr took a special interest in Leider. She saw that he had a skill that she craved to help him develop, hoping that she too might learn this skill. Medr joined the roving bandits, initially under a hidden persona, knowing that her skills would eventually have her rise in the ranks and rule by Leider's side. 

Medr eventually became Leider's right-hand woman, and then she revealed herself as the God of Skill to him. Leider had read the pamphlet of the true nature of the gods, so he knew that Medr was not a god to be worshipped but a god who only appeared to those with whom she wished to train a skill with. Together, Leider and Medr roved around the six continents killing cattle and stealing food. Together, they became the best bandits that could ever exist, and Medr and Leider pushed the boundaries of martial art. 

Medr's secrecy did not last for long. Shortly after she was pillaging alongside Leider she was called out as the God of Skill. It became known to the world that the God of Skill, one of the original six gods who was normally seen as a positive god who helped people become skillful, was burning their villages and stealing their food. When Leider and Medr had a mortal child together, the people found it even more outrageous. Leider, Medr, and the Roving Bandits had touched every corner of the Mortal world.

Medr had always seen the capacity of ascension in Leider, but she kept it a secret from him until the moments of his death. Leider reached the old age of seventy-five when he died. The mortals would not know it for a hundred years, but Medr had ascended Leider to godhood, and Leider had become the God of Banditry. The Roving Bandits were lead by Leider's sons for a while after his death, but they eventually settled permanently after conquering some lands in Ouranxo.

# Tharn, the Necromancer
Tharn the Necromancer is a character in recent memory. Across all of Shildo, crypts were sealed up in hopes that one of Tharn's conduits would not sneak in and raise a city's leagues of ancestors. Tharn first rose and conquered a large section of southern Terlaye. From there, he continually expanded his Kingdom of the Dead for thirty years. 

He was only defeated when a hero united six pieces of a legendary staff and united the Kingdoms of Shildo in one final glorious battle in which the Hero killed Tharn and his legions of the dead were left without a king. Throughout the twenty years of Tharn's rule he had created millions of undead soldiers to his cause, so cleaning up after his death was a challenge shared between across all of Shildo. 

The mortals were aware of Tharn and Medr's potential collaboration, and they begged the God of Skill to not help Tharn train his powers. As a god with a domain, Medr knew that she could not let such an opportunity pass, so she trained with Tharn anyway. But Medr is disciplined, and only cares for her domain. The hero that defeated Tharn had some unique skills that Medr helped train with too, but as much as the Hero willed it Medr would still return to Tharn to train necromancy with him. 

Medr saw the capacity of ascension in Tharn, but she knew it would be a bad decision for she knew the mortals would not trust her for centuries to come. She had already balanced on the fine line in a previous affair with Leider. She told the Hero of Tharn's capacity of ascension as she could not keep it to herself. She promised that she would not ascend Tharn, but she could not promise that another god would not try to anyway. 

When Tharn died, Medr saw Gwrphcim, the God of Antisocialism, nearby. She watched as it was he who ascended Tharn to Eternity. The hero knew Gwrphcim had done this, and tried his best to stop Gwrphcim's actions, but it was to no avail. Tharn had ascended to godhood. 

In his new god form, Tharn unleashed a massive plague on Shildo out of spite: Dehentharn's plague. Dehentharn's plague killed one-third of all the world but Annwvyn, where it did not reach. Tharn changed his name to Dehentharn in attempts to distance himself from his past of conquest, but the mortals were now aware that a God of Necromancy was now present in Eternity.
