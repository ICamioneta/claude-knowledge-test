# Prefaces
Before anything we must address the name of the nation, as internally it is referred to the Republic Union of Gazi, Lale, and Darie. Locals often refer to the country as Gazi Lale Darie while foreigners often drop the Gazi. Locals often use ‘the republic’ in reference to the government.

The native inhabitants of the Dithas are diverse themselves. The most common populations are the Dimad and the Buemad. The other subspecies of Mad are also present in Dithas. In addition to this, populations of Difmuz are quite common in Dithas, and are also called natives. Often, military interaction between the Mad and the Domuz also include the Difmuz.

# Introduction
This account delves into the colonial dominions of the Republic Union of Gazi, Lale, and Darie. Information about this nation seldom reaches our shores, only through the word-of-mouth of sea traders and the little information that crosses the many internal divisions of Kyeron do we hear about their nation. However, as technology is entering a rapid development and our political spheres develop the educated public yearn for insights into our huge world. While the geographical mapping efforts of the likes of Jeisei Oera and geographical institutions are helpful, we can’t help but wonder what life is like in these regions.

I, and a group of unaffiliated adventurers from the Adventurer’s Guild in Caroset (credited at the end) took it upon ourselves to expand the pool of public knowledge on the government of Lale Darie. As we confirmed pre-existing information about the mainland government gathered from other adventurers and hearsay, one specific element of the government was of special interest to me: its overseas colonies. Little did I know at the time that this part of the discovery would take me across the world, from the beautiful islands of Raidal to the dangerous Ashland of Llyhai, and even to the continents beyond Barogate.

## Context and Ambitions
During 3189, Shildo was dominated by the Second Eresonian Empire and the Ralean Empire of the Reimig Dynasty. These two giants fought politically and territorially over Shildo, with the Terlayan kingdoms weaving their way in and out of each political sphere. In the far-off lands of Lale Darie, the ideas of the relationship between the bourgeoisie and the king were shifting rapidly. Lale Darie underwent a ferocious political revolution in 3189 that saw the formation of a federated republic. Later, the country had another major shift in government structure, becoming a republic in 3324 with a governmental structure similar to those proposed by the Polbegeo group during the Eresonian revolution of 3277, and the Ralean Republicans.

The Domuz Republic was a pioneer of the private corporation economical structure, and this did make life better for many citizens, but for workers in some shops and factories work is still dangerous and exploitative. However, in the modern years, life for the Domuz commoner sees as much nicety as modern-day Eresonian life. Don’t be fooled however, because this common luxury is built on the back of the colony's massive wage disparities, resource exploitation, and domestic policies.

Exploitation is the context and the ambition of the Domuz Republic’s expansion into other territories. Despite this common goal, each colony seems to serve a very unique purpose to the Domuz republic, and it is this purpose I will highlight throughout this document.

## Scope of the Report
This report encompasses a wide array of facets surrounding the colonial ventures of the republic. From the economic underpinnings that sustain these distant outposts to how the governance is woven across the regions. Moreover, our specific job at this bureau is to scrutinise the complex interplay of cultures and societies that emerged between the Domuz colonists and the indigenous lands. We will also confront the challenges and conflicts facing each of the colonial enterprises.

# Chapter 1: Colonial Overview

## Yphofthle
The history of the Republic’s first colony actually predates its own existence by several centuries. The colony of Yphofthle has evidence of Domuz presence as early as the 1500s – as far back as Nao Techia. The first domuz to step foot in the land was unlikely part of an organised colonisation attempt and was likely a couple people looking for somewhere to live using tools they brought from their homeland in Lale Darie. The first evidence of the land being controlled by a government back in Lale Daire is a tablet charter dated to 2604 referring to the colony as a territory of a monarch who at the time held dominion over much of Gazi.

Since then, control of the colony shifted de jure between several monarchs from Gazi Lale Darie until the nation’s unification. However, de facto control was often handled domestically as frequently, monarchs weren’t even aware of the colony’s existence. Even under the unified Empire of Gazi Lale Darie, the region was left alone until 2731, when the monarch was informed of the colony’s existence and decided to send troops to it to ‘make the final move in unifying the Domuz people’

The colony from then would experience its trials and tribulations being part of the Empire of Gazi Lale Darie’s internal and external struggles until the Domuz Revolution in 3189, where it served as an escape for much of the old nobility to hide in. The revolutionary intent spread to the colony, and many nobles would land in Yphofthle only to be immediately assaulted and stabbed by the locals.

Since the Republic, Yphofthle has seen a massive population increase mostly due to its economic role in the Republic, which will be discussed in a later section. What will be said here is that the colony has territorially expanded onto the mainland due to the discovery of a few sparce resources.

## Mepheisatha
The history of Mepheisatha starts early in 2641, when a local lord had frustrations attempting to influence his neighbours through military, political, and economical might. This caused him to try to conquer his next nearest neighbour, the Dithas. The lord himself lead the expedition into the territory, but him and his expedition met an end when the native Mad killed them.

Despite this initial failure, the survivors of the lord's expedition learnt that there was a high quality iron source somewhere in the area. Later adventures would confirm this by analysing the iron tools of the natives, however the source was not yet known. The source was noted as promising given the relative lack of iron in nearby Darie regions.

After the unification of Lale Darie, the monarchy of the time would lead a dedicated military expansion into Dithas located at this site in 2742. The battles following were filled with Domuz casualties but eventually the monarchy was able to establish a stronghold in the region, and more importantly find the metal resources that they were promised. For one reason or another, control of these resources would be given to local tradespeople, who pressured the local lord into lowering oversight and taxes.

The region itself is like any other in Dithas, a wet topsoil with a diverse set of fauna and flora. The current territory of the colony revolves around the Mepheisatha river, it's tributaries, and it's delta streams. Control and colonisation is often handled with guerrilla ‘posse’ tactics against the mad natives.

## Cyrox
During the 3270s, there was a massive effort to find resources across Dithas. Such a search wasn’t limited to raw materials, as the companies of the time were also looking to expand their agricultural production with ‘new exotic fungus’. Many locations were scouted by the following wave of colonists, but the inner land of Dithas remained highly dangerous. The coastal regions were available due to a recent expansion to the republic’s navy. During the search for resources, the republic made an exhaustive catalogue of the raw materials, flora, fauna, and local mad tribes. 

The colony of Cyrox started in 3271 as a small naval outpost to help supply ships adventuring across the northern coasts of the wastes. It was established just before the effort to find resources as a purposeful effort to prepare for it. After the effort, several resources were identified inland.  Spots for the raw materials of Lead and Aluminium were found near the coast, while further inland a particular species of mushroom tree was later identified to be useful in the production of adhesives. Other species were identified as useful producers of rubber and textiles. Much later in the colonial expansion, the islands off the coast of Cyrox were inhabited as a place for wealthy company owners who control the resources of eastern Dithas.

## Asthenel
A little later in 3278, a large effort was made to establish a colony on the eastern face of Dithas. This region was previously identified as extremely resource rich on all fronts, housing a large deposit of high-quality iron, many production-useful species of mushroom tree, topsoil suitable for agriculture, but a strong Mad presence. Eventually, a beachhead was established called Asthenel.

The colony would experience a number of trials and tribulations with and against the local mad population, which will be detailed in a later section. In the modern day, the colony describes a number of settlements dotting the eastern coast so far as to meet the border with Cyrox. Because of Asthenel and Cyrox, the entirety of the eastern coast of Dithas is controlled by the Domuz.

## Isles of Mourn
During the 3280s, there were ideas floating around amongst the ruling classes about ‘tackling the white hell’. This white hell referenced was Llyhai, a vast land covered in ash and under the absolute dominion of ichmenre. The history of person settlements in Llyhai at the time began and ended with the immensely powerful Kan-Koronar. They built settlements on the western shores of Llyhai during the 2050s, and these colonies were covered in blood until they were completely demolished by 2110.

The idea was discarded as a needless loss of life, until a myriad of materials were reported by so-called ‘ash-wanderers’. The ash wanderers were the group of prospectors risking their lives in Llyhai looking for any source of resource. Once the first reports came back, it became clear to the Republic that Llyhai wasn’t shy of resources at all. So, in 3290, the Republic sent a massive military section alongside a number of eager colonists to the closest part of llyhai, now called the Isles of Mourn.

In the modern era, the islands themselves are well protected from ground assaults, although there are still subterranean and airborne dangers. The modern colony also consists of a few settlements on the mainland, but the fight for territory there is an ongoing struggle especially with the proximity to Ichmenre.

## Bhethfei
Llyhai is not a barren Ashland. Few species of plant life manage to survive in the open. There are plenty of geographical features, from mountains to ruined buildings. Llyhai even has a river flowing through it. Although the water will kill you if you drink it, thanks to modern advancements in water purification it can be a source of water.

It is near the mouth of this river that the Republic decided to establish its second Llyhai colony in 3310. Specifically selected to be near rare-earth metal deposits of antimony, bismuth, and tungsten, the modern colony describes a wide area of separated towns.

## The Lost City of Fanthel
Littered across the wastes are the silver remains of a civilisation long gone. The exact nature of these remains remain a mystery and each of the remains themselves are dangerous to delve down. It is theorised that what we see on the surface may only be the tip of a massive, sprawling underground network where this lost civilisation used to live.

Ash runners found many silver ruins on their travels and considered many of them as potential stronghold locations, but ultimately each of them were too far from the shores to properly use. One of them, situated on the southern shores of the largest ash-covered island, was reachable.

In 3340, the Republic sent a massive military force alongside a large group of colonists in hopes to set up a colony to explore the ruins, called ‘the lost city of Fanthel’ because there are some markings near the entrance to the ruins that roughly – with a stretch- look like the word ‘fanthel’ in Carelia. The exact series of events will be told in a later section of this document, but initially the colony was a small outpost outside the ruins and in the modern day the upper floors of the ruins themselves are occupied.

## Baron-Gazi
Once the year 3350 had been reached, and the Domuz colonies in Llyhai remained intact and with no signs of immediate ruin, there was massive celebration among the ruling class. Kan-Koronar had settlements on Llyhai for 60 years, and they had met that record. By 3370, the Domuz colonisers were filled with motivation, crowning themselves the most powerful and far-reaching state in history. New prospectors sailed across the surface of the world and found themselves in new, exotic locations.

There is more to this world than Barogate and Anula. Even nearby to Ereso, the islands of Raidal aren’t well known despite our councilist comrades on the islands being an important trade partner. But beyond Raidal, directly west of Ereso, there is the continent of Mothdao. South-west, the continent of Raixzer, and north-west, the continent of Oranax.

The Republic sought to touch on each of these. If they could visit foreign continents and maintain a military presence on them then they would be far more powerful than whatever local government was there. This would bring the great opportunities of business, and expansion to their empire. This first expansion game in the form of the colonisation of Raidal, and the establishment of Baron-Gazi.

Baron-Gazi is a conglomerate of islands with a majority Domuz population thanks to massive migration efforts from the Republic. They hold a number of islands well supported by the Republic’s modern navy fleets. Their main and largest island is Acadym in the archipelago of Llynllanes.

Although Baron-Gazi does maintain some exotic exports to the mainland, the main purpose of this colony is to demonstrate the globality of the Republic’s influence. Despite the Republic’s attempts to establish a colony on Mothdao, Baron-Gazi remains their furthest colony.
## Fauson
By 3380, the economy of the Republic was exploring many new types of materials, supplied almost exclusively by their colonies. It was around this time that the colony of Cyrox expanded south in order to harvest much of the useful flora in the area. One spot in Llyhai was of particular interest for having deposits of silver, tungsten, and platinum.

A large military force and several colonists were sent to the region – a practice now well-performed by the Republic’s colonial administration. The colony was establishes, but to this day Fauson remains the colony with the most pushback against Domuz occupation. Because of the rare materials, many Domuz companies fight hard to protect the region and extract the wealthy tungsten from the area.

## Oedreci
At the turn of the century, the Republic’s colonial administration sent well-prepared ‘invasion forces’ to several other continents. The first to land did so on the continent of Oranax. Not much is known about this continent but its climate. Oranax is a cold place, far north and without any volcanic activity like Llyhai to keep it warm.

The colony of Oedreci is a settlement in the political sense between the Domuz Republic and the local government. It is an outpost of cultural and economic exchange between the Domuz and the local government, the Wafthulham Principality. The treaty was made in 3400 at the end of a military conflict. In the modern era, it remains the Republic’s only colonial settlement outside of Barogate.

## Phemfbharei
Within the frame of recent history, the Republic continues to set up colonial settlements. The most recent of these is the Bay of Phemfbharei, which is a large collection of settlements on the eastern coast of Llyhai. The geography of the bay is quite special, a underwater raised bank protects the inner bay from the harsh underwater weather of the ocean. Many islands poke up on the outside of the bay, continuing the line of terrain on the coast.

Phemfbharei is a unique colony in that it is the only Llyhai colony that is attempting to live underwater. Underwater settlements are uncommon in Lale Darie but still present. In Lale Darie, there are underwater settlements in the Lephio, Lale-Deor, Dalmaw, Oenes, and Gazi regions, but no-where else on these two continents. That is, except for in this bay.

The first settlement in Phemfbarei was a small outpost town established in 3418 that is nearby a specific area of flora that’s useful for magery. Since then, many more small towns were established across the waterfront of the bay until an underwater military operation took place in order to secure the bay. Although the bay isn’t clear of threats, construction efforts began in 3462 on the underwater town.

# Chapter 2: Economic Activities
Colonial trade has become an important part of the Republic’s economy. Many tradespeople rely on colonial exports and imports to maintain their trade, and thanks to the special materials and minerals many trades are available in the Republic that there is simply no economic precedent for in Shildo. Although much of production from the colony’s resources is handled in Lale Daire, many of the trades handling colonial goods is handled in the colony that produces them.

Many of the colonies are not self-sufficient, especially those in regions with bad soil. Food in the Dithas colonies is mostly harvested to be exported, and much of the worker’s food is common grains grown in the agricultural powerhouses of Lale Darie. Food in the Llyhai colonies almost relies on imports with the harsh infertility of the grounds. Domestic Llyhai food production is almost exclusively fishing and hunting.

## Yphofthle
Yphofthle’s economy comes in two parts: the islands and the Dithas mainland. On the islands, the economy is largely tourist-based as the islands serve a great vacation for the well-off of the Republic. Most of the people living on the islands are actually colonial investors with stakes in companies, or rich guild owners taking month-long breaks from their management jobs.

Local food production primarily consists of mushrooms and a few local fruits. Grains are a large import as home species struggle to grow in the soil. Delicate mushrooms are sometimes farmed, but these are difficult to grow and more often foraged.

There are rumors that during the Domuz’s first inhabitation of the island there were Mad tribes living on them, but any mad presence has been completely eradicated long before the colonial period of the Republic. There is a nearby settlement of Mad on the mainland who claim to descend from the Mad inhabitants of the islands. All foraging is kept strictly outside of these borders, although it is common for Domuz foragers to meet Dimad foragers on the job.

There are some efforts in the colony to grow the best of the mushroom delicacies on farms. Most of these efforts have limited success, and wild foraging remains a more economically viable way to obtain the delicacies. It has been confirmed that advanced enchantment-enhanced growth is crucial for farming these plants.

### Erigandra
In 3268, rare insulative fungal plants called Erigandra were discovered, so a colonial outpost was established on mainland Dithas and began exporting Erigandra to Lale Darie. Slowly, parts of the insulative process moved into the colony. First, the trunks were cut into smaller, manageable, and shippable trunks in the colony around 3271. Then, the grinding and milling process went to the colony which made the trunks ship as a fine dust. This section of the process required the trunks to be dry, so a small section of skilled worker was moved to the colony in order to construct the necessary facilities to do so. Soon after that, the entire refinement process moved to the colony in 3272.

## Mepheisatha
This colony mostly mines metal and exports it to mainland Darie for refinement. The specific metals mined are Iron, copper, and zinc. These exports may seem unimportant, but the nearby Darie regions don’t have their own domestic production for these metals and previously had relied on imports of material from Darie and the islands.

Mepheisatha actually maintains its own food supply. Soil has been imported from mainland darie and used to fertilise common grains grown here. This colony has a fairly large foraging industry, but there is no agreement with the local Mad tribes about territorial boundaries so the industry sparks some conflict.

Many mad communities have integrated the colony, accepting Domuz military presence in or near their camps in exchange for co-operation, protection, and trade with the domuz. These Mad tribes benefit from some trade, where they have a craftsman culture involving the high-quality iron sources found in the region. Small trinkets, tools, and weapons they make with their unique production are traded for common goods in a massive bulk. Mepheisatha is one of the only colonies with an export of Mad culture, in fact Mad migration from Dithas to Lale Darie often happens through this colony.

This talk of cooperation from the Mad locals may seem positive, but in reality Mepheisatha is covered in the blood of the locals. This colony began as a military effort to locate iron sources by the monarchy, and they didn’t politely ask to take it. There was a war between the immediately local tribes and the Domuz invaders, and thanks to their recently unified country the Domuz came out on top. Since, all efforts of expansion have been met with resistance from the natives. In every instance, the Domuz were able to gather a military force to quell this resistance.

In the modern day, the people of Mepheisatha are well armed. Frequently, some dispute is made with the natives and a small band of Domuz locals form and cause a small scene. This is commonly called ‘posse-ism’, where a leader with a small band of locals takes suppression of the natives into their own hands. In complacent Mad and Difmuz tribes, there are movements among them asking for reparations for the deaths of their family members from killed tribes, although these movements have little to no momentum.

## Cyrox
Cyrox’s economy is largely dependent on the export of Tynoacrethi, Hipsxegler, and Erigandra, as well as the material exports of lead and aluminium. There is lots of local refinement of these goods, but a lot of their uses require advanced tools and methods that are only available in Lale Darie. Tynoacrethi is used for the production of a special adhesive, hipsxegler is used in the production of a type of rubber, and Erigandra here is used for both insulation and textiles.

In order to feed the colony, there is a large agricultural economy in Cyrox. Soil is imported from Lale Darie in order to grow usual grains, and there is a large farm industry for the domestic edible mushrooms. There is also foraging efforts for luxury mushrooms and magery ingredients, but these aren’t as viable than the same efforts made in Yphofthle and Mepheisatha. There is a large hunting industry that produces a good amount of meat for the colony, as well as efforts to domesticate and farm some of the local animals.

Interactions with the natives have been mostly antagonistic with the high presence of useful mushroom trees driving the need for control over the land. In many cases, tribes of Mad are made complacent and absorbed into the colonial industries, often sharing their own traditions of production to help develop the colony that suppresses them. The towns of Cyrox are well protected from hostile Mad and Difmuz tribes, and it has been documented that the Mad tribes have been using soldiers of different subspecies than the Dimad and Buemad, such as the Guarmad and Cuemad populations. In addition to this, there is a strong pushback from the Difmuz communities present in the Dithas against the colony.

### Tynoacrethi
Tynoacrethi is used in the production of adhesives, which are used extensively everywhere. It is because of this that Tynoacrethi is the primary industry of Cyrox. Some efforts have been made to refine the plant locally, but it is much more economically viable to ship the plant to Lale Darie for their developed industries to handle the refinement instead. The industry of Cyrox has been slowly developing throughout the last century to the point where domestic production does occur, but this is mostly shipped eastwards towards other colonies.

### Hipsxegler
The production and refinement of Hipsxegler is a complex and advanced process that at this time has no hopes of becoming domestic to the colony. Hipsxegler is refined into rubber, which at the moment is used for luxury shoes, sealing, military grade tents, boat repair, medicine, and for the production of waterproof undergarments.

Domuz fashion is special and colourful, but these dresses are often worn over a tight waterproof garment. Underwater cultures only wear the garment as the sea ruins most other fabrics they wear over it. This waterproof garment has been part of Domuz tradition for centuries, and is supposed to signify the waterborne nature of the people. Up until recently, these garments have been entirely leather-based. With the recent production of rubber, some people have switched to rubber as their material of choice for such clothing. This movement hasn’t gained much attraction since the rubber is a noisy and uncomfortable material.

## Asthenel
The first settlement in Asthenel was chosen because of its capacity to maintain many of the crops farmed in Lale Darie. Because of this, Asthenel is used to grow common grains and fibres that it not only uses for self-sustainability but also to export to Llyhai colonies. It doesn’t have enough production for all of the Llyhai colonies, so it is also used as a port to ship crops to, reducing shipping costs overall as ships that leave with Asthenel’s goods come back with grains.

Apart from grains, Asthenel is also a prime region for iron and magery ingredients. A specific magery ingredient collected here is Capselith. This small mushroom has a strong fire-producing component when used in reactions. It is gathered and exported around the world to adventurers and is used by part of the Republic’s military due to its strength. A special way to prepare the mushroom was invented by a tribe that used to be in Asthenel. The tribe taught the Domuz this method, the domuz rewarded the tribe by pushing them out of their land and eventually incarcerating them with ‘possy-ism’

There is a large deposit of high-quality iron in Asthenel which has turned many of its towns into metallurgy factories. In these factories, the iron is turned into a high-quality steel before being shipped to the rest of the world. Although Lale Darie has a domestic steel industry more than capable of supplying the country, due to its higher quality Asthenel steel companies have been slowly taking over the market causing Lale Darie to have some reliance on Asthenel steel. A good chunk of Asthenel steel makes it to Ereso.

There is some cooperation between the colony and the native Mad population, and it is true that this population is treated better than those in Mepheisatha and Cyrox. Neglecting this colony’s bloody history is not an easy feat however, as eastern Dithas was once littered with Mad and Difmuz colonies. The integrated mad populations are traded with much in the same way as those in Mepheisatha, for their exotic goods. This is the only colony on Dithas where it is normal – but not accepted – to see Difmuz roaming around who aren’t trying to burn the operation to the ground.

## Isles of Mourn
For the sake of brevity, every Llyhai colony receives all of their grains and vegetables from either Asthenel or the mainland. Every Llyhai colony has a domestic food industry that comprises entirely of hunting down and cooking the dangerous Llyhai creatures and fishing, the former of which isn’t usually done on purpose but instead in defence. The meats of these creatures isn’t usually tasteful but there are exceptions to this. In addition to that, some goods are produced from these creatures such as magery ingredients. These are usually exported to Lale Darie, sometimes making a stop in Asthenel or Cyrox.

The Isles of Mourn has established mining operations on sources of platinum, iridium, cerium, and neodymium. While there are mining operations for these metals, there isn’t much demand for them across the world. Mining operations extend to the mainland of Llyhai, where most of the mining is done on high-quality copper and zinc sources. These are sent to Lale Darie and turned into brass.

Llyhai is littered with dangerous and powerful creatures. These monsters pose a threat to any person who wants to live on the ash lands. But these creatures aren’t all bad, as killing them can provide the best and most exotic materials present on the two continents. The Isles of Mourn for example lies right next to the volcanoes of Ichmenre. I have done extensive research into Ichmenre, the findings of which lay outside of the scope of this report, but I will summarise what is relevant for understanding the economics of the Llyhai colonies, especially the Isles of Mourn.

There are fourteen volcanoes in Ichmenre, each of which commands an army of ash. These armies periodically attack any living colony in Llyhai in an active war. These wars are always purely destructive, but many of the creatures that make up these armies can contain creature stones. The foot soldier of these armies is the Ashmuz, a creature made of pure ash that is in the shape of a muz. These creatures often contain creature stones. When an Ashmuz dies, the ash that it consists of falls to the ground with the stone buried somewhere inside of it. If the stone is found, it can be made into a useful magery ingredient.

The Kaddenf is a large stone bat-like monster bound by fire. The Porellysag is a person-shaped creature of pure fire. The Kaddedan is an airborne fire beast that look like vultures with Lovyn wings. All of these creatures are in the armies of Ichmenre, and all of these creatures sometimes have creature stones that are especially powerful as magery ingredients. This is by no means a conclusive list to the creatures in the armies of Ichmenre that have creature stones, but mentioning every single one will take too long. Put simply, the collection and export of these creature stones is a major industry for the Isles of Mourn.

## Bhethfei
Bhethfei is unique in that it sits on somewhat fertile land next to the river. The colony has a developed water purification industry that makes this water drinkable. The river and its surrounding regions are good locations for the flora of Llyhai to grow. Little of this flora is actually edible, but a few plants are actively farmed in this location for food. Bethfei still requires a hefty import of food, usually from Asthenel.

Bhethfei’s primary mining operations are far from the river and mine antimony, bismuth, and tungsten. These resources have found a good use as hidol-sensitive materials and are often used when forging objects intended for enchanting. They are exported to all over the world, but this source of these metals is Lale Darie’s main supply. There is a small domestic refining industry, but most refinement happens in Lale Darie.

## The Lost City of Fanthel
This colony, usually called Fanthel, has little to no industry outside of the silver ruins themselves. The ruins are thoroughly explored in the upper levels, but deeper down there are secrets adventurers could only hope to find out one day. The surface levels and the other upper levels are fully looted for any historical artifact, which were quickly shipped over the world to be sold for extreme prices. On the lower levels, adventurers have managed to loot a great deal of the important artifacts, but a few cheaper ones still persist. There are levels deeper and further still that could contain more things.

The Lost City of Fanthel also contains a slither of knowledge from whatever civilisation used to inhabit it. Fringe researchers find out what they can about the creations of this civilisation. The structure also contains a number of automata, the study and looting of which brings more export to the colony. 

Onto the talk of specific things that have come out of the colony: the Archetech. Devices found in the ruins still function to some capacity, but are unmaintainable with current technology. The Archetech Repeater is an ancient machine that fires high-speed projectiles of varying types, but the bolts used to fuel this weapon cannot be produced and are only found in the ruins. The Republic has made their own design which they call the Republica Firearm, which is produced in Lale Darie.

Present in the Lost City of Fanthel are several automata believed to be used by the ancient civilisation to perform many of its tasks. These automata are mostly in ruin, however some still operate. The living automata are quite volatile, as disruption to their tasks often has them idling. Some automata were clearly built for defence, as one of the dangers of delving into the silver ruins are these combat automata.

## Baron-Gazi
Taking a sharp turn from colonies in the Wastes, Baron-Gazi's economy is self-driven. For food, it manages its own fishing and agricultural routes. Its industrial economy is also self-driven, having its own thriving artisan community. Much of its economy is sustained by its ongoing conflicts with the neighbouring Kingdom Naxdyme. This war is draining on the economy of Baron-Gazi, where the country's war economy is held up almost entirely by the republic's own military. 

In times of peace, Baron-gazi's exports back to the mainland have been minimal, almost entirely cultural products from other Raidal islands and local magery ingredients. 

The foundation of Baron-Gazi was violent, and during it the indigenous cultures were almost entirely displaced. While some remaining local Caran folk continue to practice their native culture, most remaining have fully converted to a new Baron-Gazi culture highly based on the Lale cultures.
## Fauson
Like many Llyhai colonies, Fauson's agricultural economy is entirely supplied by Cyrox. It maintains a large fishing economy as all of its self-supplied food. 

The industry of Fauson revolves entirely around the extraction of silver, tungsten, and platinum. The latter two aren't currently in high demand, but silver is used directly for currency so its extraction and refinement by private companies under the Domuz banner is causing many of those companies to get very rich. 

On the other side of the Fauson economy is the high demand for war. It has received the majority of militant resistance from a myriad of forces. These forces include: local fauna, aggressive Ashlander tribes, and Ichmenre ash armies. For this reason, and its central location, the Domuz Admiral of the Colonial Fleet is stationed here, directly coordinating defence and expansion of the colony.

## Oedreci
Oedreci is largely a colonial outpost. The majority of its local economy is focussed around trade with Oranaxan nations. Although it does have some local agricultural produce, it economically relies on trade to supply its food and other aspects of its economy.

Its major exports are furs and timber. Native Oranax nations have a long history of farming furs of all qualities, and the cold climate of the continent necessitates that the creatures living there are covered in furs. As for timber, Oranax is home to a number of unique tree species, the wood of many of which have qualities in high demand in the Domuz Republic. A variety of Oranax trees produce the hardest wood, the most flexible wood, and durable woods.

## Phemfbharei
Bhemfbharei is a unique colony for having the only underwater town outside of Lale Darie. Its current economy is highly focussed around finishing the construction of this town, primarily importing relevant goods directly from relevant nearby colonies and the mainland. As for its on-land territory, resource extraction is low but is slowly picking up. The colony is prospecting for rare minerals while temporarily maintaining its economy on the resources gained from local fauna. 

As a colonial project, Phemfbharei clearly looses money for the government, but it acts as a monument to the Republic's wealth and reach. Among this display of wealth and reach, many adventurers and private entities are trying to found a road directly from the colony to Fauson, which is a long route that ultimately won't be able to replace the efficiency of naval transport around the coast.

# Chapter 3: Governance and Administration
The Government of the Domuz Republic is centred around a democratic central government similar to the modern government of Vania after the recent 3456 constitution. The Eresonain criticisms of the 3456 constitution are largely applicable to the Domuz Republic. One of the primary differences is the election of a head of government. While Vanian parties need to agree to put a head-of-state in charge (an event that is unlikely to occur until the complete collapse of Labour), the Domuz Republic's President is elected on a system best described as wealth voting, where individuals with more money are afforded more votes. This has lead to a president with massive favour to the rich, and the majority of Lale Darie's most wealthy people have huge steaks in the colonies.

As for colonial administration, there are four types of colonial government officially recognised by the government. These are Imperial Colonies, Self-governing Colonies, Frontier Colonies, and Autonomous Territories. These four government types generally encapsulate the Domuz's colonies in their entirety.
## Imperial Colonies
The Imperial Colonies are colonies very closely connected to the central government. The two imperial colonies are Yphofthle and Mepheisatha. Imperial Colonies have locally elected councils where members are chosen from a colony-wide pool. Votes for these elections are only available to the colonial elite, determined based on colonial tax contribution. Imperial colonies also have a colonial governor appointed directly by the president, and as the president is also elected by tax contribution the members of the colonial councils and the governor tend to be massively in favour of industrialists.

The Imperial Colonies are integrated into central government. Citizens get a vote for a member of the House of Representatives, and need a citizenship from a particular cultural region to vote for senator of that cultural region. They are also militarily integrated, receiving similar recruitment efforts and being under the jurisdiction of the Admiral of the Home Fleet.

Imperial colonies act a lot like devolved regions, managing local taxation, resource allocation for infrastructure, education and schooling, and policing and public order within the limits defined by national law. 

The President of imperial colonies is responsible for oversight of the council and can veto or override almost any decision with the caveat of two-thirds council approval.

Following are the interactions between specific imperial colonies with their native populations, as well as how many seats they have on their council.
### Yphofthle
Yphofthle has 17 seats in council. 

The local Yphofthle government manages relations with 20 settlements near its border. These native settlements were guaranteed independence and that the colony would not expand with a recent declaration made in 3454. Many Mad tribes live within the border. While their presence and lifestyle is often tolerated by the local government, these tribes have no guarantees and are sometimes forced to move to make way for plantations.
### Mepheisatha
Mepheisatha has 11 seats in its council. 

Mepheisatha has a continually straining relationship between the government and indigenous population. The colony maintains a veneer of cooperation between indigenous tribes and the local government by allowing them private ownership of their own territory within the colony. Tribes who accept this deal often find themselves with private plots consisting uniquely of their settled territory, and the land that they forage and survive from is industrialised and turned into farmland, mines, and privately managed forage-land. 

Tribes that reject this offer are frequently misplaced, as the land they settle is sold to other private investors. Tribes on the colonial border are generally resistive to movement, but the small military units known as posses are highly effective in driving them out.

## Self-Governing Colonies
The self-governing colonies are colonies with economic and sovereign ties to the Republic. The two self-governing colonies are Cyrox and Asthenel. Although they often have their own government which manages matters of colonial development, the majority of local government is actually handled privately, as corporations manage their own private armies that essentially enforce a corporate law in their private land. 

The self-governing colonies have no council, instead the colony's own policies are managed entirely by the governor, appointed by the President of the republic. The governor is allowed to make huge legislative departures from the laws of the Republic to the point where the colonies essentially act as their own small nation. 

The policies enacted by the colony itself are only really enforceable in land directly owned by the colonial administration. The rest of the land, owned directly by private entities and corporations enforces its own law, which is sometimes strictly defined but often loose and unmanageable. The colonial governance is supposed to act as a mediator between the complaints of the people and the corporations, but since the governor is often a heavy industrialist, these disputes rarely go in favour of the person. 

The colonial administration is technically allowed to petition the central government for support in enforcing disputes between itself and private corporations, but this rarely happens.

Historically, the self-governing colonies have been extremely hostile to native populations, showing no respect for the indigenous and violently throwing them out. 

### Cyrox
Cyrox's colonial government owns a very small portion of the land. The majority of the land belongs to private plantation owners, who usually also own their own villages which their workers live in. These villages are also supplied exclusively by the private entity, and more often than not the wages of the plantation staff are just enough to afford their housing and their feeding. 

This relationship is essentially slave-like, with the primary difference being that plantations of employees are legally allowed to leave. The caveat to this legal right is that the villages of the plantations are usually completely isolated, and travel between them is dangerous. Without any spare money, workers seeking to escape their plantation have to walk for several hours through the treacherous Dithas, sometimes even days, to reach the nearest colonially governed town. Even then, there are often no job opportunities and food is still expensive. Escape from Dithas is even less possible, as passage on naval transport costs money that these workers simply don't have.

The private corporations of Cyrox have enforced the growth of their company and their private land by forcefully evicting native inhabitants. These inhabitants are always given the option to join the plantations as a workforce, sometimes with premium wages, but as previously discussed this is a trap. Almost all natives retreat further into Dithas, or further up the Cyrox river. 

### Asthenel
Asthenel is in similar conditions to Cyrox, but many of the farms located here are owned by the colonial administration and the Central Government by proxy, and used specifically to feed Llyhai colonies. These places are often accepting to new hires who are escapees of the private farms, so its a lot easier to escape Asthenel than it is Cyrox.

As for relationships with the natives, Asthenel has a similar habit to Cyrox of using private armies to forcefully evict natives. However, in the southern part of the country, a union of Mad tribes and Okung Kinships (of Duela) has formed in attempts to slow down Asthenel's expansion, sometimes going on the offensive to reclaim some of their lost land.

## Frontier Colonies
The Frontier Colonies are the colonies of Llyhai: Isles of Mourn, Bhethfei, Fauson, Fanthel, and Phemfbharei. Given the hostile nature of the region, frontier colonies are military governed by commodores of the Colonial Fleet. They have near complete governmental control over the colony, but the economy is almost entirely managed by private corporations.

Commodores are high-ranking members of the Republic's military. They command squadrons of ships within the larger fleet, where fleets are controlled by the Admirals. The Admiral of the Colonial Fleet is responsible for colonial oversight and military cooperation in the frontier colonies, and has appointed their commodores as governors of Llyhai's five colonies. 

The frontier colonies are given this near perfectly autonomous stature because of the hostile nature of Llyhai. It is believed that an extremely high military presence is crucial for the survival of these colonies, and generally this is entirely true. frontier colonies are constantly under attack by the Armies of Ichmenre, local Ashlander tribes, and the hostile fauna of Llyhai. 

### Isles of Mourn
As part of a massive military-colonial project that began in 3290 to establish a beachfront on the Island of Mourn. Since, the colonial government has been highly focussed on safeguarding the island settlement, slowly eradicating the local fauna from the immediate area and then the island as a whole. Still, the island is dangerous, as the creatures of Llyhai are well known for being subterranean and aerial. 

The commodore has since managed to arrange a land invasion, taking full control of prospected high-quality copper and zinc resources. These mines are protected fiercely by the commodore and the admiral against frequent attacks by the armies of Ichmenre. 

### Bhethfei
Bhethfei's government isn't at all unique, and as a military-colonial project it was generally standard, with the military leading a huge landing party at the colony's foundation in 3310. Since, defensive efforts have focussed around the far-out mining operations of Hidol-sensitive materials, which the commodore and the admiral have used to fuel their military administration and defensive efforts. 

### Fauson
The fourth colony established on Llyhai as a military-colonial project, as this section breaks from the normal chronological structure to discuss the Llyhai colonies from west to east. Fauson is currently being governed by the Admiral of the Colonial Fleet, or their right-hand commodore when they are required elsewhere.

The military contribution of Fauson extends beyond the navy and includes private militaries. The resources extracted by Fauson are extremely wealthy, especially silver which is used directly as a raw resource as a currency in the Republic. This additional military contribution is incentivised by the increased hostility Fauson receives from Llyhai's dangers. 

### The Lost City of Fanthel
The Lost City of Fanthel has a large defensive wall build around the settlement for the express purpose of defending against fauna. Due to the city being positioned on an island, there is no threat from the armies of Ichmenre, so defensive efforts here are a minimum. The commodore governing this territory often leads expeditions into the silver ruin with select captains, high-ranking marine officers, and random adventurers. 
### Phemfbharei
Phemfbharei is the Republic's most recent military-colonial project. The military is currently launching naval campaigns to free the bay from dangerous creatures. The Admiral has assigned his Marine General (in command of the army directly under the admiral) to command land expansion of the colony.
## Autonomous Territories
The autonomous territories are entirely self-governing and completely disconnected from the Central Government in both governance and economy. They are, however, under the sovereign control of the Republic and frequently receive military and industrial aid from the mainland. The two autonomous territories of the Domuz Republic are Baron-Gazi and Oedreci.

### Baron-Gazi
Baron-Gazi's governance mirrors that of the main land. It has a single central governance with a president and a national assembly, except Baron-Gazi's national assembly only consists of a house of representatives without a senate. The President is also elected using a proportional system, different to the wealth system of the Republic. This legislative branch acts with full independence from the Republic, but the President of the Domuz Republic is still allowed to veto and propose laws for this territory.

As for interaction with native populaces, the Domuz lead a huge military campaign against the native Caran government, Kingdom Naxdyme, to solidify Baron-Gazi's foundation on the island of Acadym, part of the Llynllanes island archipelago. Since, many Caran have been forced to integrate into the Baron-Gazi culture (colloquially called Baron-Laleite), or they have relocated themselves to Kingdom Naxdyme territory.

There is an ongoing conflict between Baron-Gazi and Kingdom Naxdyme over ownership of the island of Texo, which is called "Blava" by the Baron-Gazi government. It is a small island situated in between the part of Llynllanes controlled by Baron-Gazi and the part controlled by Kingdom Naxdyme. Currently, the conflict is at rest, but this will likely change very soon. 

Baron-Gazi's own military is almost entirely defensive, with a strong focus on local milita, whereas Kingdom Naxdyme has a fully formed offensive navy that it has historically used to invade and influence other Raidal nations. The Domuz Republic's Admiral of the Trade Fleet regularly operates protecting trade on Barogate's west coast, and protection of Baron-Gazi is their responsibility. 

### Oedreci
Oedreci is more officially a city-state than a fully fledged autonomous territory. It's inherently attached and tied to its neighbour nation, the Wafthulham Principality. Its governance is almost entirely in the hands of its mayor, which uses governmental structures similar to those seen locally in Oranax. It is still sovereignly tied to Baron-Gazi.

Its formation and relationship with the native government began actively hostile, but throughout the landing war Domuz diplomats were able to convince the local prince (in charge of the Wafthulham Principality) to cede the town in exchange for positive relations and increased trade with Lale Darie. The Domuz's own internal reports have shown that the relationship has been beneficial for the Wafthulham, with Lale Darie having significantly more to give than it has to get from the economic relationships introduced by Oedreci. Although the trade relationship is comparatively more beneficial for the Wafthulham, it is still partially beneficial for the Domuz.

Relationships with the indigenous population have been incredibly stable. Local tradesmen have prospered with the newfound link between Oranax and Barogatian economies. Workers too have felt the weight of these benefits, as the mayor *seems like a nice guy*. I ultimately apologise for not being able to visit the colony myself, and having to rely on often biased internal reports, but I have no reason to doubt their accuracy.

# Chapter 4: Social and Cultural Aspects
In this chapter, I'm going to dive more in-depth into the social and cultural aspects of each colony, highlighting common life outside of a purely economical focus, with an emphasis on the role of native assimilated populations.
## Yphofthle
Having been the earliest established Domuz presence on Dithas, there is a population of Domuz considered native to the region. These people have cultural lineages tracing back to the 1500s, as part of a culture called Yphofth. 

In recent years, with the integration of Yphofthle into the Domuz economy, Yphofthle has received a massive migratory population from a migration wave beginning around 3328. This colonial wave has generally drowned out both native Yphofth culture and many native Mad cultures with a highly culturally mixed Domuz population. In terms of species, integrated Mad folk are part of a small minority in the population, with none inhabiting the islands that are part of the colony.

More waves of migration occurred in 3268, with the popularisation of Erigandra plantations. Further migration waves occurred in 3272 as workers moved to the colony for work in Erigandra refinement factories. Since, migration has been on the low, but since around 3380 the colony's islands have been viewed as a prime holiday home destination. Many wealthy business owners own holiday homes in the area. These people have main homes in Gazi Lale Darie, and often only live on the islands for a week or two in a year.

There is talk in the colony about full integration with the Republic as a devolved state. This movement generally comes from the small buisness owner and worker with the hopes of having a more representative government. Native Yphofth folk are making efforts to have their culture recognised as a minority Domuz culture with the hopes of any future devolved region having a Yphofth-representing senator in the National Assembly's senate.

The modern mixed cultural aspects of Yphofthle are beginning to coalesce into a defined and unique culture with aspects of certain Gazi Lale Darie cultures mixed in with native Yphofth and Mad culture. Many people proclaim this as the "real" Yphofth culture, often forgetting the definitively different native culture.
## Mepheisatha
The cultural demography of Mepheisatha is highly mixed with a clear majority mainland cultures. Due to the deals the colony makes with natives (allowing them private ownership of their settled land and integration into the colony) many of the native Mad populations have integrated into the colony and preserved their culture. 

Although native Mad settlements have historically been culturally distinct, their position as a minority in Mepheisatha has caused them to coalesce into a single cultural identity. This comes with the hopes of becoming a recognised and significant culture in the landscape. This new culture is on par in population with some smaller migratory Domuz cultures (such as Oenesite). 

Mepheisatha is beginning to gain its own cultural identity as a mix of migratory Domuz people's cultures with a distinct hint of native Mad populations. Although the colonial population is generally okay with this cultural shift, it is a clear danger to minority native cultures who are threatened with being mostly forgotten.
## Cyrox
Cyrox's social and cultural aspects revolve around the large companies that control large plantations inland. These companies, as previously discussed, have near full control over their private land including the towns their workers live in. This has caused a unique worker-lead culture that values independence and resourcefulness. Often in these areas, workers in their free time create makeshift tools from various local flora foraged often incorporating Tynoacrethi or Hipsxegler they harvest. These are all in attempts to create more self-sustainability in the worker communities, lowering their reliance on the companies. Part of this cultural separation from the companies is the evolution of secret worker dialects and the establishing of secret underground worker-based economies separate from the overlording companies.

As for other aspects of Cyrox society, such as port towns and government-controlled towns, they tend to have a similar mixed colonial culture similar to that of other colonies, where a myriad of other domuz cultures mix and maintain their own distinct facets and traditions.

Cyrox's social hierarchies are highly stratified due to the unique relationship between private companies and workers in self-governing colonies. There are the owning families who both manage and own the plantations, the slave-like workers that can also consist of integrated natives, and a class of private armies that these plantations hire for the dual purpose of policing their workers and defending from the hostile natives.

## Asthenel
Asthenel's distinctly agrarian economy has cultivated a demography of independent farmers subsidised by the government and a few private farming companies serving essentially the same purpose. Individual farming families will maintain many traditions from their place of origin, but many new cultural traditions have started to appear that are unique to the agricultural demands of the region. The best of these is the management of local fungal materials for personal tools, and a sustainable hunting practice targeting Nidxa and similar creatures for their uniquely textured flesh. 

Another cultural practice is periodic hunts against the dangerous Proohexa. These creatures have a tube-like shape with many legs protruding from the body propagating radially at seemingly random intervals, with an almost beak-like set of teeth uniquely capable of tearing fibres of all kinds. These creatures are very hostile and disruptive, so generally communal efforts are made to hunt individuals who stray too close to farming clusters. The Proohexa's bodies are uniquely adapted to moving with speed and control through trees, so these cultural hunts often involve meeting or luring these creatures in open farmland, where they are still very dangerous but significantly slower. Their meat is similar to the Nidxa but much richer and tastier. The carcasses of these creatures is turned into a feast for involving families to enjoy.

These cultural practices are generally only performed by the state-controlled farmers. The private corporations operating in Asthenel have worker cultures and stratification similar to Cyrox with the stark downside of it being easier for workers to escape the slave-like system. 

Asthenel's agricultural arms have unique ties to the military. This shows in general cultural narratives being pro-military, the Day of Mourn being a popular event in the colony, holding military personnel in high regards and farmer families often sending some members into the military.

## Isles of Mourn
The most important cultural aspect of the Isles of Mourn is the Day of Mourn. This day occurs on the same day as a slaughter of colonists and military folk during the initial landings in 3290. This day is observed by essentially all Wastes colonies, some having their own difficult landings lumped for remembrance with the Isles of Mourn landing. It is also observed by Asthenel, as previously discussed.

On the Day of Mourn, special paper lanterns constructed of local ash-related flora are made and set alight. They fly into the sky and burn entirely, re-scattering ash across the ground. It's a pretty mild event, but the more important part is the silence and remembrance. 

The Isles of Mourn, and realistically all Llyhai colonies, have a highly militaristic culture, focussed on collaborative and organised defence of any town's walls. This culture observes children and the youth joining military scouting parties who serve as an early warning system for the colonies. In the Isles of Mourn specifically, these scouting parties also hunt any creature on the island that could pose a threat to the colonies, with the hopes to eventually completely free the island of any land-based danger.

The only non-military folk inhabiting the colonies are miners and the tradespeople who supply the industry, including fishers and hunters. Being Frontier Colonies, the military oversees these activities as well, forcing pleasantries between the workers and soldiers of the colony.

## Bhethfei
Although originally an entirely military operation, like any other frontier colony, Bhethfei's farming and foraging communities have been quite attractive to some settlers. Although overall the local Ashlander tribes remain hostile, some local tribes have been supportive of Bhethfei's hold at the mouth of Llyhai's most important river. 

Culturally, this has pushed Bhethfei away from its military history and towards one more focussed on cooperation and collaboration within the settled portions. Farmer's collectives and foraging hubs are popular institutions in its few towns. The youth of this colony often aren't pushed towards joining the military, favouring group defence classes that teach methods farmers and foragers use to keep themselves safe in response to threats.

Regardless of these divergences, Bhethfei remains a strong military colony. The Day of Mourn is honoured, and the navy are seen as the rightful commanders of the territory.

## The Lost City of Fanthel
Culture in the Lost City of Fanthel revolves heavily around the silver ruins themselves. With little to no agricultural economy of its own, deliveries of provisions are large events for those who loot the ruins, where they exchange most of their fanciful trinkets for the food they need to fuel their adventurous lifestile.

This adventurous life has attracted a fairly diverse demography. While the Domuz population remains largest, this is only due to their ease of travel as a citizen of the republic. Due to the attractive lifestyle, peoples from all cultures find their way to Fanthel.

Looting in Fanthel can be split into two categories, surface looting and deep looting. Much of the immediately available structure of Fanthel has already been fully stripped of everything valuable. Deeper inside, the most valuable items have been taken and less valuable clutter left for the surface looters. The deep looters, then, delve into unexplored portions of Fanthel in search for rarer trinkets and valuable Archetechs, including supplies of Archetech Repeater cartridges. 

Fanthel's culture is stratified around these categories of looters. At the top of society is the navy, who supply the colony with food and protection. Then, the deep looters, who in exchange for risking their lives battling the functional automata receive the most valuable archetecs and items. Then are the traders who help pawn off all goods to the mainland. Following that are the surface looters who work for traders gathering mundane ancient objects. 

## Baron-Gazi
Baron-Gazi's initial conquest resulted in a Republic-controlled but Caran-inhabited land. Since, efforts have been made by the local government to attract settlers of a more _familiar_ culture. Many native Caran were unhappy with their forced cultural integrations, and left their homes to preserve their culture. Since Caran homes and infrastructure is incompatible with Domuz cultures, these buildings when left behind were demolished or left in disrepair. 

In the modern day, Baron-Gazi's demography is a mix of settler Domuz, native converted Caran, a large minority of Kaemuz, and the typical minority distributions. As Ereso's most familiar Domuz colony, it is rather unsurprising that there are large colonies of Eresonians living there, who are predominantly Kaemuz. These Eresonians settled on the island of Tindenfara and were not required to abandon their culture to live there. In fact, the local government used this movement only to push more Caran from the island. 

The colony's culture is a unique mix of the cultures of the Domuz who migrated there. Although this blend is somewhat unique, the culture of Baron-Gazi is very distinctly Domuz and maritime, with sects of Eresonians adopting a unique blend of Domuz maritime with their predominantly Sapyat and Kiyvan backgrounds.

## Fauson
Fauson diverges from the typical Frontier military culture in its inclusion of companies who independently hire their protectors, who often work alongside the navy. The colony promotes joining the military young, but rather than directing people directly into the navy, the private military sector is presented as a reasonable option. 

Low-culture is quite popular on the island, but is little more than miners gathering in their institutions for socialisation. Often, non-workers will join in with the intention of creating a comradery between the protectors and the workers of the colony.

## Oedreci
Oedreci's Domuz population is a highly respected minority. No major efforts have been taken by the Republic to change the cultural demography of Oedreci, so it is dominated in its modern population by native Ouranxans. Regardless, there are many more Domuz living in the area than there would have been otherwise.

The vast majority of traders and company heads are Domuz. This somewhat re-inforces the colony as more of a trade outpost than a settler institution. These traders, and other Domuz in the area, practice their culture separately from the locals. There is little cultural intermixing. 

The dominance of the Domuz in positions of trade power has caused the society to stratify somewhat. The Domuz population are wealthier than the native Ouranxans. They also hold most of government. There are, however, no legal definitions on stratification and generally the Domuz treat the natives normally in their trade.

## Phemfbharei
As an alarmingly young colony, Phemfpbharei has yet seen its own culture diverge from the initial settlers who continue the practices of their home. All that is to be said is that as a young colony, most focus is on aiding the navy in securing the safety of the region. The little other focus is in prospecting and securing a finantial future for the colony.

# Chapter 5: Challenges and Conflicts
In this chapter, I will detail the histories of conflict that the colonies of the Domuz have, their modern colonial resistance, and certain challenges their administration face on a daily basis. These histories are analyses of Domuz historic record alongside local understanding. 

## Yphofthle
As an old colony, there is little record of conflict in its origin. The general belief is that early Domuz settled rather peacefully on the islands and in their portions of the mainland. As mentioned in another chapter, there is some evidence that there used to be Mad communities living on the islands. Given modern Domuz histories, it seems a likely assumption that these peoples were violently expelled.

### Medieval history
In terms of military conflict, Yphofthle has a long and patchy history of internal struggles and its involvement as a Gazi territory during Lale Darie's medieval period. None of these conflicts can be tied to modern colonialism and are much more traditionally aligned with feudal histories, therefore they are out of the scope of this report. 

The first conflict that could be considered under the banner of colonialism would be the 2731 Empire of Gazi Lale Darie's conquest of the region. In the conflict, the emperor's navy showed up near the colony's castle capital. The navy was equipped with naval trebuchets which fired for only a few days before the Yphofth monarch surrendered to the emperor. 

The histories of the Empire of Gazi Lale Darie are long and strenuous topics, and Yphothle was too insignificant a territory to seriously impact that history in any way. While there are several mentions of Yphothle during this time, they are insignificant to the purpose of this document. 

During the Domuz Revolution beginning in 3189, much of Gazi Lale Darie's royalty and nobility fled to Yphothle, seeing them as untouched and remote islands in which they could hide from the violent uprising. In 3197, the Revolution was nearing its bloody end and it was clear that the nobility had lost. In this year, a boat full of Laleite revolutionaries toured the islands of Yphofthle killing any known nobles they could find. They also toured mainland Yphofthle, which at the time was small and wild. It is likely that many nobles survived, but in fear for their lives they had integrated with local peasantries and workers. The local Yphofth people were, largely, collaborative with the revolutionaries.

### Early conflicts with the Republic
The colony's proper colonial history begins here, under the control of the Domuz Republic. 

The first serious set of conflicts were local Yphofth resistance to mainland migration waves beginning in 3328. New settlers, arriving in boats, were poorly treated by the locals and their regional government, particularly in the largely inhabited islands. Often, civil hostilities would escalate into street battles and "boat throwings". In a boat throwing, locals would carry foreigners to the docks and throw them onto often crude boats, threatening them with death if they attempted to step foot back on the land. 

In 3334, the issue was presented to the National Assembly who passed motions to allow for military intervention in the local Yphofthle government. This military was generally successful in ending civil hostilities and boat throwings. After some work, local guard institutions were rebuilt with a loyalty to maintaining their monopoly over violence. These efforts were mostly successful in suppressing the hostilities, but the Iphofth people retained their hostile disposition towards foreigners.

### Modern Colonisation Conflicts
Yphofthle's period of high conflict came with the popularisation of Erigandra plantations on mainland Yphofthle in 3268. In the initial decade, the local Yphofth mainlanders were receptive to the growth of their pre-existing Erigandra industry. Later decades, they would be overwhelmed by larger companies from the Republic homeland establishing high-produce plantations. Some locals objected, but generally their local companies remained competitive. 

As Erigandra production and processing increased later that decade, settling homeland companies would develop land in frequent use by local Dimad for foraging. Initial objections were civil, however moving onwards toward 3280, hostile resistance cells formed. These cells were often focussed on destroying Erigandra crop and industrial buildings, often causing the deaths of colonial people. Due to the low scale of these attacks, responses were handled by local police and militia.

From 3310 to 3334, the native Mad launched a series of raids and skirmishes across Yphofthle territory. These skirmishes were in response to the rapid expansion of the colony's borders due to an increased demand for Erigandra and food production brought on by the establishment of the first couple frontier colonies. These skirmishes were empowered by the involvement of Mad communities from Vugaix, including Guarmad and Buemad people. Many native Yphofth people also involved themselves in the conflict. During this conflict, a large army of Mad and Yphofth peoples formed and was defeated by the Domuz navy in 3331. 

The colony continued to expand its borders until 3454 when diplomatic efforts between the collaborating Mad and Yphofth successfully had the colony sign a declaration of its borders, including the "legal" borders of Mad territory. In the 30 years since, the declaration hasn't been violated. The vast majority of native resistance has subsided, but some people continue to fight in hopes to reclaim lost territories, or in solidarity with other colonies.

## Mepheisatha
In 2641, a Darie lord lead a conquest into Mepheisatha in hopes to subjugate the Mad living there. The native Mad tribes were able to form an army and essentially slaughtered the expediting lord's soldiers. A few accompanying people were spared and made it back to the mainland, where they could share their story. One aspect of this story was the masterfully crafted iron weaponry of the defending tribes.

### Medieval History
In 2865, the Emperor of Gazi Lale Darie ordered the establishment of a stronghold on the coast of Mepheisatha. A small armada was sent to survey the coast to find the perfect dockyard position for the fort, and then a large ground invasion force was sent alongside hundreds of workers to chop down the surrounding forest and construct palisades and an initial wooden keep. During its construction, the native Mad had managed to forge an army similar to the one described by the survivors of the Darie lord's expedition, but this army was held back by the Domuz army at the expense of many lives.

Once the first castle was complete, a sect of native Mad approached the fort in hopes to establish a democratic relation, representing a coalition of the natives. This diplomatic mission was essentially a welcome greeting, suggesting to the denizens of the fort that they had foraging rights of their surrounding territory. The mission was mistaken in the priorities of the early colony, as it quickly focussed on clearing the trees back and establishing some agricultural practice. When the soil was found mostly unfit, some soils were imported to good effect.

This castle would come to be known as Fort Calanhan, and its surrounding town Calanhan, and the medieval colony shared this name as well. During this early age, the territory of Calanhan was small and didn't encroach on many native communities land. The one Mad tribe whose land was on Calanhan's territory had fully integrated into the colony, building a more familiar town with permanent buildings. They enjoyed frequent trade for their craftsmen goods, as well as good pay for knowledge of Mepheisatha.

During the revolution, much of the monarchy fled to Mepheisatha as well as Yphofthle. They managed to maintain some good control in Calanhan, as it was self-sufficient. The revolution was over and the Republic had been well formed while remnants of the monarchy still controlled Calanhan. In 3253, the Republic's navy sent a force to reclaim Calanhan from the monarchy. After a long siege, they captured the keep and the rest of the monarchy capitulated.

### Colonial Expansion
Mepheisatha's modern history of conflicts is one of quiet and legal expansion. The colony has borders defined by its estates and owned land. This list expands as enterprising individuals claim land deeper into Mepheisatha. Often, this claimed land overlaps with native Mad foraging grounds, or even the Mad settlements themselves. The Mad have two options: declare themselves part of Mepheisatha or move out. 

Unlike in other colonies, there was never a targeted war between the colony and the natives. Their land was peacefully stolen with compliance. Some native Mad, however, have caused a bit of a stir within the Mepheisatha legal system, causing it some challenges.

Modern Mepheisatha land claiming works by bid. A party claims a bit of land, creating a document outlining the claim's purpose for development including details about financial investments and returns. The colony's government reviews and sometimes verifies these claims. If the claims are approved, a citation of development is then produced and sold to the highest bidder. All Republic inhabitants are allowed to claim land.

Native Mad are allowed to become official colonial inhabitants and are allowed to keep the land of their village. Their foraging land is forfeit in this agreement, and while they may continue to forage in undeveloped land there is nothing stopping a party claiming this land. In the past, there have been several cases where Mad tribes have taken the agreement of colonial habitation, and then submitted claim documents using their well-informed knowledge of the land to outline low-profit developments that essentially keep the land as foraging grounds for their tribe. 

These proposals are rarely approved, but there have been a few cases where, with a sympathetic homeland Republic citizen's support and money, these land claims have been approved and the Mad were able to win the bid on the citation of development. These successes are the only legally recognised Mad foraging land, and stand as a final cultural bastion to the colony's otherwise hostile expansion.

The most common outright conflict between Mepheisatha citizens and Mad natives who have not integrated are hostile evictions. During these, some Mad land is claimed but the Mad refuse to move out, or attack developments. Instead of any qualified military or guard response, the Mepheisatha citizenry personally arm themselves, forming "posses". These small groups of armed citizenry use force to evict the local Mad tribes.
## Cyrox
Cyrox's history of conflicts is by far the bloodiest in all of Domuz colonial history. The Cyrox river of its namesake was highly populated by the native Dimad to the point where early societies were forming. These native towns were slowly mastering local agriculture, and given enough time may have been able to agriculturally revolutionise Dithas. 

### Early colonisation
Following the establishment of the Republic of the Domuz and a relatively unified Domuz people, culture and industry soared. Without many internal worries, the attention of the government and the wealthy elite turned outwards. Kyeron was not a target, the monumental Nvxin-Chalsie Treaty forbade any conflicts between the Domuz and the Ximuz. The Heabe was resourcefully barren, and the Aredvic tribes hold a tight control over it. Further expansion into Dithas was a naturally viable route. 

Explorers set up small outposts across the coast, including at the mouth of the river Cyrox, where they established nearby to a Dimad settlement.

Exploration of the Cyrox river revealed the unique plants the Dimad of Cyrox were cultivating. The revolutionary agricultural procedures were of little concern to the explorers, instead they focussed on the plants which had unique and useful produce. Those plants being Erigandra, Tynoacrethi, and Hipsxegler. These special fungi had been cultivated by the Cyrox natives for their own small-scale productions.

Early on in the colony's formation, the native Dimad were cooperative with the colony's few plantations. Tribal farmers were eager to teach others how they cultivated their prized plants. Once the produce reached Lale, ways of industrially processing them were discovered. For capitalists, these materials were a revolution. So they demanded more.

## Modern Resistance 
Since the demands of industry hit Cyrox, the colonial administration rapidly claimed and controlled land upstream of the Cyrox river. The local Dimad had no power to resist, capitalising colonists often came with well organised private militia. Those natives who have survived have done so simply because their land hasn't been claimed yet. Many Dimad have chosen to integrate with the colony, abandoning their fledgeling civilisation to join the Republic's systems of capital. 

In the modern day, factions of Mad frequently commit acts of hostile resistance against the new plantations. These are usually small scale, targeting a single plantation. These attacks are more similar to civil protest than any meaningful action; their damages are often easily absorbed into the gross profits of those who own plantations. Integrated Dimad have challenged the hostile action, many stating that civil reform is the best chance any mad has of survival against the colonial regime.
