The Merye Felk are an insurgency group operating primarily in [[ralean#Northlake]] and the north of [Terlaye](terlaye.md). Specifically, they operate in Calaehebadinum (Caleheb), Jonatidinum's control over the region, Maevessfellanterum (Fellan), and Maevesnillanterum (Nillan). The leader of the group is [Robin Howitha](/wastelife/characters/merye-felk/robin-howitha).

The group operate on a set of egalitarian principles.

1. No force
2. We shall only do well enough
3. Do no harm to them who plow land
4. Do no harm to the roaming people
5. Do no harm to Sebhar, Knight, nor soldier who are good people
6. The Soris and the Sardavars of Ralean, are fair game.
7. The so-called nobility are fair game
8. The dukes are fair game.

Most of these principles are fairly self-explanatory, and outline who exactly the merry folk should steal from. The first two require some explanation:

"No force" in this context seems to refer to the group's principle that they are not to be an organised force, and are to use cunning and intimidation where possible. The group also has a culture of duelling, direct conflict is encouraged but killing and unwarranted harm is not. 

"We shall only do well enough" refers to the group's idea that they are not to indulge in the riches they steal but give them away freely to those in need, while keeping only what is required to do well enough.

Meyre Felk consists of around 120 members only some of which are notable members (according to Robin Howitha, all members are equally important, but he's a commie radical)

Their modern exploits primarily consist of stealing from Nobles and Dynasties through caravan raiding and burglary. They also often thieve from caravans sent by powerful people such as the soris, local sardavars, nobles and dukes.

## History
Not much is known about the history of the group apart from that it has its roots in Ralean's feudal golden age in the 1880s. A group under the name "Merye Felk" of the time was based in Sefare Eysenre's Handle and lead operations extending far through Ralean and Terlaye. This incarnation of the group is said to have been thousands of people strong, and had many sub-groups located across the two regions. One of these subgroups were located in Northlake, and this is the one that the modern Merye Felk claim to be a continuation of. 

The original Merye Felk diminished towards the end of Ralean's golden age of feudalism, but its infamy carried on in the cultural consciousness throughout the next millennia, especially popular in the Naoxan and Sefars cultures. Throughout these millennia, several independent outlaws and small groups claimed to be connected to the Merye Felk despite many of these claimers performing completely different types of actions to the original. 

The origins of the current group only trace back to 2978, where a group of outlaws established themselves in Northlake and took on the name "Meyre Felk". The group really kicked off when the Reimig dynasty started exerting its control over the land. Many disgruntled landowners were cast out from their previously soundly held territory under the Warlord of Jonati as dynastic rule took over the city and its surrounding regions. Alongside this, many former soldiers of the old regime joined outlaw groups. The Merye Felk were one of these, however the group quickly exceeded its original scope.

During this time, the group went through conflicting clashes of interest. Many former soldiers had joined the group hoping to re-live the legend of Merye Felk. At the time, however, the actions of the Merye Felk were similar to regular banditry. It is said that a charismatic person from among the hopefuls began leading a movement to bring the Merye Felk closer to the stories of legend. This caused a civil conflict within the group. One side of this conflict was the precursors to the Merye Felk, who later in the 30th century dedicated themselves to rooting out warlord bullies from the countryside, and eliminating bandit factions that targeted the poor.

The original goal of the 30th century Merye Felk carried on into the 32nd century until about 3121 where an Empire clampdown on the area eliminated banditry and the group's actions became obsolete. The group seemingly diminished again, entering a sort of dormant state over the centuries where the group's actions had little cause. From 3122 until about 3390 the group had no base of operations and acted more of a heritage club in the area in which it used to operate. 

In 3390, large-scale covert operations were launched into Terlaye in preparation for the Empire of Ralean's large-scale invasion. Although the scouting operations were largely hidden, they were based in places along the border which were generally sparsely populated, which is exactly where the now heritage-group Merye Felk were keeping track of. The resurgence of Merye Felk began around this time, with it slowly regaining members throughout the primary scouting operations, with their new prerogative being keeping track of scout operations and making sure they don't impact the common folk too harshly. 

The group received massive popularity and support on the outbreak of the war, particularly from Terlayans joining it directly. This is generally how it came to have its modern numbers, although there were more members during the war for Terlaye. The group expanded its commoner-protecting activities into northern Terlaye and gathered the reach it has today.

Even after the end of the war, in 3412, the group continued going strong desperately attempting to alleviate the affects of the harsh Ruion government on the Terlayan peasantry. Momentum generally waned deeper into the 35th century until Robin Howitha picked up the mantle of managing the group in 3478 and began transforming its activities into the egalitarian banditry that it is today. 

A turn towards egalitarian banditry isn't unwarranted under the Merye Felk name. The original group tended towards common anti-establishment actions before the modern Merye Felk group generally started protecting and supporting the little guys in direct opposition of the powerful. Outright "stealing from the rich and giving to the poor" was never a core aspect of the Merye Felk but instead a thing that happened as a means to the end of supporting the locals.
