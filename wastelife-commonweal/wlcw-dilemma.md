In Wastelife Commonweal, a dilemma is a conflict centred on uncertainty, incomplete information, or a problem that cannot be resolved without understanding the situation. Dilemmas range from elaborate puzzles and mechanical obstacles to practical problems such as a cart stuck in the road or an unstable structure.

# Sway

Insight measures how much the participants understand the situation.
Pressure measures how close the situation is to resolving, either through player action or external forces.

As Insight increases, players gain clearer understanding, better options, and greater control over the outcome. As Pressure increases, time runs out, risks rise, and the situation becomes harder to influence.

# Starting a Dilemma

When a dilemma begins, the GM describes what is clearly and immediately observable and what elements are uncertain or unexplained.

This establishes a shared starting point for the players and defines what is currently known versus unknown.

## Banked Insight

During rests, players can bank insight by preemptively researching the dilemma. Players must have recognised the dilemma they would face for the banked insight to apply.

# Actions

During a dilemma, players may take the following base actions:

- Observe – watch, listen, or notice details
- Examine – closely inspect a specific element
- Interact – manipulate, test, or adjust something
- Apply Force – push, break, lift, restrain, or block
- Wait – allow time to pass and see what happens

Each action may increase Insight, Pressure, or both. The amount gained depends on how relevant and effective the action is within the context of the dilemma.

Advancements grant additional actions that allow characters to interact with the situation in more meaningful or specialized ways. These actions often provide more insight or allow it to be gained with less risk. For example, lockpicking allows a character to interact with a lock in ways unavailable through brute force alone.

## Action rolls

Many dilemma actions require a roll. When an action calls for a roll, the player rolls a d20 + skill proficiency. The result represents how effectively the action is carried out.

- A result of 10 represents an average or expected success.
- Results of 18–20 represent exceptionally effective actions.
- Lower results indicate limited success, complications, or misinterpretation.

Whether a roll increases Insight, and by how much, depends on the difficulty of the task. The GM determines a threshold based on the situation. For example:

- A simple mechanical lock might require a result of 5
- A complex or unfamiliar mechanism might require a result of 15

The roll does not determine whether the dilemma is solved. Instead, it helps the GM decide what information is revealed and how Insight advances.

# Designed and Improvised Dilemmas

Some dilemmas are designed scenarios. In these cases:

- Useful actions and relevant skills are predefined
- Difficulty thresholds for rolls are established in advance
- Pressure increases according to specific triggers or timelines

Designed dilemmas provide a more structured experience and are suited for high-stakes or central challenges.

Most dilemmas, however, are improvised. In these situations:

- The GM determines which actions are useful
- The GM decides how much Insight an action provides
- The GM controls how Pressure increases

In improvised dilemmas, the d20 roll serves as a guide for the GM to judge the quality of the action and determine what information to reveal and how the situation evolves.


# Resolution

A dilemma ends when pressure reaches a point where the situation resolves. The archon depends on how much insight the participants have gained. a high end sight will lead to a controlled and informed resolution. Low insides could lead to a messy, costly solution or Unintended outcomes.
