Wizard culture generally refers to the culture of any hidosis user, primarily wizards, mages, and gemcasters. 

The vast majority of hidosis usage is practical and integrated into society in normal ways. Certain areas with a greater hidol density have more magic in their society. Specific wizard culture is more of a scholarly clique of fascinated wizards.

The origins of hidosis and magic study vary by continent. On Barogate and Anula, wizard culture originates in ancient Nao with the Nao institute. The original wizard cultures are lost to time, but modern wizard cultures are somewhat traceable to their roots. 

The prevailing aspects of all Wizard cultures is that they are generally extremely powerful, or apprentices. Self-taught wizards will often convert to a wizard culture once they involve with their peers. 

Wizards are also usually quite wealthy for a whole host of reasons. Their services, of course, are highly sought after. Otherwise they are wealthy through their parents or are up-kept by some society. 

## Tower Culture

Tower culture is very isolationist wizard. They often build their towers in remote locations far from any towns, perhaps with a few ground buildings as a compound or estate. To have a tower. Towers of tower culture must have at least five floors. There is a certain prestige with having more floors, but 7-10 floors is considered the "sweet spot". Having more floors is taboo and seen as excessive, but certain sub-cultures (known as skyscraper wizards) aspire to build towers as tall as they can possibly be. Skyscraper wizards can be a bit competitive, so their towers generally try their best to be resistant to sabotage. 

The isolationism is not only geographical but societal. Tower wizards are known to have a dead drop system of goods delivery. They will often have spots for townsmen to bring them supplies, and leave money out for payment. More often than not, tower wizards will have a domestic worker or two to carry out errands that involve any amount of socialising. Being a tower wizard's servant usually pays exceptionally well and offers great benefits including the wizard's favour and lots of time off. 

A meeting with a tower wizard is a special communion, even with other tower wizards. Tower wizards most often speak with their domestic worker/s, at most maybe five discrete times a day.

## Hat Culture

Hat Culture are rather social wizards. They pool in friend groups consisting of similarly capable wizards. These friend groups are typically 2-8 people. Larger convocations are possible. 

Although many wizards wear hats, hat culture wizards notably wear large and pointy hats, often paired with robes. The colourful expression of these hats means a lot to these wizards. Sometimes, friend groups will have a uniform. Boring gray, brown, or other drab robes are taboo, notably with the exception of some older wizards who are allowed to wear these clothes because they too are drab. 

Hat wizards are often very mobile. Although they often own houses, they are more often than not disproportionately squalid for their wealth. Hat wizards like to _roam_. They just walk around, eat at restaurants or other hospices. Or they will forage in the field. If they find a way to, they will almost always fly. 

If a hat wizard asks you if you want to go on a walk, don't accept. Hat wizard "walks" are week long treks across the entire country. If you leave mid-walk, you will be shunned.

In line with their social activity, hat culture wizards will often form "societies". These advertise themselves as authoritative tribunals. Most of their activities are social parties and competition. Notable in Shildo, there is the Hat Society of Redel which brings in wizards from as far as Reumanas. 

## Vault Wizards

Vault culture is obsessed with collecting artefacts. The most recognisable aspect of a vault wizard is their vault itself. It is considered disingenuous, cowardly, and an affront to decency to hide the location of one's vault. The prestige is in the protections of the vault. 

Vault wizards are obsessed with trapping and securing their vault. They've mastered magical locks, proximity enchantments. Magic isn't required for one's vault either: mechanical traps are common. Wizard vaults usually have a dozen or so layers of protection. Vault wizards are simialrly obsessed with breaking the vaults of their peers. It is custom to only take one item from a fellow wizard's vault. Two if you're feeling cheeky. 

Wizard vaults are barely static. Wizards will often take items out and lease their use to whatever locals can afford them. Theft of leased items is rare because the wizards are often deeply integrated into the community and will hunt down thieves. If a thief takes it directly from the vault, however, that's a fair cop and the wizard lets them keep it. They may try to steal it back. These wizards are also known to sell or auction their items. Vault wizards bring lots of wealth into communities. 

Although highly focussed on their vaults, these wizards are very adventurous often spending more than half of their time on the quest for more artefacts. While they often do these alone, they also commonly recruit some mercenaries or have a rag tag group of friends. Vault wizards also often travel with other wizards, although when with other vault wizards they may struggle to fairly divide the rewards.

## Scholarly Wizards

The scholar community of powerful wizards is a fairly lame community, but still worth noting. While all wizards generally push the boundaries of hidosis, scholarly wizards do it with the logistical pretence of science. 

Scholarly wizards are always associated with some academic institution usually relevant to hidology. As powerful wizards, it's usually a higher up position, but often they can be fringe and somewhat wild affiliates that the university doesn't want to hire for some reason, perhaps because they are too much compared to regular academics. 

They are often dissapointed that other powerful wizards keep their findings to themselves, or wait too long to publish any of their breakthroughs. Scholarly wizards are known to harass other wizards to learn their expertise, for the greater good of public knowledge.
