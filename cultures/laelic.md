Laelic is a cultural family present across [Gazi Lale Darie](gazi-lale-darie.md). It's origins are primarily prehistoric Fellan, however the language comes from an [Allyiscereen](/wastelife/creatures/misc/allyiscereen) that was present in Lale during the first millennia of the culture's linguistic development. 

Laelic culture is split into four sub-families themselves split even further. The larger families follow:

- [[Darieite]]
- [[West Laleite]]
- [[Laleite]]
- [[Gazite]]
- [[Yphofthite]]
- [[Dithas Colonial]]
- [[Llyhai Colonial]]

The colonial groups are incredibly recent developments, having many aspects of each homeland culture as well as certain influences from native [Shaiou](shaiou.md) cultures. Yphofthite is not to be confused with Colonial Yphofthite, the former was established before colonial efforts and the latter has the colonial similarities. 


# Cultures

![Gazi Lale Darie Cultural.jpg](/wastelife/region-maps/gazi-lale-darie/gazi-lale-darie-cultural.jpg)

 The country is primarily divided into its three sections: Gazi, Lale, and Darie. Gazi is a small collection of northmost islands, Lale is a collection of larger islands, including a few smaller ones. This is where most of the country’s population, industry, and diversity comes from, so often it is split between Primary and West lales. Even after this split, Primary Lale still consists 40% of the country’s population. Darie is the part of the country on the mainland Barogate.
 
There is also the cultural division. Gazaite has one uniform culture, Gazite. The Laleite culture is split between 8 sub-cultures. By order of population, they are Lale Iophelite, Lale-Deornite, Oenesite, Gahrite, Lephiolite, Dalmawite, Spaerite, and Seosite. The Darieite culture has three subcultures. In the same method of ordering, they are Darieddite, Ephuesanite, and Feixienite.

Gazi, Lale, and Darie cultures are very unique when it comes to regional cultures. Because of their Domuz majority and general regional ties to the people of that region, cultures here tend to put a lot of emphasis on the water and spending extended periods of time under it. This poses a problem to many people who do not have the ability to be underwater for that long without the use of enchanted gear, who may find difficulty fully integrating into these cultures. To offset this, most doctors in Gazi Lale Darie know enchantments that can help people be underwater for extended periods of time. These same enchantments are useful to Domuz, since they only extend the time they can spend underwater

## Gazaite Wetsuits

Gazaite wetsuits are an important part of many cultures of these regions. Although originating from Gazi, they are much more often produced in Lale. Underwater-based cultures like Lephiolite, Dalmawite, and Gazaite, have their own traditions around decorating and wearing these garments. Some surfaced-based cultures have wearing these garments under other pieces of clothing a large part of culture.

The traditional Gazaite wetsuit is made using cured aquatic leather from dolphins, whales, etc. These materials are specially cured and treated to become form-fitting, insulative, and aesthetic. Cultures that heavily use these make rituals of maintaining their wetsuit. Usually, only one is owned by a person, but more may be owned by the wealthier for different occasions and styles. 

### Colonial Wetsuits

The colonial wetsuit describes a new type of wetsuit using raw materials harvested in Cyrox from the Hipsxegler fungus. Hipsxegler is used to produce a versatile rubber. Although industrially intensive, cured and shaped Hipsxegler fabric is incredibly versatile and used for all manner of things. It is also the main fabric used in the colonial wetsuit. The other two main fungal plants harvested in [Dithas](/wastelife/regions/barogate-wastes/dithas) (Erigandra, Tynoacrethi) are sometimes also worked into the fabric increasing structural integrity and insulative properties. 

Industrially made colonial wetsuits are often slightly more expensive than traditionally made gazaite wetsuits and significantly more expensive than the streamlined production of industrially made gazaite wetsuits. This is because of the rarity of Hipsxegler production. The price of Hipsxegler is rapidly decreasing as the Cyrox colony increases its production, so soon colonial wetsuits may be cheaper than traditionally made gazaite wetsuits. 

Colonial wetsuits are easier to dye than leather wetsuits, so they generally have brighter and more vibrant colours. In addition to this, the flexibility of the material makes adding pockets and customisations easier.
