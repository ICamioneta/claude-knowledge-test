In Wastelife, species is completely separate from culture. Group identities are solely based on cultural lines, and never species. 

Species populations is spells defined by non-cultural migration and suitability. 

# Early demography

Before society and culture, after the Gods had finished making some people to live on them, Barogate and Anula's demography generally consisted of hunter gathered societies and other basic tribal structures. 

The Muz, originally placed in a highly forested Barogate, spread across all Barogate and Anula. They originally found no land particularly suited, but did not live well in the desert. Their subspecies would later naturally diverge to their habitats.

- Kaemuz would adapt to the monsoon forest of Ereso. 
- Danmuz would adapt to the rivers of Terlaye
- Kaemuz would adapt to the fertility of Senolado
- Ximuz would adapt to the rainforest of Kyenel
- Domuz would adapt to the Archipelago of Lale, Darie, and Gazi.
- Difmuz would adapt to the mushroom wetland of Dithas.


The Nebdos were originally placed in the forests of Neble near the coast around modern day Xynt. They would spread generally, but not much past Neble's geographical boundaries.

The Nidos were placed in modern day Karagen. They would spread across Vezoen's natural boundaries and through into Roughwalk. 

The Nudos were placed in modern day Merida, and would also spread naturally across Thagryene. 

The Cut were placed in Cadole, and found the region well suitable.

The Ens were already present in Sefare Eysenre, Gordolmonre, and the Heabe.
- The Colsens of Sefare Eysenre
- The Reyens of Gordolmonre 
- The Nadens and Popens of the Heabe

The Fall people were already present across Barogate.
- The Rutefall pretty much everywhere, including the desert).
- The Tudofall alsp pretty much every, excepting the desert, the Heabe, and the mountains.
- The Matafall who were concentrated in Duela, Dithas, and Kyeron.
- The Laura fall who were common in Kyeron.

The proto-mad were generally present in Dithas. The Phax were common across Kusao and Cronar although significantly less populous in the latter. The Ysgews were present in every forest but Kyeron, and soon birthed the Codews recognising the potential of the future. Muz-based and Dos-based Codews were originally very different, but the design converged.

# Birth of Language
Around the -5000s, the Era of Civilisation was in full swing. Societies had been established everywhere, primarily around significant rivers. Most major towns date back to original foundations layed around this time.

The Muz had fully diverged and out-populated native Fall. 

The birth of the Vugaix and the Cynnag-diverged species of Mad flourished in the new mushroom forest environment. 

The Lords of Ich Mena Re would establish themselves and the volcanic communities would begin in Ichmenre. 

Shortly after this date, major deforestation in Gazi Lale Darie, Senolado, and Ereso would occur. The connection between the Ysgews and the power of the forest was not well understood, and many Ysgews simply moved out of discomfort or local challenges.

The Difmuz would also begin spreading across much of the wastes, supported by local existing peoples. This birthed the many recognisable Difmuz cultural groups which remain barely homogeneous. 

- Sand campers found a nomadic life in Kyenel following and feeding from Pydred's fallen giants. 
- Ash Landers would rely on a hybrid of a deep fishing trade and learn how to feed from the unique Llyhai fauna.
- Mudscrapers, not yet known for their iconic mud huts, would join the Matafall in Duela.
- Madmuz would join the new Mad species in Vugaix.
- Steppe children, not yet Aredvic, would learn to live with the Nadens in the Heabe.
- Mountaineers would join the Reyens in Gordolmonre. 

These are the "God Roots". Language super-parents that share nothing in similarity, not even species and therefore means of pronunciation. 

### Shaiou
Originated from the Mad in Dithas, it was eventually adopted by the Mad and Madmuz in the Vugaix. 

### Laurien
Originated from the Laurafall living in Kyeron. Later it was heavily modified by the Ximuz species entering established Laurafall circles. Laurien developed independently from Fallet as Laurafall more or less evolved in Kyeron.

### Fallet
Fallet was originally spoken in Duela circles and was carried by the Rutefall across Barogate. Non-Duela Fallet variants are often wild from one another, most are lost with some lexical content being absorbed into local languages. 

### Garas
Originated from The Heabe and it was a language family that spread across all of Ens population. The family was also spread across Gordolmonre and northern Shildo. It diverged natively in The Heabe and Gordolmonre, and its presence in northern Shildo was evolved by the Muz integrating into Colsens circles. 

### Shildoan
Although they have completely different lexical roots, Llofil and Fygh's gramatical root in ancient Ysgews non-verbal language ties them together. Together, they form the Shildoan language root, which is generally wholly Muz-based (of course with some Rutefall Fallet lexical inclusions). 

### Fellan
Often also called "Proto Terlayan", Fellan came from Bracai lexical and grammatical roots. It was primarily common in northern Shildo around the Terlayan rivers and would spread south.

### Laelic
The Domuz living in Lale may have picked up on some Rutefall languages pre-present, however the actual roots of modern Domuz languages come from an Allyiscereen that lived in the shallow waters at the time.

### Phax Non-Verbal
This list generally covers verbal languages, but Phax Non-Verbal is not to be looked over. Many Phax would learn the Fallet of the local Rutefall to communicate with them when relevant, but Phax society revolved entirely around their own non-verbal pheromonal construction. 

There are notable slight differences in the divergence of Pre-Alluvial Non-Verbal. Alluvial non-verbal is a short lived language.

# Identity in Civilisation
By -2000, genuine organisational civilisations had emerged and each birthed their own unique sense of identity. This identity was oft in language and language families generally diverged into them. 

Where a more formal civilisation didn't arise, there was still a more general evolution of society and demography.

Here are the divergences, by origin.

## Shaiou
In Dithas, civilisation generally tended around the rivers as geographical anchors and consistent freshwater sources. These were:
- Mepheisatha
- Yphofth. "Yphofth" is a modern term used more specifically for Yphofth bay and the Yphofth islands, but I don't want to think of a name for the river now so I'm going to use this. 
- Cyrox
- Inner Dithas. Again, I'm lacking a proper name, but there is a freshwater river here that drains into Duela. It was historically closer to the cultures of Duela.
- Asthenel. This too is a modern name for the region, but it developed its own regional culture and language departed from Cyrox. 

In the Vugaix, Shaiou was still uncategorisable between the few clearings that began to establish as towns. Other than these clearings, there were no cultural cornerstones like the rivers of Dithas.

## Laurien
The cultures formed of Ximuz and Laurafall had some distinct language families birthed at this time. The most notable would be Kyeric, the language spoken primarily in central Kyeron and largely untouched by outside influences. Kyeric shared more similarities in pronunciation with the Laurafall word roots rather than the Muz phonetic-isms. 

The Cavills of Llesndijti (modern name) and Kiav produced their own variants with more Muz-focused pronunciations. In the south, Abdigszi formed with heavy linguistic infiltration from Llofil, forming a sort of Laurien-Shildoan hybrid (which was gramatically Laurien).

## Fallet
Fallet had a large step forwards in cultural rigour as the sandcamper lifestyle moved from a fad of a few families to an established disorganised practice. It's language was a heavily inspired Fallet, generally called Sandcamper Fallet. 

Duela Fallet is also noteworthy for diverging more generally between the Fallet spoken amongst the mangrove swamp tribes, those Tudofall establishing in Edlyue, and the more Difmuz-centered communities establishing in Barthe. The primary differences between these was phoneme formation, inspired by the speech of the most common species. 

## Garas
Garas remained fairly similar in its organisation. No major civilisational changes happened in the places that spoke Garas. This was, of course, except from the Garas present in northern Shildo. It generally split into Sefars Garas (that spoken in Sefare Eysenre) and Terlayan Garas (that spoken in northern Terlaye). 

## Shildoan
Llofil Shildoan would spread to Ereso and then Kiyvan. Fygh Shildoan would spread to the Niez and later to Ithun. 

Llofil Shildoan would also highly influence Abdigszi, but it is a Laurien language through and through. 

Fygh Shildoan would influence the Muz lexemes of Sefars Garas. 

A unique phenomenon would happen once these two languages "met" at the mouth of Terlaye's rivers. In collaboration with Terlayan, these languages would form a sort of "True Shildoan" - a unique construction of languages borrowing elements from Shildoan, Fellan, and even in some way Laelic language. The Laelic element comes from an Allyiscereen that lived in the bay during a culturally significant time period. Allysicereen live for around 1600 years, and this one was in Shildo from around -1900 to -200. 

## Fellan
Fellan would remain surprisingly homogenous, or not very ambitious in its divergences. It's locality was primarily central Terlaye.

## Laelic
Laelic would generally split between Gazi, Lale, and Darite Domuz languages. Lale is the "closest" to the root of the family, but they are all truly derivational. 

## Phax Verbal - Krykis
As non-Phax species began living in Cronar and Kusao, Phax Verbal variants arose. These are called Krykis more generally. They are very syntactically challenging.

At this time, there was simply a difference between Kusao and Cronar Krykis languages. Within these there are a few dialects.

# Ancient Times
This is generally when vague language categories became more strict language families. Between 0 and 1000, civilisation went from loose collections and collaboratives to massive decentralised empires united under some name and often a legend. In Barogate, huge demographic shifts like the unity of Shildo, the Gan migration.

## Shaiou
Shaiou remained relatively unchanged in Mad circles, however with the birth of the Aredvic (chariot-riding Heabe nomads), a new variant of Muz-focused Shaiou would popularise itself in the Heabe. Both Aredvic language and Heabe Garas would co-exist given the two separated lifestyles of the Aredvic and the Heabe nomads. 

Alongside this, the incredibly isolated communities in Vugaix would diverge their language into the unrecognisable "Vuga". Vuga would become most popular in the Mad-centric communities, relying heavily on Mad-style phonemes. Dithas communities were well mixed.

There were also large influxes of Difmuz into the Duela at the time. While many would adopt Duela Fallet, elites and the powerful would insist on Shaiou leading to major attacks on Fallet languages.

## Laurien
Things in Kyeron would remain relatively unchanged, but Ximuz populations would explode and overshadow Laurafall, leading to Muz-centric Laurien variants dominating in culture.

## Fallet
Fallet languages would see a large demographic collapse during this time. Outside of Duela, any value it held would be absorbed into local non-Fallet languages. With Difmuz migration into Duela, Fallet would come under attack in some circumstances. 

## Garas
Garas' main demographic change during the ancient era was the diminishing popularity of Sefare and Terlayan Garas. These declines are discussed elsewhere, but Garas would remain strong in Gordolmonre and The Heabe. Many trade goods brought by the fledgeling Aredvics would introduce many new words into Heabe Garas. 

## Shildoan
Shildoan would see massive changes. The unity of Shildo under Techia would cause a unique shared identity. The "Shildoan" shared heritage believed at the time was a mix of Fygh, Llofil, and Fellan-originating languages. Each of these origins had since syntactically wildly diverged, although Fygh and Llofil would still share syntactic similarities when compared to Fellan. 

The Language of Shildo, that which was spoken in Naternegorna (before Nater's legend) would be a prime example of the purest "Shildoan" mix. It shares language roots with all Fellan, Fygh, and Llofil. It would be the language of Techia, but Techia as a decentralised empire wouldn't really exert much demographic importance. These three roots would also split Shildo into its three sub-regions: Ereso for Llofil, Ralean for Fygh, and Terlaye for Fellan. 

Shildoan unity would also include Garas variants up north, but this was not a recognised form of Shildoan identity. For that reason, Garas generally fell out of popularity in these regions and the Colsens adopted either Fellan-rooted languages or Fygh-rooted languages. Their new variants would reflect the old Garas in some way.

## Fellan
The emergence of Nater's legend was foundational in Terlayan culture. The legend was only ever told in Fellan, spreading the language across all of Terlaye. In Naternegorna, the city at the end of Nater's journey, Shildoan was widely spoken, but the legend put its influence into the city's language.

Krykis would remain fairly unchanged, but the Gan migration to Cronar would make Alluvial Krykis extinct. 

## Ataken
With the Gan migration into Cronar, Alluvial Krykis would fade into the background as the dominant Ataken languages of the migratory people became the centre of culture. Alluvial Krykis gave its desert-related words to the Ataken, who hailed from a rainforest and were unfamiliar with the local climate.

Ataken itself is a fork of an Annwvyan language that itself was independently developed by the Ce'gan. In Annwvyn, Ce'gan is the only language root. It itself has visible Allyiscereen and Ews influences. 

## Nyrmish
The rise of the Obsidian Road, a trade dominated by sandcampers moving between Ichmenre and Ralean, would altogether remove Fellan sandcamper variants in favour of a new language developed from the volcanic communities. 

# Medieval History
Medieval history would generally see the origins of the modern cultures and the solidification of actual languages over vague language categories.
