## Social
Tedyr is an incredibly social Eternal. They maintain a good relationship with every other Eternal as well as a deeply interconnected web of mortal contacts. Tedyr's main activities are acting in, facilitating, and hosting theatre in both the Mortal Realm, their own realm, and the Eternal City. Tedyr's own social tendencies are mixed up in their acting; they don't have their own personality as much as their own personality is always acting altogether.

# The Cannon Commedia of Haerul

As they came into their domain, Tedyr began to recognise a certain set of cannon comedic archetypes. These infectious roles were the most identifiable part of the Haerulian theatre that had spread across the mortal world. 

## **Nahr**
The Nahr are traditionally the servant-class in Haerul. They were the retinue, courtiers, and actual servants of the Almalic. 

### Daki
Daki, sometimes Daith or Dake, is usually a retainer of an Almaic shown to be overly competent for their role. They are often portrayed of being capable of overthrowing the Almaic or amassing great power, but won't due to some circumstance usually their humble nature. They are also often very willing to show their competence, which makes them somewhat insufferable to the other characters. 

### Teeb
Teeb is often a labourer or a boat person. They are good at what they do and are respectable for it, but have a cruel and pessimistic outlook on life. They are often radical and violent during the events of any show, but we are often made to empathise with them. 

### Muharie
Muharie is often a lowly errand runner, a messenger, or a junior servant. They are shown as acrobatic, impulsive, and often starved. Muharie is the subject of much physical comedy. They also find themselves comedically interjected into the main conflict of the theatre. 


## **Almaic**
The Almaic were the land-owners of Haerulian society. They came of all ages and genders, but were generally conservative. Conservatism represented the maintenance of a social structure that justified their possession. 

### Mesir 
Mesir is often seen as illegitimate but permissible. Traditionally, they make most of their wealth by tolling important roads and bridges, while neglecting their actual estates. They often have incompetent Jari, but traders accept the tolls because it would be too much a bother to challenge them. Mesir is seen as being too caught up in the income of their tolling. Mesir is often also posed as a modernist, well read on how society is developing towards wealth and the guild, but this archetype usually only shows up in more developed experiences, and only later.

### Polithe
Polithe is a respected elder and usually a straight man trope. However, they are set in their ways and stop at nothing to collect taxes. They are seen as generally righteous due to their outward kindness and respectability. They are traditionally paried with the brutal Jari, the trope being that the Jari is manipulating the Polithe while in reality the Polithe keeps a fear over the Jari. 

### Alane
Alane is an overly proud Almaic who defines themselves by their lineage while being personally unremarkable. They rely on invoking their ancestors over having anything real to show for themselves. Moreso than Mesir and Polithe, Alane is often the villain of the story because they are unrightfully powerful.

## **Jari**
In Haerulian society, the Jari were a class of captains employed by the Almaic to maintain order. Fortunately for Haerulians, their society was very peaceful and generally unserious. When things did get serious, only bold Jari would survive, which lead to almost every Jari embodying this bold character to appeal to the Almaic. 

### Fahkir
Fahkir is the archetypal braggart captain. Sometimes a mercenary leader, other times a guard commander, Fahkir is always loudly courageous and notoriously flaky. Any conflict that occurs, Fahkir will be there initially to try to intimidate people to stop, before making excuses for their absence before any blood is actually spilt. Fahkir is usually employed by Polithe.

### Reiun
Reiun is the archetypal zealous enforcer. Often lacking any real combat skills, they are inspired by real captains but fail to do any actual reading. They are quick to jump to arrests and use an unrefined brute force when opposed. Reiun is often employed by Mesir.

### Hanak
The quiet veteran, Hanak has usually won a few wars in their past but barely speaks of it. When conflict breaks out, their mere tense presence is usually enough to stop things in their tracks. Hanak is also usually combat avoidant, and is reluctant to actually pick up a sword because they know death more than any other. Often employed by Alane. 

## **Jisur**
The Jisur were a stereotype of people who were family to Almaic who engaged in social drama, which often escalated to violence. The Jisur are both feuding family members and lovers. The lovers became more pervasive in the Haerulian commedia due to their appeal. 

### Esuha and Linsa
The two lovers Esuha and Linsa are traditionally from two different families. These families are sometimes friendly, might have small conflicts, or are outright feuding. Esuha and Linsa are often overbearingly public and dramatic with their relationship, leaning into the smallest of miscommunications as if they were the end of the world. 

### Danial and Mayt
These two characters are usually fierce enemies. Often it is a friendly rivalry, but many depictions show them to want to kill each other. There is always some comedy in how Mayt is always the one to die, in many shows Mayt will be revived by an Eternal or a wizard only to be killed again. They are often equals, but when there is dominance it is always Mayt is better than Danial in a fair game, but usually looses to luck and circumstance (often dying as a consequence of that same luck and circumstance). Daniel, naturally, always insists that his victories are on his own merit.

## Other narrative roles
Certain personifications of any character comes with overlapping narrative roles. Sometimes, one of Danial or Mayt are betrothed to Esuha or Linsa in a reluctant political bond. Often, a character sometimes called Jisur will be caught between two sides of the conflict while also fulfilling another comedic role. A Jisur or a Nahr may play the role of a captain while being wholly unprepared for it.
