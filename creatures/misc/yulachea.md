The Yulachea is a sister-creature to the [Yulaesin](/wastelife/creatures/misc/yulaesin), both being almost identical other than the Yulaesin having additional limbs protruding from their back.

Yulachea, or Yulachi, are large chitinous creatures with 4 legs. Their bodies are flat and crab-like, with their thick and bulky legs extending out in each direction bludgeoning the ground. On the front of their body sits a large head with a shark-like jaw.

Yulaechea are incapable of surviving upside down. This is because their circulatory and digestive system can't handle the orientation. Even if they could survive this orientation, they have no way to flip themselves.

There is a particular breed of Yulachea present in [Acvidaras](/wastelife/regions/barogate-wastes/acvidaras) with thinner green shells.

# Origin

Their names have no origin, they were just the words given to creatures of ancient Gazi legend. That’s right! This legend extends back before the Kithral migration. According to the myth, the Yulachea were the evil crab inhabitants of a certain island. They were sentient crab overlords to all of the crabs on that island, and it was a very crabby island. The Yulachea used their smarts to grow extra limbs and become the Yulaesin so that they could conquer Gazi, but they never did because a hero named Kite stopped them.

After the Great migration, Kite’s Tale fell out of favour when the land was nothing like before. A few families did continue to tell the story, such as around the islands of Rhoso. More notably, the Kithral Ddo found lands almost exactly like their old islands in Gazi, so the legend could still persist. One of the islands were actually called Yula in the Legend’s respect. It was a crabby island.

When Daklyn went to Ddo he was told this legend, and he turned the creatures from it into beasts in Wastelife. Granted, now in Wastelife they are an animal species, but who knows, maybe earlier in Wastelife the Yulachea threatened Barogate, and it was the hero Kite who defeated them and made them like animals.
