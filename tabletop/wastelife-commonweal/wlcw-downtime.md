Daring combats, inquisitive discourses, and puzzling dilemmas only make up a small proportion of an adventure. The majority of time is spent walking between towns and resting within them. Choosing what to do in this downtime is pivotal to the development of your character. 

There are two types of downtime in Wastelife Commonweal:

- Travel time, counted in days.
- Rest time, counted in half-weeks (3 days).

A downtime block is a continuous period of either type. The GM declares the block's type and length, then players declare their actions. Players can declare actions that aren't here, if they wouldn't require some advancement.

## Provisions

Each day, the party consumes water, food, and fuel. When in a town or a civilised area, these can be found easily at inns and can be paid for with the local currency. When adventuring into the Wastes, they matter a lot more.

Water and food units is consumed one per day, per person. One unit of fuel is consumed by the camp per day. The unit of water is the bottle (3 litres, 3 kg), the unit of food is the ration, and the unit of fuel is the coal.

In desert regions, water consumption doubles.

## Resolution rolls

Downtime actions often need a resolution roll. This is a d20 + skill proficiency.

1-7 causes a setback. This is a fail or a partial completion, as the GM wills.

8-14 is an action done. The action happens simply as described.

15-19 is an action done well. It completes as described, plus a small bonus at the GM's will.

20+ is an exceptional completion. It completes as described plus a lasting or significant benefit.

## Practice and advancement

Advancement's soft requirements are marks of practice, often done in downtime. There are three types of marks: trial, practice, and study. Trial marks are earned outside of down time.

Practice can happen in either downtime type, but study requires rest time as the stress of moving is unfitting to study.

## Enervation

Enervation tracks how travelling tires your character day-by-day. Each point of enervation reduces your maximum save points and composure by 1, and incurs a penalty of 1 to dilemma rolls. At 5 enervation. you cannot earn training marks. 

Enervation is caused by exerting travels and bad rests. It is restored by good rests.

## Sleeping

If your party is in a populated area, you might not need to make camp and can instead rent a room. 

Rough sleeping with a camp recovers 1 vitality every consecutive 2 weeks. On a bad camp roll, adds an enervation and only recovers 1d3 save points per character.

Basic sleeping conditions are having any solid building to sleep in with your own camp gear and public rooms. Will recover 1 vitality per week and good camp rolls remove 1 enervation.

Comfortable sleeping condiions mean an elevated bed and somewhat private rooms. Recovers 1 vitality per half-week, and removes 1 enervation. Doesn't need a camp roll.

All sleeping restores save points. 

# Travel

During a day of travel, each player picks an action, then two rolls must be made.

The distance roll is how far you get at the end of the day. This d20 roll is a simple 0.5km extra for each point above 10, and 0.5km less for each point below 10. Default travel is a march in a day, that's 25km. At best, your party travels 30km by foot. 

Your party can decide to ignore a bad distance roll, but each travelling member gains a point of enervation.

The camp roll measures the quality of your camp. It is a simple downtime resolution roll.

## Travel time

Distances are measured in marches, which are roughly 25km each. Since Wastelife is fully mapped in a GIS software, the GM may be accurate with travel times. 

Most methods of travel stick to this speed, but aids make travel easier. Horses don't improve travel time, but reduce enervation. 

Horses can be used to rush two marches a day if travelling lightly in developed areas where they might get some rest. Relay couriers maintain breakneck horse speeds of four marches a day. Downstream sailing allows for an effortless two to three marches. Sea ships take 3-7 marches over the ocean depending on winds.

## Travel Actions

One character may perform one action in a travel block. This action is "done" during short breaks, or just before settling down. Different locations will change the effectiveness of these actions, and in developed areas actions might have some consequences. Many actions are exclusive, which means only one travelling member may use it in this day of travel. 

Additional actions are provided by advancements, such as entertain.

**Wayfind**

You climb up a tall tree or nearby hill to help track your direction, making reference of any maps you have or descriptions you remember. Useless if following a road. If successful, add 2 to the distance roll of this days travel. Exclusive action.

**Watch**

You scout ahead and watch ahead of the party. Alleviates otherwise surprising encounters. Exclusive action.

**Camp building**

Prepare some things for the camp. If successful, Add 2 to the camp roll.

**Forage**

You look for food around the route. If successful, the yield depends on the climate, but is usually 1 ration unless the area is particularly abundant in forage. If available for the region, the GM can use forage tables.

**Hunt**

If the climate allows, hunts result in small game for successes and medium game on good successes. Small game might not provide any food, but medium game usually provides at least 2 rations. Gained resources depend a lot on what is hunted. If available for the region, the GM can use hunting tables.

**Practise**

If possible, practice for an advancement training requirement. Grants 1/3 a practise mark. Any fractional training marks are discarded at the end of travel, so you need to perform these in groups of three to make them worthwhile.

**Road study**

If possible, study for an advancement training requirement. Grants 1/3 of a study mark. Any fractional training marks are discarded at the end of travel. 

# Resting

Resting in a permanent camp or settlement. Actions cover the half-week.

## Resting actions

**Practise**

Gain 1 practise mark towards an advancement, if you have the things to do it. 

**Study**

Gain 1 study mark towards an advancement, if you have the things to do it

**Prepare for Dilemma**

Name a specific problem the party expects to face. On a success bank 1 Insight. Good successes bank 2 and exceptional banks 3. Banked Insight is spent at the start of the relevant dilemma, adding directly to its Insight sway. Maximum 3 banked at a time, and it expires if the dilemma never comes.

**Prepare citation**

Research into a specific claim in hopes to gain a potential citation in an expected discourse.

**Work a trade**

Your character must find some trade they can work in. Real trades fund parties, busy work fills gaps. Pay varies wildly.

**Network**

Attempt to build standing with a local faction.

**Service**

Do some good service for a faction, for free. 

**Shop**

You can shop in downtime at available places.

**Craft**

You can make things during downtime.
