Combat in Wastelife Commonweal is physical, visceral, and tactical. Its about blades, fists, and survival.

# Melee Combat

## Vitality and Save Points

Combat's sway is vitality and hit points. 

Vitality measures how many serious wounds you can take. Deep cuts, broken bones, or other mortal injuries. Most people have 5 vitality points, and this maximum cannot be raised. As vitality drops, your body begins to fail you. Fighting, running, and even speaking becomes harder. 

Alongside vitality are save points, which act as strokes of luck or bursts of sharp awareness that let you avoid deadly blows. By default, you have 5 save points. You can get more save points by earning advancements. 

Incoming damage always reduces save points first. Once you are out of save points, damage goes directly to vitality.

When you reach 0 vitality, you die.  

## Reach

A combatant's reach is their maximum threat range, extended from their body by their weapon's length.

At the start of combat, the GM declares how far apart combatants are, and if any are already within reach of others. 

When a combatant is within another combatant's reach, a d20 can be used to express the distance between them in decimetres.

In group combats, reach must be tracked on a person-by-person basis, with one dice between each opposing combattant. If group battles get too large, a more hollistic approach may be used by the DM, such as discarding reach or not allowing shorter weapons at all. 

When fighting large monsters, such as anything larger than a polar bear, the minutia of reach matter less. In these circumstances reach is to be discarded as a mechanic for the monster combattant.

## Attacking

### Attack types

Attack types define how damage is dealt. Physical attacks fall into five main categories:

- Light slashing, which deals 1d2 damage.
- Light bludgeoning, which deals 1d2 damage.
- Heavy slashing, which deals 1d3 + 1 damage.
- Heavy bludgeoning, which deals 1d3 + 1 damage.
- Piercing, which deals 1d3 + 3 damage.

Beyond these are the more unusual types that come from enchantments or claps:

- **Acid**, corrosive damage that eats biological and often even non-biological matter. Damage varies with severity.
- **Burns**, caused by damage to the skin either by fire or by frost. Burns will often deal save point damage while requiring additional severity to deal any vitality damage.
- **Lightning** damage is cause by electric charges surging through a body. Since electricity hasn't properly been invented, lightning damage is caused by claps and enchantments. It is often decisively fatal. 
- **Poison**, a chemical attack on the body, introducing some unnatural chemical that targets body systems. 
- **Psychic**, mental strain that's so severe that in enough quantity could end up just breaking your brain.
- **Thunder**, a pressure-based damage caused by shockwaves and barotrauma. Generally not very damaging, but also often bypasses save points. 
- **Necrotic**, a rare type of damage that represents the body just dying, usually due to the lack of circulation.

Some species or advancements provide resistances and weaknesses to certain damage types. Resistances reduce incoming damage by 1, while weaknesses increase it by 1.

### Attack

PD - Proficiency Dice
A - Athletics skill score
BD - Bonus dice from weapon profile
M - Motorics skill score

adding an O as a prefix to these denotes opponent combatant's equivalents.

Proficiency dice per proficiency skill:

1 - d4

2 - d6

3 - d8

4 - d10

5 - d12

If on your turn you chose to attack, you may pick one target within your reach and one attack your weapon can make.

1. Assess reach. If your favoured attack is out of reach, you may decide to reposition. 
2. If you reposition, you roll PD+A and the opponent you're repositioning against rolls the BD of their in range attack.
	1. If your roll's score is above your opponent's, you move the difference between those roles closer or further, depending on your goals. 
	2. If your reposition roll is 3 values lower than the opponent's corresponding roll, they get a free attack against you.
3. Attack roll. The attacker (you) rolls BD+PD+ M. The defender rolls BD (if in range)+PD+M. If the attacker is greater or equal, they hit.
4. Roll Damage. If hit, roll d2 for light slashing/bludgeoning, d3+1 for heavy slashing/bludgeoning, and d3+3 for piercing.
5. Armour Save. Roll to meet or beat your armour score. If met, half damage. You will always take 1 save point if available, even if you beat the score. Heavy attacks subtract 1.

## Shields

Shields allow defenders to use a bonus die to block when their other weapons are out of reach. They also provide larger bonus die to use for defending when compared to most weapon's bonus die.

When repositioning, you can also use your shield's bonus die if it's greater than your proficiency die. 

Shields are categorised into size. There are no hard rules on what size you are allowed to carry, it is based on realistic practicality. If you're weapon is two-handed, you won't be able to use a shield. If your weapon requires fine balance and large, manoeuvring sweeps, you won't be able to use a regular-sized shield. These restrictions might be noted on the weapon.

# Ranged Combat

Ranged combat works similarly to melee combat, but you don't need to track the minutia of reach. Instead, ranged weapons have an effective range and an extended range. Within the effective range, the attacker can use their weapon's bonus die. Within the extended range, the attacker can't use their bonus die. How many ranged attacks can be made in a turn depends on the weapon and the proficiency. 

The attacker rolls their bonus die (if within effective range) + proficiency die + motorics, while the defender rolls their shield's bonus die (which also applies to natural cover) + evasion proficiency + athletics. If the attacker's result is higher, the shot lands and deals damage as normal.

In a scenario where a ranged combatant is shooting at a combatant who cannot shoot back, the GM might just say how many attacks the ranged combatant can make before the other combatant closes the distance. This is determined by how far away the combatants are when shooting begins, and how fast the mobile combatant is. 

## Step-by-Step Ranged Attack

EP = Evasion Proficiency.

1. Determine concrete distances. If you need to move closer, you can find out how many times you will get shot at before you close the distance by asking the GM.
2. Take turns firing. Attacker rolls BD+PD+M, defender rolls EP+A. 

## Ranged Weapons

All ranged weapons are two-handed, unless they have one-handed.

# Taking Turns

Turn order may be holistically determined by the GM as not to slow down combat. Alternatively, all participants can roll a D6 and add their athletics skill to determine order of combat. Some skills may suggest adding to this roll or otherwise ways in which your character can go before others in combat.

# Armour

Armours provide armour save chances to succeeded attacks. The exact value of this save depends on the armour and the type of incoming damage.

The armour save is a d6 roll. The table defines the value needed to pass the partial save. If you roll this value, you only partially save. If you roll above, you fully save. On a partial save, you take half of the damage rounded down. You will always lose at least 1 save point, even on a full save.

You need at least athletics 1 to wear thick gambeson, chain, or brigantine. You need athletics 2 to wear scale and plate. Even with the required athletics skills, you will tire faster in heavier armours. 

The armour values are also presented as if you are wearing a full set of the said armour. If you are only wearing a partial set, such as armour that only covers vitals, then add 2 (to a maximum of 6) to the value required to pass the partial save. Partial sets also reduce the athletics requirement by 1

Partial armours are much more practical than full sets. Many full sets of armour are impractical to wear as you adventure, as they will get very hot in most climates or will wear you down fast. Partial armour balances practicality with protection.

A dash in the table means the armour does nothing against that type of attack. Heavy attacks subtract 1 from the armour save roll.

Atypical damage bypasses armour saves unless stated otherwise. Armour may still prevent delivery of atypical damage, at the GM's adjudication. 


# Board Combat

This arena is designed without a board in mind, but players can still use one.

If you're using a grid or a hex map, from a tile edge to the opposite tile edge is 1.5m.
Larger creatures may occupy multiple tiles. Several regular or smaller creatures can occupy the same tile, but during combat they need the full space of the tile to move. 

If two combatants become adjacent on tiles, put a d20 reach die between them set at 15 decimetres. 

For longer distances across the board, I recommend you calculate the hypotenuse for square tiling and count hexes between points for hex tiling.

you may also use a grid smaller than the space a single person takes up. In this case, the GM needs to decide how many dm each tile represents. These are purely for representation, the distance die always represent the actual useful number. 

# Combat Scenarios

These scenarios serve as examples of how the combat system works. 

## 1. Spear vs Dagger

A militia recruit with a spear faces a bandit armed with a dagger. Both have motorics 2. The recruit has proficiency tier 1 with their spear, the bandit has proficiency tier 2 with their dagger. In this scenario, these weapon proficiencies provide no added benefits. The recruit is wielding the spear two-handed, so they get the +2 bonus dice, meaning their _piercing_ attack profile is d8 instead of d6.

**Turn 1: Spear thrusts at range**
	They begin 10dm apart - the maximum range in the combat is the spear's maximum range.
	The recruit rolls an attack: bonus die (d8) + proficiency die (d4) + Motorics (2). They roll a 10 in total.
	The bandit defends: their dagger has a smaller range than the spear, so they can defend with it: bonus die (d4) + proficiency die (d6) + motorics (2). They roll an 8.
	The spear hits. The recruit rolls for damage (1d3+3). They roll a 5, so the bandit looses all 5 of their save points as the spear misses muscles and hits a bone in their leg.
**Turn 2: Bandit closes in**
	The bandit tries to reposition into dagger range 1 (which is just out of the fist range of 0).
	The bandit rolls proficiency (d6) + Athletics (2). They roll a 7.
	The recruit rolls their spear's bonus die (d8) and get a 6.
	The bandit succeeds in repositioning. They are now 1dm apart, and the bandit can attack.
	The bandit attacks with a bonus die (d4) + proficiency die (d6) + motorics (2). They roll a 9.
	The recruit's spear is too far ranged to defend against the dagger, so they only roll proficiency (d4) + motorics (2). They roll a 5.
	The dagger cuts with its light slashing attack (d2). They roll a 2, and the recruit loses 2 save points.

This example is intended to show that the spear is the deadlier weapon. It's attack is far stronger and they can keep on moving back to keep the bandit within optimal spear range. The dagger isn't helpless, they can bypass the spear's range with some challenge to close the distance.

## 2. Archer vs Knight
An archer with a bow shoots at an approaching knight in full plate armour. The archer has motorics 3, and proficiency tier 2. The knight has athletics 3, evasion proficiency 1 (d6), and a regular shield (d8). The archer will get 2 shots at the knight before they close range, at which point the archer might just run hoping that the knight can't catch up. 

**Shot 1**
	Archer rolls attack: bonus die (d6, within effective range) + proficiency (d6) + Motorics (3). They roll a 13 total.
	The knight defends: shield (d8) + evasion proficiency (d6) + Athletics (3). They roll a 12. 
	The attack succeeds, the damage is 1d3+3 piercing. This rolls 4 damage.
	The knight rolls their armour safe. For piercing, it's 1 to partially save. They roll a 2, get a full save, but still lose 1 save point.
**Shot 2**
	The archer rolls attack: d6+d6+3. They roll 9.
	The knight defends: d8+d6+3. They roll 12. 
	The attack fails. It either hits the shield, or misses entirely.

Now that the knight is too close, the archer runs away. Their only hope is to tire the knight, as the knight can keep up just fine even in full plate armour thanks to their athletics score.
