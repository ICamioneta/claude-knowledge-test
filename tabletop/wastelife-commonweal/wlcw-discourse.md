In Wastelife Commonweal, a discourse is a conflict centred on discussion. It is convincing and convicting. They are events like bartering or interrogating. 

# Sway

A discourse tracks three things per participant, always relative to a single stated claim.

**Position** - By default no participant has position. Winning a roll while no-one has position grants it. Losing a roll while holding position loses it.

**Composure** - A participant's surface-level poise: rehearsed lines, patience, the will to keep deflecting without actually reconsidering anything. Untrained participants start with 5 composure, while skills can increase this. 

**Conviction** - The participant's actual, lasting stance on the claim, from 1 (fully opposed) to 5 (fully convinced), starting at 3 (neutral) unless the GM sets it otherwise.

## Winning discourse

During discourse, the participant with position makes attacking arguments, and the defender makes defending arguments. If the defence fails, then the argument's impact is assessed. 

Impactful arguments lower the opponents cohesion. Once composure reaches 0 and an argument with impact is made, the targeted participant changes their conviction and resets their cohesion. 

# Starting a Discourse

Discourse only starts when conversations turn into conflict. Usually, one party will try to push a claim and try to convince others of it. Players entering discourse need to define their goals in conversation. 

A GM may then describe what players read of other participant's starting dispositions and beliefs. Generally, alignment will be shared with players to give them a concrete measure of how good they are doing. 

Alignment is always measured relative to a single stated claim. Each discourse concerns one claim. Sequencing turns on consecutive instances of discourse is managed by the GM and is kept realistic to conversation. For example, if several claims are being thrown around and a character gets position on one of them, they may focus their action made from position on increasing the alignment of other participants on their stated claim. Alternatively, they may choose to dissuade on another stated claim. 

# Argument Types

These are basic argument types available with their respective first skill level. Assert and Rebut are available without any Logic skill, but without any proficiency die. 

Extra dice are a single additional argument's bonus dice for only when the event is triggered.

# Resolution

Proficiency dice per proficiency skill:

1 - d4

2 - d6

3 - d8

4 - d10

5 - d12

1. **Choose actions.** If no-one has position, all active participants choose an attack. If someone has position, only they choose. For every attack, defending players choose their defence. 
2. **Attack roll.** The actor rolls their attack skill's proficiency dice + attack bonus. Each defending participant rolls their defensive skill's proficiency die + defence bonus. If the actor's total is equal or higher, the argument connects.
3. **Impact.** On a connect, roll the action's Impact Die. Apply the result to the target's Composure. 
4. **assess conviction.** If Composure is already 0, conviction must be assessed. There may be some reason why the conviction cannot change, and the GM must reveal this reason. Otherwise, the target's conviction changes by 1 towards the arguer's claim, and they regain 5 composure. 

## Determining position

When no one has position, everyone chooses an attack. To determine the order these attacks are resolved, have each participant roll a d6 + leadership proficiency die. The highest scores go first. The first won attack gains position. All following attacks still need to be evaluated.
