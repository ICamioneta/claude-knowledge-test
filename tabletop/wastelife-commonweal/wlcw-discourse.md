In Wastelife Commonweal, a discourse is a conflict centred on discussion. It is convincing and convicting. They are events like bartering or interrogating. 

# Sway

Alignment measures how aligned participants are to other's position. It is a scale from one to five:

1. Not aligned. Not convinced or completely disagreeing.
2. Considerate. Perhaps open to ideas or hearing out the other position.
3. Neutrally aligned. An opinion truly on the fence, ready to lean either way.
4. Slightly agrees. The target ideas are beginning to make sense. 
5. Fully agrees. The person agrees in full with the conviction.

Another sway is position. By default, no actor has position. An actor may "take position" to assume control over the discourse. 

# Starting a Discourse

Discourse only starts when conversations turn into conflict. Usually, one party will try to push a claim and try to convince others of it. Players entering discourse need to define their goals in conversation. 

A GM may then describe what players read of other participant's starting dispositions and beliefs. Generally, alignment should be shared with players to give them a concrete measure of how good they are doing. 

Alignment is always measured relative to a single stated claim. Each discourse concerns one claim. Sequencing turns on consecutive instances of discourse is managed by the GM and is kept realistic to conversation. For example, if several claims are being thrown around and a character gets position on one of them, they may focus their action made from position on increasing the alignment of other participants on their stated claim. Alternatively, they may choose to dissuade on another stated claim. 

# Actions

Discourse revolves around conversational actions. When no participant has position, all active participants choose an action. When a participant has position, only they choose an action. 

Actions have a primary skill and a defensive skill. To resolve an action, the character that made the action rolls a bonus die based on their primary skill proficiency. Defending participants role a defensive die based on their proficiency in their defensive skill. 

The winner of this roll takes position if they do not already have it. If several participants take conflicting position on the same turn, then no participant claims position. If the roll is won by a participant with position, then it may increase or decrease (dissuade) the target's alignment. If a roll is failed by a participant with position then they lose position. 

When a roll from position succeeds but cannot change alignment, the GM must instead offer a concrete compromise that would allow alignment to be increased if pursued and / or reveal a hidden condition, belief, or stake blocking the progress of alignment. 

There are default actions that can be taken. Other actions are skills that can be learnt. 

| Action        | Primary Skill | Defensive Skill |
| ------------- | ------------- | --------------- |
| persuasion    | charm         | empathy         |
| interrogation | empathy       | charm           |
| Rallying      | leadership    | willpower       |
| stonewalling  | Willpower     | Leadership      |

Each time a participant consecutively repeats an action, targets get +1 to their defence to it. this bonus ends when the participant chooses another action. This bonus caps at +4. 

## Action rolls

Skills in Wastelife Commonweal come in five levels. Here are the proficiency die for each level. 

1 - d4
2 -d6
3 - d8
4 - d10
5 - d12

To recall, the character taking the action rolls their primary skill level's bonus die and needs to get higher than their opponent's defensive skill for that action. 

Other skills may add other bonuses or change rolling rules. 

# Resolution

A conflict of discourse ends when either party gives up or because their alignment reaches stage 5 with a proposed argument.
