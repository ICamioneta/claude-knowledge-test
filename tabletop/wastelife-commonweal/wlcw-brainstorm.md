So advancements. 

We have the 60 base advancements formally called "skills".

I have a few options on how I design the rest of them, and it will change on what a skill is. 

I think what I'll do is make advancements relatively specialised except from where they might be skills with high skill ceilings. The benefit of a holistic system is that advancements don't need to be equal in usefulness to be balanced. More useful advs can be harder to obtain. 

The skills have to each be useful in all arenas of conflict. Many already provide direct bonuses to arenas. Of body, motorics is used in combat. Of mind, all are used depending on the action. Of spirit, what is used depends on the speech done. It might be relevant to give every one a concrete addition to each arena. For dilemma, this can be certain investigatory actions with bonuses. For discourse similarly they can each provide a new action in talking. For combat, it might have to be some sort of bonus ability or action. 

Maybe it's a good idea to have a copy of my old rules to see what I need to transfer over in advancement form. 

let's go through each skill to see what I might be able to add.

# Advancements from Classes

Begone, the old D&D class system! Now I shall have something entirely proprietary!

WTTTRPG has 7 classes: Velews, Fighter, Jack, Mage, Paladin, Caedyll, and Wizard. Paladin never really fit in with the rest especially lore-wise (like, the gods are there, but do they really care?). Paladin is best dissolved entirely. 

So what main skills would the 6 classes have?

**Velews:** Endurance*, Memory*, Leadership, Willpower^
**Fighter:** Athletics*, Endurance^, Motorics*, Awareness^
**Jack:** Stealth^, Logic^, Charm*, Empathy*
**Mage:** Motorics*, Academics^, Logic*, Charm-
**Caedyll:** Athletics^, Stealth*, Awareness*, Empathy*
**Wizard:** Leadership-, Academics*, Memory*, Willpower*

Tally:

Athletics: Fighter, Caedyll
Endurance: Velews, Wizard
Stealth: Jack, Caedyll
Motorics: Fighter, Mage
Academics: Mage, Wizard
Awareness: Fighter, Caedyll
Logic: Jack, Mage
Memory: Velews, Wizard
Charm: Jack, Charm
Leadership: Velews, Fighter
Willpower: Velews, Wizard
Empathy: Jack, Caedyll

Very conveniently, 6 model classes and 12 base skills gives us the tasty tasty distribution of two classes per skill and four skills per class. 

**_why_**

Velews: D&D Druids are mechanically a tank, so endurance and willpower as focus skills are mechanically meaningful. Lore-wise, druids whole stick is attuning with the forest's power, and trees are pretty hard. Maintaining that kind of power requires willpower. For memory, Velews maintain a vast recollection of lore. For leadership, it's probably mechanically useful for a tank to have skills like that and in lore I'm not entirely sure.

Fighter: Athletics and motorics are a given. Fighter need to be fit and finnicky. Awareness, in combat and in general I think also needs little justification. Endurance is mechanically useful.

Jack: Stealth is probably the hardest to justify, but I think the class overlaps into rogue stuff a bit. Logic is needed when deducing organisational and class systems, and is a core part of the jack outlook. Charm and empathy are by far the two main conversational tools in the jack toolbox. 

Mage: Motorics is used to clap. Academics is kinda a general hidist skill but it's important for mages to be smart more generally. Logic, in claps, is useful since mages logically deduce the effect of a spell from the properties of the ingredient. Charm is a little harder to justify, but it covers the warlock style fantasy.

Caedyll: As the rogue-ranger hybrid, stealth and awareness are natural. Athletics is for running and constant movement and empathy is generally needed when you're job is either protecting community or nabbing stuff. 

Wizard: They learn enchantments by memorising certain planar alignments and again academics serves as the generic hidist trait. Willpower because it canonically takes mental endurance to be able to change your own planar vector. Leadership is hard to justify but it's about letting your presence be known so it might work with lecturer or soyner archetypes. 

## Dissecting classes

### Velews
I'm not entirely sure how to work with the Velews' tree stuff since it's a very incredibly specific set of skills. What I'll probably do is create a number of advancement trees based on the different Velews sub-types that now exist in lore. 

To expand on Velews lore, they as druids are simply non-Ysgews that learn to tap into the power of the trees, specifically to use their torfol energy for their own magic. In modern lore, Ysgews have become very much their own thing: a _luminary_. It only makes sense, then, that Velews are merely mortals who try to tap into the same power sources that make the luminaries so powerful. Abilities for Velews that make them be the ones that tap into trees can be adapted to each of the luminary power sources (lps):

- river (Bracai)
- forest (Ysgews)
- desert (Arcan Dida)
- ocean (Allyiscereen)
- magma/tectonic (Fridnyrm)

Anyway, here's the skill disection.

- Root Trick: can be part of each lps ability tree.
- Wild Shape: its own advancement/enchantment that might be locked behind much later skill levels given its lore difficulty, and probably lps dependent.
- Timeless body: likely removed entirely or lumped in with lps trees.

Here's disected skills that would become specific to the velews lps tree or other lps trees:

- Wild Form Enchanting: I mean, your ability to enchant or clap depends on what you physically shapeshift.
- One of the Roots: being able to use lps abilities 
- Combat wild shape. Bonuses to wild shape can come naturally as part of that individual skill.
- Powerful Forms. Form restrictions can be part of the individual skill advancement.

Luckily for me, the Velews ability list is shalow. I knew no-one was ever going to use them, so designing them felt meaningless. Similarly, I probably won't design as many high level advancements unless I get players that actually go there. 

### Fighter

Fighting style is probably the most relevant thing. Their benefits and abilities are very easily transferred directly into skills that may hinge on specific weapon skills. Here's a dissection:

- Archery: Maybe a super skill for weapon-specific proficiencies or advancements. Generic buffs may be meaningful to add to show that if you can shoot a longbow, you can probably also shoot a shortbow. 
- Duelling: An empty offhand bonus for some weapons might be reasonable? I'm not a huge fan of random bonuses though.
- Micro-spacing: A unique skill to focus on micro spacing in combat, which is a core component of WLCW's system, may be very powerful and very relevant.
- Protection: Ally protecting skills. The previous one imposes disadvantage when wearing a shield.
- Two-handed Fighting. An idea for generic two-handed fighting style weapons. The previous rerolls bad rolls. 
- Yeeting: Generic thrown. Previous adds range and a little bonus.
- Weapon Ambidextry. There's a lot of potential for dual-wielding rules. 

Manouvers similarly.

- Commander's Strike: Give an ally one of your attacks. Not sure if this fits into my combat system really well.
- Disarming Attack: Disarm skill may be relevant and weapon-dependent.

- Evasive Footwork: gives more EP but since WLCW doesn't have EP it's probably a bonus to repositioning.
- Feinting Attack: Advantage on a next attack roll. I'm sure I can come up with something more mechanically involved though.
- Goading Attack: Forcing an opponent to attack you with your attack. I haven't really done group combat rules but something like this kinda makes sense perhaps part of intimidation.
- Lateral Attack: Attack with an ally.
- Lunging Attack: Increased reach for an attack.
- Manoeuvring Attack: Move a friend with your attack. Might be applicable in some way to a skill tree. Maybe with other group or manoeuvre focussed attacks for a more unified system.
- Menacing Attack: Frighten with a hit. WLCW doesn't yet have status effects. 
- Parry: Didn't I already do this one? I'm not sure I should include it as it's just a part of combat and not any specific manoeuvre. Not like the other manoeuvres are specific.
- Precision attack: This is a good idea probably, maybe also not? The previous thing doesn't do much at all but I might think of something for WLCW.
- Pushing Attack: disrupt enemy position again.
- Rally: Whatever bolstering resolve means, it can probably just be lumped into a leadership advancement.
- Riposte. Again, like parry, it's not really a maneauver so much as it is just a part of combat, but I can probably add something for when the opponent misses. 
- Sweeping Attack: Still need to figure out group combat before thinking about this.
- Trip Attack: A serious disruption to positioning. 

Here are the other fighter skills for dissection.

- Second Wind: regain save points, probably best suited for athletics over endurance. 
- Impressive exploit: Free action, probably best for athletics.
- Extra Attacks: Likely best for athletics + motorics advancement, but I'm not sure how I could fit this idea into the combat system. Otherwise, this is the main way fighters are balanced against classes that can do more, but in WLCW you're only limited by your ability to gain advancement. 
- Indomitable: in the context of WLCW it's probably just second wind but for vitality.
- Competitive Athlete: Just extra skill checks. Whatever this was is just part of the Athletics skill now
- Survivor: Passive save point regeneration.
- Punishing hits: extra crit range.
- Reconnoitre: Learning opponent capabilities is easily shoved into a leadership / awareness / empathy ability.
- Micromanage: Bonus to ally attack rolls.
- Captain: This is just part of leadership now.
- Sword & Spell: A unique sword and spell skill would make sense as a motorics skill.
- Magic strike: This is just an enchantment.
- Magical Propellant: The mage-style speed stuff would be really cool to keep, but in general the spellsword subclass is kinda implied by the system even if it needs certain skills that facilitate it.

### Jack

I like the Jack. My perfect answer to the Bard, it does everything they do without the annoyance of tying magic to playing instruments. Magic through instruments was the idea of someone who never played one. Why can't I pluck a string in magical peace?

Aanyway, in WTTTRPG, Jacks were basically just the "everything talking" role because D&D was kinda like that. Having classes focussed on combat, or in fact a game system that focusses on combat in general, is unbefitting to Wastelife. 

Jack Arts is the thing I'm most proud of. Some nice little skill sets focussed on combat utility over combat damage. In WLCW it's now going to have to be split into weapon-specific skill trees, which would make certain weapons or tools more focussed on combat assistance over anything else. 

Here are the Jack Arts

- Ball Bearings: Knock creatures over.
- Caltrops: Damage creatures.
- Net: Restrain creatures.
- Whips: Forcefully move creatures, or special rules depending on where the whip hits.
- Hook: Moving targets again, but more precise. 

Here's the abilities dissection.

- Jack of All Trades: Proficiencies don't exist any more, so it really doesn't matter. Same applies to extra proficiencies.
- Vicious Mockery. This doesn't fit into the magic system, but having it just as a skill makes it so much better. Naturally, an empathy skill.
- Calming Words. Empathy and charm skill. Just calms people, too many rules written for such a simple thing.
- Haggling Consistency: Haggling in general is absorbed into the discourse system as a whole.
- Social Seed: Seeding ideas that they will do doesn't make a lot of sense given how discourse is presented in WLCW, but it's still an idea for a more subversive discourse experience. 
- Read like a Book: Literally just part of the empathy skill now. 
- Unparalleled Skill: Jack's Aid stuff.
- Jack's Potion: Just part of the base liquid skill now?
- Consistent Enchantment: Really not sure why this is a thing.
- Greatest Aid: More Jack's Aid.

### Mage

In D&D, Wizards, Sorcerors, Warlocks, Bards, ect, they all interact with the magic system in fundamentally different ways in lore. In Wastelife, you either do wizardry or you don't, or you do magery or not. They're just skills, there's no reason a fighter wouldn't be able to learn either one. This goes for everything ever; this is exactly why I'm doing this advancement system. Anyway, dissection calls. 

- Field Realisation: Feeling torfols is probably a good idea for some basic skills. Torfol increase and magic as a skill in general would be a great tree to write. 
- Magical speed: or otherwise representing speed gains from magery helps upkeep a pretty big part of the mage fantasy. Having some torfol-promoted speed gaining would be nice. 
- Effect Specialty: Not sure how I would ever implement this into a cooler system, it might just be scrapped. 
- Aether Flight: For me, this is one of the core fantasies of a super-powerful mage. It would require a number of top-tier skills to do that kind of thing straight with the torfol, but I think it's worth it. 
- Casting Master: A specific spells allowing for casting way above normal ingredient potential.
- Distant Spells: increasing distances would be a very relevant.

Advanced linking lets you directly mentally be your golem and do a few more actions, I'll list them next.

# Magic System

No, it's not magic. It's the Hidol and the Torfol and all that stuff. 

With a complete systemic rebuilding I'm no longer tempted to game-ify Wastelife's magic system. In WTTTRPG, enchantments were just like D&D spells and spells were this whole system with slots and stuff, I thought it was kinda cool but overly complex. 

I'm tempted to properly represent what "magic" is in Wastelife. I want an underlying system of vectors and matrix operations and a nice discoverable system over the top. 

The same underlying system can be used for both adaptive and reactive torfol. The zero vector is in lore the average common direction of adaptive torfol, and any "effect" is a number of effective "hotspots" in the vector space. 

For adaptive torfol and enchanting, wizards have to put in some mental effort to physically change their torfol's planar vector to a point of hotspot to catalyse the effect's manifestation and put it into an object. The most common mental abstraction of this process is moving an imaginary "ball". To rotate this ball is to change the vector, and once they put it on a hotspot they get an enchantment. 

For reactive torfol, the two torfol overlap under the pressure differential produced by clapping, and their planar vectors temporarily combine in the overlapping region. If this combination results in a hotspot, the hidol manifests and some effect is produced, almost always destroying the ingredients involved. Mages don't ne ed to know the underlying vector operations, only what ingredients combine with what. 

## The Vector Spaces

The Hidol is a shit ton (potentially infinite) of abstract dimensions. When it comes to hidosis, these dimensions can be abstracted into mathematical vectors with real values. 

Designing the hotspots I want for the underlying effects is a complex manner. It may be worth it to pick, say, a few significant vector combinations and stick with these. 

The hardest part is making hotspots available from multiple ingredients. If it requires a combination too specific of vectors then every ingredient ends up acting identical. In the last system, every ingredient had 4 effects it could cause, and it would be nice to mirror that somewhat in this base system. If all ingredients that can cast a fire projectile share a vector, that is against the design requirement. 

I think what I'll do is choose like 12 vectors and give them random names. "Krenbichness", "Reduflex Vector", "Giyt's Dimension", nonsense words and etc. Then I'll define some combination hotspots of these for manifestation. 

## Player Interaction

The player interaction side of things must be abstract. Perhaps find a way to simplify the whole system into lookup tables. Exposing any player who wants to dabble in the magic system to vector operations is not super gucci. 

## Skills Representation

Character skills need to affect what types of things they can do in the magic system. For enchantments, a simple limit on how the player can move the vector space, perhaps influenced by dice rolls, would be sufficient. Dice rolls may be the best way to abstract skill-based vector alignment casting. 

For spells, it's a bit trickier. Imposing skill-based limits on usage of ingredients of a greater strength is fine though and might be the most elegant way to do it. Dice rolls for pressure, perhaps.
