This guide is your on-ramp into Wastelife. A broad, unchanging view of the continents, peoples, cosmology, and core ideas of the setting. It will help you:

- Understand the tone and logic of the setting
- Find inspiration for your character's origin, culture, and worldview
- Communicate with the GM (me) to weave your character naturally into the setting

Campaign overview documents will fill you in on what's happening in a specific area, it'll give you more information on the potential stories that will happen in that locality.

# The Tone of Wastelife

**Historical Analogy**

Roughly Earth's 1750s, the world is mostly pre-industrial with pockets of early industrial experimentation.

**Social Structure**

Class hierarchies common to societies in the historically analogous period are present, but gender equality and romantic / sexual freedoms are far more common, especially among the lower classes.

**Weapons**

Guns exist but are rare on the two main continents of the setting. Most warfare is fought with developed late-medieval weapons. Hidosis (magic) is commonly used in combat. 

**Magic**

"Magic" is derived from the multi-dimensional substrate of energy known as the Hidol, which is similar to common fantastical understandings of an "Aether". There is a scientific field of study called "Hidology" that studies the Hidol as a natural phenomena. Actual performed "magic" is called Hidosis and is formally understood as "the process and phenomenon of using the Hidol as a source of energy or power".

**Philosophy**

Wastelife has a theme that no person wins alone. It is built on the idea that the world is ultimately just full of people doing things. Power is about people and the systems they create. Every person is just navigating this complex world. In line with realism, there are no monsters, only animals.

## Barogate - the Heartland of the Muz

Barogate is the most detailed continent. It's the birthplace of the human-like Muz.

**Major Regions:**

- **Shildo**, a fertile subtropical land that's highly managed with organised states.
	- _Ereso_, geographically diverse and well developed.
	- _Terlaye_, a land dominated by rivers, a renouned seat of learning.
	- _Ralean_, a large cultural and artistic heartland, dominated by empire.
- **The Wastes**, an equitorial and tropical desert with varied biomes.
	- *The Heabe*, a steppe dominated by nomadic chariot-riders and goat-like peoples.
	- *The Vugaix*, a dense and dangerous mushroom forest.
	- *Dithas*, a mixed fungi & trees forest.
	- *Kyenel*, a typical dry sandy desert dominated by silver ruins.
	- *Duela*, a salty mangrove swamp.
	- *Ichmenre*, a volcanic mountain range.
	- *Llyhai*, a huge ash-covered land.
	- *Kusao*, dry river canyons with an organised Phax state.
	- *Cronar*, an oasis rich desert with wet coasts.
- **Other regions**
	- *Kyeron*, an equatorial rainforest.
	- *Gordolmonre*, a massive mountain range.
	- *Gazi Lale Darie*, a temperate island chain and mainland.
	- Sarel, an unruly and anarchic savanna 

## Anula - the Homeland of the Dos

Anula is temperate and politically varied. It's native to the bird-like Dos and the guinea-pig like Cut.

**Regions:**

- _Neble_, a land divided between the militant Nebdos and the forest people of Codia.
- _Vezoen_, a land split between a technological powerhouse and a defensive imperium.
- Belkis, a vast and dry area. 
- _Thagryene_, a land of tense cooperation between states.
- _Calaeddodd_, the central mountain range dominated by large beasts.

## Mothdao - the Far Continent

Far across the western sea, Mothdao is known of but rarely known about in Barogate and Anula. Not all regions are known, but those that are carry a name of prestige in the two continents. 

## Annwvyn - The Distant Paradise

The homeland of the Gan, Annwvyn, is often mythologised as a paradise. It's separated by the stormy Rahaomor ocean, which boats rarely pass. because of this, there is little exchange between Annwvyn and the two continents. Firearms are common here, but abroad Hidosis is far more convenient and useful.

## Ouranxo - the Wild Forest

The cold, forested continent to the north-west of Barogate. Home to several mammalian beast-folk and the cephalopod Deg. 

## Raixzer - Crustacean Cliffs

A highland continent of harsh coastal cliffs, inhabited by the crustacean-like Ran.

## Elvas Eldu - the Frozen South

Elvas Eldu is a huge continent to the far east and far south of Anula. It is vastly a cold and frozen tundra, mostly only habitable on its coasts. It is known to be inhabited by the giant fish-like Ryr. 

# Eternity and the Hidol

Quick facts for players:

- the _Mortal Realm_ is where the mortal world is located, where all species of person live.
- the _Hidol_ is a multidimensional substrate upon which realms are built, and through which they are all linked.
- _Eternity_ is a collective term for the realms, the Hidol, and their history
- Gods are consciousnesses obsessed with a specific domain; they are like forces of nature rather than benevolent dieties.
- Magic (Hidosis) is the study and application of the Hidol's properties. Anyone can learn it.

The setting of Wastelife covers several "Realms" all of which are connected through the Hidol, which is some sort of multidimensional substrate. Often, this "Hidol" and its history is called Eternity. 

The realm in which the species of people live is called the Mortal Realm. It existed before the Gods came into Eternity. The Gods discovered the Mortal Realm only 16000 years ago, and it is theorised that the Gods came into existence not too long before they discovered the Mortal Realm. Before the Gods, there was no society of peoples, as this would be formed as a consequence of the Gods creating people. 

All other realms (but one) are the realms of the Gods themselves. They are their purposeful creations in which they have full omnipotent control. They are inhabited by Esten, living autonomous creations of the Gods. Mortal people also come to inhabit the Gods' realms, but this is rare.

Among scholarly circles concerned with Eternity, there is knowledge of the "Realm of Sand" or the "Sanded Realm". This is another realm that exists without the Gods and predates them. It is entirely covered in sand, and nothing more is known. 

## The Gods

The Gods are consciousnesses in Eternity that ascribe themselves to a domain. A domain is some vague concept. A God's Domain is their special obsession. The Gods often describe their own purpose as "furthering their domain", a process which they define themselves. For example, for the God of Knowledge, furthering the domain of Knowledge means documenting all that is known and the pursuit to know more. 

Eternity is not static: new Gods come into Eternity frequently. So far, not a millenium has passed without a new God. The distribution of new God's arrivals is uneven. The mechanisms behind the arrival of new Gods is unknown, but it is theorised that events in the Mortal Realm are causal in the introduction of new Gods. Mortals can ascend to godhood, but its complicated. 

The nature of the Gods is well known to every mortal: they are not benevolent beings, but are seen as forces of nature. They care only about their domain, often to the detriment of Mortals and their wellbeing. The Gods are not often worshipped, as it is well understood that worship provides no return to mortals. 

There are hundreds of Gods in Wastelife, and it is not important to know each one. When relevant, I will name them by their domain, but they have names. 

In Wastelife, "Magic" is a well understood scientific subject called Hidology, the study of the Hidol. Some properties of the Hidol are exploitable to create "magical" phenomenon. The process of invoking these phenomenon is called "Hidosis", which is more commonly describable as magic. Anyone is capable of Hidosis, so long as they have the time and effort to learn the expertise.

Wastelife is inhabited by a wide array of what is known as a species of person. It is a defined category - 'Species whose members are capable and actively participate in the society of peoples'. As people travel often, any species of person can be found anywhere, but as with Earth's cultures they are often more populous in certain areas. 

As the categorisation implies, different species of person are sexually incompatible. Typically, members of different species cannot naturally produce offspring together. Within species are subspecies. There is no single "Muz", there are only members of their subspecies. 

Stereotyping is common in fantasy worlds. Think dwarves as the stubborn but crafty mountain dwellers. Wastelife necessarily has stereotypes, but their truth is much weaker than in other fantasy worlds. I use a very specific wording, "known", to reference when a subspecies' capabilities are a combination of actual physical differences and cultures. For example, while certain subspecies like the Danmuz are _known_ for their magical aptitude, any other species living in Danmuz culture is likely to share this magical aptitude due to the cultural upbringing.

For this reason, you should take your character seed from the cultures that are often loosely based in the physical capabilities of its majority subspecies. 

Species of person fall into two categories: evolved peoples and created peoples. The Gods in Wastelife have the power to create living things. Often, they create species of person. Their creations are almost always derivations of pre-existing creatures.

Following are short descriptions of each species of person and their subspecies. For more details, ask the author for additional material. They are ordered in terms of importance and relevance to the detailed parts of the world, so the further down the list you go the more obscure the species of person.

**Muz**

The Muz are the human-like species of person and are native to Barogate. They were created by the original Six Gods ten thousand years ago. 

- Lemuz, a sociable and community-forming subspecies native to Ralean.
- Danmuz, a subspecies known to be particularly adept at Hidosis, native to Terlaye.
- Kaemuz, a subspecies known for their feats of engineering and architectural aptitude, native to Ereso.
- Ximuz, a hairy subspecies with articulate tails, native to Kyeron.
- Domuz, a subspecies akin to merfolk with colourful scales and webbed hands and feet, native to Gazi Lale Darie
- Difmuz, a highly adaptable subspecies native to the Wastes.


**Dos**

The Dos are a bird-like species of person native to Anula. They were created by four of the six original gods, three of which made their own subspecies.

- Nebdos, a falcon-like subspecies known for their aptitude in combat, native to Neble.
- Nidos, a corvid-like subspecies known for their intelligence, native to Vezoen.
- Nudos, a swallow-like subspecies known for their social aptitude, native to Thagryene.

**Gan** (Barogatian and Anulan)

The Gan are an amphibian species of person. Barogation and Anulan Gan subspecies are separate due to importance ordering. They are frog-like and quite short.

- Korongan, the Gan of Cronar who are known to be strong and steadfast.
- Akiiogan, the Gan who cohabit Thagryene's eastern coast, known to be sneaky and cunning.

**Ens**

The Ens are a goat-like species of person who naturally evolved. They are frequently present across Barogate and Anula despite being native to the Heabe.

- Colsens, known to be generally more intelligent than other Ens subspecies, they are more frequently seen among Muz-dominated societies than in their own societies.
- Nadens, the most populous subspecies who live nomadic and settled lifestyles in the Heabe. 
- Reyens, a short and lanky mountain Ens, native to Gordolmonre.
- Popens, a large and beefy Ens known to sprint using their knuckles. They are rare, especially outside of the Heabe. Restricted playability.

**Ews**

The Ews are a species of tree-like peoples who live in and draw power from forests. They are known to be natives to Neble, in Codia. 

- Ysgews, large oak-like tree beings, they are incredibly powerful both magically and physically. They form the core of Ews societies. Not playable.
- Codews, a more human-like subspecies who are independent and often live in non-Ews societies.
- Caddews, a warrior Ews whose purpose is to protect the Ysgews. Restricted playability.
- Calews, a unique "revived" Ews warrior present in a certain part of Codia. 

As a note, the Ysgews cannot be played by any prospective player. They are far too large and inherently powerful for balanced play, and are almost never seen outside of the villages that surround them. 

**Cut**

The Cut are a species of short and hairless guinea-pig like peoples. They are creations of five of the original six gods. They are frequent all over Anula, although they are more concentrated near the mountain range Calaeddodd.

- Chōcut, who are known for their strength and hardiness, native to Calaeddodd.
- Lescut, who are a sociable, populous, and widespread subspecies. 
- Cafcut, who are known for their sociable, comedic, and heroic culture.
- Dancut, known to be more adept at hidosis than other cut.
- Diacut, who are known for their great engineering works in their early industrialising city, Brwrd.

**Mad**

The Mad are a species of mushroom-like people. Mad are an evolved species, however some gods have diverged the Mad and created their own subspecies.

- Buemad, the most populous Mad who live in forest clearings in Vugaix, and are native to it. 
- Dimad, a subspecies with slimmer limbs and rounder bellies that are native to Dithas
- Cuemad, a miniature subspecies common in southern Vugaix and its border with Ralean, but are found all over the Vugaix and Dithas. They are naturally capable at Hidosis. 
- Pucmad, grey-skinned "goblin"-like mad who are visually similar to the Dimad.
- Guarmad, 4 meter tall Mad with trunk-like limbs who live across the Vugaix. Restricted playability.

**Fall**

The Fall are an evolved species of reptilian people.

- Matafall, a subspecies that resemble geckos, native to Duela.
- Rutefall, a lizard-like fall most closely resembling Komodo dragons, native to Duela.
- Laurafall, an iguanid-like subspecies native to Kyeron
- Tudofall, a tortoise-like subspecies with a low population, native to Duela and Dithas. 

It is known that there are more subspecies of Fall on other continents. If prospective players have ideas for unique reptilian-people, I encourage you to suggest them.

**Phax**

The Phax are a scorpion-like evolved species of person. They are native to Kusao.

- Sosophax, the "regular" phax whose population far exceeds all other Phax combined. 
- Gofophax, a thinner subterranean subspecies known for their colourations and affinity for climbing.
- Tolusphax, a larger and more physically capable Phax who often live semi-nomadic lifestyles on Kusao's canyon plateaus. Restricted playability.
- Allophax, a large and somewhat monstrous Phax variation. Restricted playability.

**Gan (Annwvyn)**

The larger Gan group in their homeland Annwvyn covers a wider range of amphibian-likes. 

- Ce'gan, a lanky and crocodile-like.
- Deimgan, a nocturnal salamander-like.
- Dregan, a large and bulky snake-like.
- Coesgan, a smaller frog-like.
- Sangan, agile gecko-like.
- Ellegan, a hardy crocodile-like.

**Çin**

A mammalian cat-like people. They are curious to a fault, and usually quite religious. 

- Telikuçin, thin-haired savanna cats. 
- Kahrçin, fire-and-grain obsessed red-fur cats. 
- Shinkuçin, fluffy fellers obsessed with ancient subterranean ruins. 

**Lindi**

An example of a species of person without any subspecies. They are caterpillar-like and present nearly everywhere. They create small, insulated societies often where the winds are harsh. 

**Calapeth**

A unique species of shadowy shapeshifters. They are often violently excluded from society, so many members live in secrecy. They are a very challenging species of person to play in a TTRPG context, so please coordinate with the game host.

**Ran**

A crustacean-like species of person native to Raixzer. There is only one defined individual subspecies, the Caran, who are crab-like. From now and onwards, there is not a whole lot of development in the species and subspecies, and any ideas prospective players have would be more than welcome. 

**Ryr**

A species of giant, bipedal creatures resembling cold water fish, common on Elvas Eldu. As they are often giant and physically capable, as well as being from a continent that's inhospitable and nearly impossible to reach, they should rarely be played. 

As with all undeveloped species of person, I urge prospective players to come up with their own ideas for a playable Ryr subspecies.

**Pri**

A species of spider-like people native in some regions of Mothdao.

**Deg**

A cephalopod-like species native to Ouranxo.

# Getting Started in Wastelife

By now, you should have a broad sense of the world Wastelife takes place in, enough to imagine where your character might fit into it. Think of this document as your map of the big picture. The finer details like local customs, daily life, faction politics, and so on live in other resources and documents you can explore later. 

The key thing to know is I am flexible when it comes to fitting things into Wastelife's lore. If you bring me an idea that excites you, I'll work with you to make it fit Wastelife's tone and logic. Sometimes this means inventing an entirely new thing together (like the Calapeth, which began as a player's eccentric concept and grew into a fully realized species of person). Sometimes it means adjusting your idea so it works within what's already established. In the worst case, I'll allow things with an understanding that it won't be accepted into Wastelife's canon lore.

Most Wastelife campaigns take place on **Barogate** or **Anula**. If your character is from elsewhere, that's great, but you should think through:

- Why did they leave their homeland?
- How long have they been in the campaign's area?
- How much do they know about local ways of life?

If you're character's from a species that is extremely rare on Barogate and Anula, but grew up there, you have some more things to think through:

- Do they have a family of their species in the area, or were they adopted?
- What made their family move to the area?
- If they were adopted from outside the area, how did they get there as a child?

You don't need to become an expert on the lore to start. Pick a place that sounds or looks nice, pick a species, and get a rough idea of your character's personality, values, and goals. If a location sparks your imagination, but you're unsure of the details, ask me about lore (and don't hesitate to tell me to stop when you've had enough!). I'll help you shape your character into something that fits seamlessly into the world.

Sometimes, a backstory might touch on something already well detailed in Wastelife, like a city's history, a culture, or an area's politics, in a way that doesn't quite match the established detail. If that happens, I won't say no. I'll suggest an alternative place or culture that aligns with your idea while keeping the world consistent.

Think of it this way: Wastelife is a big, living world. Your character can come from almost anywhere and be almost anyone, as long as they play by the same physical, social, and historical rules as everything else in the setting.
