The Spire is a pretty big mystery in Ereso, and I feel its only right to let players know about it and give them the tools to solve it without explicitly stating the truth.

So we all know what the Spire is. Its a kilometer tall stone structure with a base about 10 metres in diameter that gradually tapers into a tip at the top. If you *trust* what wizards say, it has a torfol that extends really far from the base (about 250 metres) and it heavily influences the Hidol density of other torfol that sort of go into the area of its torfol. 

# setup
Introduce the spire. Get to know the spire. No, really, get a picnic blanket and serve it and yourself some tea. The players might start to realise just how *tall* the thing is. Its unabashedly -huge-. You can see it from cities away. You can see the top parts of it from mouvasir, you can still see the tip from Galresir and Kerosir. Its visible from half of the Sapyat fields.

Any wizards partaking in this comfortable picnic will notice the Spire's torfol. The ability for a creature to sense a torfol is a learned skill, but even the most basic of wizards will notice the Spire's torfol if they get close enough to it to serve it some tea.

If they really start to get to know it, they might start asking picnic-related questions. What's the spire's middle name? How's the Spire's mother doing? What does the Spire think about the Eresonian economy? Where did the spire come from? Its that easy for them to get you, so stay diligent. You dont know where the spire came from, neither do they, so they have to *figure it out*. 

Or, maybe they're too scared to approach the spire, thinking it may topple over on them, or crumble when they get too close. This is where the rumours and folk tales come into play. The Spire is known by everyone in Ereso and widely across the rest of Shildo. A lot of people think they know. Here are the silly things they might say.
- Its an enchanted monument built by the ancient empire to commemorate something, like their existence or a great battle or something.
- I don't know why it is, but its clearly very magical and if I were a wizard I would use it to get stronger.
- Its a dungeon for the most powerful wizard ever! Its so tall so that gravity holds it down, no wizard could ever move it.
- It was put there by the god Bwydo who wanted to eat Ereso. He pierced it with a giant chopstick, and then realised that Ereso might be a bit too heavy to lift.
- Why do you care so much about why it is? Whatever its original purpose, its now a symbol that unifies Ereso and the Eresonian people.

# Library Research
No-one wants to take a break from uni to go into a fictional uni and plunge into their library, but crazy folk might. And your players *are* crazy. So, what will they find in books?
Well, seemingly, not a lot. Books will say the Spire dates back to the Dolom Empire, an academic name for the Ancient Eresonain Empire which existed between 1200 and 1900, the longest lasting empire. 

So then, players might start looking into the only lead they have. But what, prithee, will they prith-see?

## The Ancient Eresonian Empire
To comb through the entirety of Eresonian chronology on the Dolomite Empire is a difficult task, but there are generally a few key takeaways.

- The Dolomite Empire started as a federative republic between central Eresonian towns, but as the federation expanded, the republic saw more internal discourse and the empire became unstable
- Following this instability, around 1350 the Dolom Republic became the Dolom Empire as a senator managed to appoint themselves as a head-of-state and was granted emergency powers in the face of tensions with Nao Techia.
- Nao Techia was destroyed in the War of Iavan Thell, a war where the Dolom Empire was made aggressive against Nao Techia with the assistance of the Vystxikmli Empire, a Rodxan early kingdom, and internal revolts.
- At this point, a book might mention that when the Dolom Empire was at its height, it set itself the goal to defeat the White Tyrant, a massive stone golem that once wandered Barogate and Anula wrecking things. This goal was achieved, and the world was freed of this agent of destruction.
- Later in 1900 after a steady decline, the Dolomite Empire eventually fell and made way for feudal kingdoms to take power.

They might notice the key stand out in this list of points: the White Tyrant. There is very little on this guy, but passing research may reveal:

- The White Tyrant was once a dominating force on Barogate and Anula, treading seemingly random paths of destruction, leaving many cities and towns destroyed in its wake. It is said the white tyrant reigned across the continents for over 7500 years - three quarters a decamillennium. But, the Ancient Eresonians eventually lead to its crumble and the town of Tramon was founded to commemorate one of the heroes crucial taking down this beast.

The exact story of the White Tyrant is more hidden than books, which can only give you so much.

# The Word of Mouth
Lorekeepers and Caedyll tell ancient stories passed down through millennia. Luckily for any party seeking information on events that occured millennia ago, they are still around and quite happy to share what they have.

"The stone that sleeps, the mountain that dreams, whispers of the old world echo". An ancient mantra kept by followers of Adnabyd that reminds Caedyll use to remember the origins of the White Tyrant in Gordolmonre, saying that one day the mountain crumbled and the White Tyrant awoke and began to wander Kyenel crushing the forest as it went. It then, as is common knowledge, travelled elsewhere.

"When the White Tyrant falls, Shildo shall rise." A little motif kept by Caedyll since before the White Tyrant's apparent demise sometime between 1600 and 1800. It's apparently true because the White Tyrant stands for death itself, whereas Shildo stands for life.

"Life lies in death, and death in stone; be kind to the cradle from which things are born". A mantra that Caedyll use to remember the role of life and death caused by the White Tyrant, how eventually its death required a sacrifice of something else. Maybe an egg?

"In the mirror's egg, life turns twice; break not the shell nor tempt its vice." a very cryptic mantra that refers to a legend that there's a nest and an egg at the top of the Spire that was used to kill the White Tyrant.

"Listen not to the voice of stone, for what it says is never alone" again a cryptic mentre that many caedyll will insist is about not listening to narcissists or people who don't listen back because of their anterior motives. Alternatively, its about the White Tyrant's voice carrying not only rage, but destruction. Or whatever.

"The tyrant danced, the world turned pale. Winds carried its mighty hail". Comes with a bit of a silly dance that involves lots of jumping. It's supposed to symbolise the White Tyrant's demise using the very essence of nature itself, representing trees tying it down as the wind crumbles it to pieces.

"The feather falls, the stone obeys; heed not the voice but the wind that sways". A mantra that reminds druids of the following stories: Pydred, represented by the entropy of a falling leaf, went head-to-head with the White Tyrant and destroyed it with the corrosive nature of wind. Among other stories, its contradictory with the narrative that the Dolom Empire's efforts took down the White Tyrant.

"One tear of the Tyrant fell, where the roots of the world swell". This mantra actually refers to Raikeor, and reminds Caedyll of the first time a great victory was won against the White Tyrant in northern Ereso, it shed a tear that grew into Ereso's northern forest.

Aside from the silly Caedyll, people generally say that the Dolom Republic and empire used the stone they did *because* of its symbolic nature as the stone which the White Tyrant is made out of. It symbolised strength and perseverance. Why beat them if you can join them sort of attitude. 

There is also the related story of the bird that lives on top of the Spire. They say that long ago, there was a mythical bird that only ever lay one egg for itself. When it died, the embryo in this egg would grow and become the next incarnation of the bird. However, the egg was smashed by a wizard and used to defeat a great evil, sacrificing an innocent life to take one that was causing the death of many other innocents.
