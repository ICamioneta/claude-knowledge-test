Magic in Wastelife isn’t called magic. This is because, to the people in the world, the things we would call magic are just facts of life, each with well explored and still expanding backbones of knowledge based upon the scientific method. The regular person wouldn’t commonly call the phenomena of Hidosis ‘magic’ either, as much as the common person wouldn’t call a fire detector ‘magic’ just because they don’t fully understand the types of radiation, particle decay, or the ways the fire detector detects radiation particles.

# Dictionary

Because of the limitations of the English language and because I also want to actually communicate ideas, here’s a list of terms I made up, definitions, and synonyms.

- Hidol / Ether: The medium that permeates everywhere that contains the energy left over from the early days of the universe.
- Hidosis / Ethermancy / Magic: the process and phenomenon of using the Hidol as a source of energy or power.
  - Magery: a branch of Hidosis that focuses on using the reactive property of torfol
  - Wizardry: a branch of Hidosis that focuses on using the adaptive property of torfol
  - Moltery: a branch of Hidosis that focuses on using the expansive property of torfol. It is commonly lumped in with magery as the method is similar if not indistinct from casting in magery. 
- Hidist: One who follows or practices hidosis. Almost never used in favour of specifying the nouns it envelops.
  - Mage: One who practices hidosis by casting spells
  - Wizard: one who practices hidosis by imbuing enchantments.
  - Molter: one who practices hidosis by casting gemstones
- Hidologist: One who dedicates to the field of scientific hidology.
- Torfol / Tyrfal / field: a mass occupying the hidol that surrounds living things.
- Manifest: The product of a hidosis, the manifestation of the hidol's energy in a realm.
- Spell: a phenomenon that results from the interaction of two distinct torfol, causing a change in the physical and mental state of matter.
- Enchantment / Enchant (n): the change in the properties or functions of non-living objects done by means of imbuing.
- Casting / Clapping: the process of causing the interaction of two distinct torfol by manipulating the density of the hidol causing the boundaries of two torfol to overlap, usually done with common act of clapping.
- Imbue / enchant (v): the process of manipulating the planar direction of one’s own torfol and implanting that change onto a non-living object to cause it to be enchanted.

# Classical Hidol Theory

Classical hidol theory states that Every Living Thing has a Torfol. Through this, it is understood that the torfol is this sort of pool of energy that we can use to draw from and cast or imbue. This is common knowledge in Wastelife, and often offered as a layperson's explanation to hidosis.

# Hidol

Torfol exist in a medium called the Hidol. The Hidol is a medium that is everywhere and has a density. It is practically impossible to remove from any area. It is possible to make it less dense, or more dense. Density affects the strength of manifestation.

The density of a region of Hidol is difficult to measure scientifically. Skilled wizards often can ‘feel’ the density of the Hidol. More scientific measurements involve testing the strength of hidosis, but strength is also affected by skill.

# Torfol

A Torfol is a mass within the Hidol that each independent living thing has. This mass has a border that usually completely surrounds the living thing, but can be pushed, squashed, and squeezed around a centre point by interacting with other torfol. 

Torfol are vectors, with a magnitude and direction. Their mass is their magnitude. Their direction is reffered to as their Planar dimensions. The properties of the Planar strongly affects how hidosis is performed with the torfol.

Generally speaking, torfol that are of different directions repel each other. These two torfol are said to be repulsive to each other. It is possible to get two torfol of different directions to overlap, and as we’ll get into later, this causes a reaction.

"Zeroed" torfol are those of larger sentient creatures. These torfol have a shared planar and encapsulate the torfol produced by that creature's organs.

# Planar Theory

A sound understanding of Planar Theory is not required to make use of its application. It is generally abstracted as such: the Planar is a collection of vectors called formants. In spell casting, a set of twelve formants determine the resulting spell. In moltery, the same set determines catalysts for gemstones, and a number of other formants determine the golem. In enchanting, one multi-dimensional formant is fine tuned by the enchanter. 

# Magery

A Clap describes the act of a person bringing their hands together in order to produce a sound. This sound is created by the air being expelled from our hands as our hands rapidly create a region of high pressure between them. When two torfol are placed in this high differential position, they may overlap. This overlap causes a reaction, converting much of the torfol's energy into a manifestation. The twelve formants determine the exact matter of the manifestation and a mage's skill will determine its shape and direction.

Claps often require a good mix of skill, delicacy, and precision depending on the strength of the torfol involved. Weaker torfol often have lots of leniency in how exact they must be performed, while with stronger torfol the mage must be careful, else the reaction may fail or backfire. There is a level of skill involved in directing the effect of the reaction as well.

People who clap often wear insulative gloves for several reasons. The first is to avoid the produced effect directly harming the skin. The second is to avoid reacting with the skin. 

Although the name "clap" makes it seem biological, mechanical methods of inducing reactions have been devised and are somewhat effective at producing the lenient low-powered reactions. The main functional weapons are the SPCC, the Springburst Pneumaflux Confluence Catalyst, made by master craftsmen in Brwrd and the Archetech Repeater Copy made by machinists in the Domuz Republic. 

## Ingredients

The items which hold the torfol used in a clap are called ingredients. They are alive or once-alive things that have a torfol strong enough to produce a spell through a clap. Although all living things have a torfol, the vast majority of living things do not have a torfol significant enough to produce a spell. Ingredients generally fall into the following categories:

- Plant parts. These are roots, stems, flowers, or seeds of plants with a strong torfol. Usually, plants will have parts that are vestigial to the torfol. These parts can be cut off for ease of storage without seriously affecting torfol strength. 
- Organs. Larger conscious creatures have several torfol. There is the zeroed torfol and there is the individual torfol of their organs. Organs that frequently have strong torfol are livers, poison sacs or toxin sacs, thyroids, nematocysts, and statocysts. 
- Fungus. Similar to plants, often a specific part of an individual species of fungus will produce a desirable torfol, and other parts can be discarded without compromising its strength.
- Small creatures. Critters like bugs and any tiny critters that don't have a zeroed torfol can still have strong non-zeroed torfol. These can be flies or ants or other small species.

**Farming**

Ingredient farming is common. Hidol-sensitive plants are those with strong torfol, and they will have a certain density they grow well in. 

Modern ingredient farming methods to certain degrees decrease the density of the hidol where plants grow. It is generally true that farm-grown ingredients aren't as potent as naturally grown ingredients, however a mass farm in a place of high hidol density will likely produce farmed ingredients as potent as wild ones. 

The phenomena of hidol density isn't well understood, but there are areas predisposed to a higher density. Rivers and forests, usually, have high Hidol densities. Certain random areas also receive higher densities, such as around Ereso's Spire. Areas of density are not often static, and density gradually shifts over periods of months.

When a part of the hidol is made less dense through ingredient farming, the density often recovers within weeks of no growth. The main limitation is that if all ingredients grow at once, the hidol density may be reduced enough to stunt growth.

Ingredients with powerful Torfol grow in conditions that makes it difficult to impossible to farm them. This is especially climate restrictions.

# Moltery

Contrary to classical Hidol theory, many well-formed gemstones have torfol. The planar properties of gemstone's torfol are unique. Often, they will react to certain ingredient formants in a spell, and transform into a golem. The golem's abilities and shapes depend on the gemstone itself. The behaviour of the golem depends on the formants of the ingredient that was clapped with it. 

Gemstones are often clapped with two other ingredients: one that will cause the golem to manifest and another that will tune its behaviour.

# Wizardry

Enchanting is a phenomena where someone consciously alters their planar from its default attributes to a different set of attributes. Through a process known as the Sping movement, the wizard can leave some torfol residue on an object as their planar rapidly returns to its default attributes. This torfol residue utilises its own strength in some steady manifestation.

Teaching and learning enchantments requires no knowledge of the hidol at all. Often, student wizards are told to 'visualise and rotate a ball'. The visualised rotations change the planar attributes of their zeroed torfol. Enchanting requires mental effort and practice to perform consistently.

The primary difficulty of enchantments is making their visualised rotation correlate with actual planar manipulation, and directing torfol residue created by the Sping movement.

It happens that simpler enchantments require less mental effort and precision, while difficult enchantments are often hard for one of several reasons. These have their basis in planar theory:

- Enchantment effects further away from the default planar require more mental effort to reach. They tend to be more potent, useful, or stronger.
- More specific enchantment effects need lots of precision in reaching. It is common to miss precise enchants and instead enchant something with a more general enchantment.
- Effects are inherently lacking potency, and require larger torfol masses to work properly. 

When considering planar theory and the idea of vector constraints on planar space, it is theoretically possible for a wizard to shift the vector of their torfol beyond the traditional adaptive category and into undefined, reactive, or expansive category spaces. 

## Constraints of Enchantments

Apart from the skill-based constraints, there is the constraint that enchantment can only be done to objects without a torfol. This is because the pre-existing torfol will repel any torfol residue from the enchant.

Enchantment can only occur on objects within the zeroed torfol. Residue cannot be "flung" to stick onto an object: the object must already be there for the residue to attach to.

In general, the torfol extends a little further than the skin, and unpractised wizard can enchant thing in their hands.

Enchantments are inherently tied to the enchanted object. Effect potency is also affected by the size of the enchanted object. In generalities, the standard wizard ring is a good mix of potency and usability. 

Another constraint is enchantment wear. The vast majority of enchantments have a 'use-by' date, as their effect's potency slowly lowers and completely fades usually in matters of hours. There are specific enchantments that maximise duration, for example many health-based potion enchants can last a week or more. In general, however, enchantments are done on-demand.

There are permanent enchantments. They are incredibly difficult to do, and are often a whole project for a Wizard. They require the knowledge and practice of creating a self-sustaining energy loop in the enchant's torfol residue. Even these are not really permanent. Ways to remove a "permanent" enchant include extreme environments, unstable hidol densities, and purposeful removal of the torfol residue.
