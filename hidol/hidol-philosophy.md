### Historical Summary of Classical Hidol Theory

The philosophical implications of the Hidol and the Torfol are untouched by Hidology and are generally regarded to be part of philosophical study. But, I don't have a separate "philosophy in Wastelife" document (yet) so I have to detail the philosophies surrounding the Hidol and Torfol here.

In classical hidol theory (before the scientific method was properly formalised and applied to the Hidol) it's a stated assumption that every living thing has a torfol, and by transience everything with a torfol must be living. Classical hidol theory dates back to the first organised exploration of the Hidol by the Nao institute. Founded in -3100, this was the first organised group of people dedicated to studying Hidosis. Although initially devoted to the catalogue and distribution of hidosis practices it evolved into an exploratory organisation by -2600. A large part of this exploration was developing the earliest theoretical ideas of Hidosis.

Hidosis and Hidol Theory would get significant development during the lead-up to Techia uniting Shildo in 0. The discovery of the Hidol was attributed to Techia organisations around -1000. The existence of the Hidol was known to the organisation, as it had asked the God of Knowledge much on theory during its first few centuries of exploration, but this was its first proper discovery by wizards. 

Discovering the Hidol was done by "feeling" it - a process that is still done by wizards and mages today. This additional sense is largely unconsidered by the conscious self although it is known to affect subconscious activity. Someone can only attain a conscious perception of this sense with training and increasing their torfol's mass. This sense, and the idea of the torfol in whole, was integral to the discovery of the Hidol.

A bunch of -5th century wizards ran around feeling the torfols of things, and they determined that all living things had a torfol. This was essentially the founding knowledge of classical hidol theory, separating it from the completely archaic and unfounded ideas of the mythic era (which are scarcely documented and entirely discarded by the public). This generalised observation massively effected existential philosophy, where many philosophisers trusted the observations of the classical hidists and came to empirically define life as anything that had a torfol. 

### Modern philosophical connections of life and the torfol

Modern conceptions of the Torfol have muddied the waters still. There are currently no scientific instruments of precisely measuring the torfol mass of anything. This data is still based on a person's perception. But, advances in measuring tools in other fields such as the development of the microscope have shown micro-organisms that may or may not actually have a torfol.

Methods of actually measuring torfol mass are very poor. There are enchantments that glow when inside a torfol, and have certain light intensities that depend on the size of the object enchanted (there are standards established by the Redel institute) but these are completely incapable of measuring torfol on a small level. It is generally accepted that most autonomous things smaller than 25 milligrams don't actually have a torfol. This is about the size of a [Paedocypris](https://www.fishi-pedia.com/fishes/paedocypris-progenetica). This is still inconsistent, as it is known that some creatures smaller than this have useful reactive torfol.

To summarise, the mantra of classical hidol theory, "Every living thing has a Torfol, and everything with a Torfol is living", has been completely broken down by modern scientists and this has reflected on modern philosophy. Only the most stubborn philosophers insist that the Torfol is a definitive measurement of life, and that creatures such as ants aren't alive. 

### Consciousness

What is still vehemently unclear in philosophy is the boundaries of consciousness. Classical hidol theory wagered that enchantments by adaptive torfol were the capabilities of conscious minds, and since only people are capable of enchanting things then only we are truly conscious. Unfortunately for classical Hidists, modern scientific hidology has completely redefined "adaptive torfol" into a classification of torfol based on vector boundaries. For adaptive torfol specifically, they all happen to point in the same direction, and this is defined as 0, 0. The main property that actually separates adaptive torfol is the ability for a conscious mind to shift its angle, and for the resulting 'snap' back to its original position and concentration of the resulting energy into a particular, non-living (meaning without torfol) object. This produces the phenomena of enchantment.

As previously mentioned, classical Hidol theory sees the three types of torfol as completely separate classifications, while modern Hidology sees these types as boundary classifications of the torfol vector. The classical understanding of categories placed emphasis on the adaptive torfol's ability to cause enchantment, whereas modern Hidology understands this as a thing that can be done by conscious minds. Classical philosophers hinging their understanding of consciousness on classical hidol theory would link personhood and therefore consciousness directly towards the ability to cause enchantments. But, under modern Hidol theory, we have discovered that many creatures have adaptive torfol and are theoretically capable of causing enchantment but often lack the intelligence to do so.

Much like the presence of a torfol, the circumstances that cause an adaptive torfol are largely unknown, but it is certain that adaptive torfol are made for creatures with several smaller organisms that each create their own torfol. This is a generally loose and untested theory. The idea is that these larger organisms, created out of several smaller organs, get adaptive torfol.

What size creatures that get adaptive torfol is somewhat consistent within species classifications but inconsistent between them. The smallest creatures with some form of adaptive torfol are around the size of small birds or mice. These adaptive torfol usually never have a mass large enough to cause enchantments. They usually have enough mass when creatures reach around cat-size, albeit theoretically very weak enchantments. Pig-sized creatures are generally large enough to cause significant enchantments. 

Creatures that have adaptive torfol always have a chance of a "creature stone" forming on the focal point of their adaptive torfol. Creature stones are unique in that they have their own reactive torfol, suggesting they are organs of some sort. They are also the focal point for a creature's adaptive torfol, suggesting that they as an organ are inherently linked to the adaptive torfol. Creature stones are often simply not found in creatures with adaptive torfol, and there is no correlation between any physical abilities and the presence of a creature stone. 

In summary, philosophies of consciousness based in the classical hidol theory of the adaptive torfol are unfounded in scientific reality, and philosophers exploring consciousness through the lens of torfol have to struggle with how intelligent small mammal species gain adaptive torfol as they grow up.

### Death and the Torfol

Another philosophical aspect influenced by the hidol is death. Before scientific hidology, ties between death and the torfol were unfounded in the ideas of classical hidol theory. Distinguishing between adaptive and reactive torfol when physically feeling for them was difficult, so knowledge that the adaptive torfol completely disappeared on death was not popular or common. Knowledge that reactive torfol generally deteriorated as the cells of organisms died was known, but this was rarely philosophically tied to death.

However, modern hidology discovers the phenomenon of the adaptive torfol rapidly disappearing as the functions of conscious life cease. Some modern philosophies tie the moment of death and the moment the adaptive torfol is completely gone. The exact moment of this is unknown and undetectable without actually killing a creature, and since the adaptive torfol would attach itself to the inside of the creature, to measure its size close to death you would have to essentially kill a creature trying to detect the remainder of its adaptive torfol. 

But one thing is clear: things like an arrow to the brain, rapid blood loss, hearts stopping instantly, cause the adaptive torfol to lose mass and completely disappear within seconds.

There is also the concept of the creature stone as the focal point of an adaptive torfol. Generally speaking, a procedure that would extract a creature stone from a creature would kill it, but certain procedures for some creatures do exist that can extract the stone from a creature without killing it. The act of removing the stone from the body causes unparalleled pain to a creature, even when it is detached from the rest of the body's lining and otherwise smoothly extracted. But, if the injuries sustained from the procedure doesn't kill the creature they will survive normally as if they never had a creature stone.
