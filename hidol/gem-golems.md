# Information Collection Section
There are common families of gemstone. These are shown in the list below.

- **Beryl:** beryllium aluminium cyclosilicate. Hexagonal crystals can be up to several meters in size, but terminated crystals are relatively rare. Pure Beryl is colourless. It is frequently  tinted by impurities: possible colours are Green, blue, yellow, and Red (The rarest) Beryl can also be black in colour.
  - **Aquamarine / Maxixe:** Blue or Cyan variety of beryl, occurs at most localities which yield ordinary beryl. Green-yellow beryl is sometimes called *Chrysolite Aquamarine*. Deep blue version of aquamarine is called Maxixe. Maxixe de-colours due to light and heat. This may be due to ion transfer as seen below.
    - **Aquamarine** is attributed to Fe<sup>2+</sup> ions.
    - **Chrysolite Aquamarine** is attributed to Fe<sup>3+</sup> ions.
	- **Maxixe** is attributed to both Fe<sup>2+</sup> and Fe<sup>3+</sup> ions.
  - **Emerald:** Very breakable, highly included. It’s very rare and valuable. Its colour is attributed to around 2% chromium and sometimes vanadium within its beryl surface.
  - **Golden beryl / Heliodor:** a form of beryl that’s in a colour range from pale yellow to brilliant gold. Heliodor comes from a Greek word meaning sun gift. It generally has very few flaws. Its colour can be attributed to Fe<sup>3+</sup> ions.
  - **Goshenite:** This is colourless Beryl. The name derives from the location it was found in Massachusetts. Its value is generally low, but it can be coloured yellow, green, pink, blue, and in intermediate colours by irradiation. The colour depends on the content of Calcium(pale yellow), Scandium(yellowish), Titanium(darker), Vanadium(any depending on state), Iron(pink probably), and Cobalt(blue) impurities.
  - **Morganite:** pink beryl is a rare light pink to rose coloured beryl. Orange/yellow varieties also exist and the colour banding is common. Heating it removes patches of yellow and can be irradiated to improve its colour. The colour is attributed to Mn<sup>+2</sup> ions.
  - **Red Beryl / Bixbite**. A form of red beryl. Its name comes from the name of a mineralogist. It’s very rare and has only been seen in a handful of locations. While gem beryls are found in pegmatites and certain metamorphic stones, red beryl occurs in topaz-bearing rhyolites. Its colour can be attributed to Mn3+ ions.
- **Quartz:** Quartz is a hard crystal composed of silicon dioxide. The atoms are linked in a continuous framework of SiO<sub>4</sub> tetrahedra with each oxygen being shared between two tetrahedra. It has two different varieties of varieties. The first variety is their microstructure variety, which are just the varieties according to the microstructure of the gemstone. The other variety is the colour variety, which are the varieties according to the actual colour. I will first list the microstructures.
  - **Herkimer Diamond:** Not actually a diamond, but a colourless quartz that’s double terminated.
  - **Rock Crystal:** Just a colourless transparent quartz.
  - **Amethyst:** Amethyst is a light lavender to a dark purple coloured quartz. It does not have cleavage. Its colour comes from irradiation, impurities of iron, and sometimes transition metals and other trace elements. It’s not really that precious, but collectors look for especially deep purples.
  - **Citrine:** The colour of this gem ranges from pale yellow to brown. This colour is attributed to a sub microscopic distribution of colloidal ferric hydroxide impurities. Natural citrines are rare, most commercial ones are just heated amethysts or smoky quartes. The gem has a cloudy or smoky appearance. They are visually identical to yellow topaz, but topaz is harder.
  - **Ametrine / Trystine:** This gemstone is actually a mix of amethyst and citrine. It has zones of purple and zones of yellow or orange. The differing colour zones are due to differing oxidation states of iron within. The citrine parts are oxidized. This happens due to a temperature gradient during its formation. You can also use beta radiation to make the amethyst from citrine. It’s only mined naturally from one location.
  - **Rose Quartz:** This quartz ranges from pale pink to rose red. This is attributed to trace amounts of titanium, iron, or manganese. Some rose quartz contains microscopic rutile needles that produces asterisms, which are star shaped light reflections on the gem. There is another rare type of pink quartz that gets its colour from phosphate or aluminium.
  - **Calcedony:** This is a cryptocrystalline form of silica composed of fine intergrowths of quartz and moganite. Quartz is triagonal, and moganite is monocilic. This gem is its own family and will get its spot. Also, moganite, which takes part in the forming of this rock, is a polymorph or quartz.
    - **Agate** is a common rock made from Calcedony and Quarts. So it’s really made from moganite, quartz, and quartz. It has curved bands.
    - **Onyx** is a parallel banded variety of Calcedony. Onyx is also used to describe parallel banded varieties of other gems. 
    - **Carnelian** is a further form is calcedony to be described later. So is Sard.
  - **Aventurine:** Commonly green quartz that’s translucent to opaque. It also comes in the colours orange, brown, yellow, blue, or grey. Chrome bearing fuchsite gives it the silvery green or blue sheen. Oranges and browns are attributed to hematite or goethite.
    - **Adventurine Feldspar** is Sunstone and can be confused with orange and red Adventurine Quartzite.
  - **Jasper:** A rock made of quartz and / or chalcedony. It is opaque and red, yellow, brown, or green in colour. The red is attributed to Fe<sup>3+</sup> ions. It’s not that rare.
  - **Milky Quartz:** This is the traditional view of Quartz, white fine rock. The white is caused by fluid inclusions trapped during crystal formation, making it pretty opaque.
  - **Smoky Quartz:** A translucent quartz usually brownish grey. The smoky colour is attributed to free silicon formed by natural irradiation.
    - **Morion** is a very dark brown to black opaque variety. This word is also used by several languages to describe smoky quartz as a whole.
    - **Cairngorm** is a smoky yellow-brown version found in Scotland.
  - **Tiger’s Eye:** another member of quartz with a golden to red-brown colour. It and hawk’s eye get their appearance from parallel intergrowth of quartz crystals and altered Amphibole fibres.
  - **Prasiolite:** A green quartz. Very rare and precious. Can be artificially produced by heat treating amethysts. Most amethysts will turn to citrine when heated but some will turn green and become prasiolite. Ionising radiation also helps.
  - **Rutilated Quartz:** A variety of quartz which contains needle-like inclusions made of rutile, which is titanium dioxide. The most common appearance is golden, but silver, copper red, or deep black are other appearances it can take.
  - **Dumortierite Quartz:** Contains dumortierite (aluminium boro-silicate). Makes standard quartz have silky splotches of blue. Shades of purple or grey can also be present. Blue Quartz is a minor gemstone. 
- **Garnet:** This crystal group all contain different chemical compositions but share similar physical properties and crystal forms. It has different species: pyrope, almandine, spessartine, grossular(varieties of which are hessonite and tsavorite), uvarovite and andradite. There are plenty of garnet group end member species. Each of them has a general formula X<sub>3</sub>Y<sub>2</sub>(SiO<sub>4</sub>)<sub>3</sub>
  - <b>Almandine Fe<sub>3</sub>Al<sub>2</sub>(SiO<sub>4</sub>)<sub>3</sub>:</b> Almandine is a red gem in the Pyralspite group, which all have aluminium in the Y site. These stones are deep red, and are often called “precious garnet”. Almandine occurs in metamorphic rocks associated with minerals such as staurolite, kyanite, andalusite, and others.
  - <b>Pyrope Mg<sub>3</sub>Al<sub>2</sub>(SiO<sub>4</sub>)<sub>3</sub>:</b> Pyrope are deep red to black in colour, and in the formula the magnesium can be replaced in part by calcium and ferrous iron. 
  - <b>Spessartine Mn<sub>3</sub>Al<sub>2</sub>(SiO<sub>4</sub>)<sub>3</sub>:</b> Spessartine can be orange-yellow (as seen in Madagascar) or violet reds which are found in rhyolites.
    - **Pyrope-spessartine**: Blue pyrope-spessartine garnets exist. They change from a blue-green hue to purple depending on the colour temperature of viewing light as a result of high amounts of vanadium.
  - <b>Andradite Ca<sub>3</sub>Fe<sub>2</sub>(SiO<sub>4</sub>)<sub>3</sub>:</b> Andradite is a garnet of variable composition which may be red, yellow, brown, green, or black. It is in the Ugrandite group, which has calcium in the X site. It is found in deep-seated igneous rocks like syenite.
    - **Demantoid** is the green variety of Andradite
    - **Melanite** is the black variety of Andradite
    - **Topazolite** is the yellow or green variety of andrite.
  - <b>Grossular Ca<sub>3</sub>Al<sub>2</sub>(SiO<sub>4</sub>)<sub>3</sub>:</b> The calcium in this formula may in part be replaced by ferrous iron and the aluminium by ferric iron. This garnet comes in shades of cinnamon brown, red, and yellow.
  - <b>Uvarovite Ca<sub>3</sub>Cr<sub>2</sub>(SiO<sub>4</sub>)<sub>3</sub>:</b> This is a rare garnet that is bright green in colour, found as small crystals associated with chromite in peridotite, serpentinite, and kimberlites.
  - Of note are the less common species of garnet, such as Goldmanite, Kimzeyite, Morimotoite, Schorlomite, Hydrogrossular, Hibschite, Katoite, Knorringite, Majorite, and Calderite. The Wikipedia article on Garnets will be very useful if you’re looking for new gems to golem.
  - <b>Knorringite Mg<sub>3</sub>Cr<sub>2</sub>(SiO<sub>4</sub>)<sub>3</sub>:</b> Pure endmember knorringite never occurs in nature. Pyrope rich in the knorringite is only formed under high pressure and is often found in kimberlites. It is used as an indicator mineral in the search for diamonds.
- **Chalcedony:** Chalcedony is a crystal formed of both quartz and morganite. It is related to the Quartz family, but not quite.
  - **Agate:** Agate is a chalcedony characterised by its colour patterns, which are curved or angular bandings of orange-brown and white.
    - **Jasper** is a common reference to opaque varieties of agate.
    - **Fire Agate** is a type of agate that shows iridescent phenomena on a brown background. It contains layered microscopic particles of iron that cause this effect. It can even come in a wide range of colours like red, orange, yellow, green, blue, and purple.
    - **Iris Agate** shows exceptional iridescence when light is shone through the stone.
    - **Onyx** is a variant of agate with black and white banding.

# Making Gem Golems with family in mind
So now I’ve got to make all of these relations mean something. I’ve got to make it make sense in the sense of minerals and chemical formulae. Inclusions have to mean something on a general level. Of course, the colour of a gem golem depends on the gem it came from, but so does the form, and that’s a little complex.
## Beryls
Beryls seem quite rare or traditionally high-value gems. Because of this, I will probably make them all powerful. It’s a weak reference but perhaps the gem golem form should be a six-legged bastion. It sorta makes sense.
### Aquamarine
Regular blue aquamarine is due to +2 iron ions. This is also the most common aquamarine. The +2 iron ion aquamarines can be some sort of standard for all beryl six-legged bastions. The Aquamarine Gem Golem can be about 4 meters in diameter.

There’s also Aquamarine’s other versions. Maxixe is the richer blue, therefore it makes sense that it’s just a stronger version of Aquamarine. Chrysolite aquamarine, then, would be a weaker form of aquamarine. As an extra mechanic, Aquamarines can decolour from Maxixe to Aquamarine to Chrysolite Aquamarine due to light or heat, and since lots of heat is given during a golem transformation, maxixe slowly becomes chrysolite the more you use it.
### Emerald
This beryl is because of around 2% chromium and sometimes vanadium. It is traditionally more precious, so it’s golem will have a diameter of 3 meters but it will be much more powerful. Emerald is also called “Very breakable”, meaning that it’s golem will be a glass cannon.
### Heliodor
This is a golden beryl that does not have many imperfections. It’s attributed to +3 iron ions which gives it a relation to chrysolite aquamarine. It should be roughly the same, but with its slightly different nature it will use different types of attacks, and because it does not have many imperfections it should be slightly more durable than aquamarine.
### Goshenite
Goshenite is colourless beryl, but can be coloured depending on material imperfections within. The mineral impurities will change how the golem looks or functions. In general, a Goshenite golem shares the same form and is about 3.5 meters in diameter. Calcium-rich Goshenite golems (pale-yellow) are somewhat regular and weaker. Scandium-rich Goshenite golems (yellowish) are also not rare but do have improved capabilities over calcium golems. Cobalt-rich golems (blue) have limited magical effects and aren’t quite as strong as iron-rich golems. Iron-rich golems (pink) are a good and sturdy golem, as reliable as its richest mineral. Vanadium-rich golems (anything really) which has unique merits that place it above titanium in certain situations. Titanium-rich golems (Dark colours) are just hardy tanks with a fairly decent damaging nature.
### Morganite
Morganite is actually the second most expensive non-diamond stone (so the third overall) Morganite is the rarest of the beryl. Therefore, it is the strongest beryl Golem. Despite this, its gem golem is small. Only a meter and a bit in diameter, following the typical beryl six-legged object. It is strong. It is hardy, and moves quickly. Bludgeoning force alone, it absolutely slays. Not to mention the several magical effects it can give.
### Bixbyte
Whatever was said about Morganite, Bixbyte completely shadows it. Red Beryl, which is Bixbyte’s more ‘scientific’ name, is so rare the deposits and rarity section on Wikipedia can’t stop but compare it to the rarest materials in existence. It can also come in tints of strawberry, bright ruby, cherry, and orange. In the gem golem world, the Bixbyte golem might just be the strongest of them all. It dwarfs all of the other beryl golems in its diameter. A brilliant 10 meters in diameter and towering at that, there are few Bixbyte gems in circulation. When cast, it has taken down entire armies on its own. Stopped huge threats to entire civilisations by its mere threatening presence.
## Quartzes
So in the quartzes is amethyst, which I already gave a floating humanoid shape to. I’m going to roll back on that decision, as it would mean that most if not all of the quartzes would have to be humanoid. There’s too much variety in this gem to let that happen, so instead humanoid figures are going to be given to the Garnets. Quartz doesn’t really have a prominent and consistent form, but many resemble familiar creatures.
### Rock Crystal
Regular old quartz, or Rock Crystal, is a pretty standard and low-level gem golem. It takes the form of a pretty flimsy four-legged object without many other features to it but it’s four legs, each of which have two segments and a hinge connection to the main body. This creature stands about 1.2 meters tall, although this varies. It’s not particularly capable of much. It makes a nice field table, if you cast it right.

The Herkimer diamond is basically just regular quartz but a bit cleaner because it’s double terminated. Casting these regular quartzes changes nothing about the essence of the golem, but now it can bend its knees both ways, and runs backwards if it deems that a good choice.
### Rutilated Rock Crystal
Regular old quartz, but with needle-like rutile visible within. As an attack, it might expel these inner rutile spikes. It can only do this so many times before it is no longer rutilated.
### Dumortierite Rock Crystal
Dumortierite Quartz is similar to Rutilated Quartz in that it is almost identical to the Rock Crystal with a single spin. The inclusion of the blue dumortierite makes the golem slightly hardier, and gives it access to a very weak degree of touch magic effects.
### Amethyst
A common gemstone, nothing too fancy about it. It’s a lot better than regular old quartz though. Amethyst golems manifest themselves as a sort of floating star-like object, like a high-level angel in Christian mythology, the “be not afraid” types. Amethyst gems aren’t always roundish. They are only ever about a meter and a half tall They are good at spike magics.
### Citrine
This gem is quite rare when synthetic creation is not an option. Citrines are also very closely related to amethysts. In the same vain, their golems are floating objects. Instead of being star-like, they are more rounded and might even be equated to the likeness of a bee when they are especially pure. Citrine golems tend to use more heat-oriented magic.
### Ametrine
If an amethyst golem is one end of a spectrum, and citrine the other, a perfect Ametrine would be somewhere in the middle. To produce an amethyst golem from citrine, you expose it to beta radiation. To produce a citrine from an amethyst, you heat it up. Ametrine are most forms intermediary, or forms where one half of the gem is clearly citrine and another amethyst. Ametrine are mixtures of this spiky star-like amethyst and the more rounded bee-like citrine, having rounded spikes or a rounded core with spikes coming of it. This exact form varies. Regular ametrines are generally weaker than the pure forms of Amethyst and Citrine, but a perfectly made ametrine is a formidable beast. The golem of a perfect Ametrine is a floating perfect circular halo with spikes pointing towards the centre and outwards from this smooth halo. The perfect Ametrine is a good user of spike and fire magics.
### Rose Quartz
Rose Quartz is like rock quartz, except it includes certain imperfections of strong metals. It is seen as a direct upgrade from the otherwise mediocre Rock Crystal golem. The golem is of a similar shape to rock quartz, an uninteresting body with four limbs on each corner. Where Rose Quartz differs is that this body is given some shape, and a head-like protrusion. During combat, it is also able to stand on two of its identical leg protrusions effectively freeing up the other two to be used more effectively as bludgeoning devices. It notably dabbles with fire effects, but these are mostly upon touch. The Rose Quartz golem is significantly taller too, while the rock quartz golem is a mere 1.2 meters tall on all fours, the Rose Quartz golem stands at 1.5 meters. 

A Rutilated Rose Quartz has asterisms on its body. It can also use a rutile spike attack, but it can only do this so many times before it is no longer rutilated.

Pink Quartz is another gem whose golem is incredibly similar. A pink quartz golem is significantly more magical than the regular Rose Quartz golem, as it uses projectile fire effects. It is also significantly more durable. One notable thing about pink quartz is that it discolours in the sun. After a certain amount of discolouration, pink quartz turns into Rock Crystal.
### Adventurine (Advanturine Quartzite)
Adventurine isn’t special unfortunately. Its most common colour is green, and these regular adventurine golems manifest themselves as mobile plants. They look a lot like small shrubs when cast, and often don’t like moving. They exhibit no physical attacks but do perform incredibly weak air and wind attacks.

If the adventurine gem contains chrome fuchsite, another rock, it ends up being a little more stable and stronger, resisting receding from natural elements just that little bit longer.

Orangey or brownish Adventurine gems that contain either hematite or goethite still become this shrub-like form, but notably without the inclusion of formations that resemble leaves. These ironey adventurines are a lot stronger and resist a lot more, but tend to do even less. They will, however, rapidly expand one of their spiky branches to impale anything it views as an enemy if it comes close enough.
### Jasper (Jasper Quartzite)
Jasper is just a common gem. It’s not fancy. Its colour is due to +3 iron ions. Jasper golems appear as a cuboid shape with fairly secure dimensions: 1 meter tall, 0.4 meters wide, and 1.6 meters deep. This quartzite golem has 5 limbs that act as feet, three on the left and 2 on the right. On its front, there are interesting formations that make a very odd face. It tends to use its fifth leg to attack with, as it is on the front and out of place. It does dabble with magic effects.
### White Quartz (Milky Quartz)
Milky Quartz, or the traditional white rock as seen in Minecraft does actually manifest as a golem. The golem manifests as several different boid-like particles that all act as one, but have distinct individual movements. These aren’t that strong but in large quantities have known to shred and hurt at higher aggressive levels.
### Dark Quartz (Smoky Quartz)
Dark Quartz is less traditional quartz that’s just darker really. Dark Quartz manifests itself as a floating cone-like shape with many crystalline faces. The size of this golem changes with the size of the sample used, as is somewhat usual. The Dark Quartz golem acts most usefully as a roadblock but does do some magical effects.

Morion is a darker variant of Dark Quartz that’s usually more magically powerful. That’ it, really.

Cairngorn is more yellow-brown variant of Dark Quartz. It prefers to manifest with four limbs on the top of its downwards facing cone. It uses these limbs to attack physically, but since it’s also a good magic user these limbs are practically useless.
### Tiger’s Eye
Tiger’s eye is a very basic but cool-looking gemstone. Tiger’s Eye manifests as the basic Quartzite structure of a golem, that being the basic body and four limbs. It’s limbs, however, are much girthier and stronger than the basic quartzite. Also, it has a head, which consists mostly of a large crocodile-like mouth that it uses as a biting attack. 
### Eagle’s Eye
Eagle’s Eye is named that because its gem golem manifestation is one of a few to use wing-like structures. It follows the basic quartzite shape being quite similar to the Tiger’s Eye golem, but it has large undefined wing-like structures that come from its back. These wing structures are large enough to almost encase the golem fully. The wings are mobile and sharp.
### Prasiolite
Prasiolite is rare, so its gem golem is powerful. It’s a sort of light greenish gem. Also, it can be manufactured by heat treating amethysts, but the conversion is rare and uses a specific heating method. Prasiolite is the strongest Quartzite. The golem has six limbs, two which appear as feet and four as arms, which protrude out of the same spot on the body. Its head is similar to the Amethyst golem, being a mess of spiky star-like structures. The head floats from the body, but often stays close to where the neck might be. The Prasiolite golem has extremely powerful magic and can use some form of telekinesis by moving parts of its floating head to use as floating needles and swords.
## Garnet
Ah, Garnet. Finally we have arrived at the humanoid gem golems. Garnet golems are all characterised by this appearance, and the material of their gem defines it. As we know, garnets have an X Y style formula. The material in their Y slot defines their shape and their form. The X slot only defines their characteristics. Having Aluminium in their Y slot tends to give them an average humanoid figure, which varies on gender presentation. Iron in the Y slot gives them a bulkier figure, that again varies on gender presentation. Chromium in the Y slot seems to give them a powerful but thin non-binary figure. Calcium in the X site shows that it is in the Ugrandite group of garnet golems. The Ugrandite golems don’t have much going for them, and are often seen as the regular form of their Y slot’s mineral. Combinations of gems are also possible in the Garnet series. When cast, they seem to have a mix of abilities from both gems in the combination, and their appearance is often a mix or a median of both appearances.
### Almandine
Almandine has aluminium in its Y slot and iron in its X slot. The aluminium gives it a regular humanoid appearance. The Iron seems to give it a sword-like structure in one hand. As expected, this golem’s main method of attacking during combat is using this sword-like structure. Strangely, this golem seems to gain skill the more it is used. Its fighting style adapts with this learning method in the same way. 
### Pyrope
Pyrope has aluminium in its Y slot and magnesium in its X slot. The aluminium gives it a regular humanoid appearance. The Magnesium gives it access to rudimentary magical fire effects. This golem during combat tends to stay away from enemies and use these magical effects. It can only use them so many times before it resorts to a hands-on fighting stile. Its hot to the touch and is known to burn when engaging in close quarters combat.

Iron Pyrope is a version of pyrope that has parts of its structure replaced by a garnet with calcium and ferrous iron in the X site. The additional iron seems to give it a similar ability to almandine where it manifests with a sword-like structure. This sword is known to scorch after cutting which does deal extra fire damage. The golem’s fire effects are also weakened.
### Spessartine
Spessartine has aluminium in its Y slot and manganese in its X slot. The aluminium gives it a regular humanoid appearance. The manganese in the golem gives it a few abilities. The first seems to be the ability to spontaneously create a javelin-like melee weapon, which it uses to engage in close quarters with. This seems to drain a lot from some pool of energy that the golem draws from. Spessartine golems are also able to use certain limited magical effects, like lightning or spike. These are weak. Spessartine golems are slightly weaker than other aluminium-based golems.
### Andradite
Andradite has iron in its Y slot and calcium in its X slot. The iron gives it a large and bulky almost gorilla-like appearance. The calcium denotes that it is an ugrandite. In combat, the Andradite golem’s main goal is to inhibit the perceived enemy, either by grabbing it or straight up bashing it. Andradite has special variations dependant on colour (the normal colour is a brown).

Demantoid Andradite is green in colour and is ferric. Demantoid is extremely rare, and the golem it manifests into can spontaneously grow vine-like gem structures from the ground. It is also hardier.

Melanite Andradite is black in colour. It is more brittle and has access to limited magical effects

Topazolite Andradite is yellow orange in colour and is rarer than demantoid. It has a single extremely powerful spheric fire attack that makes it recede after using it.
### Grossular
Grossular has aluminium in its Y slot and calcium in its X slot. The aluminium gives it a regular humanoid form. The calcium denotes that it is an ugrandite. In combat, grossular will pick up random objects in its surroundings and use them as weapons. It will specifically chose more effective weaponry if they are available, and seems to have no preference. It has some level of learning in it.

Hessonite golems are almost identical to Grossular but is slightly weaker. It has no advantages.

Tsavorite golems are identical to grossular in every way except that they often search for weapons they can throw.
### Uvarovite
Uvarovite has chromium in its Y slot and calcium in its X slot. The uvarovite golem is a small (about 1.5 meters tall) floating humanoid figure without distinguishable feet and hands. The calcium denotes that it is an ugrandite. In combat, uvarovite will be surprisingly mobile in air, and will use its sharp hands and feet as a easy close quarters weapon. It also is able to use magical effects to a minor but powerful degree. Uvarovite is quite rare, and its golem’s mobility pays of for whatever price was on the gemstone. 
### Knorringite
Knorringite is a very hard to get gemstone and often requires special methods to help procure it. It has chromium in its Y slot and magnesium in its X slot. The chromium gives it a floating humanoid figure, and unlike uvarovite golems, knorringite golems are often the average size of a dos. The presence of magnesium makes the Knorringite golem a spectacular user of magical fire effects. Knorringite golems are the most powerful Garnet golem listed here.
## Chalcedony
Chalcedony as a material is a mix between Quartz and Beryl, the specific beryl being Morganite. We’ve already covered the typical Beryl shape and the typical Quartz shape (although many gemstones do not chose to take that shape). Chalcedony golems sometimes do take a beryl shape, or a quartz shape, or a shape between the two, but they also take different shapes.

Chalcedony is a bit of a difficult topic to cover so I might leave it till later. It just reminds me of how much easier Garnet was to make.
## Corundum
Corundum has 2 gemstones assigned to it in my research, both of which are influential and rare. For the sake of interest, Rubies are actually just very saturated red sapphires. In gem golems, corundum gemstones manifest as dragon-like shapes (four-legged like the Welsh dragon) They do have wings, but don’t visibly use them when they fly.
### Ruby (Erythphire?)
Ruby is quite powerful (Erythphire is a made up name following similar naming conventions to Xanthphires and Porphyphires). Usually with its neck fully extended vertically it has a height of 5 meters. Ruby golems not only have access to physical attacks using their dragon form, but also have access to very powerful magical effects. Ruby golems are extremely hardy and hard to recede.

Rutilated Ruby not only has access to the signature spike attack that most rutilated gems have, but also contains an asterism and makes the golem significantly hardier.
### Sapphire (Kyanphire?)
Sapphires are also powerful and manifest in a similar way to ruby gems. Their dragon form is usually smaller, with an average height of about 3 meters. All other characteristics are the same or similar. Sapphires also use the same attacks, but their physical attacks are weaker as a consequence of them manifesting smaller. Sapphires can also be rutilated in the same way as rubies.
### Xanthphire (Yellow Sapphire)
Xanthphire is a made-up name for yellow, orange, or brown sapphires. In Wastelife, Xanthphire gemstones manifest themselves as small dragon about 1.5 meters tall. Despite this, they have powerful magical effects, namely touch ones. They are also quite agile, even when on the ground. Xanthphires can be rutilated in the same way as rubies.
### Porphyphire (Purple Sapphire)
Porphyphire is a made-up name for violet, purple, or any similar shade of sapphire. In Wastelife, Porphyphires are the largest of the corundum dragon-likes, being just over 6 meters high. The trade-of for their height is their hardiness, as they are not as sturdy as other corundum gems. Their magical attacks are also weaker. Their physical strength pays of for this.

Apart from a couple that I changed, like garnet and amethyst which were largely based on the gems in TES, I made a few pretty interesting golem shapes before this.
### Opal (Dark Flame)
Opal is a gemstone that exists. In another document, I have it described “This is a very useful but expensive golem. Their posture is that of a gorilla, with a curved back and strong front arms. Their attack is comparable to that of a decent fire ball clap, and they are weak to all types of magical attack except ice and water. They are resistant against physical attacks. The companion ingredient is Desert Jewel, and the guardian ingredient is New Ash. Do not cast with parsley, it will create a frenzy golem.” The way I handle ingredients is different, but I like these names that I could just make up on the go. It’s a lot harder to do that now ☹
### Jade
I haven’t mentioned jade at all in this document before. In the description, I call it a little goblin with a dagger that attacks with poison magic. They also do mirage and silence instant spells. Their companion ingredient was “Desert Hound Claws”, guardian was “Nightshade”, and parsley of all things made a rogue golem.
### Moonstone
Yet another thing that’s probably a gemstone not yet mentioned in this document. There’s a lot of them. Moonstone, in previous versions, took the shape of a warhorse, which as you might know don’t exist in Wastelife. It was highly resistant to all attacks but barely attacks itself. It’s a mount, basically. Hove attacks were available for frenzy golems. Companion was parsley.
###  Sapphire (OLD)
The old sapphire took the form of an angel with large wingspans. Obviously, it could fly. It had a strong light projectile, which might not be a spell available via clapping. It had a weak calm illusion clap, and restore attribute and restore mind for its creator. The golem was “extremely powerful” and weak only to dark projectiles, which I’d interpret as necrotic damage. Its raw ingredients were “glass and Heaven’s Ice”. Its companion was Enokitake and its guardian was Quartz.
### Diamond
Since diamonds are common in Wastelife, their golem was a small person holding a sword and shield, like a metre tall soldier. They sound like they were a glass cannon, with swords extremely sharp but bodies that were weak. They also have a weak light projectile. The companion was parsley and its guardian was “white cap”
