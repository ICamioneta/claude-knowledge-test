The Hidol is a multidimensional substrate to eternity. It consists of several n-dimensions. It can be mathematically modelled:


$$\boxed{ \mathcal{H} \text{ is the uncaused category.} \quad \mathsf{Type}_{\text{realm}} \subset \mathsf{Type} = \mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{H}). \quad \nexists; X \to \mathcal{H}. }$$

Something about the bases of the Hidol being defined by their structure; they are not numeric but structural themselves. The other Hidol bases are therefore different structures, many of which do not relate to realms. 

Let's define some set  $\Sigma$  of all possible structures, this set is uncountably infinite. 


NOTE anything beyond this point I do not like any more. 
$$
\mathcal{H}=\bigsqcup_{p\in \mathcal{B}}{V_p}
$$

$B$ is the manifold, the meta-structure of reality. The Hidol consists of elements $V_p\cong \mathbb{R}^n$, where there exists $n\in \mathbb{N}$ and $\mathbb{N}$ is the set of possible dimensions of vectors in $\mathcal{h}$

It's important to note that these are conceptual dimensions. Realms map dimensions of hte Hidol to space-time. All realms map a three-dimensional space and a one-dimensional time. Although they don't necessarily need to stick to these exact, the Eternals aren't really that creative with things (plus, they wouldn't be able to move things between their own realm and other realms). Similarly, they will always map their dimension of time to the one trustworthy ever-increasing time-vector to keep their realms in sync.

The mortal realm $\mathcal{M}\cong \mathbb{R}^3\times\mathbb{R}$

There is one suitable time dimension that the Mortal realm and the Eternal's realms share, often denoted as $\mathcal{t}\in \mathcal{B}$ . The mortal realm's space vector isn't that important in the grand scheme of things as it's one of many suitable three-dimensional vectors in $\mathcal{B}$.

There is a sort of translation that must occur to bring the 3-dimensional space vector into workable space. This space-mapping function can be denoted, and results in realms of several different shapes. The mortal realm is a generally relative 3-torus. 

The spacial translation:

$$
\Phi_\mathcal{M}:V_s\subset V_p \rightarrow \mathcal{M_\text{space}}
$$
Where each $\Phi_{\text{realm}}$ has its own spacial mapping. For the mortal realm:

$\Phi_\mathcal{M}:\mathbb{R}^3 \rightarrow \mathbb{T}^3$

The universal time dimension $\mathcal{t}$ mapped is simply denoted $\tau$. A general time mapping function $\tau (V_p), \text{for } V_p\cong \mathbb{R}^n, n = 1$. Again, there's no reason to get into time mappings where $n \ne 1$

The "Torfol" is a tensor object. Traditionally it is thought of as a magical field that every living thing has. However, within Eternity, it can be mathematically modelled.

If we denote the space vector of the realm as $V_s$ and the time vector of the realm as $V_t$ then any torfol existing within that realm can be denoted:

$$
\mathcal{T} = \left(V_s, V_t, \left\{V_q\right\}\right)
$$
where $q$ is the Torfol's set of relevant Hidol vectors. $V_q$ then is a set of relevant vectors. In study, only around 12 vectors are seriously useful, but many others remain. For simpler study, 8 vectors are most useful. For individual spells, only a few vectors may be relevant. $V_q$ is again several instances of $V_p$ from $\mathcal{H}$.

Torfol position and time is given with the appropriate dimensional mapping: 
$$
\Phi_\mathcal{M}(V_s), \tau(V_t) \text{ for } V_s, V_t \in \mathcal{T}
$$


The conditions for torfol interaction depend on $V_s$ (or a _region_ of $V_s$ that the torfol occupies) and $V_t$, so the actual effects produced in the mortal realm depend entirely on the vectors $V_q$ and their interactions. 

Torfol strength can be given by a function $\sigma(\mathcal{T})$.

There exists a comparability metric $\kappa(V_{qi}, V_{qj}) \in \mathbb{R}$ that defines the interaction strength of two interacting torfol. High values denote stronger effects, whereas zero or negative values denote little to no interactivity. This is also generally considered the maximum energy throughput of the spell effect.

 Hidol density is spatially dependent. Given a realm, a mapped location and mapped time, we can denote hidol density:

$$
\delta_{\mathcal{M}}(\Phi_\mathcal{M}(V_s), \tau)
$$

The actual throughput of any given interaction is limited by the torfol involved. All torfol involved, from $\mathcal{T_1}$ to $\mathcal{T}_n$:
$$
\sigma_\pi(\mathcal{T_1}, ..., \mathcal{T_n})
= \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n\left(
\delta_\mathcal{M}(\Phi(V_s), \tau)
\times\sigma(\mathcal{T})\right)}
{n}
$$
At the reaction location $V_s$ in the mortal realm (providing spacial mapping $\Phi_\mathcal{M}$ and time $\tau$) 

The actual manifestation of energy from the hidol into the realm depends on the projection, which can be denoted as follows. 

$$
\pi_\mathcal{M}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
$$
$$
\text{Effect} = \pi_\mathcal{M}(V_R))
$$
Where $V_R$ is the resultant vector from a reaction. This is the _type_ of the effect. Combined with magnitude, an actual idea of the full effect can be given.

There exists a bi-linear operator $\star$ producing $V_R$ from two sets of vectors. 

Mages, who cast spells by causing ingredient's torfol to interact, can calculate their effect given the two ingredient torfol's $V_q$ as $V_a$ and $V_b$.

$$
\text{Effect} = \pi_\mathcal{M}(V_a\star V_b)
$$
And the magnitude of this effect (the energy throughput) as:
$$
\sigma(V_a, V_b)
$$

Wizards, who use their torfol to enchant nonliving objects with a whole diversity of enchantments, physically transform their own torfol. They must "imagine" the constituent vectors of their torfol as something mentally tangible and will them to change. This is difficult, but doable, and eventually a wizard need not mentally touch the object they make torfol tangible as.

They move their $V_0$ towards a $V_\text{target}$ with the appropriate transformations of each relevant component, minimising distance from their $V_\text{evental}$ and $V_\text{target}$. There is some tolerance, but perfect alignment ensures the best manifestation of the target enchantment. Enchantment's maximum strength itself is defined by the eigenvalues of $V_\text{eventual}$, and will diminish the $\sigma(\mathcal{T})$ of the wizard.


Moving onto working out workable examples, each of the vectors in $V_p$ can be called _forms_. These forms are arbitrarily indexed. The first "8" most useful forms can be indexed 1 - 8:
$F_n$. The operation $\star$ on two sets of vectors where each vector is in its own $\mathbb{R}^n$.

The $\star$ operation on two sets of forms produces a matrix whose eigenvectors are used by the $\pi_\mathcal{M}$ function to determine effect.

The interaction matrix created for each formant by the $\star$ operator is calculated as follows. We can denote this the formant interaction function $f(F_a, F_b)$.
$$
F_{nc}=\frac{1}{2}\left(F_{na}\otimes F_{nb}+F_{nb}\otimes F_{na}\right)
$$
so for two sets of dimensions $V_a$ and $V_b$ each containing some set of formants, 

$$
V_a \star V_b = M_{ab} = \{F_{1a}, F_{2a},... F_{na}\}\star\{F_{1b}, F_{2b},... F_{nb}\} = \{F_{1c}, F_{2c},... F_{nc}\}
$$
where we use the equation above $f(F_a, F_b)$ to calculate each individual resulting matrix.

The eigen decomposition of each $F_{nc}$ gives:
$$
F_{nc}=\sum_k \lambda_k^{(n)}\textbf{e}_k^{(n)}\left(\textbf{e}_k^{(n)}\right)^\top
$$

## Formants Design

I really want the formants to have vague meaningless names that I can interpret however I want. The "Crombulence" formant might not literally mean anything, but crombulence might coincide with some effects. 

For this i should really discuss what i want from the vectors. What the actual "effects" are. Then I can backwards-infer the formants.

**Offensive Effects**
The traditional attacking spells are cultured manifestations of Hidol energy.

"fire"

Fire spells are hidol manifestation into combustion and usually some combusting or burning material. The exact material isn't (usually) natural but some unique hidol-formed chemical structure. It's probably some hydrocarbon chain, but i'd want it to be unstable enough to decompose into natural chemicals if manifested without combustion.

For research look at "Silane". A "combustion" 

"Ice"

Ice spells are unique manifestations that actually unmanifest energy. The amount of energy taken away is rather small, since lots of the energy form produced by the spell is in the exact shape and form of the cold. More unique projectiles like "ice spikes" usually manifest some other unstable chemical that breaks down into gasses quickly once melted. In a nice world, this could be the same chemical that the fire spells combust. Raw directed "cold" is likely different though. 

For research, perhaps ammonium nitrate.

"Lightning"

The manifestation of the Hidol as directed electrical energy is likely in a surplus of electrons (since a deficit would probably cause other issues). The direction method is likely the same type of thing that directs cold. Then, this natural electron movement is the lightning / electrical discharge. When weaker, these spells would likely be invisible. They would require some significant power to be visible at all. 

"Air"

Probably best called force, this is likely just the hidol manifestation causing some physical turbulence or movement to a degree that's quite significant, enough to push an unprepared foe from their feet. Such a force might apply to gasses, but I'm sure any liquid would be fine. Alternatively, the effect could be to push smaller solid particles or render weaker structures. 

"Sharp"

The Hidol manifesting as solid unstable sharp objects is the hardest to literally imagine, but alas. I've wanted it to be some material that dissolves quickly into nothingness after contact, but is a solid at room temperature (unlike the material involved in ice spells). 

**The Other Spells**

In my previous D&D-adapted rules, I had three "schools" of spells. Destruction is the easiest translated, but the others might have some value still. 

"illusion" included things like calm, frenzy, muddle, paralyse, mirage, silence, sound. These might be harder to justify in a more material manner. Similarly, "detriment" were sort of stat detriments to targets. Their names were generalised into "dumben", "slow", "anxious", and more mechanics-focussed things like "miss" and "disadvantage"

I think in this rework more materially-based spells other than the "destruction" ones would be nice, or even some new destruction ones coming out of the framework i'm building from how the existing destruction spells work. 

**More Destruction**

"Acid"

Could be a very simple material manifestation like fire. May even sit between fire and the sharp-material who knows.

"Heat"

If fire isn't self-combusting (or if some elements of the "destruction spell" aren't) then directed heat much like directed cold may be necessary. It would also be both funny and fitting to be able to heat up a tea with a spell.

**Potential Manifestations**

Here are some misc ideas on how else the Hidol could manifest in a spell.

Foam. A rapidly expanding polymer or other form of foam. Something like pyrophoric foam. Expanding and occupying, decomposing slowly into some gas. Maybe combined with other ideas for a highly combustible foam that lightly scorches, or even a sharp or acidic foam. 

Oxidising. Just playing on the more chemistry-focused ideas of this system, some manifestation that rapidly causes oxidisation could puff snow or make flames burn brighter. At its best it may corrode metals and decay organic material.

Force generally covers this, but a separation between the sustained and the instant gives force some texture. It goes from a brutal gust of wind to a violent pressure wave.

Light manipulation is another fun idea. One angle is the creation of light, like photon beams that at high intensities may do whatever materially makes sense. The opposite may be employed, clouds of light-absorbing gas. 

Adhesive. A more long-lasting manifestation maybe. It would have much larger implications for the world than the other spells, but making it a non-permanent strong bond may be a way to still promote future normal adhesives

Desiccant would be an interesting effect, but it would need some chemical meaning. Like, the manifestation would be of some real desiccant in the air in dust form. It would be quite devastating, but potentially balanced by difficulty or strength, or through other limiting factors.

A lubricant is another material manifestation. It wouldn't be too world-changing as it'd decompose too fast for any industrial or even commercial purpose, but since it could be projected as a spell it may come in handy tactically during combat. The event may just arise where some adventurers would need some good lube. 

Smoke, similar to darkness, may be useful too. Less particles that literally absorb light and more just a genuinely dense fog compound. With spell projection, it could be distributed usefully. Unlike most other spells, this may even continue to be useful into a period of modern warfare. 

Curiously, this framework supports one of the first things spells ever did in Wastelife, that I later abandoned because it felt more an enchantment thing. A spell that conjures a temporary hard wall that collapses under damage and time. The material of the wall would likely be light, durable, and collapse within an hour or a few. 

Sleep spells might very simply be an anaesthetic gas or compound. 

A lock spell could cause some rapid crystallisation of a salt on some target surface. Undoubtedly could bung up hinges for some time, or any moving mechanism. I'm sure it'd also have some effect on people. 

We may even revisit the "illusion" spells by imagining some compounds that increase adrenaline or induce other hormonal changes. I'm less sure about the actual chemistry of this though.

Although there is no technical upper bound to how many axes any of the Hidol things have, it is "easier" to find lower dimension thingies. The median is 3. If you want a reason, $\pi$ or something. 

Theoretically, I can just keep on adding formants. But I think after a point it gets hard to design. I think 8 is enough for most spells, with 4 more formants for more exotic effects if necessary. The rest of the potentially infinite design space can just be unused for this specific system. 

Enchantments then have the adaptive torfol shift along different axes. They are also dimensions of the hidol in the same way formants are, but since the fundamental system is different and yet developed I'll leave it there. 

Gem Golems can occupy their own formats too. Casting gem golems then comes to finding which ingredients have a formant that aligns with that specific gem golem's formant. These formants won't be of the 12 "indexed" formants listed above.
